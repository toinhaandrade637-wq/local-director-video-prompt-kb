We launched an AI influencer account, and within seven days it had attracted thousands of followers. Brands were already reaching out to collaborate.




That’s what makes this worth exploring. You can build a recognizable character, grow a page around it, and use that attention to introduce products. Or start with a business and create an AI host specifically for its content. You can also sell this work as a service, making hosts and videos for other businesses.




We’ll show you how to do it with Higgsfield and Claude: create a character, turn a trend into your own video, add a product, and build an original beauty ad. Along the way, we’ll explain the choices that make the videos worth watching.

Before you start

We’ll begin in Higgsfield AI Influencer and make the first video in Genjutsu. Then we’ll connect Claude through Higgsfield MCP, which lets it run Higgsfield’s tools from the chat.




Have clear product photos ready for the advertising examples. We’ll create the other references as we go, including the character sheets that let us bring the same people back in different scenes.

1. Create a character people remember

Our first character is a gym guy with proportions you can’t ignore. Before he even moves, you have an idea of who he is. That gives us something to work with: push his strength to a ridiculous extreme, or have him do something completely unexpected.




Open AI Influencer and choose a template. There are everyday-looking characters and more exaggerated options. Adjust the face, hair, body and clothes, or upload your own photo as a starting point.

Once you like the result, save the character sheet. It gives you a face and full-body reference for future generations. We’ll keep using ours so the guy in the next video looks like the one people already know.

2. Turn a familiar scene into a video worth sharing

A big character standing in a gym is a picture. Give him a gorilla to squat with, and there’s something to watch.




We use a simple checklist to develop ideas like this. It’s called STOP:

Surprise: What stops someone scrolling?
Tension: What makes them want to see what happens next?
Outcome: What’s the payoff?
Pass it on: Who would they send it to?

The gorilla catches your eye. Then you want to see whether our guy can actually finish the rep. He stands back up, and you’ve got something to send your gym buddy. We’ve kept the exercise familiar and changed one thing that makes the whole scene surprising.

Genjutsu lets you adapt an existing clip by changing the character or adding something unexpected. You can start with a built-in trend template or use your own reference.

3. Monetize your AI influencer with product integrations

Attention becomes commercially useful when there’s something relevant to offer. Our gym character could feature a sportswear brand, promote clothes from our own store, or direct viewers to a workout course or app.




You can apply the same approach to beauty or fashion. A makeup video can send viewers straight to the lipstick shade they’ve just seen. An outfit video can link to the collection it features. Choose the product to fit the audience, then make it easy to find.

4. Build an AI host around a brand

A brand can also have a character designed around its products from the start. We’ll use a makeup ad to show how that works.




Just putting an AI face into a familiar format doesn’t give people much reason to stop scrolling. Before creating the host, decide what the product contributes to the story:

Audience insight: Our viewer wants makeup ideas for a night out.
Brand expertise: If the lipstick has been tested for long wear, that’s a benefit we can build the concept around.
Angle: Our host needs to get ready for a date and chooses the worst possible place to do her makeup.
Format: A getting-ready video lets her use the lipstick throughout the story.

So we made a grandma who talks like she runs BeautyTok and put her on a roller coaster. She’s more worried about getting her lipstick right than the drop. Here’s the finished ad:

“My date’s in ten minutes. I’m not showing up without lipstick.” That gives her a reason to keep going once the ride starts.




The contrast between her appearance and her personality delivers the surprise. The moving coaster creates tension: can she finish her makeup? The mirror check gives us the outcome. For a lipstick with verified long wear, that ending ties the joke to the product benefit.




Then she delivers the last line: “Now for the scary part. The date.” If you’ve got a friend who does her makeup absolutely anywhere, you know who you’re sending it to. That’s STOP working inside a brand video.

5. Turn the workflow into a service

You don’t need your own product or a large audience to get started with client work. Make a concept ad for a new product launch and post it on your page, along with a quick look at how you made it. Label it as your creative take on the launch.




That gives a brand something specific to react to: its product in a campaign it could actually imagine running. Build a series of these posts and your page becomes a portfolio. A video that takes off might get you noticed by the featured brand; the same work can also help you approach other businesses.




When there’s interest, agree on the concept and price for a first project. If the work goes well, bring the host back for the next launch. You already have the character and know the brand, so you can spend more of your time on the next idea.




There’s room to work with several clients this way, each with its own host. Creating videos like these with actors, locations and a crew would be a substantial production. With saved references and this workflow, you can keep trying ideas you’d normally rule out as too difficult to shoot. That’s how one character can become the start of a production business.

Start with one character

Create your first character sheet, put it into a Genjutsu template, and give the scene one unexpected twist. You’ll have a video to build on—and a much clearer idea of what you want to make next.
