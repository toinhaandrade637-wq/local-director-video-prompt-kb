What AI Video Restoration Can Fix

Old footage breaks down in predictable ways. Resolution goes soft, compression adds artifacts from repeated re-encoding, color fades or shifts, and analog sources pick up flicker and grain. Faces and textures lose detail the more a file gets copied. AI restoration targets each of these separately, not with one blanket filter across the whole clip.

What to Do Before You Restore Old Footage

Track down the best available source file before restoring anything. A copy that's already been re-compressed several times carries artifacts that restoration has to fight against. The original file, or the closest thing to it, gives noticeably better results.
Keep the original frame rate and aspect ratio intact going in. Changing either before restoration adds an extra layer of interpretation for the model to work through, on top of the actual damage already in the footage.
Watch for footage that's been heavily filtered or stylized already. A clip with a strong color grade or an intentional grain effect applied on top of the original damage can confuse what restoration is supposed to fix versus what's a deliberate creative choice.

What Makes a Good AI Video Restoration Result?

Natural detail, not an oversharpened, artificial texture that looks obviously processed.

Stable motion, no new flicker or jitter introduced by the restoration pass itself.

Clean image, free of the noise, blocking, and compression artifacts the source started with.

Preserved identity, faces and objects that still look like themselves, not reinterpreted into something generic.

No obvious artifacts, nothing that reads as clearly AI-generated sitting on top of otherwise real footage.
