Pro Tip #1: Multi-Face Videos

At the moment, 
Higgsfield’s Character and Face Swap features
 are at their peak productivity in the case that a user uploads a reference with
 only one person clearly visible in the frame. 

If your reference video has multiple faces, the AI will almost always prioritize the 
closest 
one, meaning that this swap will target this specific character.

So keep in mind that in case of two people, only the one closest to camera will be swapped. For both swap accuracy and consistency of skin texture, I highly recommend: 

Keep your scene simple

Frame on main character 

Remove or blur everyone else

Pro Tip #2: Skip the White Background

Here’s something most users don’t realize: a pure white background on input image can actually hurt your win rate, which is, in simple terms, the ability of the model to blend your character naturally into the scene, and this happens because white lacks depth cues. The 
AI Image Generator
 interprets it as a flat, undefined environment, which makes shadows, edges, and reflections harder to match.

So, whenever possible, use a 
soft-colored or textured
 background instead. Even a simple wall or room adds points for realistic integration.

ai photo ai video faceswap

Pro Tip #3: Keep Hands Empty

Empty hands. If the character in your input image or video is holding something tangible (not your grudges) – like a cup, bag, or a phone, you may expect a surprise.  

The swap engine reads these as part of the body geometry, and can project them onto your final clip.

The fix is simple: 
choose a clean reference
. Avoid scenes where the actor interacts with props, especially if you’re aiming for a professional or commercial output.

Pro Tip #4: Match the Color Palette

To achieve the most natural integration, make sure that your 
source photo
 and 
reference video
 share similar tones.
If your original image has warm lighting and the reference is cold or neon-lit, the skin texture might not align perfectly. In other words, harmony in color grading = harmony in realism.

Pro Tip #5: Capture Smartly, Not Sideways

Your input video matters more than you think.
Always shoot yourself with a 
frontal camera
 and not from the side. This allows the model to clearly map your facial geometry.

Avoid fast, abrupt movements like head turns or active turning around or acrobatics. Subtle continuous motion (walking, sitting, looking around) produces the most cinematic and coherent output.

And if you’re filming yourself sitting, I recommend choosing a 
chair without a high back
.
It keeps your body outline clean and lets the 
AI Video Generator
 map posture naturally without distortion.

Pro Tip #6: Keep the Face Clear and Unobstructed

The AI needs to see the face clearly, especially in the first frame. Objects like hands or microphones covering the face, or extreme facial contortions, can cause the replacement to fail.

Pro Tip #7: The Importance of Proportional Matching

Avoid swapping characters with drastically different physical builds (e.g., a very large or high person for a very petite one). Mismatched proportions will likely cause visual distortion and low-quality results.
