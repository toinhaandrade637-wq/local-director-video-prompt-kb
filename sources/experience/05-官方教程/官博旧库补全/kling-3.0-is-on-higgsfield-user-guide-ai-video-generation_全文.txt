Kling 4.0 and 3.0 Prompt Guide: 10 Prompts and Settings That Work (2026)

Higgsfield

Oct 25, 2026

6 min

Kling 4.0 and Kling 3.0 are both available on Higgsfield, and they suit different tasks. Kling 4.0 generates videos up to 30 seconds in a single generation, with up to 10 keyframes, automatic multi-shot and video editing. Kling 3.0 is built for clips up to 15 seconds, with up to 5 shots, Elements for consistent characters and native audio. This guide shows how to write prompts for both models, which settings to choose, and 10 prompts with results you can copy.

TL;DR

Use Kling 4.0 for scenes longer than 15 seconds, for exact moments set with keyframes, and for changes in a finished clip. Use Kling 3.0 for multi-shot clips up to 15 seconds with native audio.

Start with the subject, action and camera. For complex scenes, add timing, references, lighting, audio and locks.

For Kling 4.0, test at 720p before the final version. For Kling 3.0, use Turbo for faster drafts.

Kling 4.0 or Kling 3.0: Which One Should You Use?

Kling 4.0 is the newer model: longer clips, keyframes and video editing. Kling 3.0 is still a good choice for short multi-shot clips with sound.

How Do You Write a Prompt for Kling?

Start by describing the subject, action and camera movement. For more complex scenes, add timing, references, lighting, audio and what should stay consistent. You don’t need every block for a simple shot.

How Do You Write a Prompt for Kling?

Block

What to write

Scene

What happens and how long the clip is, in one or two sentences

References

Who or what comes from a reference, tagged with @, and what must match it

Camera

Lens, distance, one camera move per shot

Action timing

What happens at each second, for example 0–2s, 2–4.5s

Physics

How bodies, cloth, hair and objects should move

Lighting

Light source, direction and palette

Audio

What we should hear, or “No music, no dialogue”

Locks

What must not happen: no logos, no text, no extra people

For Kling 3.0:

Keep each shot focused on one main action and camera move. If you need three events, split them into shots in Custom mode.

Tag the same element in every shot where it appears, and describe only its action: its look comes from the element.

With an end frame, describe motion that can arrive at that frame.

For Kling 4.0:

Choose the Kling 4.0 mode based on what you want to control:

Frames:
 keyframe images set specific moments in the video.

References:
 images, videos, Elements and voice references guide characters, movement and style.

Edit video:
 upload an existing clip and describe what should change.

Then:

With keyframes, let the keyframes set the key moments, and use the prompt to describe the action and the camera between them.

If something doesn’t look right, change one thing at a time: the prompt, a reference or the duration.

In Edit video, describe what should change and say what should stay the same, such as the character, background, movement or lighting.

1. Dialogue in two languages: a French and English street scene

Twelve seconds and five shots, with three references and two characters who speak different languages.

SCENE CONTEXT

A 12-second cinematic short-form drama on a quiet city sidewalk at night, in front of a small corner shop whose storefront glows under a red fascia: the girl walks in tense and guarded to face a curly-haired guy waiting by the planters — a confrontation across a language gap: she speaks French, he speaks English, and neither really speaks the other&#x27;s language. They get by on a few shared words, gestures and tone — until he opens his hand and reveals her lost vintage wristwatch, which needs no translation. Anger cracks into disbelief, then into fragile warmth; the ending holds the two of them small against the glowing storefront on the empty street. Sparse natural dialogue, real voices, no speeches. Filmic, intimate, handheld.

ACTIVE REFERENCES

<<<image_1>>> (the guy): late-teens man, loose brown curls, fair skin with a few small blemishes, soft friendly features now held tense. Identity AND wardrobe inherited from the reference as-is: a washed-red duck-canvas work jacket open over a navy hoodie with the hood lying flat inside the collar, medium-blue straight jeans, chunky cream sneakers — all unbranded, no lettering anywhere on the clothes. Face 100% matches the reference, ALIVE every frame — blinks, swallows, held breath, guilt-to-hope micro-expressions. VOICE: a real young American male voice, speaking English only — light, unpolished, a little tight in the throat at first, loosening at the end; natural pacing with hesitations, slowing down and simplifying his words when he sees she doesn&#x27;t follow, never announcer-smooth. He does not understand French.

<<<image_2>>> (the girl): late-teens/early-20s European girl, long straight light-brown hair loose under a brown houndstooth-check baseball cap, chunky grey translucent rectangular glasses, soft features set hard. Identity AND wardrobe inherited from the reference as-is: silver wired earbuds hanging OFF her ears around her neck, cable running to her right front jeans pocket; oversized boxy white tee with its faded navy abstract emblem print and small red circular stamp (stamp lettering unreadable), plaid burgundy boxer waistband over a brown croc-texture belt, baggy faded dirty-wash jeans, grey suede sneakers, silver rings. Her LEFT wrist is BARE — the vintage rectangular watch from her reference is the story prop in HIS hand. Face 100% matches the reference, ALIVE every frame — blinks, jaw tension, welling micro-emotions. VOICE: a real young French female voice, speaking French with natural native pronunciation — low, clipped and guarded at first, cracking slightly on the turn, warm and quiet at the end; her two English words are spoken with a clear French accent. She understands only a few simple English words.

The watch (story prop, from her reference): a small vintage rectangular watch — silver case, cream rectangular dial, slim dark-brown leather strap; unbranded, dial text unreadable.

<<<image_3>>> (location): the night storefront street — inherited exactly AS IS: a pale vertical-siding building with dark upper windows; a long glossy red fascia band with a plain white ring emblem; under it a lit storefront of steel-framed glass — a glass double door showing a counter with red stools inside, a window of shelves on the left, wide frosted glass panes glowing on the right; two wooden planter boxes of green shrubs and small pink flowers on the sidewalk in front of the door; grey brick-paved sidewalk; a dark two-lane road with white lane lines in the foreground; to the right a darker apartment building with balconies, a black fence and a light-blue street lamp with a cool white head. ALL posters, signs and any writing in the windows render as unreadable shapes — no readable text anywhere. The shop interior is empty.

Exactly two people in the location; nobody else, no cars passing.

LOCATION MAP

Per the reference geography: the storefront runs across the frame along the sidewalk, red fascia above, the glass door and planters at the center-left, the frosted windows center-right, the apartment building, fence and blue street lamp at the far right, the empty road in the foreground. The GUY waits standing on the sidewalk just right of the planters, in front of the frosted windows, one hand closed at his side, facing frame-right toward the street lamp side. The GIRL enters along the sidewalk from frame-right, from under the street lamp, and crosses to face him at one meter. AXIS LOCK: their eyeline runs along the sidewalk, planters-to-lamp; in every two-shot and over-shoulder the guy holds frame-left, the girl frame-right; screen direction never flips.

FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING

First frame (Segment 1): a slow-burn handheld wide from chest height on the road edge, facing the storefront — the storefront glowing under the red fascia, the guy standing beside the planters at x 38%, y 52%, weight uneasy, closed fist at his side, the frosted windows lighting him from behind; the girl entering along the sidewalk at the frame&#x27;s right edge at x 78%, mid-step under the cool street lamp, cap low, arms crossing as she walks. Both visible from frame one.

FORMAT MODE

Controlled five-segment sequence with HARD CUTS at 2.5s / 5.0s / 7.5s / 9.5s. Real-time speed inside every segment — no slow motion. Continuity is continuous action and continuous sound: each segment resumes the exact state of the previous cut.

OPTICS

One physical lens character per segment, honest filmic depth. LENS LOCK SEGMENT 1 = 47° wide two-shot. SEGMENT 2 = 29° over her shoulder onto him. SEGMENT 3 = 29° reverse, over his shoulder onto her, the opening hand large in the near frame. SEGMENT 4 = 29° lateral tight two-faces profile. SEGMENT 5 = 47° pulled-back low wide. No zooms anywhere.

CAMERA

TRUE HANDHELD in every segment — quiet documentary weight: breathing pulse, micro-shake, small human corrections, one soft settle after each cut as the operator finds the frame; no gimbal float, no whips, no crash zooms:

S1 — chest height on the road edge, side-on to their meeting line, watching her cross to him.

S2 — over her right shoulder ON the axis, his face carrying the frame, the planters and lit door soft behind him.

S3 — reverse over his left shoulder ON the axis, her face carrying the frame, his hand in the near foreground, the street lamp and dark apartment block soft behind her.

S4 — lateral tight profile two-shot from the road side, both faces a meter apart, the frosted windows glowing behind them.

S5 — pulled back low-wide from across the road: the pair small mid-frame on the sidewalk against the whole glowing storefront and red fascia, the dark empty road in the foreground.

ACTION TIMING

SEGMENT 1, 0.0s–2.5s (47° wide): the approach — she walks along the sidewalk in hard clipped steps, arms crossed, cap brim low; he straightens up beside the planters, swallowing, fist tightening. At 1.2s she stops one meter from him and says flat, guarded, in French: « Bon. Pourquoi tu m&#x27;as fait venir ? » His brow knots, his head tilts a few degrees — he didn&#x27;t catch it. Blinks: her 0.8s, him 1.7s — CUT

SEGMENT 2, 2.5s–5.0s (29° on him): the guilt and the gap — his eyes drop to his fist, back up to her; a hard swallow; at 3.0s he starts in English, slow and simplified, voice tight: "Sorry, I don&#x27;t— okay. I... found something." He taps his own chest on "I", and his closed hand rises slowly between them into the lower frame. At 4.4s she answers off-screen, dry, catching just the one word and mixing it into French: « Found... quoi ? » His face eases a fraction — she got that part. Blinks: him 3.3s and 4.6s — CUT

SEGMENT 3, 5.0s–7.5s (29° on her, the reveal): his hand OPENS in the near foreground — the vintage watch on his palm, strap curled, dial catching the storefront light. No words needed; her face detonates in micro-emotion: eyes flying wide behind the glasses, lips parting, a sharp inhale; at 6.2s, barely voiced, cracking in the middle, in French: « C&#x27;est pas vrai... Où est-ce que— » and the sentence just stops. Blinks: her 5.6s and 7.0s, each visibly resetting her face softer — CUT

SEGMENT 4, 7.5s–9.5s (29° profile two-faces): the thaw — she takes the watch with both hands, thumb brushing the dial, and looks up at him with a questioning lift of the chin; he reads the question from her face, not her words. At 8.1s he says quietly, half-smiling, slow and simple, in English: "Park. By the... courts." — and mimes a small one-handed basketball shot with his free hand. She gets it: a shaky exhale turns into the smallest laugh, a tiny nod. Blinks: her 7.9s, him 9.0s — CUT

SEGMENT 5, 9.5s–12.0s (47° wide, the ending): the breath — pulled back and low from across the road: the two of them small and warm mid-frame on the sidewalk, the storefront glowing behind them under the red fascia, the blue street lamp at the right, the dark road empty in front; she fastens the watch onto her bare left wrist, shakes her head in soft disbelief, and at 10.2s says quiet, warm, almost to herself, in French: « T&#x27;es vraiment un idiot. » He clearly doesn&#x27;t understand the words but gets the tone, and shrugs with a crooked smile. At 11.2s she adds in English with a strong French accent: "Thank you." — and bumps her fist lightly against his jacket shoulder; he rocks with it, grinning at the pavement, one breathy laugh. Blinks: her 10.0s, him 11.6s. END on the held wide — quiet, warm, resolved in faces.

PHYSICS

Real intimate mechanics: her clipped walk carries true weight on the brick paving, sneaker soles scuffing; arms cross and uncross with honest joint motion; his chest tap and the small basketball mime are loose and natural, not theatrical; his fist opens finger by finger, the watch&#x27;s slim strap uncurling with real leather stiffness, the case rocking a millimeter on his palm; the take is two-handed and careful, the strap fastening with a tiny believable buckle fumble; the fist-bump lands soft with canvas compression. MATERIAL TRUTH — the pair belongs to the street&#x27;s light but never to its surfaces: washed duck canvas, heavy cotton hoodie, denim, suede and jersey knit all read with their own distinct weave, nap and wear against the street&#x27;s frosted glass, brushed steel window frames, glossy red fascia, rough wooden planters, grey paving bricks and dark asphalt — fabric folds softly where the street&#x27;s materials stay rigid; nothing about the clothes mimics or melts into the set. Lips sync exactly to the scripted lines in their own language and stay STILL when not speaking — no idle mouth movement. FACE PRIORITY: both faces carry continuous living micro-motion — mandatory visible blinks at every timecoded beat AND naturally between (never more than ~1.5s without a blink on the face carrying the frame), swallows, breath, welling eyes, trembling lip corners, the small frowns and eased brows of half-understanding, the full anger-to-warmth arc in micro-expressions — zero frozen or waxwork frames; critical. Skin matte and pore-real on both, no beauty smoothing.

LIGHTING

Per the reference, night, and it UNIFIES the pair with the street: the storefront is the key — the warm-white light strip under the red fascia and the glowing frosted windows wrap both of them in soft light from the shop side, faces fully readable; the red fascia adds a faint red glow on the tops of their heads, her cap and his shoulders; the light-blue street lamp at the right lays a cool white rim on their far edges and catches the lenses of her glasses; the sidewalk bricks hold a pool of storefront light that falls off to darkness at the road. One continuous light world, characters and street lit as a single whole while every material keeps its own honest texture response: matte cotton drinking the light the glass reflects, denim holding shadow where steel frames spark. Clean catchlights in both pairs of eyes so every blink reads; filmic contrast, true night shadows, the road and upper building dark but not crushed, no vignette, no stylized grade, no glow effects.

AUDIO

One continuous diegetic bed across all cuts: quiet night street tone — the soft electrical hum of the storefront lights, a refrigerator compressor faintly through the glass, distant traffic far off, a faint train passing somewhere at 6.8s, leaves stirring in the planters — her clipped steps on the paving, his swallow close in S2, the leather creak of the uncurling strap, her sharp inhale at the reveal, the shaky exhale-laugh, the buckle&#x27;s soft click, the canvas thump of the fist-bump, his breathy laugh. DIALOGUE: only the seven scripted lines, timed as written — hers in natural native French except "Found" and "Thank you", which she says in English with a French accent; his in natural American English, slowed and simplified. Real voices, conversational volume, imperfect and human, open-air outdoor acoustics with a slight reflection off the shop glass, ambient dipping gently under each line; no extra words, no ad-libs, no translation, no narration, no music, no subtitles.

POSITIVE LOCKS

Exactly five segments with hard cuts at the stated timecodes — real-time inside each, straight cuts only. Axis lock holds the entire edit: the guy frame-left, the girl frame-right in every two-shot; screen direction never flips. Both stay on the sidewalk in front of the storefront for the whole scene; the road stays empty; the shop interior stays empty. Exactly two people in every frame possible, faces 100% matching their references. The girl speaks French and the guy speaks English for the whole scene; they understand each other only partly — through a few shared words, gestures, the watch and tone — and their faces show it: his confused head tilt in S1, his relief at her "Found" in S2, her reading his mime in S4, his shrug at her French in S5. Only the seven scripted lines are spoken, exactly as written and in the stated language, lips synced, mouths still otherwise. The watch is the only story prop: unbranded, dial unreadable, absent from her wrist until she fastens it in the final segment; all clothing unbranded with no lettering. The fascia carries only the plain white ring emblem; all posters, signs and window writing unreadable — no readable text or subtitles anywhere. CRITICAL FACE LOCK: both faces continuously alive with visible blinks at the timecoded beats and naturally throughout, full micro-expression arcs — no frozen faces, no mannequin stillness, not for one frame.

2. Six references in one static shot: a busy Tokyo crossing

Ten seconds with a locked-off camera, a crowd, and characters and creatures from six references.

SCENE CONTEXT

A vivid, magical-realism moment at a big Tokyo crossing on a sunny afternoon, filmed as one static wide shot. The pedestrian light is green and a colourful crowd of all kinds of people — and a few bright, friendly creatures — streams across the zebra crossing in both directions. A blue-haired guy walks with his pink pet axolotl, a mint fox spirit trots through the foreground, a rider in a fox-eared helmet waits on her scooter, and above the street a colossal tangerine koi swims slowly through the air beneath the elevated expressway with a courier sitting on its back. Everyone treats it all as perfectly normal. Photorealistic, cheerful, full of life.

ACTIVE REFERENCES

<<<image_1>>>: THE CROSSING — the bright Tokyo intersection: the wide zebra crossing with the red-painted band in the foreground, the massive elevated expressway sweeping across the upper right on grey pillars, the orange bus and the red-and-cream bus stopped under it, the rounded glass building with the turquoise LED screen, the abstract billboard and the striped shop awning on the left, the traffic lights, the towers and the blue sky with white clouds. 100% matches the reference.

<<<image_2>>>: THE GIANT KOI — the colossal flying koi carp, about fifteen metres long: tangerine-orange and pearl-white scales with cobalt-blue patches, long translucent silky fins and a huge flowing tail, long barbels, calm golden-amber eyes. 100% matches the reference.

<<<image_3>>>: THE PINK AXOLOTL — the dog-sized hot-pink pet axolotl walking on four short legs, with frilly magenta gills, a gentle smile and black bead eyes. 100% matches the reference.

<<<image_4>>>: THE MINT FOX SPIRIT — the large mint-teal fox with a cream chest, three bushy tails with coral-pink tips, coral-lined ears and amber eyes. 100% matches the reference.

<<<image_5>>>: THE BLUE-HAIRED GUY — the young Japanese man with spiky cobalt-blue hair, an oversized white t-shirt with red sleeves, very baggy charcoal cargo trousers, a silver chain and chunky white-and-grey sneakers. 100% matches the reference.

<<<image_6>>>: THE RIDER — the young Japanese woman with cherry-red hair in a glossy white full-face helmet with two fox-ear shapes and an amber visor, an olive-green bomber jacket with a round white patch, black cargo trousers, black boots and riding gloves. 100% matches the reference.

FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING

The exact <<<image_1>>> view, with the scene already in motion:

- FOREGROUND LEFT (x 28%, y 70%): the blue-haired guy (<<<image_5>>>) walks across the zebra crossing from left to right in 3/4 view, the pink axolotl (<<<image_3>>>) waddling right beside him at his knee.
- FOREGROUND BOTTOM CENTRE (x 45%, y 88%): the mint fox spirit (<<<image_4>>>) trots across the bottom of the frame from right to left, close to the camera, its three tails swaying.
- FOREGROUND RIGHT (x 72%, y 72%): the rider (<<<image_6>>>) sits astride a white retro scooter with a blue top box behind the stop line, seen from behind at a 3/4 angle, one foot on the ground, waiting at the light.
- MIDGROUND, UNDER THE EXPRESSWAY (x 50%, y 35%): the giant koi (<<<image_2>>>) swims slowly through the air from right to left between the pillars, about six metres above the road, below the deck of the expressway. A courier in a dark blue jacket and a cap sits on its back with a big hot-pink delivery backpack.
- MIDGROUND ROAD: a motorcyclist in a bright yellow helmet and a lilac jacket on a blue retro motorbike rides slowly from left to right along the road behind the crossing.
- THE CROWD: about twenty other pedestrians cross the zebra in both directions at different distances and speeds:

- an office worker in a navy suit with a briefcase;

- two high-school girls in navy uniforms sharing earphones;

- a grandmother in a lavender cardigan walking a huge fluffy white cat on a leash;

- a skateboarder in a yellow hoodie carrying his board;

- a tourist couple with a paper map;

- a delivery cyclist walking his bike;

- a young man in a bright orange puffer jacket;

- a woman in a green trench coat with a clear umbrella as a sunshade;

- a little boy holding his dad&#x27;s hand, pointing up at the koi;

- and several more ordinary people in colourful street clothes.

Everyone has their own clear path and nobody walks through anyone else.

FORMAT MODE

Duration 10 seconds, 16:9, ONE continuous static shot — no cuts, no transitions. Real speed. No text, no titles.

OPTICS AND CAMERA

A 24mm lens at eye level on the corner of the crossing, exactly the <<<image_1>>> framing, deep focus. The camera is LOCKED OFF — no movement at all: no pan, no tilt, no zoom, no push-in, no shake. Only the scene moves.

ACTION TIMING

0.0s to 3.0s — THE FLOW: the crowd streams across the zebra in both directions. The blue-haired guy walks at an easy pace with his axolotl, which looks up at him. The mint fox trots through the foreground. The koi glides slowly into the gap under the expressway, its fins rippling, its shadow sliding across the road. The yellow-helmet motorcyclist rolls along the road behind the crossing.

3.0s to 6.5s — THE KOI PASSES: the koi swims slowly across the middle of the frame with one lazy, powerful flick of its huge tail. The courier on its back adjusts the pink backpack and looks down at the street. The little boy points up at it and tugs his dad&#x27;s hand. The rider on the scooter turns her head to watch it pass. The guy glances up, nods, and keeps walking. The fluffy cat stops for a second to stare up at the koi, then follows the grandmother again.

6.5s to 10.0s — LIFE GOES ON: the koi drifts on toward the left edge of the frame, its tail still in view at the end. The crowd keeps flowing. The guy and the axolotl reach the right half of the crossing. The fox&#x27;s tails disappear past the bottom left of the frame. The traffic light countdown keeps ticking. The rider revs her scooter lightly, ready to go. The shot ends mid-flow.

PERFORMANCE

Natural, everyday city behaviour — relaxed walking, small glances and small reactions. Nobody panics; the koi and the creatures are simply part of everyday Tokyo. The creatures are friendly and curious. No dialogue.

PHYSICS

- Real walking with real weight, and clothes and hair moving naturally.
- The koi swims through the air exactly like a real fish in water: a slow body undulation, rippling translucent fins, a flowing tail and drifting barbels. Its soft shadow passes over the road and the buses.
- The axolotl waddles with a little side-to-side sway and its gills flutter. The fox&#x27;s three tails swing independently.
- The scooter and the buses are stationary with their engines idling. A few green leaves drift down across the frame.

IMAGE STABILITY — HARD RULES

- One static shot — no camera movement, no cuts.
- Every hero keeps exactly the look of their reference in every frame: the koi, the axolotl, the fox, the guy and the rider. The koi always has one head, one tail and the same scale pattern, and never morphs into a dragon or a snake.
- The crowd stays stable — no people merging, splitting, duplicating or appearing out of nowhere — and nobody walks through the buses or the pillars.
- The location stays locked to <<<image_1>>>.
- No readable text, no real brands, no logos on any screen, sign, bus or clothing, no watermarks, no flicker.

LIGHTING & COLOR GRADE

The bright, warm afternoon sun of <<<image_1>>> from the left: crisp shadows of the expressway across the road, cool blue shade under the overpass, warm sunlight on the crowd, the koi&#x27;s scales flashing orange and pearl as it moves between shade and light, and glossy reflections on the buses and the glass. A vivid, saturated, cheerful cinematic grade with real skin tones, natural contrast and fine film grain.

AUDIO

A lively Tokyo crossing ambience: the chirping pedestrian-signal melody, many footsteps, a soft crowd murmur, idling bus engines, the scooter idling and revving lightly, the motorbike passing, distant traffic on the expressway above, and a soft, deep whoosh as the koi&#x27;s tail sweeps through the air. No dialogue. No music — music is added in post.

3. 30 seconds in one generation: a herder in a blizzard

Eight shots from a text prompt only, with no references.

SCENE CONTEXT

A 30-second cinematic short in a frozen steppe during a blizzard. An old herder finds a newborn lamb half-frozen, carries it into his yurt and warms it by the stove, pulls his bone snow goggles down at the doorway, tucks the lamb inside his fur coat, mounts his horse and rides out into the white storm. What Are the Most Common Kling Prompt Mistakes?

Too much in one shot.
 Too many actions or camera changes in a short shot make the result harder to control. Keep each shot focused, or split a complex sequence into several shots.

A prompt that fights the frames.
 If the prompt describes motion that can’t reach the end frame or the next keyframe, the result drifts. Keep the prompt in line with the frames.

Too many shots for the duration.
 Five shots in a short clip leave each one a couple of seconds, too little for a readable action.

Expecting motion transfer from the base model.
 To copy movement from a reference video onto a character, use Kling 3.0 Motion Control.

Testing at the final resolution.
 For Kling 4.0, test at 720p before the final version. For Kling 3.0, use Turbo for faster drafts, then render the final.

Got any questions left?

Which is better, Kling 4.0 or Kling 3.0?

It depends on the task. Kling 4.0 suits scenes up to 30 seconds, keyframes and edits in a finished clip. Kling 3.0 suits multi-shot clips up to 15 seconds with native audio.

How long can a Kling video be?

Kling 4.0 generates 3 to 30 seconds in a single generation. Kling 3.0 generates 3 to 15 seconds.

How do I keep a character the same across shots?

In Kling 3.0, tag the character as an element with @ in every shot. In Kling 4.0, add the character as a reference.

Can Kling 4.0 change something in a finished video?

Yes. In Edit video, you can change a character, product, background, camera angle or visual style.

Can Kling 3.0 edit a finished video?

Not in the base model. Use Kling 3.0 Omni Edit for clips of 3 to 10 seconds at up to 1080p, or the Edit video tab in Kling 4.0.

Can I use keyframes and video references together in Kling 4.0?

Not in the same generation. Use Frames to control specific moments, or References to guide the result with images, videos, Elements and voice references.
