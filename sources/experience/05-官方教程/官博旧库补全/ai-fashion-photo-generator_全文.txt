10 min

Staying on-brand in AI fashion photography comes down to what gets locked in before generation starts. Four levers do that: a Moodboard for style, Soul HEX for color, Soul ID for the face, one preset held across a series. Skip all four and the prompt alone has to carry everything, which is usually where flat lighting and that overprocessed AI sheen come from. Soul 2.0 is built around fixing that.

The Brand Lock: Four Anchors That Hold a Campaign Together
Style anchor: Moodboard. 20+ photos, one cohesive style. Fixes the aesthetic register before generation starts.
Color anchor: Soul HEX. Pull a palette from a brand reference and use it to guide color from the first generation. Color Transfer can carry the finalized grade across the rest of the series afterward.
Identity anchor: Soul ID. 20+ photos, same face across every image and video. Core for a campaign built around a model.
Look anchor: One preset per series. Pick the preset that matches the campaign direction and keep it fixed across the set.

Why AI Fashion Images Look Flat

AI fashion images often look flat when too much of the visual direction is left to the prompt alone. A model can understand “leather jacket,” “Y2K,” or “editorial lighting,” but those descriptions still leave a lot open to interpretation: the exact color relationships, camera feel, fabric texture, styling references, and the way a specific era should actually look.

That is where generic AI polish tends to creep in. The image may technically match the prompt, but it does not feel specific enough to belong to a real campaign.

Soul 2.0 handles that by separating creative direction into different inputs. The prompt sets the direction; references lock the details that need to stay visually exact.

What's Better in a Prompt, and What's Better as a Reference

Use the prompt for decisions that can stay interpretive:

Aesthetic direction, era, mood, and overall look
Clothing, when it is simple enough to describe clearly
Location type, such as studio, street, or interior
Pose and body language

Use references for details that need tighter visual consistency:

Face and skin tone
Light temperature and color
Fabric texture
Background details
A specific visual language or palette

The practical split is simple: use text to direct the image, and use references to lock what should not drift. In Soul 2.0, Moodboard handles the aesthetic world, Soul HEX controls color, and Soul ID keeps identity consistent.

How to Generate a Fashion Campaign or Lookbook

Step 1: Set the four anchors once. 

Step 2: Generate the hero shot. Set quality to 2k for a final deliverable, write the prompt with aesthetic direction up front, add emotion cues in brackets, and pick the aspect ratio for the primary use case, 3:4 or 2:3 for editorial, 9:16 for social vertical. 

Step 3: Approve the hero shot before moving on. Confirm that the anchors are holding: the face, color, and aesthetic should all read consistently before you generate the rest of the series.

Step 4: Multiply into the full series. Switch to 1.5k for faster iteration across variations, generate 4 at a time with the 1/4 control, and change one element per pass, lighting, then composition, then styling. Re-render any approved variation at 2k once the direction is locked.

Step 5: Apply Color Transfer across the series. Once the hero image's grade is finalized, apply that exact palette to every other image in the set.

Step 6: QC the finished set. Check that the anchors held across every image: same face, same color grade, same aesthetic register.

One Look Across Every Channel

Once the four anchors are locked, the same campaign look can carry across channels without rebuilding the aesthetic for every format.

Product page: clean, on-model shots at 2k, same color grade and preset as the rest of the campaign.
Paid ads: the same anchors, resized to whatever aspect ratio the platform needs.
Social carousel and email: multiple crops from the same locked look, no separate shoot required.
TikTok and vertical video: Soul ID carries the same face into video, so the model stays recognizable moving from stills to motion content.
