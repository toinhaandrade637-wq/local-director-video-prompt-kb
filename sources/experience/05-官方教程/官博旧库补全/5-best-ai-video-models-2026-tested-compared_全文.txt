Higgsfield aggregates 35+ models and tools under one subscription and adds its own tools on top: Cinema Studio for camera control, Soul ID for character consistency, LipSync Studio, and a Claude MCP connector for generating inside Claude. To see how the top models actually perform, our Prompt Team compared ten of them on realism, motion stability, narrative control, cost and creative flexibility. The aim is to show which model fits which job, where each one falls short, and when a single-model tool would serve you better.

How did Higgsfield&#x27;s Prompt Team test each model?

Every model was judged on the same five criteria, the parts of production where AI video usually breaks:

Prompt responsiveness:
 how accurately the model interprets written descriptions.

Motion stability:
 whether movement stays consistent between frames.

Lighting behaviour:
 the realism of light interaction within dynamic scenes.

Character consistency:
 identity, voice and expression preserved across shots.

Editing control:
 the ability to refine, modify or direct a scene after generation.

The five models added in this update (Cinema Studio 4.0, HappyHorse, Grok Imagine 1.5, Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 and FLUX.3 Video) went through the same five criteria; the video examples below are from the original round of testing.

What is Seedance 2.5 best for?

Seedance 2.5

 is the best model for multi-shot films that need synced audio. It accepts up to 50 reference inputs, generates picture and sound in one pass, and holds character consistency across clips up to 30 seconds, so a sequence plays as one coherent piece with no stitched clips.

Seedance 2.5 is ByteDance&#x27;s flagship and Higgsfield&#x27;s most capable engine for coherent, multi-shot video. It takes text, images, video and audio (up to 30 images, 10 video clips and 10 audio clips in a single generation) and times sound to motion with no post-sync step. Region Edit replaces an object, polishes a face or re-rolls one shot without re-rendering the whole clip, so you pay only for the part you change. On Higgsfield, Seedance 2.5 runs under one subscription alongside 35+ models and tools, and you can feed it the same Soul ID character and presets you use elsewhere, with no separate ByteDance account or export step between tools.

Highlights from testing:

Joint audio + video generation in one pass - no separate sync step

Up to 50 reference inputs (30 images, 10 videos, 10 audio clips) plus text

Region Edit: fix one object, face or shot without a full re-render

Multi-shot character and brand consistency, clips up to 30 seconds

Cheaper drafts:
 Seedance 2.5 Draft Mode renders 480p previews first, then re-renders only the take you keep at full resolution with the same prompt, references and seed. Availability of draft modes may vary by region.

Where it falls short:
 Seedance is one of the more credit-heavy models: a 10-second 720p clip runs 70 credits, so it suits finished sequences, and quick drafts belong in Draft Mode or MiniMax. Seedance 2.5 also isn&#x27;t on Higgsfield&#x27;s entry plan: the $19/month Starter includes Seedance 2.0 Fast and Seedance 2.0 Mini (quicker, cheaper, a step below in detail), while Seedance 2.5 opens on Plus at $59/month. Its content protection may block generations containing real human portraits without identity verification or legal authorization. For a DTC brand iterating ad concepts before committing, MiniMax is the cheaper starting point.

What is Google Veo 3.1 best for in AI video?

Veo 3.1

 is the best model for wide, outdoor and weather-driven scenes. Its global illumination and environmental handling make landscapes, cityscapes and natural motion (water, fog, wind) feel convincing at scale, with native audio, where most models flatten depth or light.

Veo 3.1, from Google DeepMind, specialises in global illumination and spatial reconstruction. It excels at open environments where light, movement and depth interact naturally, reads long prompts well, and handles weather and depth of field in large compositions. Running Veo 3.1 through Higgsfield lets you pair its wide establishing shots with closer character work from Kling 3.0 in the same project, with no separate Google AI subscription needed for occasional wide-scene use.

Our observations:

Best performance for outdoor and atmospheric scenes

Excellent colour tone and environmental motion handling

Native audio generation

Cheaper drafts:
 Veo 3.1 Lite generates 4, 6 or 8 seconds at 720p or 1080p with native audio for 16 credits per 8-second 1080p clip. It has no lip-sync and no 4K, so use it for drafts and re-generate the final in Veo 3.1.

Where it falls short:
 Veo 3.1 is among the most credit-intensive models at 80 credits per 8-second 1080p clip, and its consistency softens on close-up human shots compared with wide scenes. For tight character work or dialogue, Kling 3.0 is stronger and far cheaper. If you only need Veo, Google&#x27;s own Flow may cost less than an aggregator subscription.

What can you do with WAN 3.0 that other models can&#x27;t?

WAN 3.0

 is the best model for long takes with several characters on screen. It generates up to 30 seconds at 480p, 720p or 1080p and keeps faces, clothing and details stable across frames, so a multi-character scene holds together without stitching clips.

Higgsfield WAN 3.0, built on Alibaba&#x27;s Wan, is the longest-running model in the WAN family. It works from a text prompt or an image, syncs audio to the motion, and handles physics and facial expressions more consistently than the 2.x versions. Inside Higgsfield, you can hold a Soul ID character through a WAN 3.0 take, so a long scene stays consistent with the rest of your project.

What makes WAN stand out:

Up to 30 seconds per generation at 480p, 720p or 1080p

Multi-subject consistency: faces, clothing and details stay stable across several characters

Audio sync, improved physics and facial expressions

35 credits per 10-second 1080p clip

Where it falls short:
 WAN 3.0 is a text- and image-to-video model, so the video-reference restyling of earlier WAN versions is not part of it; for a reshoot of existing footage, Seedance 2.5 Region Edit or HappyHorse Edit Video are the tools to pick. For maximum realism and physics on a single shot, Kling 3.0 or Cinema Studio 4.0 are the stronger picks.

How do you keep the same character and voice across shots with Kling 3.0?

Kling 3.0

 is the best model for character-driven, multi-shot stories, in 4K, with consistent voices, and among the cheapest of the ten at 10 credits per 5-second 720p clip. Voice Binding assigns a voice to each character across cuts, so identity and speech stay stable shot to shot.**

Kling 3.0, from Kuaishou, is built for directed storytelling. Its multi-shot storyboarding generates up to six camera cuts in one pass. You set shot size, perspective and movement per segment, and shot-reverse-shot transitions are automatic. Omni Native Audio generates dialogue, effects and ambience, and Voice Binding holds a unique voice per character across five languages. It outputs up to 4K, runs up to 15 seconds, and simulates cloth, hair, fluid and collisions.

From our results:

Multi-shot storyboarding - up to 6 camera cuts per generation

Omni Native Audio + Voice Binding (consistent voices, 5 languages)

Up to 4K, up to 15 seconds, physics-aware motion

Among the cheapest models tested, 10 credits per 5-second 720p clip and 25 per 10-second 1080p clip

Pair Kling 3.0 with a Soul ID to carry the same face across a whole project. This is useful for a solo filmmaker cutting a short with recurring characters. On Higgsfield it sits beside Seedance 2.5 and WAN 3.0, so you can storyboard in Kling and finish in another model without re-uploading assets or paying a second subscription. On the $59/month Plus plan, 1,200 monthly credits cover 120 five-second 720p Kling generations, enough to treat it as a daily driver, though additional generations or alternatives may increase the cost.

Where it falls short:
 Kling&#x27;s base cost is low, but 4K and longer clips consume more credits, and it rewards setup. Define shots, references and voices before you lean on one prompt. For fast, low-effort short-form, MiniMax is quicker.

Is MiniMax Hailuo 2.3 good for fast AI video generation?

MiniMax Hailuo 2.3

 is the fastest, lowest-effort model for short-form, stylised and anime content. Its Fast mode turns minimal prompts into clean clips, holds colour and style across frames, and keeps logos and on-screen text sharp, ideal when turnaround matters more than 4K.

MiniMax Hailuo 2.3, from MiniMax, has two modes: Fast 2.3 for quick iteration and Standard 2.3 for higher-precision finals. It needs less direction than the others, producing usable results from minimal prompts, handy for a social editor shipping daily TikToks. A 6-second 1080p clip costs 10 credits. Because MiniMax sits in the same Higgsfield workspace, you can draft fast here and promote the best take to a Seedance 2.5 or Kling 3.0 final without re-uploading or switching tools.

From our results:

Fast 2.3 (iteration) and Standard 2.3 (final) modes

Colour and style stability across frames; frame-consistent anime

Sharp logos and typography through movement

Clean realism with minimal direction, 10 credits per 6-second 1080p clip

Where it falls short:
 MiniMax trades depth for speed. It&#x27;s built for fast short-form, and directed multi-shot cinema is a job for other models. For complex sequences, fine camera control or 4K finals, Seedance 2.5 or Kling 3.0 produce stronger results.

What is Cinema Studio 4.0 best for?

Cinema Studio 4.0

 is the best model for directed cinematic clips where camera, light and colour are set before the first frame. It is Higgsfield&#x27;s own tool, generates up to 30 seconds from up to 50 references, and extends or edits a clip without re-rendering it.

Cinema Studio is how Higgsfield handles cinematography: production-style video with explicit control over the camera, lens and look. Version 4.0 adds a Director&#x27;s Panel with genre, era and tempo, camera and lens choices (Modern, 35mm Film, 8mm Film or DV Camcorder; clean, anamorphic and vintage lenses; f/1.4 to f/11), 50+ colour palettes and six lighting presets. Three video modes cover a new clip from references, Forward and Backward Extend for continuation, and Edit Video for region fixes. Character emotions sit on a slider, and the clip lands in the same library as every other model here.

Highlights from testing:

Director&#x27;s Panel: genre, era, tempo, camera, lens, colour palette, lighting

Up to 50 references and clips up to 30 seconds

Forward and Backward Extend plus Edit Video (region edit)

Emotion control per character

Where it falls short:
 Cinema Studio 4.0 is the most credit-intensive option in this guide at 1080p: 16 credits per second, so 160 for 10 seconds and 480 for a full 30-second take. 480p runs at 3 credits per second and 720p at 7, which is where drafts belong. It uses credits on every plan, and the panel rewards planned shots over quick prompts.

What is HappyHorse best for?

HappyHorse

 is the best model for motion quality and scene coherence with sound generated in the same pass. It produces 3 to 15 seconds at 720p or 1080p, and a 5-second 1080p clip costs 23 credits.

HappyHorse, from Alibaba&#x27;s ATH Future Life Lab, is a unified transformer that generates video and synchronized audio in a single forward pass, so ambient sound and music land with the motion. On Higgsfield it has three modes, Create Video, Edit Video and Motion Control, and starts from an image you upload, generate or paste from the clipboard.

Highlights from testing:

Joint audio-video generation with ambient foley and music

Instruction-based Edit Video and Motion Control modes

720p or 1080p, 3 to 15 seconds

23 credits per 5-second 1080p clip

Where it falls short:
 HappyHorse takes one image and a prompt, so reference-heavy work with many inputs belongs with Seedance 2.5 or Cinema Studio 4.0, which take up to 50. For frame-accurate anime, Hailuo 2.3 held style more consistently in our test.

What is Grok Imagine 1.5 best for?

Grok Imagine 1.5

 is the best model for talking heads and animated stills with dialogue. It generates lip-synced speech, sound effects, ambience and music in the same pass as the motion, keeps the original light and detail of the source image, and runs 1 to 15 seconds.

Grok Imagine 1.5, from xAI, works in image-to-video and text-to-video modes. The image becomes the first frame, video extension continues from the last frame with the same subject and lighting, and up to 7 reference images hold a character or style across separate generations. Shot type, one camera move and action timing land as written in the prompt. A 10-second 1080p clip costs 80 credits; Grok Imagine 1.5 Lite is the lower-cost variant for drafts.

Highlights from testing:

Lip-synced dialogue, SFX, ambience and music in one pass

Animate a still as the first frame with its light and detail kept

Up to 7 references plus video extension for multi-part sequences

480p to 1080p, 1 to 15 seconds, 80 credits per 10-second 1080p clip

Where it falls short:
 Silent output usually means the prompt had no audio cue, and lip-sync drifts without a front-facing portrait and short lines. It is tuned for one beat per clip, so complex choreography and heavy VFX go to Seedance 2.5 or Kling 3.0. At 8 credits per second at 1080p it sits in the upper half of this list on cost.

What is Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 best for?

Gemini Omni Flash 1.1

 is the best model for iterative editing through conversation. You attach any mix of text, image, audio and video, then refine the result with follow-ups in the same thread, and faces, clothing and voice stay consistent across the turns.

Gemini Omni Flash 1.1, from Google DeepMind, is an any-to-any model: an image sets the style, a clip sets the camera movement, an audio file sets the music, all in one prompt. Edits build on earlier turns ("change the background but keep the character"), and reference images and short clips keep identity and setting aligned. Output runs 3 to 10 seconds at up to 4K with synchronized audio; a 10-second 1080p clip costs 45 credits. An Extend variant continues an existing clip.

Highlights from testing:

Native multimodal input: text, image, audio and video in one prompt

Conversational editing that builds on previous turns

Character and voice consistency across cuts within a conversation

3 to 10 seconds, up to 4K, 45 credits per 10-second 1080p clip

Where it falls short:
 The 10-second cap keeps it out of long takes, and for single-shot maximum quality HappyHorse, Kling 3.0 and Cinema Studio 4.0 produced stronger results in our test. Rewriting voices on someone else&#x27;s footage is outside its purpose; use a reference you own or have permission to use.

What is FLUX.3 Video best for?

FLUX.3 Video

 is the best model for clips with native audio and dialogue in several languages, and for carrying a character from a reference video into a new scene. It generates 5 to 20 seconds with sound in every output and accepts text, image or video as input.

FLUX.3 Video, from Black Forest Labs, is a multimodal foundation model that learns images, video and audio together, so sound matches impact and motion follows mass. It supports text-to-video, image-to-video and video-to-video, takes up to 10 frame references, handles natural dialogue across languages, and renders typography and animated text cleanly. Output goes up to 4K; a 5-second 720p clip with audio costs 30 credits.

Highlights from testing:

Video and native audio in one generation, up to 20 seconds

Text-, image- and video-to-video with up to 10 frame references

Multilingual dialogue and expressive faces

Strong typography and animated design styles, 30 credits per 5-second 720p clip

Where it falls short:
 Clip length and resolution scale the credit cost quickly at 4K, and the 20-second ceiling sits below Seedance 2.5, WAN 3.0 and Cinema Studio 4.0 at 30. For multi-shot storytelling with many references, Seedance 2.5 remains the more predictable choice.

What about Runway Gen-4.5, Pika and Sora 2?

Three notable models sit outside this comparison, each for a different reason.

Runway Gen-4.5
, from Runway, is a top-tier cinematic model, strong on directed, controlled shots, but it&#x27;s tied to Runway&#x27;s own platform (from $12/month, credit-based). If your entire workflow lives in Runway&#x27;s ecosystem, it&#x27;s a legitimate alternative to everything above. This comparison covers models available inside a single multi-model subscription, which Gen-4.5 is not.

Pika 2.5
, from Pika, starts at $8/month and is built for stylised, effect-driven social clips (Pikaffects, swaps, additions). It&#x27;s the budget pick for playful short-form, but its raw generation quality trails Seedance 2.5, Kling 3.0 and Veo 3.1 on most public benchmarks.

Sora 2 is retired.
 OpenAI shut down the Sora app on April 26, 2026 and discontinued the API on September 24, 2026, and creators who used it for cinematic motion have largely moved to Seedance 2.5 and Veo 3.1.

Why use several AI video models together?

Each model serves a different purpose, and using them together inside Higgsfield is where the workflow gets practical. Seedance 2.5 anchors multi-shot narrative with native audio, Veo 3.1 expands scale and lighting, WAN 3.0 holds several characters over a long take, Kling 3.0 owns character and voice in 4K, MiniMax turns concepts around fast and cheap, Cinema Studio 4.0 sets the camera and the look, HappyHorse brings motion quality with sound, Grok Imagine 1.5 handles talking heads, Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 edits through conversation, and FLUX.3 Video covers multilingual dialogue and video-to-video. Because credit costs differ so much, from 10 credits for a Kling 3.0 or MiniMax clip to 160 for ten seconds of Cinema Studio 4.0 at 1080p, picking the right model per shot also controls your spend.

The models complement each other, and one workspace is what makes combining them practical.

What&#x27;s a practical multi-model workflow on Higgsfield?

A repeatable pipeline most creators can follow:

Block out the scene
 in Seedance 2.5 or Veo 3.1 to get base motion and audio.

Run the long, multi-character take
 in WAN 3.0 when a scene needs several people on screen for up to 30 seconds.

Add the speaking character
 through Kling 3.0, using Voice Binding and a Soul ID for a consistent face and voice.

Iterate cheaply
 in MiniMax, Veo 3.1 Lite or Seedance 2.5 Draft Mode when you need quick alternates before committing credits.

Finish
 by running the cut through Higgsfield&#x27;s upscale tools before export.

Costing the shots by model, cheap Kling, MiniMax and draft-mode passes, selective Seedance, Veo and Cinema Studio finals, keeps a 30-second sequence well within a single Plus or Ultra plan&#x27;s monthly credits.

Where Higgsfield falls short

A multi-model subscription isn&#x27;t automatically the right answer. Know the trade-offs:

Seedance 2.5 is not on the entry plan.
 The $19/month Starter includes Seedance 2.0 Fast and Seedance 2.0 Mini; Seedance 2.5 requires Plus ($59/month) or Ultra ($129/month).

Credit budgeting is on you.
 Per-clip costs range from 10 credits (Kling 3.0 at 720p, MiniMax) to 160 (10 seconds of Cinema Studio 4.0 at 1080p), so heavy use of premium models drains a month&#x27;s allowance faster than the plan page suggests.

If you only use one model, its native platform may be cheaper.
 Veo-only users should price Google&#x27;s Flow; Kling-only users, Kuaishou&#x27;s own app; Runway loyalists, Runway itself.

New models arrive on a rolling basis.
 Access to the latest releases and advanced features may not reach every account at launch.

Platform tools need setup to pay off.
 Soul ID, for example, requires 20 to 80 photos and ~3–5 minutes of training before it helps maintain consistency, it rewards planned projects more than one-off clips.

The bottom line

There is no single best AI video model in 2026: there&#x27;s a best model per job, and the cost gap between them is the main budgeting lever. Quick guide:

Multi-shot film or ad with synced audio
 → Seedance 2.5 (on Higgsfield, Plus plan and up; Starter runs Seedance 2.0 Fast and Mini).

Wide outdoor, weather and atmosphere
 → Veo 3.1, or Google&#x27;s Flow if Veo is all you need.

Long take with several characters
 → WAN 3.0.

Character-driven stories with consistent voices, on a budget
 → Kling 3.0 (10 credits per 5-second 720p clip, 4K when needed).

Daily short-form, drafts and stylised content
 → MiniMax Hailuo 2.3 Fast, or Pika at $8/month if effects matter more than fidelity.

Directed cinematic shots with camera, lens and lighting control
 → Cinema Studio 4.0.

Motion quality with sound from one image
 → HappyHorse.

Talking heads and animated stills with dialogue
 → Grok Imagine 1.5.

Editing by conversation
 → Gemini Omni Flash 1.1.

Multilingual dialogue and video-to-video
 → FLUX.3 Video.

Draft cheap, finish expensive: iterate in Kling, MiniMax, Veo 3.1 Lite or Seedance 2.5 Draft Mode, then spend Seedance, Veo or Cinema Studio credits only on the takes you&#x27;ll publish. That single habit keeps a multi-model workflow inside one plan&#x27;s monthly credits.

Which AI video model is best for multi-shot films with audio?

Seedance 2.5 is the best model for multi-shot films because it generates picture and sound together in a single pass. It takes up to 50 reference inputs, holds character and brand consistency across shots, and produces clips up to 30 seconds with native audio sync. How do I keep the same character and voice across shots in AI video?

Use Kling 3.0&#x27;s multi-shot storyboarding with Voice Binding, which holds a character&#x27;s voice across up to six cuts and five languages, or train a Higgsfield Soul ID on 20 to 80 photos and reuse it across generations. Both give the model a fixed identity to anchor to, which helps stop faces and voices from drifting between shots.

Which AI video model is the cheapest per generation?

Kling 3.0 at 720p and MiniMax Hailuo 2.3 are the most credit-efficient of the ten tested, at 10 credits per clip, while premium models like Veo 3.1 run 80 credits per 8-second 1080p clip and ten seconds of Cinema Studio 4.0 at 1080p costs 160. Can you restyle or "reshoot" existing footage with AI?

Yes. Seedance 2.5 Region Edit changes one object, face or shot without re-rendering the rest, HappyHorse and Cinema Studio 4.0 have an Edit Video mode, and Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 edits through follow-up prompts in one conversation. Use footage you own or have permission to use.
