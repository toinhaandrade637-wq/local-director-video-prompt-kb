How We Compared Pricing

Every platform was checked on the same questions: what the plans cost, how money converts into credits, what one finished clip costs on the entry paid plan, and how many models the subscription covers. Prices were verified against official pricing pages and in-product counters in August 2026. Several platforms price by region or run rotating promotions, so figures marked with a tilde are estimates and the checkout price is the one that counts.

The table figures are the estimated cost of a single generation, calculated from each platform's plan price and its published or in-app credit rates. Retry counts stay out of the math: how many attempts a clip takes depends on what you ask for and how precisely you ask. Retries still shape the real bill, because failed generations consume credits on most platforms, so read the per-clip figures as the floor of what a video costs.

Understanding the Cost Per Finished Video

The credit trap works like this: a low monthly price looks affordable until the credits meet reality. Failed generations still consume credits on most platforms, some credits buy a silent draft that needs a separate audio pass, and a 1080p render can cost three times the 720p rate on the same plan. The number that cuts through is the effective price per second of usable video: divide the plan price by the seconds you actually publish. Several models on this list, including Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.5, and Veo 3.1, generate native audio, so their credits buy a more complete output per attempt.

More direct control can help reduce unnecessary retries by letting creators define the shot before generation. Cinema Studio 4.0 is how Higgsfield handles cinematography: film setup, camera, color palette, and lighting controls, plus up to 50 references per scene, so those choices are made before credits are spent. The fewer attempts a clip takes, the lower its effective price per second, whatever the plan costs.

If the Lowest Price Is the Goal

If the goal is simply the lowest monthly fee, several tools here are cheaper than Higgsfield, and that is a reasonable choice. Google AI Plus at $4.99 is the lowest subscription with video generation in this list. Kling Standard, PixVerse Standard, Vidu Standard, and Pika Standard all start at $10. For a creator who publishes a few short clips a month from one model and does nothing else with them, a single-model plan at $10 covers the job, and Higgsfield's $15 entry point buys capability that workload does not need.

The math changes when the workload grows past one model or one stage. Each single-model tool prices its own line only, so testing Kling against Veo against Seedance means holding multiple subscriptions, and the monthly fees stack.

One Subscription or Several Separate Tools

The stacked-subscription total is the number the per-tool tables hide. Kling Standard at $10, Google AI Pro at $19.99 for Veo, and Runway Standard at $15 for the editing layer come to about $45 per month across three separate balances, none of which transfer. On Higgsfield, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1, and Seedance 2.5 draw from one $15 balance, and different models carry different credit prices, so budget drafts run through cheaper models and premium models are reserved for finals.

Multi-model access itself is no longer unique: Luma, Krea, Hailuo, and PixVerse also resell third-party models. The difference is what happens after generation. A clip made in Cinema Studio keeps the same character in the next clip through Soul ID, continues into ads through Marketing Studio, and scales to volume through Supercomputer, without exporting between platforms.
