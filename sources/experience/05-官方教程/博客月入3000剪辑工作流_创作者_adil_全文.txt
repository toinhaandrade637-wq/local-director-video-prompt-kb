Before You Start

Go to Higgsfield Katana, click Install, and follow the setup to connect it to Claude through MCP. Then open Claude, select Opus 5.5, and type /katana to bring up the presets. Pick one to get started, or follow the workflow below to make edits for your own page, your business, or a paying client.

Prepare your footage, a style reference, and a separate music track before you begin. Reusing the same reference and music direction helps a batch feel consistent.

Stage 1 — The Assets

Each edit starts with 3 inputs:

Source footage: the film, podcast, stream, or original recording you want to clip. Use your own footage or material you have permission to edit and publish.
An editing reference: use one of our example videos below to guide the pacing, shot rhythm, and transitions. Attach the reference alongside your own source footage.
A music track: attach it separately so the edit can build toward its strongest beat while keeping any dialogue clear.

For a podcast, choose a source with clear speech. For an action or transformation Reel, make sure the main subject and the payoff can stay visible in a vertical crop. For the Korean version, use an edit you have already approved.

Our videos beside the prompts are style references for your own edits. Choose a reference because its style fits your source, then pay attention to what viewers respond to: the opening, the timing, or a particular moment.

Stage 2 — The Prompting Framework

Choose Recreate this prompt to start your edit. Attach your own source footage and music, add the example video you want to use as a reference, and send the prompt. Keep those 3 inputs clearly identified as separate attachments.

A useful clipping request specifies the duration, the format, the moment to build around, and the role of the music. It also asks for a short report after the analysis, edit plan, and preview. That gives you a chance to adjust the direction before making more versions.

An aura edit builds around a character, statement, or visual payoff. A teaser builds curiosity about what happens next. Choose the goal first, then use the matching prompt below.

Turn Your Edits Into a Client Offer

Your first finished edits give you something concrete to show. Start with a focused offer: a batch of clips from one episode, a set of character edits, or teasers for a release. Define the number of clips, the formats, the delivery schedule, and the revisions included.

There are 3 places to start: campaign platforms with posted briefs and payment terms, direct outreach to creators or businesses whose content you understand, and a public portfolio. Agencies serving those clients can also need help producing clips.

And you can start with an industry you already love. Gaming, sports, music, beauty, business—browse the campaigns and look for content you actually understand. Check a few platforms, compare the budgets, and pick a brief that fits your style. Every new stream, episode, and release creates more material to work with. Start with 1 strong sample and use it to get your first conversation going.

Campaign work and direct client work pay differently. For a campaign paying $0.50 per 1,000 eligible views, 100,000 eligible views would equal $50. 10 clips reaching that number would equal $500 before fees. This is an example calculation; the campaign’s terms determine which views and submissions qualify.

A direct client can pay for a defined package instead. For example, you might offer 10 clips for $500 per month. 6 clients buying that package would mean $3,000 in monthly gross revenue, before production costs, fees, and taxes. That is an illustrative target for planning an offer, not reported earnings or an average result.

Start with 1 paid batch, use the client’s feedback to improve the next delivery, and offer a recurring package when the fit is right. If you agree on a performance bonus, put the qualifying result and measurement window in the offer.
