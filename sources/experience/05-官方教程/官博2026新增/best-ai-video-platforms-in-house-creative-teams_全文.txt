Best AI Video Platforms for In-House Creative Teams in 2026
Higgsfield
Oct 23, 2026
12 min

Choosing the best AI video platform for an in-house creative team greatly depends on the production mix, from brand films to everyday ads and social videos. Look beyond generation quality to reusable brand assets, collaboration, editing handoffs, and usage costs. In this guide, we compare six leading platforms: Runway, Higgsfield, Adobe Firefly, LTX Studio, Canva, and Invideo.

What Should In-House Creative Teams Look for in an AI Video Platform?

Your team might be developing a brand film one day and turning product images into social ads the next.

The right platform should support that range without making everyone rebuild assets, repeat instructions, or move files between tools at every step.

Check these six things before choosing a plan:

Support for your actual content mix. Check whether the platform can handle the formats you produce regularly, including product demos, ad variations, presenter videos, and cinematic sequences. A great sample clip tells you little about how it fits your weekly workload.
Brand assets you can reuse. Look for ways to save and reuse products, characters, and visual references. A brand kit with logos and fonts is great, but you also need tools that help maintain a character’s appearance across scenes.
Collaboration that covers the review process. Can colleagues access the same assets, leave feedback, and approve a version? Check which features your plan includes and where you’ll need another tool.
A practical path to the finished video. Consider how you’ll assemble clips, add sound, make revisions, and hand off work to an editor.
Costs that account for iteration. Compare included usage, generation costs, extra credits, and paid seats. Budget for rejected takes and revisions alongside the clips you publish.
Security and access controls. Check who can access confidential assets, whether your plan supports SSO, and what its data-training policy covers before uploading unreleased campaign materials.

1. Runway for Cinematic Campaigns and Shared Production Workflows

Best for: Creating footage for brand films, product campaigns, and social ads, with shared references and feedback before the final edit.

Runway is worth considering when your team needs to develop a visual concept, generate several versions, and pass selected shots to an editor. Their platform includes Gen-4.5 alongside other image, video, and audio models, giving teams options for different campaign assets.

For instance, you could start a product campaign with approved reference images, explore different settings, and turn selected visuals into moving clips. Gen-4 Image References lets you save and share references for products, characters, and styles across sessions. While those references guide generation, they still need someone to check the results for brand accuracy.

The Team plan adds practical features for keeping that work together:

Shared campaign assets. Projects hold generations, references, sessions, and workflows, so colleagues can continue from the same materials. Content created outside shared projects stays private until shared.
Feedback on specific frames. Reviewers can comment on a generation, mention teammates, and resolve notes inside Runway.
Reusable brand context. Team includes up to 100 Brand Kits and 20 shared agent skills for repeatable workflows.
Connections to existing tools. Figma, Dropbox, and Notion connectors bring in working materials. Plugins connect Runway with Premiere Pro, After Effects, and DaVinci Resolve for editing.

2. Higgsfield for Brand Films, Product Ads, and Repeatable Campaign Work

Best for: Producing a brand film, product videos, and everyday campaign variations using shared characters, references, and assets.

Higgsfield is an AI-native creative suite that brings cinematic production, advertising tools, and multiple image, video, and audio models into one workspace. For an in-house team, that means you can develop a campaign’s main film alongside the shorter videos and supporting visuals needed to keep it running.

Cinema Studio handles cinematic sequences, Marketing Studio supports product and presenter-style ads, and the newer Ads Studio covers brand-based static ads, and its Ad Multiplier creates video-ad variations from a reference, with changes to products, characters, hooks, and CTAs.

Key features for in-house teams:

Cinema Studio 4.0 for directed scenes. Generate clips up to 30 seconds with controls for camera, lens, lighting, emotion, and pacing. Up to 50 reference images can guide a generation, while forward/backward extension helps continue existing footage.
Tools for recurring campaign work. Marketing Studio’s 1,500+ templates cover product visuals, UGC videos, and motion content, starting from a product image or website. Ad Multiplier offers another starting point: an existing video ad whose products, cast, or message you want to change.
Reusable brand materials and footage. Shared workspace assets include Soul ID characters and Reference Elements. Ads Studio saves brand kits for static-ad batches, while Genjutsu’s motion-transfer and object-swap workflows help adapt existing footage.
Supercomputer for multi-step production. Separate from Marketing Studio, Supercomputer takes a brief, plans the task, selects models and tools, and runs generation steps across image, video, and audio. It gives teams an agent-driven option when a deliverable requires several production stages.
Collaboration at workspace and project level. Business plans provide shared assets and generation workflows. Cinema Studio separately supports collaborative projects with real-time comments; confirm availability for your selected plan. Enterprise adds approval workflows, sub-workspaces, and advanced permissions.

3. Adobe Firefly for Teams Already Working in Adobe

Best for: Developing campaign concepts and generating supporting visuals for an existing Adobe production workflow.

Adobe Firefly fits teams whose designers and editors already work in Adobe applications. It brings image, video, and audio generation into that environment, while Firefly Boards gives colleagues a shared space to explore references, storyboards, and creative directions.

Key features for creative teams:

Shared visual development. Boards combine uploaded references, generated images, and video clips on one canvas, keeping campaign ideas together.
Feedback alongside the work. Invite colleagues to edit or review boards, set permissions, and leave pinned comments on specific elements.
Multiple generation models. Firefly offers Adobe and partner models, allowing teams to explore different approaches to campaign imagery and footage.
Supporting campaign assets. Paid plans include image tools and credit-based video, speech, music, and sound-effects generation.

4. LTX Studio for Storyboarded Brand Films

Best for: Turning a campaign script into a storyboard and developing connected scenes with recurring characters, products, and locations.

LTX Studio suits teams that want to plan the story before generating footage. A product launch film can begin as a script, become a storyboard for review, and move into shot generation and timeline editing. That makes it particularly relevant when creative direction and continuity matter across several scenes.

Key features for in-house teams include:

Script-to-storyboard planning. Turn a brief into frames your team can review, then adjust individual frames without rebuilding the entire board.
Reusable visual Elements. Save characters, objects, and locations to guide consistency across scenes.
Shared production and editing. Collaborators can generate, edit, and review assets together. Pro allows three people per project, including the owner; Enterprise supports unlimited collaborators. Each person uses their own account, credits, and plan features. The built-in timeline supports assembly; check editing-package options for external handoffs.

5. Canva for Everyday Branded Videos and Social Content

Best for: Turning product footage, images, and campaign assets into branded videos for regular social and marketing use.

Canva fits teams producing a steady stream of content alongside other design work. You can combine uploaded footage with generated clips, add branded graphics, and prepare different campaign formats in the same workspace.

Its practical appeal is making everyday production accessible to colleagues who don't spend their day in a dedicated video editor.

What Canva offers in-house teams:

Reusable brand assets. Brand Kits and Brand Templates keep logos, fonts, colors, and approved layouts available across projects.
Built-in review. Business supports live collaboration, comments, locked elements, and design approval requests.
Editing and asset connections. Finish everyday videos in Canva’s editor and access materials through connections such as Google Drive. Brand-management integrations also include HubSpot and Mailchimp.
AI-assisted video assembly. Magic Video styles, cuts, and sequences uploaded clips, while Highlights helps identify moments for shorter content.

6. Invideo for Agent-Assisted Marketing Video Production

Best for: Combining product footage, generated scenes, and narration into marketing videos through a shared, prompt-based editing workflow.

Invideo suits teams that want to describe a video, review the result, and refine it with an AI agent. Its current workflow supports uploaded footage alongside generated media and a timeline editor, giving teams a way to develop campaign videos and social cuts without handling every production step manually.

Features worth considering:

Agent-assisted creation and editing. Agent Two works through a brief and uses different models to generate media. Lite, Pro, and Ultra modes offer different levels of generation quality and resource use.
Shared campaign context. Collaborators work with the same project context and assets, helping keep instructions together across revisions.
Project-level access. Folders let teams separate projects and invite internal or external collaborators without exposing unrelated work.
Built-in timeline editing. Adjust the assembled video inside Invideo. Multiplayer editing supports joint production, while model access and team capacity depend on the plan.

How to Test an AI Video Platform Before Your Team Commits

Pick a small task from your actual content calendar, like a product teaser or a short social ad. Give the same brief to two shortlisted platforms and take each result through your usual review process.

Here’s how to make the test useful:

Bring your own assets. Upload the product photo and references you’d normally give a designer. Does the packaging still look right? Do the colors match your campaign?
Make a realistic change request. Ask for a different background or camera movement while keeping everything else consistent. You’ll quickly see how much work a typical revision creates.
Get a teammate involved. Have someone leave feedback, make the changes, and send the result to your editor. Notice where you need extra downloads, messages, or explanations to keep things moving.
Count what it took to finish. Include rejected takes, credits, editing time, and manual fixes. A useful comparison tells you what it takes to get an approved video onto the content calendar.
