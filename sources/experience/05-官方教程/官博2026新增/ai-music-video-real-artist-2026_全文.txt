How to Make an AI Music Video for a Real Artist in 2026
Higgsfield
Oct 1, 2026
10 min

To make an AI music video for a real artist, start with the finished track, approved references of the artist, and a visual concept built around the song. Soul ID helps maintain the artist’s identity, Soul Cinema creates visual references, Cinema Studio generates the shots, and Lipsync Studio syncs performance footage to the vocals. This guide covers the workflow from references to final edit.

What Is an AI Music Video?

An AI music video generates its shots directly from a real artist's likeness and a finished song, no traditional shoot with a crew, a location, and a camera involved. The artist's face and performance carry through every scene, cut on time with the track, while the visual world around them, locations, lighting, transitions, gets built from references and prompts, no capturing it on set.

Before starting, you need:

A finished track. The song the whole video gets built and timed around.
Photos or video of the artist. Enough clear references to train a consistent likeness across every shot.
Visual references for the style and world of the video. Locations, color, mood, whatever the video's look is meant to draw from.
Permission to use the music, likeness, and voice. Make sure the artist has approved the song, reference material, AI-generated likeness, and any synthetic or cloned voice used in the project.
How Do You Build a Concept Around the Song?

Break the track down into its sections before generating a single shot, each one plays a different role, and treating them all the same way produces something flat. Decide early where the artist performs directly to camera, where a story unfolds around them, and where a pure atmospheric insert, a location, a detail, a transition, carries the moment instead.

Full Workflow: Step by Step Guide

Step 1 (optional): Generate the track if you don't already have one. Use Seed Audio 1.0, give it the lyrics, and pick a voice. 

00:20

Step 2: Prepare character and location references. Gather or generate photos of the artist and the key locations the video needs, through Soul Cinema if new references are needed. 

Step 3: Train the artist's identity in Soul ID. Use 20+ reference photos to lock the likeness before generating any shots. 

Step 4: Generate the shots in Cinema Studio 4.0. Build performance shots, narrative shots, and atmospheric inserts using the locked identity and the concept built around the song's structure.

Step 5: Sync the vocals in Lipsync Studio. Run the performance shots through Lipsync Studio so the artist's mouth movement matches the actual vocal track.

Step 6: Assemble the music video. Bring the performance, narrative, and atmospheric clips into your editor. Place the finished song on the timeline first, then cut each shot to the planned timestamps. Mute generated clip audio where it conflicts with the original track.

Step 7: Review and export. Check likeness, lip sync, continuity, shot timing, transitions, and audio before exporting the final master.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them
Common Problems and How to Fix Them

Problem

	

What to Check

	

How to Fix It




The artist looks different between shots

	

Face, hairstyle, outfit, and proportions

	

Reuse the approved character references and regenerate shots that drift from the artist's look.




Lip movements don't match the lyrics

	

Whether the lip-sync input uses the exact song excerpt for that shot

	

Use the correctly trimmed audio segment and check its alignment with the original track in the edit.




Cuts feel disconnected from the music

	

Where shot changes fall within the song

	

Move cuts to meaningful beats, lyric changes, or transitions between sections.




The clip feels visually repetitive

	

Shot size, composition, and the balance of performance and story scenes

	

Alternate close-ups, wider shots, and narrative inserts while maintaining the chosen style.




The song sounds different in the final video

	

Whether generated clip audio overlaps the original recording

	

Mute unwanted clip audio and use the original track as the main soundtrack.
