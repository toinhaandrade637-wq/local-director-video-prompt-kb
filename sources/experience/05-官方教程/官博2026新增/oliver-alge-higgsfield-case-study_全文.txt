Oliver Alge: How a Platinum-Certified Producer Creates Music Videos and TV Ads With Higgsfield
Higgsfield
Oct 22, 2026
10 min

Oliver Alge is a gold- and platinum-certified music producer whose catalog has surpassed 500 million streams. Today, his creative work extends to AI music videos for established German rap artists, commercials for national television, and campaign visuals for a major airline. He builds his projects in Higgsfield Cinema Studio 4.0, where he develops characters, plans shots, and refines the generated footage to fit each brief. Support from the Higgsfield Creator Partnership Program gives him more room to experiment with ideas and production techniques.

Meet Oliver Alge: From Music Production to AI Filmmaking

Based between Germany and the UAE, 24-year-old Oliver works across music production and AI filmmaking. He is signed to BMG Publishing, with two German No. 1s and “Beat of the Year” and “Song of the Year” awards among his music credits. He recently produced “Panzerknacker” for Lennsko, which went viral internationally, with around one million TikTok videos created using the track.

Alongside his music career, Oliver works with AO Agency, an AI production studio operating in Germany, Austria, and the UAE. His video work spans music videos, commercials, short films, and product videos. He is also developing a feature film made entirely with AI.

Why Oliver Needed One Place to Build His Projects

Before Higgsfield, Oliver moved between separate websites for different production tasks. As projects grew, those handoffs became frustrating.

“With Higgsfield, especially Cinema Studio, I have everything I need in one place.”

He uses the platform to keep projects organized, work with different models, and choose camera and lens settings for his shots.

Inside Oliver's Cinema Studio 4.0 Workflow

Oliver starts with an idea, a concept, and a treatment. “AI doesn’t replace that part,” he says.
He then creates still images of the characters and locations and saves them as Elements to maintain their appearance across shots. Once those references are established, he generates the footage, choosing a model according to what each shot requires.

For complicated scenes, he first blocks the action and camera setup in 3D, then uses that as the basis for generation. He also uses video-to-video workflows to improve shots and make them more realistic.

BLN: Keeping Rap Icons Recognizable Across AI Worlds

For “BLN,” Oliver had to build a visual world around three artists from different generations of Berlin rap: Lacazette, Sido, and Gangsta Ralph. He made the whole video with AI, from the first shot to the final cut. The concept cast the artists in different cinematic worlds: Sido battles aliens on Mars, while Lacazette sails a huge ship through stormy seas.

The masks demanded particular attention. Sido’s is instantly familiar to a generation of German rap fans, making even a small visual mismatch difficult to overlook.

“Bringing it back the right way was a big responsibility”

Oliver says.

Gangsta Ralph also wears a mask, so Oliver had two distinctive designs to preserve throughout the video.

According to Oliver, the video reached the top four on YouTube Trending in Germany. He credits the SLS team for trusting him with the project, which he describes as the one that put him on the map in AI production.

Bonsy: Creating an AI Commercial for German Television

For the Bonsy commercial, Oliver worked from a supplied script about a family struggling to manage its finances. His job was to create the images and video shots that carried the story, keeping the family recognizable throughout. The finished commercial aired on German national television.

Television left little room for visual errors. Distorted hands or flickering details that might pass unnoticed on a phone became obvious on a living-room screen. The footage had to hold up alongside the other commercials in the same ad break.

“That meant a lot of generating. And I mean a lot”

Oliver says.

For Oliver, the spot showed that AI isn't only for experiments anymore, and that brands are ready to put real budgets behind it.

Riyadh Air: Delivering Two AI Transitions in 24 Hours

A last-minute brief for Riyadh Air gave Oliver 24 hours to create two transitions for its Saudi National Day spot. One connected the seat’s textile pattern to the traditional craft that inspired it; the other linked the shape of the cockpit window to a gazelle’s eye.

Oliver describes both as shots that would have required expensive VFX work in a conventional production. With Higgsfield, he iterated fast enough to get the movement and texture right and still hit the deadline.

How Iteration Changes the Client Conversation

Oliver gives one example from his own production experience: a commercial he estimates would require 14 shooting days and a €300,000 conventional-production budget could take him around two days with AI. What matters most to him, though, is the freedom to revise the work.

He can show clients ten versions of a shot instead of a single storyboard, giving them actual footage to respond to before settling on a direction. Ideas he once dropped in pre-production because they seemed impossible to shoot can now move into production.

What Being a Creator Partner Changed

Becoming a Higgsfield Creator Partner has helped Oliver establish credibility with clients and pursue projects with bigger budgets and more recognizable names. He does not attribute new clients directly to the partnership, but says it strengthens his credibility in client conversations

Credit support gives him more room to experiment, test unfamiliar approaches, and develop his own workflows. He also points to the partner community as a major benefit:

“The community is easily the best part.”

He says feedback from other creators regularly introduces him to new workflows, models, and ways of approaching a shot.

Oliver's Advice: Learn the Craft Behind the Tools

AI is a craft tool, not a shortcut. With the same models available to everyone, he believes taste and direction are what separate memorable work from generic output.

His advice:

Learn real filmmaking: lenses, lighting, blocking, and editing.
Build consistent characters and worlds, and avoid collecting random cool shots.
Watch movies for inspiration, and sit down and think before you generate.

What's next for him is a feature film made with AI.
