Why Unlimited Offers Are Narrowing

The unlimited category has been narrowing in 2026, and the pattern is consistent: uncapped generation across an entire model catalog is disappearing, while scoped offers stay. Runway is the clearest example: its Unlimited plan stopped selling to new subscribers on June 1, 2026, legacy subscribers keep it through November 30, and those accounts then move to Max, a credit plan where only 4K upscaling stays uncapped. Luma removed its relaxed mode in the same period and now prices every model per second.

What remains on the market is scoped by model list, generation, or window, which is the structure this ranking compares. For users moving off a retired plan, the options are collected in our Runway alternatives breakdown.

What Should You Check Before Buying an Unlimited Plan?

Seven questions help test an unlimited offer against a real workload, and most are answerable from the vendor's pricing page and terms:

Where is the ceiling: a queue, a resolution cap, a daily window, or a term?
How many generations run at once, and does unlimited share the queue with credit users?
What does a week of your actual output look like inside those concurrency numbers?
Do commercial rights apply on that specific tier, and where in the terms is that confirmed?
What resolution does unlimited render at on your tier, and does it match your delivery format?
Does your workflow run where unlimited applies, or through an API or agent where it does not?
What is the fallback price per clip in credits when you step outside the covered scope?
