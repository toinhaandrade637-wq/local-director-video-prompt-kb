How to Make Video Ads for Your Small Business With AI
Higgsfield
Oct 8, 2026
10 min

To make a video ad for a small business with AI, you need clear product photos or a link, a defined audience, one main message, and a placement plan. An AI video platform can turn those materials into an ad, such as a person presenting the product. Supporting static ads can promote the same product across the campaign. This guide uses Higgsfield Marketing Studio to create the video ads, with Ads Studio as an optional way to create static ads for the same campaign.

What Makes a Good Small Business Video Ad?

A good small business ad does one job quickly: it shows what you sell, says who it's for, and tells viewers what to do next. Plan for people who watch without sound, so the picture and the on-screen text carry the message on their own. Focus each ad on one product, one main benefit, and a clear next step.

What Do You Need Before You Start?

Gather these four things first. Each one removes a decision you would otherwise make in the middle of generating.

Product materials: Gather clear product photos or a link to the product page.
Target audience: Define who the ad is for and which need your product addresses.
Main message: Choose one benefit to highlight and include any relevant price or promotion.
Placement and call to action: Decide where the ad will run and what viewers should do next, such as shop, book, or visit your store.

The examples below use one made-up product: a 330 ml can of sparkling lemonade from a fictional brand called Squeeze Club. The first example builds a UGC ad from a template, the second builds a product video from a prompt, and the third uses Ads Studio for matching static ads. Available settings vary by template and model.

Example 1: UGC Ads

Step 1: Choose a UGC template. Open Marketing Studio, go to UGC Videos, and select a template with a person presenting a product.

Step 2: Add your product and set the format. Upload a clear photo of the can or paste the product page link if the template supports it. Check any imported product details and correct mistakes. Then choose vertical 9:16 and a 15-second duration where available.

Step 3: Review the presenter and ad message. No prompt is required. Check the script or message options available in the template. Keep the ad focused on one main benefit, open with a clear hook, and end with a call to action such as "Shop Squeeze Club." Verify that any product claims are accurate before generating. For example:

Hook: "Ready for a fizzy break?"
Benefit: "Squeeze Club brings sparkling lemon refreshment in a grab-and-go can."
Call to action: "Shop Squeeze Club."

If the presenter is AI-generated, do not write the script as though they are a real customer describing personal experience with the product. If the ad relies on dialogue, add captions or on-screen text so the message still works without sound.

Step 4: Generate the video. Check the credit cost shown on the Generate button, then start the generation.

Step 5: Review and download. Compare the can's shape, color, and label with your reference. Check that the hook, benefit, and call to action come through, that the spoken claims are accurate, that the captions or on-screen text are readable, and that the audio and lip sync are clear. If anything is off, adjust the available settings or choose another template before generating again.

Optional: Add graphics in a video editor, such as your logo, an offer, or a call to action.

Example 2: A Product Video from a Prompt

Step 1: Upload a product reference. Use a clear, well-lit photo of the same can, with its shape, color, and label visible. 

Step 2: Set the video format. Choose vertical 9:16 and a 15-second duration where supported.

Step 3: Describe the movement. Specify the camera movement, setting, and lighting.

Step 4: Generate the video. Check the settings and credit cost, then start the generation. 

Step 5: Add the ad message and CTA. Turn the generated product shot into the finished ad by adding one main benefit, any relevant offer, and a clear CTA such as "Shop Squeeze Club." Keep the copy short enough to read comfortably on a phone.

Step 6: Review and download. Check the camera movement, framing, and product details. Make sure the can keeps its shape and the label remains consistent throughout the clip, then download the version you want to use.

Step 1: Set up your brand. Paste your website URL and click Create my brand, or create the brand kit manually if you do not have a website.

Step 2: Review the brand kit. Check the logo, colors, fonts, tone of voice, audience, and selling points. Correct or add any missing information.

What to Check Before You Publish Your Ad

Run through this list on a phone before the ad goes live:

AI disclosure is correct: Check the rules for the advertising platform and regions you are targeting. Where required, label or disclose that the creative was AI-generated or significantly AI-edited.
Rights are cleared: Make sure you have permission to use any uploaded images, logos, music, voices, or real people shown in the ad. Review generated assets for third-party trademarks or copyrighted material before publishing.
Product looks right: Compare the shape, color, label, and logo with the real item in every shot.
Text is readable on a phone: Check the size, the contrast, and how long each line stays on screen.
Product claims are accurate: Check every benefit, demonstration, statistic, price, discount, and spoken claim against information you can support. Make sure generated copy or visuals do not imply results the product cannot deliver.
Audio is clear: Listen on a phone speaker and with headphones, and check that spoken lines match the lips.
Format fits the placement: Confirm the aspect ratio and length, and keep text away from the edges where the app's buttons and captions appear.
The link works: Make sure it opens the correct product or landing page.
