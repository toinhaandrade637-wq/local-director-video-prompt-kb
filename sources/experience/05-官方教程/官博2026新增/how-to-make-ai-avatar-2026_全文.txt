How Do You Create Your Avatar’s Look?

Create the look by generating a new character from a text prompt or by uploading a clear photo of yourself. Check the image before you animate it, because flaws in the image carry into the video.

From a text prompt

A good avatar prompt describes six things: who the character is, how they are framed, their expression, what they wear, where they are and the light. Give the character one or two distinctive details, such as a hairstyle or an accessory. They make the face easier to recognize from one video to the next.

For Maya, the character we follow through this guide, we generated a character reference sheet: a full-body view for the outfit and a sharp waist-up portrait for the face, on a plain white background. From your own photo

For a single-photo avatar, use a sharp, well-lit, front-facing image with the face clearly visible. You can use it as the reference in the next step without generating anything first. For a full photo checklist, see [How to Put Yourself in an AI Video URL will be added soon]. Use only your own photo or one you have permission to use.

What to check before you animate

Before moving to video, check that the image has:

A sharp, front-facing view of the face
A plain background, so the scene can be set in the video prompt
Hands away from the face
No text, logos or other people in the frame
The details you want to keep, such as hair, outfit and accessories, clearly visible
How Do You Make Your Avatar Talk?

To make your avatar talk, make a voice track, add the avatar image and the track as references in Seedance 2.5, describe the scene in the prompt and click Generate. Maya’s first 5-second clip cost 80 credits (≈$3.92) per attempt and took 3 attempts.

Choose a voice

Higgsfield Audio is Higgsfield’s voice workspace, with three tabs: Text to Speech, Voice Change and Translate.

You can voice your avatar in three ways:

A preset voice. Open Audio → Text to Speech, type the script, pick a preset voice and a model, then download the track from History. Maya uses the Annie voice with Seed Audio 1.0; the track for her script cost 0.5 credits.
Your own cloned voice. In Audio → Text to Speech, click Create custom voice, then record your voice or upload a clean sample. This works best when the avatar is you.
Your own recording. Use a finished audio file. This works best when you need exact delivery.

To change the voice in a finished video, open Audio → Voice Change, upload the video and pick a preset or custom voice. It replaces the voice and keeps the delivery, so you don’t need to regenerate the video.

Clone only your own voice or a voice you have clear permission to use. Higgsfield allows consented voice cloning only. For the cleanest clone, record clear speech in a quiet room.

How Do You Reuse the Same Avatar in a New Video?

To reuse your avatar, keep the same reference image and the same voice and change the script. For a new scene, describe it in the Seedance 2.5 prompt and keep the same reference. For a long series, train Soul ID.

When is one image enough, and when do you need Soul ID?

You don’t need Soul ID for a simple video. One good image is enough until you need the same face in many new scenes or from new angles, and the right next step depends on whose face it is.

When is one image enough, and when do you need Soul ID?

A series with many scenes, outfits or angles

	

Generate a set of portraits of the same face, then train Soul ID on them

	

Soul ID, trained on 20–80 of your own approved photos in about 3–5 minutes

Soul ID is Higgsfield’s trained character identity. You upload 20–80 photos of the same face once: your own photos, or a set of portraits you generate first for an invented character. Soul ID then keeps that face in new images, which you can animate in Seedance 2.5. Training a Soul ID character costs 25 credits once. The images and videos you generate with that identity are charged separately according to the selected model and settings.

Why Does My Avatar Look or Sound Wrong, and How Do I Fix It?

Most problems come from the image, the audio or the prompt. Fix those before you change models.

Before you publish: use only your own face and voice, or ones you have permission to use, and disclose AI-generated or manipulated content where required by applicable law or the platform where you publish it.
