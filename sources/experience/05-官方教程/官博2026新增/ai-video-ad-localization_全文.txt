How to Localize Video Ads Into Every Language With AI: Dubbing, Lip-Sync, and Reframe
Higgsfield
Sep 23, 2026
10 min

Localizing a video ad into every target language starts with one approved campaign video. From there, the process runs in three steps: translate and re-voice the audio, sync the new voice to the speaker's lips, and reframe the finished result for each platform's aspect ratio. Higgsfield covers all three through Audio, Lipsync Studio, and Reframe.

Which Campaigns Benefit From Video Localization?

Video localization is most useful when one campaign needs to reach audiences who share the same product need but differ by language, location, platform, or buying context. One approved creative can be adapted for a wide range of audiences, without building a separate ad from scratch for each one:

International ecommerce customers shopping in different languages and currencies
Regional audiences that use different expressions, offers, or calls to action
Multilingual communities within the same country
Mobile app and SaaS users across several markets
Global product launches running simultaneously in multiple countries
Social audiences watching the same campaign on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and connected TV

Each audience may need a different combination of translated speech, voice delivery, captions, on-screen text, pricing, and aspect ratio.

What Changes When You Localize an Ad?

Localizing isn't just translation. Localization may require changes to the voice, script, captions, on-screen text, offers, and aspect ratio.

Miss any one of these and the ad reads as something adapted for the market, a step removed from feeling like it belongs there. A translated script with the wrong lip-sync looks like a badly dubbed film. Perfect lip-sync with stiff, literal translation sounds like it was run through a machine. Getting both right at once is what makes a strong localized ad should sound natural and appropriate for its target market.

The Four-Step Localization Workflow on Higgsfield

Create the Original Ad With Higgsfield Marketing Studio

Marketing Studio builds the base ad every localized version branches from. Its library covers 1,500+ templates spanning product videos, UGC ads, fashion content, and cinematic formats, pick one, add the product, and a finished ad comes back in a single pass.

A clear opening and a straightforward product interaction tend to travel best across languages, since the pacing has the least room to drift once new audio replaces the original underneath it.

Step 1: Translate and Adapt the Spoken Script

Translate the script for the target market, adapting it for timing rather than translating word for word. Some languages produce noticeably longer sentences than others, and a line that runs long can throw off pacing and lip-sync quality further down the pipeline.

Step 2: Generate or Upload the Localized Voice

Inside Audio, select Translate, upload the video, and pick a target language. Audio generates a translated, re-voiced audio track from it, or an existing localized voice recording can be uploaded directly if one already exists.

Step 3: Synchronize the Audio With Lipsync Studio

Lipsync Studio takes that localized audio and syncs it against the speaker's mouth movement, running across seven models, Google Veo 3, Wan 2.5 Speak, Kling Avatars 2.0, Higgsfield Speak 2.0, Infinite Talk, Sync Lipsync 3, and Kling Lipsync. It offers several image-to-video and video-to-video models. Availability, resolution, templates, and credit costs vary by model.

Step 4: Update Captions, On-Screen Text, and Reframe

Before reframing, update captions, on-screen text, prices, currencies, dates, CTAs, and legal copy for the target market, none of that updates automatically alongside the dubbed audio. Add subtitles even on a fully dubbed version, since a large share of social video gets watched with the sound off.

Translations should be adapted for timing instead of translated word for word. Some languages produce longer sentences, which can affect pacing and lip-sync quality.

Once that's done, Reframe resizes the finished, localized cut for whatever aspect ratio a platform needs:

9:16: TikTok and Instagram Reels
1:1: Instagram or Facebook feed post
16:9: YouTube

After each conversion, check that the framing still holds up, captions land where they should, the product stays visible, and nothing important sits outside the safe area.

Localization Quality Checklist
The translated message sounds like something a native speaker would say
Lip movements match the localized audio's rhythm and timing
Pronunciation of names, numbers, and brand terms is correct
A native speaker has reviewed the translated copy, pronunciation, tone, and cultural meaning before publishing
Regional wording, currency, and any on-screen offer match the target market
Captions, logos, and product placement stay inside the safe area after reframing
Permission is secured to clone or synthetically reproduce a person's voice
The original actor's agreement explicitly covers translated and AI-modified versions
Likeness and usage rights are confirmed for every target market
Local advertising claims, prices, disclaimers, and promotional rules have been reviewed

Some markets also need different packaging, UI, currency, or visual references entirely, not every campaign localizes through audio alone.
