How to Create AI Real Estate Videos from Photos
Higgsfield
Oct 20, 2026
10 min

To create AI real estate videos from photos, upload clear property images to an image-to-video tool, describe the camera movement, and generate clips for each space. Then combine them into a property tour with listing details and music. This guide covers how to choose your photos, write prompts, and review the results so the finished video presents the property accurately.

What Makes a Good AI Real Estate Video?

A good AI real estate video gives potential buyers a clear sense of the property’s spaces, features, and overall character. Each shot should highlight something useful, such as the kitchen layout, living area, or outdoor space. Gentle camera movement and a logical sequence make the tour easy to follow, while consistent lighting and accurate details help viewers understand what the property actually offers.

How to Choose the Right Property Photos

The quality of your source photos shapes the final result, so start with clear images that accurately show the property.

Take sharp, high-resolution photos with straight vertical lines and no heavy filters. Choose framing that suits your intended video format and leaves important property details visible.
Include at least one wide shot of every room you want in the video, and add a second angle for large spaces.
Use daylight and keep the lighting consistent across rooms, so the clips don't jump between times of day.
Clear the clutter before photographing: personal items, cables, and cleaning supplies distract from the space.
Leave people out of the frame. Keep the focus on the property and make sure furniture, fixtures, and architectural details remain clearly visible.
Choose one main photo for each room and keep additional angles available as supporting references. Avoid mixing photos of different rooms when generating a single shot.

How to Keep the Property Accurate in AI-Generated Videos

An AI video is a rendering built from your photos, so it can introduce details that the property doesn't have. Accuracy matters for the buyer and for your reputation, so treat every clip as a draft until you have checked it.

Compare each clip with the source photos for room size, window count and position, finishes, fixtures, and the view outside.
Watch for warped lines, bent walls, doors that change position, and furniture that appears, disappears, or changes shape.
Fix single errors with Edit Video, which avoids regenerating the whole clip, and regenerate short clips when the geometry is off.
Don't add features that the property lacks, such as a pool, a larger window, or a different view.
Don't rely on generated text for house numbers, signs, prices or other exact information. Add the address, price and listing details as text overlays in your editor after generation.
Disclose AI-generated content. Rules on altered or AI-generated images in listings vary by market and portal, so check your local MLS and advertising rules, and label the video as AI-generated.
How to Edit Your Video for Listings and Social Media

Arrange the clips into a clear property tour: exterior, entrance, living areas, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, and outdoor spaces. Adjust the sequence to reflect the property’s actual layout.

Trim each clip to the part that works, and keep the cuts on the camera movement so the transitions feel smooth.
Add music you have the right to use commercially, and mute the generated audio if it competes with the soundtrack.
Add text overlays for the address, price and key features where your listing platform allows them.
Export in the right format: Use 16:9 for horizontal web and YouTube versions, and 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Check your MLS or listing portal's current video specifications before exporting the listing version.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
Using low-quality photos. Blurry or dark photos produce weak clips, so reshoot before you generate.
Packing several rooms into one clip. One room and one move per clip gives you more control and easier fixes.
Choosing fast or complex camera moves. Slow, steady moves hold the geometry of the room better.
Skipping the accuracy check. A clip that looks good can still show a window that doesn't exist.
Adding features to make the property look better. Misleading visuals can create legal and reputational problems.
Making the video too long. Short clips that hold attention work better than a slow tour of every closet.
Cropping a horizontal video for a vertical feed can cut off important property details, so plan the aspect ratio before generating.
