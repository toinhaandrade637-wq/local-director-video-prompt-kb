How to Make an AI Animated Short Film in 2026
Higgsfield
Sep 26, 2026
10 min

Making an AI animated short film in 2026 usually involves five main steps: designing the characters and visual style, storyboarding the film, generating each shot, adding dialogue or lip sync, and editing the clips into a final cut. AI can now handle most of this workflow in styles such as cartoon, anime, painterly, or 3D-rendered animation. The biggest challenge is keeping the same characters and visual style consistent from shot to shot.

What Is an Animated Short Film, and What Can AI Create?

An animated short film tells a complete story in a brief runtime, often under 10 minutes. Its characters, settings, colors, and movement all help carry that story. A small change in expression can reveal what a character is thinking, while a shift in lighting can change the mood of an entire scene.

AI video tools can create animated shots from written prompts and reference images. They give filmmakers room to explore styles, from illustrated scenes to stylized 3D worlds, before settling on a look for the film. Choosing that look early makes it easier to keep the characters and settings recognizable across shots. The styles below offer different starting points, depending on the story you want to tell.

How Do You Keep Characters and Animation Style Consistent?
Help preserve the character's face, proportions, and design before generating a single shot, and maintain that consistency through the entire production
Use a trained identity system when the same character needs to appear across many scenes, and keep the reference images consistent throughout the project
Choose the color palette and rendering style early, then reuse the same visual settings and references across each shot
Use clear, well-lit character references where the face, proportions, clothing, and key design details are easy to see
Check the final frame of one shot against the first frame of the next before moving on, small mismatches are easier to catch one shot at a time than after the whole film is assembled

Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Write the script and shot list. Nail down the story, the characters, the scenes, and the dialogue before opening Higgsfield at all. Lock a shot list, roughly how many shots the film needs and what happens in each one, since a vague description here is the most expensive mistake to catch later.

Step 2: Choose the animation style. Decide on cartoon, anime, painterly, or 3D-render based on the shot list and the story's tone, and commit to it. Switching styles partway through production forces every earlier shot to be regenerated.

Step 3: Create character and location references. Use Soul 2.0 to generate a reference image for each character and location, based on the descriptions from the script. Use clear, well-lit references where the face, proportions, clothing, and key design details are easy to see.

Step 4: Save recurring characters with Soul ID. Train Soul ID on 20+ approved reference images per character, then run a few test generations to confirm the identity holds. Training on too few or inconsistent images is what causes drift a few shots into production.

Step 5: Build the storyboard in Popcorn. Turn the locked shot list and character references into a full shot-by-shot visual plan. Check pacing and composition here, it's far cheaper to fix now than after six 30-second clips are already generated.

Step 6: Generate the clips in Cinema Studio 4.0. Use the prompt to describe what happens in the shot, use Cinema Studio controls for camera and visual direction, and reuse the same character references and style settings for continuity.

Step 7: Add dialogue and lip sync. Generate or upload the dialogue, then use Lipsync Studio to sync the character's mouth movement to the final voice track where needed.

Step 8: Fix shots and add edits. Use Genjutsu to swap a prop, vehicle, or background element without a full regeneration, and Motion Transfer if a performance needs to move to a different character or scene.

Step 9: Assemble and export the final film. Before exporting, compare each shot with the previous one and check the character's face, proportions, clothing, colors, line style, lighting, and background details. Higgsfield generates individual shots up to 30 seconds long, it doesn't assemble them into one continuous film, so a separate editor like CapCut still has to stitch the exported clips together into the final cut.
