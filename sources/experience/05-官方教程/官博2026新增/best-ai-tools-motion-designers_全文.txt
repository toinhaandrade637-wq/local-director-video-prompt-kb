9 Best AI Tools for Motion Designers in 2026
Higgsfield
Oct 15, 2026
10 min

Motion designers use AI for four jobs: creating graphics, animating visuals, controlling movement, and refining finished video. This guide compares nine tools, from clip generators to tools that work inside editable compositions. It covers what each tool does, how you continue working with the result, and what it costs, including plan limits.

Where Does AI Fit Into a Motion Design Workflow?

AI helps at four stages of a motion design project:

Concept development: visual ideas and style frames.
Asset creation: images, backgrounds, and 3D objects.
Animation: movement of graphics, characters, and the camera.
Compositing and finishing: masks, cleanup, and combining materials.

The tools below produce two kinds of output. A generated video is a single rendered clip: you can trim, grade, or composite it, but you cannot separately edit its text, shapes, objects, or animation after generation. A project with editable layers and keyframes keeps text, shapes, timing, and animation separate, so you can change a headline or a duration without regenerating anything. Some tools on this list produce the first, some work with the second, and a few connect the two.

Higgsfield AI: Motion Graphics and Editable 3D Workflows

Higgsfield is an AI-native creative suite with 35+ models and tools. Its AI Motion Designer is an agent that connects Higgsfield to Adobe After Effects through ChatGPT. You describe a task in plain language, such as animating a title, building a logo reveal, or adding a transition, and it builds the result inside your open After Effects composition. The layers it creates stay native After Effects elements, so text, color, timing, and animation remain editable by hand. The agent also runs through Higgsfield MCP, so it can be triggered from Claude or Cursor.

Beyond After Effects, Higgsfield covers 3D scenes and generated shots:

3D Jutsu: A browser workspace for editable scene blocking and previz. A prompt becomes a scene with objects, lighting, cameras, and animation, which renders to video or exports as MP4, a static frame, or GLB.
Blender plugin: Adds a floating bar over the viewport with tabs including Scene Builder, 3D Model, Character Animation, Image, Video, and Camera. Scene Builder creates editable scene objects, while the other tools add models, character animation, images, or video assets to the project.
Cinema Studio 4.0: Generated cinematic shots, with clips up to 30 seconds and up to 50 references.

Pricing: Starter is $15 a month for 200 credits, Plus is $49 for 1,000, and Ultra is $129 for 3,000. The ChatGPT workflow requires active subscriptions to Higgsfield, ChatGPT, and After Effects. Other supported MCP clients have their own pricing. Higgsfield generations use your regular credit balance.

Where Higgsfield falls short:

The full workflow requires After Effects alongside Higgsfield, with additional costs depending on the AI assistant and plan you use.
Adobe Firefly: Animated Clips From Text and Images

Firefly generates video from a text prompt or an image. Prompt to Edit (beta) changes a clip with a text prompt, and the Firefly Video Editor (beta) is a browser-based timeline editor. Boards gives you a canvas for visual concepts, with 4K upscaling through Topaz Astra. Video partner models include Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Runway Gen-4.5, and Luma Ray3.

For motion designers, this covers practical jobs: bringing a style frame to life, creating a moving background, or generating an extra element to place in a composition. The results are clips, so the next step is usually trimming and compositing in your editor.

Pricing: Standard is $9.99 a month for 2,000 generative credits and up to 20 five-second videos. Pro is $19.99 for 4,000 credits and up to 40 videos, and Pro Plus is $49.99 for 10,000 credits and up to 100 videos. Monthly credits don't roll over.

Where Firefly falls short:

Adobe's own Firefly Video Model generates five-second clips at up to 1080p, so longer sequences need to be assembled from multiple clips.
Runway: AI Video Generation and Editing

Runway focuses on generating video and editing it afterward. Gen-4.5 is its own video model, at 12 credits per second. Aleph 2.0 applies edits from a modified frame across an existing video, at 28 credits per second, so you can change a clip you already have. Music, voice, and sound effects can also be generated within Runway, starting with the Standard plan.

Act-Two transfers a performance onto a character. You use your own acting as the source, and the result is a clip you can bring into a project and continue working on in your editor.

Pricing: Standard is $15 a month for 625 credits, Pro is $35 for 2,250, and Max is $95 for 9,500. A 10-second Gen-4.5 clip costs 120 credits, which is about 5 clips on Standard.

Where Runway falls short:

Results are video clips, so changes to individual elements mean editing the clip again or recompositing it in an editor
Kling AI: Image Animation and Motion Control

Kling AI supports text-to-video and image-to-video generation, multi-shot scenes, and character animation.

Standard generation animates from a prompt or an image. Motion Control takes the movement from a reference video and applies it to a character, so it fits jobs where the motion is already filmed or acted and you need a different character or look performing it.

Kling 4.0 is currently in early access, with a wider rollout planned for October 2026.

Pricing: Standard is $10 a month for 660 credits, Pro is $37 for 3,000, Premier is $92 for 8,000, and Ultra is $180 for 26,000. Monthly subscription credits expire after one month.

Where Kling falls short:

Kling covers one model family, and its results are video clips that need an editor for titles, graphics, and layered changes
Adobe After Effects: AI-Assisted Rotoscoping and Compositing

Object Matte, added in After Effects 26.2 in April 2026, isolates a moving subject without painting over it. You click or marquee-select a person, animal, or vehicle in one frame, and After Effects generates a matte and tracks it through the shot. Version 26.5 added disk caching, so mattes persist after you close a project. A beta build also adds an AI Assistant that controls After Effects through natural-language commands.

In practice, you use Object Matte to remove or replace backgrounds without a green screen, apply effects to a single object, or build clean composites for motion graphics and VFX. Adobe’s AI Assistant can organize projects, work with compositions and expressions, and generate images and videos directly inside After Effects. Higgsfield's AI Motion Designer adds an agent from outside After Effects that builds titles, transitions, and reveals into the composition from plain-language instructions.

Pricing: A single-app After Effects subscription is $34.49 a month on month-to-month billing, or $263.88 a year. The AI Assistant is a beta feature.

Where After Effects falls short:

Object Matte reduces manual isolation work but doesn't remove the need for compositing skills, and tricky shots can still need cleanup
Magnific: Multiple Video Models in One Workspace

Magnific, formerly Freepik, puts image, video, and audio models in one workspace with a single credit pool. Its video generator lists models including Google Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 with Motion Control, Seedance 2.5 and 2.0, and LTX 2, plus a Magnific video upscaler.

The main benefit is choosing the model per shot. Veo 3.1 and Kling 3.0 offer resolutions up to 4K, while Seedance 2.5 goes up to 1080p, so you can match the model to the movement, visual style, and format you need. Spaces, a node-based workspace, supports multi-step workflows.

Pricing: Premium is $20 a month with 20,000 credits. Credit use varies by model: at 720p, Kling 2.5 costs 28 credits per second and Seedance 2.5 costs 440. Unused plan credits expire at the end of the billing cycle.

Where Magnific falls short:

Video uses credits quickly, and cost differs widely by model, so the same plan covers far fewer seconds of Seedance 2.5 than of Kling 2.5
Luma: Video Restyling, Keyframes, and HDR Workflows

Luma's current Ray3.2 model combines video generation with Modify Video, multi-keyframe direction, Reframe, and native HDR/EXR output. For motion designers, Modify Video is especially useful for changing the visual treatment of existing footage while preserving the underlying motion.

A typical task is testing several visual directions on one sequence of motion: keep the movement of a shot, and compare different styles, materials, or characters without rebuilding the animation each time.

Pricing: Plus is $30 a month for 10,000 credits, with commercial use and watermark-free output included. A 1080p, five-second Ray3.14 clip costs about 400 credits, so Plus covers roughly 25 of them a month.

Where Luma falls short:

Higher resolutions and HDR workflows consume more credits, leaving less room for multiple versions within a fixed monthly budget.
Pika: Short Animations and Visual Effects

Pika covers text-to-video, image-to-video, and a set of effect tools. Pikaffects transforms objects with effects, Pikaframes builds transitions between keyframes, and Pikaswaps and Pikadditions change or add objects in existing video. Pika 2.5 is available alongside other video models.

Good fits are object transformations, short animated inserts, and effects for branded clips, where a fast stylized result matters more than precise control.

Pricing: Starter is $10 a month for 900 credits, Creator is $35 for 3,150 credits, and Fancy starts at $95 for 8,550 credits.

Where Pika falls short:

Commercial use starts on Creator. Starter includes watermark-free downloads but does not include a commercial license.
Krea: From Visual Concepts to Animated Clips

Krea combines a real-time canvas for image generation with image and video models, upscaling, 3D, lip sync, and LoRA training. Its video models include Veo 3.1, Kling, Runway, Hailuo, and Wan, and Nodes lets you chain steps into repeatable workflows.

The workflow fits concept development: develop a visual direction, then use one source visual to try different movements across models, and keep the strongest version.

Pricing: Basic is $9 a month for 5,000 compute units, a commercial license, and limited video access. Pro is $35 for 20,000 units, all video models, and Nodes, and Max is $105 for 60,000 units.

Where Krea falls short:

Full access to video models starts on Pro, and compute units don't roll over on individual plans
From Brief to Final Animation: An Example Workflow

The example is a short product presentation with a moving background, a title, and a final logo. Several tools can work together on it:

Prepare the concept and materials: Collect product photos, brand colors, and a style frame. Krea or Magnific can help you develop the visual direction.
Generate the background and product shots: Animate the background and product frames with a video model such as Kling or Runway Gen-4.5, or generate shots in Higgsfield Cinema Studio.
Add the title and logo animation: Describe the title and the logo reveal to Higgsfield AI Motion Designer, which builds them into your After Effects composition as editable layers.
Refine timing, transitions, and composition: Adjust keyframes in After Effects, and use Object Matte if you need to isolate the product from a shot.
Check the details and export: Compare the product's shape, color, and label with the real item, check that text is readable on a phone, and export the formats you need.
Which Tool Fits the Way You Work?
Editable compositions and precise timing: After Effects, with Higgsfield AI Motion Designer for titles, transitions, and logo reveals.
Animating an image or generating a clip: Kling AI, Runway, Adobe Firefly, or Pika.
Carrying motion over or changing a video's style: Runway (Act-Two and Aleph 2.0), Kling Motion Control, or Luma Modify Video.
A 3D scene: Higgsfield 3D Jutsu or the Higgsfield plugin for Blender.
Masks and compositing: After Effects Object Matte.
Several models in one workspace: Magnific, Krea, or Adobe Firefly.
