How to Master AI Filmmaking (Full Guide)
Higgsfield
Oct 24, 2026
7 min

To master AI filmmaking, plan the story and the shots first, create references for your character and location, and generate every shot with those references so the look stays as consistent as possible. In Higgsfield, an AI-native creative suite, one way to do this is with Seedream 5.0 Pro, Soul Cinema, Supercomputer and Cinema Studio 4.0. This guide is for creators who already generate single clips, and it goes through the whole process on one 10-second scene: a girl waiting for the last train at a seaside station at blue hour.

TL;DR

AI filmmaking is making a film where AI generates the images, the shots and the sound, while you plan the story, the look and the order of shots.
Use references for your character and location in every shot, and generate the scene as one multishot clip. This helps keep the face and the location the same.
Each generation costs credits, and additional generations or alternatives may increase the cost, so plan before you generate.

How Do You Prepare Before You Generate?

Prepare four things before you generate: a one-sentence idea, a list of shots, an image of your character and an image of your location.

The idea. Write what happens in one sentence. Ours: at blue hour, a girl waits for the last train at a seaside station, the train arrives, she turns toward it, steps into the car and the train leaves.

The shots. Split the idea into shots, the way a storyboard would:

The character and the location. Create the character in Create Image → Seedream 5.0 Pro and the location in Create Image → Soul Cinema. For the character, make a character sheet: one image that shows the same person from several angles. For the location, make one image of the empty place.

These two images are your references. You will add them to every shot. This helps keep the face, the outfit and the station the same from start to finish.

How Do You Generate the Scene?

Write a short idea, improve it with Supercomputer, then generate the scene in Cinema Studio 4.0 as a multishot with your references tagged in the prompt.

Step 1: Improve the prompt. Give Supercomputer your idea and the list of shots, and ask it to improve the prompt. It turns a short description into a detailed prompt for each shot. In Ask before generating mode, Supercomputer shows the credit cost and asks you to confirm before each generation.

Our starting idea:

Blue hour at a small seaside station. A young woman in an olive canvas vest, black tie, silver headphones around her neck and light-wash paint-splattered jeans waits alone on the platform, facing the sea. The last train, a cream carriage with one lime stripe, arrives and stops; she boards, walks up the aisle past one lit window, then the next, and sits down in the middle of the carriage with her bag on her lap. The doors close and the train pulls away to screen left. The empty platform stays until the frame fades. Short telephoto, blue, cold white and peach, quiet and accidentally observed - no dialogue, no music.

The improved prompt from Supercomputer:

The last train at a small seaside station at blue hour. A young woman waits alone on the platform, the train arrives and stops, she boards, walks along the carriage and sits down in the middle of it, the doors close and the train leaves. No drama, no dialogue: a quiet, accidentally observed moment from someone else's life.

Camera: static, locked off.
First frame: the train is already fully stopped at the platform of <<<image_2>>>. A cream carriage with the lime stripe stands behind the platform under the canopy. Its doors at screen-right, about x 70%, are already open and warm light spills onto the platform. The woman from <<<image_1>>> stands on the platform right in front of these open doors.
TRAIN STOPPED LOCK: from 5.0s to 8.3s the train is completely stationary: wheels still, carriage motionless, the windows and the stripe do not slide.
5.0s to 5.8s: she adjusts the bag strap on her shoulder and steps through the open doors into the carriage.
5.8s to 7.2s: inside, she turns left and walks along the aisle toward the middle of the carriage; through the lit side windows we see her pass behind one window, then the next.
7.2s to 7.8s: in the middle of the carriage, halfway between the two sets of doors, around x 45%, she sits down on the bench seat on the far side of the carriage, facing the platform and the camera, bag on her lap. She is framed by a side window, not by a doorway.
7.8s to 8.3s: after she has sat down, the doors at x 70% slide fully shut; the doorway becomes a closed door. She is now visible only through the side window where she sits.
8.3s to 9.4s: only after the doors are closed, the train slowly starts moving to screen-left with real mass, gently accelerating; her lit window slides away with the carriage and leaves the frame.
9.4s to 10.0s: the empty platform remains: the fluorescent tubes, the benches and the sea. The image fades to black.

PHYSICS
The train has real weight: braking to a full stop, complete stillness while she boards, walks and sits, then a slow smooth departure. Hair and the bag strap react to wind and the train's air wave with a short delay. Her steps inside the carriage carry real weight; she sits down with natural body weight.

LIGHTING
Blue hour just after sunset: deep cobalt sky, a thin peach band of last light above the sea. The cold white fluorescent tubes under the canopy are the only artificial light on the station until the train arrives; then the warm light from the train windows and doors takes over. Palette: blue, cold white, warm peach, one lime accent. Exposure is set for the lit platform; the sky stays deep blue, the shadows stay soft and readable.

AUDIO
Waves, wind and the hum of the fluorescent tubes. Then the clatter of wheels, the long hiss of the brakes, quiet steps inside the carriage, the door chime as the doors close, the soft hum of the departing train. At the end, silence again and the sea. No dialogue, no voice.

STYLE
35mm film, Kodak Vision3 500T, visible fine grain, soft halation around the lamps, gentle motion blur, quiet Japanese-indie cinema mood. Still, lonely, lightly melancholic, warm rather than heavy.

How Do You Add Sound and Finish the Film?

Turn on sound when you generate, then fix the finished clip with the Cinema Studio tools instead of generating it again.

Sound. With sound On, Cinema Studio generates it together with the picture. Describe what we should hear in the prompt: the waves, the hum of the lamps, the train coming closer, the doors closing. If you need voices or background sound as a separate track, you can make them in Audio with Seed Audio 1.0. See Higgsfield Audio.

How Much Does an AI Film Cost?

The cost depends on the model, the length, the resolution and the number of versions. Additional generations or alternatives may increase the cost. In Cinema Studio 4.0, a 5-second clip at 1080p costs 80 credits, ≈$3.92 at the Plus plan rate ($49 for 1,000 credits).

What adds to the budget:

Versions. Every completed version is charged, so 4 versions at once cost 4 times as much.
Retries. If the result doesn’t work, the next generation uses credits again. Failed generations are usually refunded automatically.
Supercomputer. Text requests also use credits, and Unlimited doesn’t apply in Supercomputer.
