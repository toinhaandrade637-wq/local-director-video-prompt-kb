There is no fixed price for an AI video. A finished video costs the price of one generation times the number of takes you need, plus image prep, sound and edits. Model, resolution and length set the price per take: in our check, 10 seconds cost about $1.35 on Kling 4.0 at 720p and about $7.84 on Seedance 2.5 at 1080p. For example, our 10-second clip cost 160.125 credits (about $7.85) in one take.
What Is the Real Cost of an AI Video?
The real cost of an AI video is everything you spend to reach the version you publish, not the price of one generation. It adds up image prep, every video take you keep or discard, sound and edits. The Generate button shows the price of one take; the finished video can cost several takes.
What goes into the total:
Reference images. A still that fixes the character, outfit and location before you animate it.
Video takes. Every completed generation is charged, including takes you decide not to use.
Failed generations. Credits for failed generations usually return automatically, so they do not add to the total.
Sound. Some models generate audio in the same take; otherwise you add it in a separate tool.
Edits. Upscaling, extending a clip or fixing part of a shot is charged as its own generation.
A first take that works is the cheapest possible case. Plan your budget for retakes, even if you don’t end up needing them. For a deeper look at how credits are deducted, see

What Changes the Cost of an AI Video
Five things set the price of an AI video: the model, the length, the resolution, the number of scenes and the number of retakes. In our price check, 10 seconds on Seedance 2.5 cost 30 to 160 credits, and 27.5 to 40 credits on Kling 4.0, before any retakes.

Why the same length doesn’t mean the same budget.
 Ten seconds of video are priced from 27.5 to 160 credits, depending only on model and resolution. Add retakes and the spread grows: a 10-second clip that needs three 1080p Seedance 2.5 takes costs twelve times more than one Kling 4.0 take at 1080p. We did not run a same-prompt quality test on Kling 4.0, so these numbers compare price, not results.
Scenes add up the same way. A 30-second ad built from three 10-second shots costs three takes at minimum. If each shot needs a retake, it costs six.
How Do You Calculate Your Budget?
Budget per video = (price per take × expected takes) + image prep + sound and edits. Then divide your monthly credits by that number to see how many finished videos a plan covers. Keep the subscription fee and the credits per video as two separate numbers.
A worked example based on our clip:
Price per take: check the Generate button. For our settings it was 160 credits.
Expected takes: use your own history. If you don’t have one yet, start with an assumption, for example two takes per shot, and adjust after your first videos.
Image prep: 0.125 credits for one reference image in our case.
Sound and edits: 0 for us, since Seedance 2.5 generated sound in the same take.
Budget per video: 160 × 2 + 0.125 = 320.125 credits (approx. $15.69) if you plan for two takes.
Videos per month: 1,000 credits ÷ 320.125 ≈ 3 finished clips like ours.

Two checks before you start: make sure your plan includes the model and resolution you need, and check the exact price on the Generate button. On Higgsfield’s entry-level plan, Veo, Sora 2 and Seedance 2.0 are closed (except Seedance 2.0 Fast at 720p and Seedance 2.0 Mini), and Kling 3.0 runs only at 720p.
How to Spend Fewer Credits
Spend cheap credits while you search for the shot and full-price credits only on the final take. Test motion in drafts, at lower resolution or at shorter length, and reuse the same reference images and Elements across scenes. On Seedance 2.5, a 10-second clip at 480p cost 30 credits, against 160 at 1080p.
Draft first. Seedance 2.5 drafts at 480p can be finalized at 1080p within seven days. Seedance 2.0 Fast and Seedance 2.0 Mini cost less per take than Seedance 2.5, so they also work for tests.
Test at 720p. Our clip cost 70 credits at 720p. Check the motion and framing there, then generate the final version at 1080p.
Test short. Five seconds cost half of ten on Seedance 2.5. Check the action first, then generate the full length.
Reuse your assets. Our reference image cost 0.125 credits and can serve as the reference for the next scenes too. Saved Elements (characters, locations and objects) keep the same look without new prep.
Write one change per retake. Changing one thing at a time shows you what fixed the shot.

Got any questions left?
Can I make an AI video for free?
No. Video generation on Higgsfield runs on credits, which come with paid plans. The entry-level plan already includes video, with some models at 720p.
How much does a one-minute AI video cost?
A one-minute video takes at least two generations, since Seedance 2.5 and Kling 4.0 both go up to 30 seconds per take. With one take per shot, 60 seconds at 1080p cost 960 credits (approx. $47.04) on Seedance 2.5 or 240 credits (approx. $11.76) as six 10-second Kling 4.0 shots.
How many finished AI videos can I make on one subscription?
It depends on the model and resolution. With 1,000 credits and one take per clip, you get about 6 ten-second Seedance 2.5 clips at 1080p, 14 at 720p or 33 drafts, and 25 Kling 4.0 clips at 1080p or 36 at 720p.
What should I do if my credits run out before the month ends?
Buy a credit top-up if this happens rarely. Move to a larger plan if it happens every month, since larger plans include more credits. Compare other services only after checking which models and resolutions you need.
Can I pay per video without a subscription?
Yes, through the Higgsfield API, which is billed per generation, with no subscription. The API has its own dollar balance, and credits from a plan don’t transfer to it.
