How to Automate Product Videos From Your Ecommerce Catalog With AI
Higgsfield
Sep 19, 2026
12 min

Ecommerce brands can automate product video creation by connecting structured catalog data to reusable video templates. Product images, descriptions, prices, and offers become inputs for generating individual videos at scale. Each result can then be adapted for specific platforms and markets, while manual review protects product accuracy, brand consistency, and publishing quality.

What Types of Ecommerce Ads Can Be Automated?

One catalog can be used to generate several different ad formats. Product hero ads present the product itself. Feature videos explain what it does. Promotional ads add a price and a discount. Marketplace ads adapt the format for a specific listing platform.

What Product Data Is Needed for Video Automation?

Automation needs five main groups of data from the catalog: product photos, name and SKU, features and benefits, price and offer, and market and CTA. These fields get substituted into an approved video structure, so each SKU gets its own product, message, and final frame without a new brief built from scratch.

How Product Video Automation Works in Higgsfield

Catalog automation isn't multiplying one video with one product across a batch. It's applying a small set of approved templates across many different SKUs, each keeping the brand's visual style constant while the product, its data, and sometimes its category-specific format all change underneath.

Step 1: Get the catalog data in. Export the catalog through a CSV, a JSON file, a product-feed API, or a PIM, and validate required fields and product images before anything moves further. (Based on a general product-video generation workflow. Confirm the exact import method and available connectors with the Product team.)

Step 2: Organize by category and build a master ad for each. Different product types need different formats, a try-on format for clothing, a product hero or texture demonstration for cosmetics, a feature video for electronics. Marketing Studio or Cinema Studio builds a fixed composition, camera movement, duration, and message structure per category. Soul HEX and Moodboard help keep reference images brand-aligned, though the master ad for each category still needs a deliberate setup pass. Soul ID is optional, worth adding only if the same spokesperson needs to appear throughout the campaign.

Step 3: Map each SKU to its matching template. Each product's image, name, features, price, offer, and CTA get substituted into the matching category template. Genjutsu Object Swap replaces the product while preserving camera, motion, and composition, best when the replacement is visually compatible with the original; a very different shape or size needs a new version from the category workflow instead. Seedance 2.5 Edit handles anything more specific to that SKU, a different background or prop.

Step 4: Submit and track the batch. The Higgsfield API runs a separate generation job per product, returning a request ID with status tracking through polling or webhooks. Track the SKU, request ID, template, market, output, cost, and review status in the brand's own database, spreadsheet, DAM, or PIM, this isn't something Higgsfield stores on its own.

How Each Video Is Adapted for Different Platforms and Languages

Adapting a base video for different channels and markets runs through four separate jobs. It's never one combined step:

Reframe creates vertical, horizontal, and square versions of an existing video.
Seedance 2.5 Edit changes selected content inside a clip, an object, a background, or the audio.
Audio Translate translates and re-voices the dialogue for a target market.
Lipsync Studio synchronizes that translated audio with the speaker's mouth.

A new edit or generation is usually needed when the pacing or shot structure itself changes, past just the surface content. Changing the hook or script happens at the script level, a new written variant substituted in before generation, something neither Reframe nor Seedance touches directly.

How to Control Quality, Cost, and Publishing at Scale

Before publishing, each video should go through a review checklist:

Product shape, color, material, and proportions
Logo and packaging accuracy
Correct price, discount, SKU, and offer dates
Accurate translation and pronunciation
Realistic product interaction
Correct aspect ratio and safe areas
Likeness and voice permissions
Final human approval before publishing

Prices, SKUs, offer dates, disclaimers, and legal copy are best added as controlled text overlays sitting on top of the video, kept separate from anything generated inside it. Overlay text stays exact and editable; generated text does not.

Generation costs, retries, and total credit usage should also be tracked by SKU before approved files move into the publishing system.
