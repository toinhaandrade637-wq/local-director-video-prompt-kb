How to Put Two Consistent Characters in One AI Video Scene

Two AI characters are more likely to blend when their identities are not anchored separately. Giving each character its own reference sheet, named in the prompt, helps keep the two identities distinct. In Higgsfield, generate each sheet in Soul 2.0 and attach both as references in Cinema Studio 4.0. For recurring characters, Soul ID and Elements carry them across projects.

Why two characters blend in one AI video scene

Most character workflows are built around one identity at a time. When a scene adds a second person, the model usually gets one of three weak inputs: a text description of both people, one image with both faces in it, or a reference for only one of them. In each case the two identities share one anchor, and their features can mix.

Blending. Both characters drift toward one average face.
Swapping. Hair, outfits or props move from one character to the other between shots.
Duplication. A multi-view reference sheet gets copied literally, and the same character appears twice.
Scale drift. A child and a teenager end up the same height, or trade heights between shots.
Shot-to-shot drift. The first shot looks right, and by the third shot the cast looks slightly different.
Four ways to keep two characters apart on Higgsfield

A reliable way to reduce blending is to give each character its own reference, fixed before you generate the scene. On Higgsfield there are four routes to a two-character scene, and the choice depends on where you start: an existing performance, a finished clip, a scene of your own, or characters that need to return.

How Higgsfield handles two characters in one scene

A two-person scene touches several stages of production: building each character, building props and locations, generating the shots, and sometimes reworking footage you already have. Higgsfield is an AI-native creative suite, so these stages run in one place. Character sheets made in Soul 2.0 go straight into Cinema Studio 4.0 as references, and finished clips are available in Genjutsu for motion and character changes.

Cinema Studio 4.0 is how Higgsfield handles cinematography: it takes up to 50 references per generation and generates multi-shot clips up to 30 seconds with native audio. Genjutsu is how Higgsfield handles reworking existing footage: it keeps the motion of a clip and changes who or what is in it.

Step 1: Create a character sheet for each character

Each character gets one sheet, generated in Soul 2.0 at 16:9. Both sheets use the same three-panel layout:

Left panel: full-body rear view, head and shoes in frame.
Center panel: large frontal face close-up with the full hair silhouette.
Right panel: full-body front view, head and shoes in frame.
Same outfit in every panel, described down to colors and details, on a clean background with soft even light.

The prompts spell out what separates the two characters: age markers, build, height and wardrobe palettes that do not overlap (mustard and denim for Jerry, purple and cream for Lucy).

Step 3: Write each scene for two characters

The scene prompt should reinforce which identity belongs to each character. All five use the same structure:

A reference block. One line per reference: the tag, the character's name, age, height and key traits, followed by an instruction to preserve that character's reference identity.
A count lock. "Show one Jerry and one Lucy," plus a line that the character sheets carry identity only, so the three panels never turn into three copies of a character.
A scale lock. Both heights are fixed (163 cm and 125 cm), along with the ratio between them: Lucy is about 77% of Jerry's height.
A position lock. The first frame puts each character on a fixed side of the screen, and hands stay consistent, for example the remote in Jerry's right hand.
Timed shots. Each shot has a time range, shot size, camera height and field of view, with hard cuts between shots.
Dialogue with voices. Quoted lines per character, a voice description for each, and an instruction to speak only the quoted lines.
Locations as geography only. Each location line asks for new camera positions, so the location image sets the layout of the space and never becomes the opening frame.

Step 4: Generate the scenes in Cinema Studio 4.0
Open Cinema Studio, switch to Video and select Cinema Studio 4.0.
Click + and add the references for the scene: the character sheets, props and location listed above the prompt.
Paste the scene prompt and check that every tag in its reference block matches an attached image.
Set the look in the Director's Panel (Film setup, Camera, Color palette, Lighting), then set 16:9, the scene duration and sound on.
Click Generate. Cinema Studio 4.0 at 1080p costs 12 credits per second, so the 18-second bedroom scene costs 216 credits and the full 60-second film costs 720.
Repeat for each scene with the same character and prop references, so the cast stays consistent across all five scenes.

The same setup works in Seedance 2.5 on its own: open Video → Seedance 2.5 and add the images under References. For more on the studio, see Meet Cinema Studio 4.0.

Step 5: Check both characters before you move on

Review every clip against the character sheets before generating the next scene.

Compare a close-up of each character with the center panel of their sheet.
Check that the characters did not trade hair, clothing colors or props.
Check the height difference in every wide shot.
Count the characters in every shot: one of each.
Watch each clip to the end: most consistency problems show up around the 5 to 8 second mark.
If something is off, change one thing per retry: the prompt or a reference, never both at once.

If one face drifts in an otherwise usable clip, replace only that character with Objects swap, described below.

Keep the same characters across projects with Soul ID and Elements

A character sheet holds a character within the generations you attach it to. When Jerry and Lucy need to return in a series, move them to Soul ID and Elements.

Train a Soul ID for each character in Soul → Train new character on 20 to 80 images of that character.
Trained Soul ID characters appear in Elements automatically. Save the remote as a prop and the bedroom as a location: open each image in Assets and select Create Element from the three-dot menu.
In Cinema Studio 4.0 or Seedance 2.5, type @ in the prompt field to pick characters, props and locations by name.

A Soul ID represents one identity. For scenes with two or more recurring characters, train a Soul ID for each and use the characters through Elements. Elements is how Higgsfield handles consistency for scenes with more than one character: each person, location and prop is created once and pulled into any shot by name. For how Soul ID trains a face, see How to Keep Your AI Character Consistent Across Every Generation.

Alternative: repeat a clip's motion with Motion transfer

Use this route when the motion already exists, for example a two-person performance you filmed yourself, and you want your two characters to repeat it.

Open Video → Genjutsu and select Motion transfer.
Add a reference video between 4 and 30 seconds long that you shot or have the rights to use.
Add your characters: up to 30 images from Uploads, AI Influencer, Generations or Liked, for example the character sheets from Step 1.
Turn on the prompt only if you need to describe a specific change.
Choose quality (480p, 720p or 1080p) and click Generate.

For a full overview of the tool, see Meet Higgsfield Genjutsu.

Alternative: replace one of the two characters with Objects swap

Use this route when the scene is finished and only one character needs to change: a drifted face, a different second character for a variant, or a recast for another market.

Open Video → Genjutsu and select Objects swap.
Add the finished clip.
Add reference images of the new character and, optionally, a short description of which character to change.
Generate. The rest of the frame, including the other character, stays as it was.

For more ways to change one element in a finished clip, see How to Change One Element in a Finished AI Video Without Regenerating It.

Where it falls short
Two is easier than many. Two-character scenes are easier to control than larger casts. As more recurring characters share the frame, keeping identities, wardrobe and proportions separate becomes harder even with individual references.
Small drift is still possible. References help keep each character recognizable, and extreme angles or heavy style shifts can still change details.
Sheets hold within a project. For characters that return across projects, Soul ID and Elements are the next step.
One identity per Soul ID. Two-person scenes with trained characters depend on Elements.
Genjutsu and Soul ID. Motion transfer and Objects swap do not take trained Soul ID characters directly. Use character sheet images or AI Influencer characters.
Rights to the reference. Use only footage and faces you own or have permission to use.
