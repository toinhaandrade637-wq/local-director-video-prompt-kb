What Does Unlimited Mean in AI Image and Video?

Unlimited generally means that generations on specific named models deduct no credits for a defined period. In the plans we reviewed, it is per-model, runs in a standard queue with a cap on concurrent tasks and a resolution ceiling, and typically applies inside the platform's own website.

The same word can describe very different time structures. One platform may mean a year on a fixed model list, another a few days on a single model, so it helps to check exactly what a plan covers before comparing prices.

Every figure below was checked against official pricing pages and in-product screens on September 11, 2026. Several platforms run rotating promotions and regional pricing, so the checkout price is the most reliable figure to budget from.

Which Unlimited Plan Fits Your Workflow?

Four scenarios cover many common cases:

High-volume image generation. A 365-day set fits this task: Higgsfield's six-model set, Magnific's all-year list, and Hailuo's annual image unlimited name the models upfront, so the marginal image costs nothing for a year.
High-volume video generation. In the plans we reviewed, long-term unlimited video lives in relax modes: Midjourney's SD video on Pro and Mega, Hailuo's prior-generation models on Max. Faster or newer video runs on credits or windows.
Access to new flagship models. In our check, no platform placed frontier video models in long-term unlimited, and only Magnific's top configuration covers a flagship image model for a year, so windows and short passes fit this pattern, and an annual plan matters mostly as the base they attach to.
API, MCP, or automated production. In the plans we reviewed, unlimited terms did not extend to programmatic surfaces, so the decision becomes a price-per-credit calculation.

Across the ten platforms, the pattern we saw holds: annual billing extends or unlocks unlimited on some (Magnific, Hailuo), discounts credits on others (Kling, Luma, Runway, Grok), and on Higgsfield lowers the price of the base that windows and passes attach to.

Why a Year of Unlimited on a Brand-New Model Is Rare

The arithmetic helps explain the pattern. On Luma's published rates, one second of Seedance 2.5 at 1080p with audio costs 319 credits, roughly $0.96 per second at Plus-plan credit pricing, or about $29 for a 30-second clip. A user producing ten such clips a day would consume close to $290 in compute daily, against annual subscriptions priced in the hundreds.

In our calculation based on those public rates, a single heavy user could exceed the price of a yearly plan within its first week. This may be part of why new flagships tend to ship as windows, passes, and credit pricing, and why year-long sets are usually built from models whose compute costs have already fallen.

Checklist Before Buying an Annual Unlimited Plan

Seven questions, answerable from any vendor's pricing page:

Which models are unlimited, by exact name and version?
How long does the term run, and from which date?
Where does unlimited apply: web app only, or also API, integrations, and sub-products?
What are the queue rules and how many concurrent generations run?
What is the resolution ceiling per model, and does it change by tier?
How do new model versions arrive: added to the set, sold as windows, or credits only?
What happens on cancellation or downgrade mid-term?
