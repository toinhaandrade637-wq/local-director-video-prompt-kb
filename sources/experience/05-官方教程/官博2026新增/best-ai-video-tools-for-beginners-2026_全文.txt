7 Best AI Video Tools for Beginners in 2026
Higgsfield
Oct 29, 2026
11 min

Start with the tool that matches your first task: ChatGPT for the idea and script, Higgsfield for generated images and video scenes, ElevenLabs for a voiceover, HeyGen for a presenter video, Canva for a template-based video, and CapCut or Descript to edit footage you already shot. Most beginners need one or two of these, not all seven.

The right first tool depends on what you have when you start. If you only have an idea, you need help with the script and the visuals. If you have a script but don’t want to record yourself, you need a voice or a presenter. If you already shot clips on your phone, you need an editor, not a generator. This guide is about choosing tools. For the steps of your first generation and how to pick a video model, see How to Make AI Videos as a Beginner.

Which AI Video Tool Fits Your First Task?

Each tool below covers one task in making a video. Pick by the material you start with: an idea, a script, product photos or your own footage. Six of the seven tools let you create something on a free plan, but several add a watermark or don’t allow commercial use, and Higgsfield’s free plan doesn’t generate video.

How Did We Choose These Tools?

We picked tools that cover the main tasks in a first video, from the script to export, and judged each one on four things: how fast a beginner gets a first result, whether that result is useful, how easy it is to fix, and how clearly the tool shows what you pay for.

A clear start: you can get a first result without a course or prior editing experience.
A useful result: the output can go into a real post, ad or presentation.
Easy fixes: you can change one part without starting over.
Clear costs: you can see what the free plan includes and what a generation or an export costs before you spend.

We don’t rank the tools against each other. A voiceover tool and a video generator do different jobs, so one quality score for both would mislead.

The examples in each section follow the tool’s main workflow as described in its official tutorials. Plan details come from the official pricing and help pages linked in each section.

ChatGPT: Developing Ideas and Scripts

ChatGPT helps when you have an idea but no script. You describe the video in a sentence or two, and it turns that into a short script and a shot list you can take into any of the other tools on this list.

A bakery owner who wants a 30-second promo can start with one line: “A 30-second vertical video for my bakery’s new pumpkin croissant. Friendly tone. Ends with our opening hours.” Ask for a script with timings, then a shot list with one line per shot, then ask to cut the script to about 75 words so it fits 30 seconds. The result is a voiceover script and five or six shots you can generate in Higgsfield or film yourself.

You work in plain language and fix the result by asking: “make it shorter,” “remove the second shot” or “make the opening line a question” changes only what you point to. The free plan includes unlimited text chats with GPT-6, which is enough for scripts. The Go plan costs $8 per month and adds more messages with tools, more uploads, more image creation and longer memory. To learn the basics, start with OpenAI Academy: Getting started with ChatGPT.

What to keep in mind:

ChatGPT writes the plan, not the video. You still need a tool for visuals and sound.
On the free plan, messages with uploads, image creation and memory are limited, so long sessions with reference files may hit the cap.
Higgsfield: Creating AI Images and Video Scenes

Higgsfield is a generative platform for creative production, with 35+ frontier models for generating images and turning them into video scenes. It suits beginners who have no footage: you describe a shot, generate an image, animate it and then adjust the result without starting from scratch.

A café owner who needs a short vertical clip for Instagram, without a photoshoot, can start with a text prompt: “A small neighborhood coffee shop counter in soft morning light: a ceramic latte cup with latte art on a wooden counter, a glass jar of croissants behind it, warm window light.”

Image: generate a vertical image with FLUX.2 Pro (1 credit).
Video: use the image as the start frame for a 5-second, 720p clip in Seedance 2.5 and describe the motion: steam, a hand sliding the cup toward the camera, a slow push-in (35 credits).
One fix: if the motion isn’t right, adjust the prompt and generate the clip again (another 35 credits).

The result is a 5-second vertical clip for about 71 credits in total (≈ $3.48 at $49 for 1,000 credits). Seedance 2.5 is available on plans from $49 per month (1,000 credits).

You see the cost on the Generate button before you spend credits, so you know what each try will take from your balance. For a guided start, the course AI Creation: First Steps in Higgsfield Academy suggests models, settings and examples; start with the lesson Your First Video. 

What to keep in mind:

The free plan has 0 credits, and its results can’t be used commercially.
Seedance 2.5, used in the example above, isn’t included in the $15 plan. It starts with the $49 plan.
Each new try costs credits. In the example above, one regeneration doubles the cost of the clip.
ElevenLabs: Creating Voiceovers

ElevenLabs turns a written script into a voiceover. You paste the text, choose a voice and generate audio you can drop into any video. It suits beginners who don’t want to record their own voice or who need the same voice across several videos.

A marketer with the 75-word script from ChatGPT pastes it into Text to Speech, picks a voice from the library and generates the track. If a name comes out wrong, they select that sentence and regenerate only that part. The result is an audio file to place under the video in CapCut, Canva or Descript.

In Studio, ElevenLabs lets you regenerate a selected paragraph or section twice for free if you don’t change the text, voice or settings, and you can restore an earlier take from the generation history. For brand names that keep coming out wrong, you can add a pronunciation dictionary. The free plan gives 10,000 credits a month; the cheapest paid plan is $6 per month (30,000 credits) and adds a commercial license. To learn the basics, start with the ElevenLabs quickstart.

What to keep in mind:

The free plan doesn’t include a commercial license. If you publish free-plan audio, ElevenLabs requires attribution in the title.
Free audio exports are 128 kbps MP3, and video exports from Studio on the free plan include a watermark.
HeyGen: Making Avatar Videos

HeyGen creates videos with an AI presenter who reads your script on camera. You pick a ready-made avatar, paste the text and get a talking-head video without filming. It suits explainers, onboarding videos and announcements more than cinematic scenes.

A small SaaS team that needs a one-minute product update, with no one who wants to be on camera, can start with a 60-word script. Choose a ready-made presenter and voice, paste the script, preview it, then fix pauses or wording in the text and regenerate. The result is a presenter reading your script, ready to post or embed.

The format is predictable: you edit text, not timelines, and the presenter looks the same from video to video. The free plan includes 3 watermarked videos a month, each up to 1 minute, with one custom video avatar and 500+ ready-made presenters. The Creator plan costs $29 per month and adds 600 credits, 1080p export and removal of HeyGen branding. To learn the basics, start with HeyGen Academy: HeyGen 101.

What to keep in mind:

A presenter talking to the camera works for explainers but not for product scenes or b-roll.
Free videos include the HeyGen watermark, and you can’t remove it on the free plan.
An avatar of yourself requires a consent video read by the same person who appears in the footage.
Canva: Designing Simple Videos

Canva builds short videos from templates. You drop in photos, clips and text, and the template handles layout, fonts and transitions. It suits beginners who already use Canva for social graphics and want a branded video without learning a timeline editor.

A boutique owner can turn six product photos and two lines of text into a 20-second Instagram Reel: pick a Reel template, replace the images and text, and export the video. Magic Video can also build a video of up to 60 seconds from 1 to 10 clips or photos.

Templates solve design decisions for you, and fixes are visual: drag a photo, change a font, swap a template. The free plan includes 1.6M+ templates, 5GB of cloud storage and up to 20 Standard or Premium AI uses. Canva Pro costs $18 per month for one person and adds premium templates and content, AI tools such as background removal, resize and translate, 10x more AI usage and 100GB of storage. To learn the basics, start with the Canva Design School video editing lesson.

What to keep in mind:

Free users see a watermark on Pro content. To remove it, remove the element or buy a license for it.
AI uses on the free plan are capped at 20, so test the AI features before you plan a whole video around them.
Templates work for slideshow-style videos. For precise cuts and long footage, an editor like CapCut is easier.
CapCut: Editing Short Social Videos

CapCut is a video editor for clips you already have. You put them on a timeline, trim them to length and export for TikTok, Reels or Shorts. Its AI features sit inside the editing workflow, so it’s often the last step after any of the other tools.

A social media manager with four phone clips and a voiceover from ElevenLabs adds them to the timeline, trims the video to 15 seconds, places the voiceover under the clips, sets the 9:16 format and exports a vertical video ready to upload.

You see every clip on a timeline and can fix a single cut without redoing the rest. In most cases, exports are watermark-free. The free plan covers basic editing tools and free templates. Pro costs $19.99 per month and adds 1,200 monthly AI credits, access to AI features, all premium templates and 1 TB of storage.

What to keep in mind:

If a project uses even one Pro element, export is locked until you remove it or upgrade. Pro items are marked, but beginners often add one by accident.
The free plan lists basic editing tools; access to AI features starts with Pro.
Descript: Editing Videos Through Text

Descript lets you edit a video by editing its transcript. You upload a recording, Descript transcribes it, and deleting a sentence in the text cuts it from the video. It suits talking videos: interviews, tutorials, podcasts and webinars.

A consultant turning a 20-minute Zoom recording into a 3-minute LinkedIn clip uploads the file, reads the transcript, deletes off-topic sentences, removes filler words and applies Studio Sound to clean up the audio. The result is a shorter, cleaner video with the edits matching the text.

If you can edit a document, you can edit the video: “Delete words or sentences from the transcript, and the media edits automatically”. The free plan gives 60 media minutes a month and 100 AI credits once. The Hobbyist plan costs $24 per month and adds 10 media hours and 400 AI credits a month, 1080p watermark-free export, and AI tools including Studio Sound and Remove Filler Words.

What to keep in mind:

Free exports are limited to 720p and include a Descript watermark.
Filler word removal and Studio Sound are limited on the free plan, and filler word removal works on English transcripts only.
For music videos or product scenes with little speech, a timeline editor fits better.
Which Tool Should You Start With?

Start with one tool for your main task and add a second only when a new task appears. You don’t need seven subscriptions to make a first video.
