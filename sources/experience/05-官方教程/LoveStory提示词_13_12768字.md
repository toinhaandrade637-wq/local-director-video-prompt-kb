Style: 8K IMAX. Photorealistic — no 3D render, no game engine. 35mm anamorphic film: horizontal squeeze, oval bokeh with stretched highlights on lantern strings and torch flames, subtle barrel distortion and edge chromatic aberration. NO lens flares, NO streaks, NO floating bokeh circles; nothing parked out-of-focus before the lens except the wipes written into the shots.

Lighting: NIGHT, a colossal street carnival in old Italy at full blaze. Strings of multicolored paper lanterns criss-cross high over the piazza; the palazzo windows glow; torches and wall-lanterns burn along the arcades; golden spark fountains hiss in the crowd; fireworks bloom over the rooflines and campanile. Warm festival light everywhere — every face readable, no crushed shadows. A soft breeze moves lanterns, banners and flames.

Color: 60:30:10 — warm festival gold / black night sky / multicolor lanterns and jewel-tone costumes. Red-burgundy gowns belong EXCLUSIVELY to the GIRL — no other woman in red anywhere.

Camera: 15-SECOND GENERATION, FOUR SHOTS — every cut lands on action, no dead frames. 180° shutter, 24fps, stable moves, no jitter. FRONTAL GEOMETRY: the scene is a CROSSROAD — the leads face each other across the parade's road, the parade crosses PERPENDICULAR to their sightline; every camera is straight and frontal, squared to this cross; the parade always crosses the frame horizontally, left to right; no oblique angles, no diagonals, no dutch.

Skin: pore-level realism, natural texture, capillary flush, pore-shadow matching practical light.

Acting: governed by the ACTING TASK blocks — nobody plays an emotion, everyone plays their task. Nobody on the piazza ever stands frozen.

Physics: real weight and inertia — gowns and coat tails with momentum, carriages and chariots roll heavy, the carriage horses keep correct gait, stilt-walkers balance with real weight shifts, giant puppets sway on their poles.

RULES (Continuity, in force in every shot):

1. SEPARATION — HIGHEST PRIORITY: the parade's road is EIGHT METERS WIDE; each lead stands about ONE METER back from its edge, pressed into the front row of their bank — the HERO and the GIRL stand TEN METERS APART, directly opposite each other, the procession always between them. They are never next to each other, never on the same bank; crossing the road is visibly impossible.

2. The PARADE is already marching at frame one and NEVER stops, pauses, thins or ends — a continuous river at a steady pace through every shot including the last frame; eight meters filled shoulder to shoulder, rank behind rank, no usable gaps; endlessly long, stretching beyond the frame both ways. The same procession in every shot: same drummers, stilt-walkers, chariots, carriages, giant puppets, banners, same left-to-right direction.

3. NOBODY ever walks or passes behind the leads' backs — the parade exists only in the road between the banks. Behind each lead: only standing spectators.

4. BACKGROUND DEPTH: behind each lead the square CONTINUES — rows of standing spectators recede in soft layers, and beyond them the piazza breathes in the blur: lantern light, distant arcades, more crowd. Never a wall, facade or blank surface behind either lead; the crowd is backed against nothing.

5. CROWD ATTENTION: the parade is the main show — every spectator watches THE PARADE, absorbed; nobody ever looks at the HERO or the GIRL.

6. BANKS: both leads stand FLUSH in their front rows, spectators pressed at their left, right and back; neither ever stands ahead of the row or on the road; the two banks mirror each other in density and depth.

7. The HERO does not appear in SHOT 4 — after SHOT 3 he is absent from the film.

8. Every crowd member unique — no duplicated faces, masks, costumes or body types. Characters, props and environment identical across cuts.

9. NO MUSIC anywhere on the timeline.

Audio: no score, no melody, no humming — only the carnival: a sea of voices and cheers, the parade's drums in a steady marching rhythm (percussion only, not music), wheels and hooves on stone, stomping, applause, spark fountains hissing, fireworks booming, silk and capes rustling, breath. No subtitles.

Characters (both mid-20s):

HERO — short neat dark hair, dark brown eyes, clean-shaven. Deep-burgundy baroque frock coat with black satin peak lapels and long damask scalloped tails, burgundy damask waistcoat, striped shirt, burgundy cravat with silver brooch, black leather gloves, black pinstriped trousers. His black-and-gold venetian eye mask is on his eyes AT ALL TIMES — never removed, never pushed up, his eyes fully readable through it. He NEVER RUNS — he squeezes, weaves, edges through crowds, never collides, never aggressive.

GIRL — dark hair in a soft romantic updo with loose strands and small gold pins, deep brown eyes. Opulent red-burgundy ball gown: gold-beaded off-the-shoulder bodice, red-and-gold choker, sheer champagne bell sleeves, cream embroidered skirt panel, velvet train, long gloves. A GOLD FILIGREE venetian eye mask with small red gems, tied with a gold ribbon, her eyes fully visible. The only woman in red at the whole carnival.

PARADE — the carnival's main spectacle: drummers with big carnival drums; torch-bearers; banner-carriers with tall poles and streaming pennants; costumed marchers; CIRCUS STILT-WALKERS towering over the flow; ornate CHARIOTS — festival floats on great wooden wheels drawn by costumed teams, carrying performers and lanterns; tall ORNATE CARRIAGES drawn by pairs of adorned horses, their bodies rising above the marchers; GIANT gold-and-ochre PUPPETS swaying on poles. Warm and jewel tones, no red or burgundy garments.

CROWD — a concert-dense mass packing both banks IN DEPTH in the open square: pressed body to body like the front of a stage, rows behind rows dissolving into the lantern glow, the whole city out for the main night. Moving through them costs a squeeze — people shift without taking their eyes off the procession. Old-Italian carnival attire, warm and jewel tones — no red or burgundy gowns on any woman, no frock coats like the HERO's. Nobody resembles the leads.

ACTING TASKS

SCENE DIRECTION (shared, unspoken): a crossroad — he on one side, she directly opposite, ten meters and an endless procession between them; the whole square watches the road, only these two watch each other; the vanishing is his move in her game.

HERO — MOTIVE: she is right there, straight across the road, and their eyes have finally met. GOAL: get across to her. OBSTACLE: eight meters of marching wall with no gaps; hope keeps offering seams, the river keeps closing them. TACTIC: through the crush in a straight line — sideways between shoulders, a hand raised in apology; wedge into the front row, find her through the flashing slivers, hold the eye contact; try the wall — a half-step at a seam that closes, a lean at the next gap a rank of drummers erases, rising on his toes to keep her in sight, try after try; and when the carriage crosses his sightline — simply GONE: not fleeing, not stepping back — vanished, the carnival's own trick, played by him now. (Safety: gaze warm and hopeful through the mask, never a frozen glassy stare; every move polite and nimble, never a shove.)

GIRL — MOTIVE: the game was hers all evening — and there he is, found. GOAL: let him know she is GLAD; then, when he vanishes, find him again. OBSTACLE: the never-ending procession; then his absence where he just stood. TACTIC: hold his eyes across the flow between the passing bodies, calm — and allow a sweet smile at the CORNER of her lips, small, warm, unmistakable; when the slivers show only strangers — search: left, right, rising on her toes, quick small head turns, light confusion first; the search comes back empty — the confusion settles into faint sadness, her head drops slightly, one quiet beat; then TURN and slip INTO the crowd behind her — the crowd does NOT part, no corridor opens: she squeezes between the packed bodies herself until the throng closes around the red gown. (Safety: eyes alive, natural blinks; the smile small and true; the sadness a settling, never a performance to camera.)

PARADE — the carnival itself: drummers locked in the beat, stilt-walkers striding, chariot teams hauling, carriage horses stepping steady, puppets swaying in rhythm; they notice no one, stop for no one, never run out. (Safety: constant marching pace, real weight, continuous flow in every frame.)

CROWD — every face on the procession, cheering, gasping at the stilt-walkers and carriages; nobody looks at the leads, nobody opens a path. (Safety: constant small motion, alive group behavior.)

SHOT 1 — AXIS: camera behind the HERO, straight into the crowd to the front row, 63° FOV (0–3s):

The camera follows BEHIND the HERO (match @Nate_carnival) on the hero-girl axis as he moves in a PERFECTLY STRAIGHT LINE away from the lens INTO the concert-dense crush — sideways, edging between shoulders, people shifting just enough, the camera pressing after him through the same gap — no arc, no detour. He WEDGES into the front row and STOPS a meter from the flow — spectators at his left, right and back — and before him the PARADE crosses the frame horizontally, left to right: drummers, a stilt-walker striding high, a glowing chariot, a giant puppet swaying — and through the flashing slivers, DIRECTLY OPPOSITE, ten meters away: the GIRL (match @Aila_carnival), pressed into her own front row, the red gown, the square's glow deep behind her — and their eyes find each other across the road. Roar of the crush, steady drums, wheels on stone, his caught breath — no music.

SHOT 2 — FRONTAL across the road: the GIRL, eyes meet, a sweet smile, 47° FOV (3–5s):

Head-on from his bank: the GIRL (match @Aila_carnival) at ten meters, seen THROUGH the passing marchers sweeping the near foreground as dark wipes, thin flashing slivers between them — pressed into her front row, spectators at her shoulders, the crowd receding in soft layers into the piazza's blurred glow behind her. In the slivers her eyes MEET his: she holds the look, calm and steady — and a sweet smile arrives at the CORNER of her lips, small and warm — she is GLAD to see him. The gold filigree mask glints with each reveal. Drums, boots on stone, cheers — no music.

SHOT 3 — FRONTAL across the road: the HERO's attempts, the CARRIAGE PASS — GONE, long hold on the empty place, 47° FOV (5–11.5s):

The reverse, head-on from her bank: the HERO (match @Nate_carnival) at ten meters beyond the flow, wedged frontally in his front row, the crowd and the piazza's blurred depth behind him — never a wall. FULL OF HOPE he works the wall: a half-step at a seam that closes instantly, a lean at the next gap a rank of drummers erases, rising on his toes to hold her in sight — try after try, unhurried beats, the river giving nothing. Then an ORNATE CARRIAGE rolling with the procession ENTERS FRAME LEFT — tall, drawn by a pair of adorned horses, its high body rising above the marchers — it crosses and COMPLETELY COVERS the HERO for a long beat... and when its rear CLEARS FRAME RIGHT: the HERO IS NO LONGER THERE. The camera HOLDS on the empty place — beat after beat: strangers' masked faces watching the parade, the packed row closed where he stood, the square still breathing behind — he did not run, he did not step away: he simply VANISHED. The parade keeps flowing. Drums, hooves and wheels rolling away, the roar — no music.

SHOT 4 — FRONTAL on the GIRL: search, confusion, sadness, leaving — no corridor, 47° FOV (11.5–15s):

Head-on medium on the GIRL (match @Aila_carnival) in her front row, the parade still crossing before her: she SEARCHES with her eyes — left, right, along the opposite bank through the slivers, rising on her toes, quick small head turns — light confusion, hope still working; the search comes back empty — faint SADNESS settles, her head drops slightly, eyes lowering, one quiet beat. She TURNS and slips INTO the dense crowd behind her — no corridor, nobody steps aside: she squeezes between the packed bodies, shoulders turning, and the throng CLOSES around the red gown until she is GONE from frame. The drums still crossing behind, the roar swallowing her steps — no music.
