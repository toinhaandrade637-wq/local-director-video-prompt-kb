Don't pick the businesses everyone else goes for - clothing brands, coffee shops. The money is in the plain industries nobody talks about but everyone needs, because they have real ad budgets and the worst digital ads. Let Claude find them:

Financial advisors, home services - and roofers, growing at 6% year-over-year, way faster than most traditional businesses. That's the opportunity.

Next, the offer. A good offer has to be so strong that when someone hears it, they feel like an idiot for saying no. We took the formula from Liam Ottley - the man who invented the AI Automation Agency model and built multiple multi-million dollar AI businesses on it. His five rules: 
1. sell the end result they actually want (a roofer doesn't want videos, he wants income)
2. hit their core problem
3. make them risk nothing, sharpen it with speed, an easy entry price and a gift on top, and 
4. end with a call to action - if you don't tell them what to do next, they won't do it.

Feed the formula straight into Claude:

research what AI ad agencies typically charge local businesses for video ads. Then, using this formula (sell the end result, hit their biggest problem, remove all risk for the client, add speed, an easy entry price, and a bonus, also Add a strong call to action at the end) write a short offer for roofing contractors. Keep it short.

Step 4 - The Website (with Samin Yasar)

Samin Yasar - the ex-Amazon engineer who's helped 600+ businesses implement AI - gave us his rules for a website that sells: it's not an art project, it's a conversation that answers the client's questions in five blocks. The hero answers what you do, who it's for, what I get in five seconds - and if the price looks too cheap, explain why ("launch pricing for our first 50 clients") or the discount scares people away. Then proof (videos right on the page), "How does the process work?", benefits, FAQ - and after every single block, one button with your strongest CTA: get a free banner ad.

Forms, a backend, an admin panel - that's normally a different league. With the Higgsfield MCP it's one prompt, because it launches a live website with a real link: the portfolio videos on the page actually play, the buttons work, the contact form lands in an admin panel, and it even found an available domain on its own.

Here's advice from a top expert on how a selling website should be structured. Build and launch a website for my agency using exactly this structure, with our real offer and our portfolio videos:

Hero: answers in 5 seconds — what we do, who it's for, what they get. Headline: "Professional video ads that bring roofing companies paying customers." Below it — our offer: custom ads in 24 hours, 5 videos for $500 instead of $5,000. Explain why it's cheap ("Launch pricing for our first 50 clients") and remind they risk nothing — they pay only when it's done.

Proof: our portfolio videos right on the page + two lines about us: "We're a full-automation ad agency for roofing businesses — high-quality videos that bring in new clients."

"How does the process work?": You answer 5 quick questions — everything else we dig up ourselves. We analyze your audience, find what converts in your niche, write the selling script, and make ads that sell.

Benefits: No filming days. No extra costs. A full-cycle agency takes care of everything.

FAQ — the questions a roofer will actually ask: What kind of ads will I get? Do I need to film anything? How long will it take? How do I get in touch?
