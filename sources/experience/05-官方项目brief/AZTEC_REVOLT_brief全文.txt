We’re using AI to retell one of the most transformative events in history and show why historical epics may be among AI’s best use cases.

A retelling inspired by history

Aztec Revolt | Escaping Tenochtitlan is a retelling inspired by real events that took place more than 500 years ago.

To abide by the Higgsfield Film Festival guidelines, all character names and likenesses in the film have been fictionalized. The story draws from the historical setting, conflict and events surrounding the conquest of Mexico, while the characters as depicted on screen are original creations made specifically for this film.

Why we are making this with AI

We believe historical epics are one of the most exciting use cases for AI filmmaking.

Stories like the conquest of Mexico are enormous in scale. They require lost cities, armies, ancient cultures, languages, costumes and environments that would normally demand the resources of a major studio production.

AI gives small filmmaking teams the ability to attempt stories of this scale in a way that simply was not possible before.

For us, that is what makes this technology so exciting. It gives filmmakers the opportunity to revisit extraordinary moments in history and bring worlds that disappeared centuries ago back to the screen.

Sangre de Oro is our attempt to show what that can look like.

One film, two versions, neither one dubbed

Language is a major part of that vision.

The version of Aztec Revolt submitted here is performed in Spanish and Nahuatl.

We are also creating a second version in English and Nahuatl.

But the English version will not simply be dubbed over the Spanish performances. AI allows us to create new performances specifically for each language, so the dialogue, voices and lip movements actually belong to that version of the film.

Spanish-speaking audiences can experience the story in Spanish and Nahuatl, while English-speaking audiences can experience it in English and Nahuatl.

Neither version has to feel like the secondary version.

For a story about two very different worlds colliding, we think that matters.

Where this goes

Aztec Revolt is being built as the opening chapter of a much larger project, whether that ultimately becomes a series or a feature-length film.

Whatever form it takes, our goal is to continue telling this story from the beginning in both Spanish and English, while preserving Nahuatl where appropriate.

The conquest of the Aztec is one of the most extraordinary and transformative events in history, yet much of this story has rarely been given a serious cinematic treatment.

AI may finally give independent filmmakers the ability to tackle historical epics like this at a scale that was previously out of reach.

We want to see how far we can take it.
