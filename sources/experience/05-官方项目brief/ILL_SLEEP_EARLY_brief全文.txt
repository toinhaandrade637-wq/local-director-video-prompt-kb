A surreal battle between exhaustion and the irresistible pull of the screen, reflecting the addiction we all struggle to escape.

✨ Context

Why this project exists.

“I’ll Sleep Early. Promise.” explores a habit that has become almost universal: knowing we should put our phones down, feeling completely exhausted, and still reaching for the screen.

The project comes from the contradiction between our physical need to rest and the endless digital stimulation that keeps us awake. Instead of treating phone addiction as an abstract idea, the film turns it into something physical, absurd, and increasingly impossible to escape.

What begins as an ordinary night gradually transforms into a surreal battle between a tired person and the device he cannot stop returning to.

🎭 Narrative

The story follows a tired young person who goes to bed intending to wake up at 5:00 A.M. But a single notification pulls him back to his phone.

As the night progresses, the phone becomes increasingly aggressive and surreal: emojis become physical objects, notifications attack him, the device grows arms, sticks to his face, floods the room with digital garbage, and eventually becomes a gigantic force that absorbs him.

Even after everything the phone puts him through, he keeps coming back to it.

The message is simple: sometimes the thing we know is hurting us is also the thing we keep choosing.

The film is aimed at anyone who has ever said, “I’ll sleep early tonight,” only to find themselves still scrolling hours later.

🎨 Visuals

The film uses a 2D illustrated and textured animation style. The visual language begins grounded and familiar, then progressively becomes more physical, surreal, chaotic, and absurd as the protagonist loses control.

The bedroom begins as a quiet nighttime environment dominated by deep blues, with a small warm light coming from the bedside lamp. This creates a calm, intimate atmosphere that contrasts with the increasingly invasive light of the phone.

As the addiction escalates, digital elements leave the screen and enter the physical world: emojis, notifications, reactions, messages, objects, water and digital waste become tangible elements of the environment.

The visual escalation mirrors the protagonist's psychological and physical loss of control.

The goal is to make something completely digital feel physically overwhelming.

📝 Notes

Core creative principle: The protagonist never behaves as if he knows he is inside a comedy. His reactions remain tired, believable and grounded while the world around him becomes increasingly absurd.




Visual principle: The film begins realistically and progressively breaks the physical rules of the bedroom.




Narrative principle: Every escalation represents another step in the cycle of phone addiction: notification → curiosity → interaction → dependence → loss of control.




Final idea: The protagonist doesn't truly defeat the phone. At 5:00 A.M., after everything that happened, he is still scrolling. The night has passed, but the addiction remains.
