90s multi-camera sitcom scene, continuous, 9:16 vertical.

Shot on 35mm film, bright evenly lit cosy apartment living room, high-key warm

tungsten lighting, soft fill from every side, no harsh shadows, flat theatrical

staging, characters face camera, subtle film grain, sitcom set brightness.

EVENING: deep blue dusk outside the windows, warm lamps lit inside.

All dialogue in English.




CHARACTERS —

[tag your character sheet here]: woman in her early 20s, long straight dark-brown hair with centre parting,

thick natural eyebrows, 90s brown lipstick. Oversized chunky ribbed knit sweater

with horizontal stripes in purple, mustard, orange, dark green and burgundy,

dropped shoulders, hem below the hips, midriff fully covered, sleeves pulled over

her hands. High-waisted baggy light-wash mom jeans, black high-top canvas sneakers,

beaded bracelets on her left wrist, thin gold choker. Speaks American English.




BOB: @Image 2   photorealistic real chubby grey-blue Scottish Fold cat dressed as a painter.

Small folded ears pressed flat, large round copper-orange eyes, round grumpy face,

plush dense grey-blue fur. A black artist's beret tilted at an angle on his head

with a small stalk on top, the fabric slouching to one side. Rectangular thin-framed

blue-light computer glasses slipping down his muzzle. A warm off-white canvas

artist's apron tied over his body, smeared and spattered with colourful oil paint —

no shirt, no cap. A long thin waxed handlebar moustache with dramatically upturned

curled ends sits above his mouth, over his own natural white cat whiskers.

Real cat anatomy and proportions, four legs, sits and moves on all fours, NOT

anthropomorphic, no human arms, no human hands, no fingers.

BOB'S VOICE: @audio an elegant, softly-spoken older English gentleman — a wry Oxford don

who considers himself above his employer. Warm slightly husky baritone, Received

Pronunciation, crisp consonants, long open vowels. Highly expressive and musical

delivery: pitch rises and falls, lands hard on one stressed word, then trails off.

Small pauses mid-sentence, sighs before speaking when irritated, draws out vowels

for effect. Amusement lives under everything, even when annoyed. Sounds like a real

actor performing to a studio audience with theatrical comic timing. NOT a narrator,

announcer, assistant or synthesised voice. No even pacing, no flat pitch.




BIANCA: @Image 3 photorealistic real long-haired pure white cat, big expressive blue eyes,

small pink nose, long silky fur, wearing a delicate single-strand string of small

white pearls. No clothing, no hat, no glasses. Real cat anatomy and proportions,

four legs, not anthropomorphic. She does not speak — she only hisses.




PROPS —

THE EASEL: @Image 5 a light-toned natural wood tripod artist's easel with visible grain,

three splayed legs and an adjustable canvas ledge, about chest height on Kaye. It

holds a blank white stretched canvas. The same easel appears in every shot,

identical throughout — upright at the start, toppled on its side with the canvas

half slid off after Bianca's escape, lifted upright again at the end.

THE CANVAS: @Image 5 taped to it is a glossy printed colour photograph of a white cat with

pearls, held by a small piece of clear tape at each of the four corners, one corner

curling up where the tape is lifting. The photo is noticeably smaller than the

canvas, leaving bare white canvas visible around it — obviously an ordinary

printout, not a painting. The canvas faces AWAY from camera in every shot until the

final reveal.

THE PALETTE: a small wooden oval artist's palette smeared with thick fresh dabs of

white, ochre, crimson, ultramarine and olive oil paint, resting against Bob's front

paw. Paint-covered brushes with chipped wooden handles.

SET — @Image 4 cosy lived-in apartment living room. Deep red velvet sofa facing camera,

mustard and cream pillows, a knitted blanket on the sofa, low mid-century coffee

table, geometric striped rug, cream walls, white painted brick, pale honey wood

trim, houseplants in woven baskets, warm fairy lights, cluttered open shelves.

Heavy wooden front door on the RIGHT. The easel stands centre-left. A stool beside

it with brushes and a turpentine jar. A lit candle on the side table.




0:00–0:07 — WIDE SHOT, static, the whole living room in frame. The front door on

the RIGHT opens and Kaye trudges in, exhausted — bag sliding off her shoulder, hair

messy, eyes half closed, shoulders down. She pushes the door closed behind her,

drops her keys into a bowl and lets out a long tired sigh. She lifts her head to

look into the room — and freezes mid-step, completely still.




0:07–0:17 — MEDIUM SHOT that SLOWLY ZOOMS OUT into a wider frame. Bianca lies

sprawled languidly across the red sofa in a theatrical reclining pose, front legs

extended, chin lifted, holding the pose, purring loudly, pearls catching the light.

In front of her stands Bob at the easel, back three-quarters to camera, the wooden

palette resting against one front paw, a brush clamped between his two front paws,

dabbing at the canvas and humming a contented little tune to himself.

As the camera zooms out, BOTH CATS TURN THEIR HEADS TOWARD THE DOORWAY AT THE SAME

MOMENT, in perfect unison, and hold still.

[LAUGH TRACK: big laugh + applause]




0:17–0:25 — Bianca's blue eyes go wide. She HISSES sharply, ears flat, springs off

the sofa and bolts, knocking the easel over — it topples sideways, the canvas

sliding half off it.

WIDE SHOT, static, the entire apartment visible. Kaye stands frozen in the middle of

the room, still holding her bag, watching in silent confusion and shock, saying

nothing, not moving. Bianca streaks past her legs on all fours and out through the

front door on the RIGHT, SLAMMING it shut behind her with a bang. Kaye flinches at

the sound.

[LAUGH TRACK: big]




0:25–0:33 — Bob scrambles after her on all fours, palette and brush clattering to

the floor behind him. He reaches the closed door and stops dead beside Kaye's feet,

standing with his back to camera, head lowered, beret sliding sideways, utterly

defeated. The toppled easel lies on the rug behind them.

BOB (calling out, urgent, voice cracking): "Hey— hey, WAIT!"

Long pause, 1.5 seconds of silence.




0:33–0:41 — CLOSE-UP on Bob. He slowly turns from the door to face Kaye, looks up

at her, straightens up and recovers his dignity, moustache twitching.

BOB (long weary sigh, then cold and wounded, pausing before the last word, stress

on WHY): "And... why exactly did you come in — without KNOCKING?"

[LAUGH TRACK: big laugh + applause]




0:41–0:49 — CLOSE-UP on Kaye. She stares down at him. Opens her mouth. Closes it

again. Completely speechless. Then a slow smirk spreads across her face.

KAYE (smirking): "I knew you were a little strange. But not THIS strange."

[LAUGH TRACK: medium]




0:49–0:58 — CLOSE-UP on Bob. He nudges his beret straight with one paw, looks away,

defensive.

BOB (grumbling, voice rising with indignation, stressing WHOLE): "She was under the

impression this was MY apartment. Do you have ANY idea what that cost me? A WHOLE

bag of kibble!"

[LAUGH TRACK: big]




0:58–1:09 — TWO-SHOT. Kaye walks to the toppled easel, sets it back upright on its

three legs, slides the canvas straight and looks at it for the first time. Her

eyebrows shoot up.

KAYE (genuinely impressed): "I didn't know you could draw like that."

Bob cracks and bursts out laughing, head thrown back, moustache quivering.

BOB (helpless with laughter, breathless between words, delighted with himself):

"I generated it — and printed it! Had to make a good impression."

The camera pushes in past her onto the canvas: the glossy printed photograph taped

down at all four corners, one corner curling up, bare white canvas around it.

Obviously not a painting.

[LAUGH TRACK: big laugh + applause]

1:09–1:18 — FINAL WIDE SHOT of the whole living room, slow pull back. Kaye looks at

Bob for a beat, then gives in and starts laughing along with him. The two of them

stand beside the righted easel in the wreckage of the "studio": scattered brushes,

the palette face-down on the rug, the candle guttering, the string of pearls

abandoned on the sofa where Bianca had been lying. Hold the shot, nobody speaks.

FREEZE FRAME ending.

[LAUGH TRACK: LOUD explosive audience laughter and long sustained applause, carrying

over the freeze frame into a final upbeat 90s sitcom sting]
