Step 1. How to use Genjutsu

Higgsfield Genjutsu lets you create a new video by preserving the motion, camera work, and timing of a reference video while replacing its characters, locations, clothing, and props with your own visual references.




This guide explains the Motion transfer workflow shown in the accompanying screen recordings.

About the interface 


These screenshots were captured on September 25, 2026. Button names, placement, and credit costs may change as the service is updated.


What You Need 
A reference video whose motion you want to copy (the interface specifies 4–30 seconds)
Reference images for the characters
Reference images for the background or location
Reference images for clothing and props, if needed
A prompt describing the intended result




You can add up to 30 reference images. However, too many images can make their relationships difficult to manage. For your first attempt, focus on the main characters, locations, and essential props.

Workflow 
Step 1. Open Genjutsu 

Open Genjutsu from the top menu, select Create Video in the upper-left corner, and then choose Motion transfer.







Use Motion transfer when you want to rebuild the performance or movement of a reference video with different characters or a different world. If you only need to replace a specific object, Objects swap may be the better option.

Step 2. Add the Reference Video

Click Add a reference video to extract motion and select the source video whose movement you want to reproduce.







A clear reference works best: keep the main subject inside the frame, use readable motion, avoid long periods where the face or hands are hidden, and limit unnecessary cuts.

Step 3. Add Character, Location, and Prop Images 

Click the Add your characters, products, or clothes area and select the images you want to use. Previously uploaded assets can be selected together. Make sure every required image has a green check mark.










The assets will later appear as Image 1, Image 2, and so on. Check their order after adding them.

Step 4. Enable Prompt and Enter Your Instructions 

Turn on the Prompt switch, then type or paste your instructions.










For a clearer prompt, separate what should be preserved from the reference video, what should be replaced, the role of each reference image, what must not change, and the required frame-to-frame consistency.

Step 5. Assign Each @ Mention to a Reference 

Click an @ mention in the prompt and assign it to the appropriate Video 1, Image 1, Image 2, or another item from the menu.










For example, you might use Video 1 for motion, camera work, timing, and cuts; Image 1 and Image 2 for locations; Image 3 for a prop; and Image 4 and Image 5 for characters.

The numbering depends on the order in which you added the assets. Do not rely on the number alone—check each thumbnail before assigning it.

Step 6. Select Higgsfield Genjutsu as the Model 

Open Model and select Higgsfield Genjutsu.







If it is already selected, you can leave the setting unchanged.

すでに選択済みの場合は、そのままで構いません。

Step 7. Choose the Output Quality 

Open Quality and choose 480p, 720p, or 1080p based on your intended use.







Use 480p for test generations, 720p for social-media previews, and 1080p for final output. Higher quality may require more credits, so it is usually best to validate the composition and replacements at a lower resolution first.

Step 8. Start the Generation

After checking the reference video, images, prompt, model, and quality, click Generate.







The button displays the number of credits required for the current settings. Always check the amount before starting the generation.

Step 9. Review the Result in History

When the generation is complete, open History and play the finished video. Use the icons beside each result to download it or access additional actions.

Step 3. Character sheets
Point

Each person sheet has four panels: front, side, back and a close-up of the face. Add more panels and the face starts to drift.

Show one person per sheet, standing only. If you add a panel of clothes laid out on the floor, or a flat-lay photo, Genjutsu reads it as "clothing to put on". Then the video just dresses the original person in new clothes.

For Bach, I used Haussmann's 1748 portrait as the base. The right person in the source had a long white beard and a robe, so the sheet clearly shows "no beard, no cloak".

I left the sunglasses out of the sheet and wrote "he's wearing them from 21.05 s" in the video prompt. One sheet keeps the face steadier than giving it two sheets of the same person (with and without sunglasses).

For the Girl with a Pearl Earring, I reused a sheet from an earlier video. I made it twice and went with the second one (v2).

Step 4. Location
Point

The background is Vermeer's studio. I made it a long room with light coming in from the windows on the left. In the last shot the two walk across the floor, so the floor needs to be clear in a straight line from the back of the room to the front.

Keep people out of the background image. If there's a person in it, the video gets pulled toward that person's pose.

I also made a second, blurred version of the background. In shot C, his face comes right up to the lens, so the background should be heavily out of focus. But even if the prompt says "blur the background", a sharp background image comes out sharp. Blur the background image itself before you upload it, and you get the blur. Step 5. Props
Point

The source has three props: a white table, a small keyboard and a laptop. I replaced them with a keyboard instrument from Vermeer's time, a virginal. It's based on the instrument in A Young Woman Standing at a Virginal. The landscape painted inside the lid is what makes it recognisable.

I replaced the laptop with a handwritten music book standing on the music desk. Where Bach types on the laptop, he now runs his fingers along the music.

Replace the table, keyboard and laptop together, as one object, table and all. If you replace the three separately, the white table stays, or the music book stands where the laptop screen was. When I wrote that the whole table becomes one instrument, neither the white table nor the laptop was left.

Don't write "the same size as the original table" for the instrument. If you do, you get a mix of the old object and the new one. Write the instrument's own measurements (150 cm wide, keys 88 cm off the floor). All it takes from the original is the position and the direction.

I put the music book in the same sheet as the instrument. The more prop images you add, the more things fall apart.

Step 6. The video prompt
Point

You upload one source video and five images.

Five things in the prompt's structure made the difference.

At the top, state that "only who, what and where change"
Name the two original people by their cardigan colour once only. After that, call them "the person on the screen-left" and "the person on the screen-right"
Map each original person's movements one-to-one onto the new person
Put all the "don'ts" together once, at the end of the prompt
Write the physics: feet stay on the floor, nobody walks through the instrument

Avoid describing how the original people look (beard, long hair, clothing names) as much as you can. Writing "no beard" over and over actually makes a beard more likely. Instead, describe something that a beard would hide as "visible". Here I wrote "the white cravat and the buttons on his chest are visible in every shot".

The exception is the Pearl girl's turban. To keep hair from showing under it, this is the one part I wrote in detail, in its own block near the top of the prompt.

The shape of her head is a dome of cloth. The highest point and the widest point of her head are both cloth
List every place hair must not show, one by one: forehead, temples, in front of and behind the ears, the nape…
Name the shapes it turns into when it goes wrong: "not a headband", "not a bandana on top of hair"

Write every cut time you measured in Step 2. For the white flashes, give a start and an end, like "20.90–21.05 s". Every transition came out within 0.11 seconds of the target.

What didn't work was height. In the source, the person on the left is taller. I wrote "Bach 178 cm, Pearl girl 160 cm" in the prompt, but she came out at the original left person's height. Height follows the people in the source video. If you want it the other way round, the safest way is to decide it when you choose the source video.
