How I got here

I'm a storyteller first. In October 2024 I started Fantasy Future, a YouTube channel of long-form lore documentaries and audio stories set in worlds like Warhammer 40,000 and Trench Crusade. Most episodes run one to two and a half hours. I do every part myself: the research, the script, the narration direction, the visuals, the sound and the final edit in DaVinci Resolve. In under two years the channel reached more than 31,000 subscribers and 4.7 million views across 145 videos. I also directed Sniper Priest, a Trench Crusade animated short.

Running a channel alone teaches you two things fast: how to ship on a schedule, and what people actually watch to the end.

Before that I spent four years running online communities: moderation, partnerships and the day-to-day work of keeping a community healthy. That turned out to matter.

In July 2026 I joined Higgsfield's Creator Partnership Program, and today I also moderate the Higgsfield Discord. I see every new model the week it ships, I read what breaks for thousands of people, and I test the fixes myself before I tell anyone to use them.

I'm also finishing my Associate in Arts in Computer Arts Animation at Miami Dade College, graduating in December 2026. That is where I study what sits under all of this: animation principles, 3D, design and visual storytelling.

How I work

Three rules run through everything I make.

Draft cheap, finish once. I test the idea at low resolution first, where a take costs a few credits. You give notes on drafts, not on expensive finals. Only the approved take gets finished in 1080p or 4K.

Lock the look before the shots. Characters get model sheets. Locations get plates. Products get clean references. Every shot is built from those, so the tenth shot matches the first.

Test before I claim. When I say a method works, there are logged runs behind it. My Draft Mode Workflow came from four test runs and about 2,000 credits. My Genjutsu vs Genjutsu Lite comparison ran the same five clips through both models.

I also build my own tools. I write skill files for Claude Code that hold the prompts and rules that passed testing, and Claude runs them through the Higgsfield MCP. That is how one person can make a lot of consistent work fast.

Automated content pipelines

This is the one most people don't expect. I built a pipeline where I type one line, like "a Vietnam Navy SEAL, 1968", and Claude Code makes a finished collectible figure reel through the Higgsfield MCP: the brief, the hero still, the detail plates, the video renders, the edit and the caption. I approve the hero image and post.

On TikTok that channel reached 21 reels and about 322,000 views in five weeks. The best reel has 96.7K views and was also the cheapest to make. The build is in Automated Figure Pipeline and in my post I Automated a Collectible Figure Channel with Claude Code and the Higgsfield MCP.

For a brand, the same setup can turn a product catalog into a steady stream of on-brand reels, with you approving each one.

How an engagement works
You send a brief. Product, platform, length, deadline, and any references you like.
I send a plan. A style frame, the shot list and a quote.
You review drafts. Low-res takes, fast. Notes go here, where changes are cheap.
I deliver the final. 1080p or 4K, in every aspect ratio you need, with the project files organized so you can see how it was made.

I take single spots and trailers, monthly social content, and pipeline builds that I set up for your team and hand over.
