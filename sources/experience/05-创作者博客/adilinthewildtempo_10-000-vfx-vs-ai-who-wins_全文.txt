Erik challenged me to turn his forest footage into a cinematic dinosaur chase. He made his version with traditional VFX. I used Higgsfield to create my own creature, put myself into the footage, and finish the sequence with an explosion.
The project had 12 shots, each with a different problem to solve: a face that wouldn’t stay consistent, a dinosaur in the wrong position, an arrow that needed a clear flight path, and a run that looked too mechanical.
Here’s the workflow, with the references, generation prompts, and results you can use to recreate it.
Start by watching the video on YouTube 
Before You Start
I used Claude with Higgsfield MCP to create references and write prompts, then generated the main shots in Cinema Studio with Seedance 2.5. Keep your footage, references, and results in a folder for each shot. Save recurring characters as Elements and link each @ tag to the right file.

Create a Dinosaur That Works in Motion
1. Create the First Version
I started with a feathered Velociraptor reference sheet showing full-body views, a head portrait, and a foot close-up. I tested the design in motion before creating the final version.
First-version prompt
Create a highly photorealistic natural-history reference sheet of one adult VELOCIRAPTOR MONGOLIENSIS, using this text alone, no reference images. A scientifically informed feathered reconstruction photographed as a living animal. Small, lightly built, low-bodied predator with a long narrow low skull, subtly upturned snout profile, slender neck, compact flesh-covered ribcage, well-proportioned thighs and a very long tapering bony tail held clear of ground. NOT the large bulky naked movie raptor. No beak: a narrow tooth-bearing reptilian snout. No ornamental crests or horns.
Landscape 3:2, plain warm light-gray studio with soft directional daylight, lifelike shadows, restrained photographic color, sharp detail. Design a spacious reference sheet: top half two large full-body views, exact left profile and front three-quarter view; lower half three panels, a smaller full-body slow walking pose, a large detailed head portrait with mouth slightly open, and a detailed complete foot close-up including the lower leg and all toes. All complete-body silhouettes fit with generous margins, including tail tip, feathers and feet. No text, fake labels or signatures. Same individual's coloration, head proportions and marking locations in every panel.
Feather covering: short layered contour feathers over body, neck and thighs, smoothly following fleshy volume rather than inflating into a huge bird ball; substantial long pennaceous feathers along the forearm and second finger forming folded wing-like arms; modest feathering along the long tail. Fine individual overlapping barbs and feather edges with soft irregular natural arrangement, not hair, fur or quills. Muted sandy-brown upper plumage with subtle darker barring, buff underside and gray-brown flight feathers. This color scheme and exact feather distribution are plausible artistic choices. Leave a restrained small area of fine-scaled skin around the snout and eye, and scaly lower feet. Do not expose a naked wrinkled turkey neck or bare muscular torso.
Three clawed fingers on each forelimb, palms facing each other, feathered arms gently folded against sides without anatomically fusing into chest. Show natural finger placement within the wing feathers, no extra hands. Digitigrade hindlimbs: elevated ankles, two forward supporting toes III and IV bearing weight on the ground, inner toe II held flexed UP with its distinctive curved sickle claw completely clear of the ground, small first toe above ground. The foot close-up clearly separates all digits. No giant fantasy claws. The walking panel shows one planted foot and the other lifted forward, with believable balance and small joint yielding.
Healthy flesh and soft tissues beneath plumage; subtle contour change with posture, no exposed muscle bundles or deep sculpted grooves. Fine facial skin with restrained shallow scale relief and slight eyelid folds; dry diffuse surface contrasting with wet convex cornea and moist mouth interior. Warm brown iris, round dark pupil, attentive natural gaze. Narrow jaws and slightly open mouth reveal individually varied small backward-curving laterally compressed ivory teeth embedded in gums, delicate serrations only at close range, no identical white triangular zipper or oversized T.rex teeth. Thin scaled oral margins, relaxed mouth, tongue rests inside, no snarling or roaring.
Make the animal feel alive through believable weight, subtle asymmetry, compliant exposed skin, alert gaze, soft feather overlap and realistic photographic material response. No toy sheen, resin, clay, plastic, glossy uniform scales, waxy skin, illustration, skeletal overlay, people or environment.

2. Create the Final Version
For the final version, I used two real animal photos as texture references: bird feathers and monitor lizard skin. These helped create a lifelike portrait with natural surfaces, soft light, and a believable eye.
Texture references:
Final-version prompt
A real-looking wildlife photograph of a living feathered Velociraptor mongoliensis. Use the two attached REAL photographs as the visual foundation for photographic realism and physical surfaces. Reference 1 is bird feather macro: use its delicate thin flat vanes, visible irregular individual barbs, soft overlap, variations in focus, small feather splits and subtle natural sheen. Reference 2 is a real monitor lizard portrait: use its organic face scale irregularities, soft eyelid flesh, realistic moist dark eye, natural oral margin and the way light interacts with living skin. Combine these physical qualities into one coherent living feathered dinosaur. Do not reproduce a bird or a monitor lizard; do not use a previous dinosaur render.
ONE single uncropped photographic frame, not a character sheet or collage. Close eye-level three-quarter portrait including the head, long neck, chest, folded feathered forearms and upper thighs, with tail running out into the softly blurred distance. Animal head turned subtly toward the camera, quiet alert gaze, mouth closed with thin natural scaled margins. Species has narrow long low tooth-bearing snout, no beak, light dromaeosaur build. Soft naturally filled body under feathers. Winglike arms with long flight feathers and three discreet clawed fingers partly visible; no giant arms or grotesque claw display.
Plumage on neck, torso and thighs: subdued cool gray-brown with irregular dark brown mottling and a paler gray-buff throat and underside. Short smooth overlapping contour feathers lie along body contours, not spiky fur. Larger wing feathers are thin, irregularly layered, locally slightly ruffled. Real fine feather barb texture rather than sculpted ridges. Small patches of exposed gray-brown skin on snout and around eyelids, modest scale size, slight irregular pigmentation and softness. Preserve an actual living eye with a dark brown iris, round pupil, nuanced curved wet corneal reflection and soft irregular eyelid edges. Skin reads as hydrated tissue without being lacquered; feather surfaces are dry and softly scattering.
Observe it outdoors on a neutral dusty bank in soft open shade, shallow depth of field with an unobtrusive pale earth-colored out-of-focus background. Natural available light falls unevenly through distant vegetation, with restrained contrast, an ordinary camera exposure and real shadow transitions. Telephoto wildlife photograph, focal plane through the nearest eye and adjacent facial skin; distant shoulder, rear feathers and background progressively soften. Preserve true photographic microcontrast instead of equally sharpened detail everywhere. Subtle irregularities and a few tiny specks of dust, not dirt pasted over everything. No dramatic rim light, artificial studio shadows, stylized grading or heroic dinosaur posing. The convincing natural visual character of the two input photographs is more important than 'fantasy creature' polish. Output must look like a plausible observational camera photograph, not 3D CGI, a museum model, a game asset, a wax model, an illustration, a glossy toy or an airbrushed digital painting. No text or watermark.

ACTING TASK — the hunter (he is fully invested in his tactic; the work happens in his eyes):
SCENE DIRECTION (unspoken): stay unheard and get the clean shot before the animal knows he is there.
MOTIVE (his fuel): he has come a long way for this animal and will not get a second chance at it today.
GOAL: pin down where the animal is behind the trunk and put the arrow in it on the first try.
OBSTACLE: he cannot see it from behind the trunk — only hears it — and every movement of his own could give him away; one careless sound and it bolts.
TACTIC: he hunts with his ears first, then with one eye.
