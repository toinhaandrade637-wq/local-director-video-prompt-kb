UNTIL THE END
Alex Bokor
@alexbokor

Last time, the dog survived and I was the one who turned. This time I wanted to break your heart from the other side.




Meet Bubu again. Same tiny chihuahua, same oversized jacket, a little more beaten up this time. And meet Dudu, the girl he loves. She has a pink bow, a pink hoodie and denim overalls with one strap always falling off her shoulder. She also has milky white eyes now, because the world ended and she didn't make it.




The whole story is one choice. He has the rifle. She is coming. And instead of pulling the trigger, he drops it and opens his arms.




Everything was made in Higgsfield: the characters and locations with its image models, the motion with Seedance 2.5. The film is two generations: one for the present, one for the memory. Below are both prompts, plus what I learned making them.




How to use them: every prompt lists its references as @image1, @image2 and so on. Attach your images in exactly that order.




Part 1 — The present (18s)

This is the slow, heavy half. It opens on her face, and for a second she almost looks like herself: head tilted, bow crooked, sweet. Then her lip curls and she growls. That switch is the whole hook.




Bubu gets the hardest acting in the film. He is hurt, shaking, crying, trying to aim a rifle that is almost as tall as he is. Then the wide shot, the one I borrowed from the original trend: a tiny figure between two dead towers, the rifle sliding out of his paws, and his arms slowly opening. A close-up of his eyes closing. She charges straight at the camera. Side view, she leaps.




The trick is the match cut. The cut happens inside the same generation, at the exact frame her paws touch his chest, with the exact same framing on both sides. One frame it's a zombie attack. The next frame it's the same jump, years earlier, ending in his arms in a wheat field. Generating both sides of the cut together is what keeps the poses lined up.




A lighting note from the first film: keep the sun to the side. Backlight looks epic, but you lose the faces, and here the faces are everything.

SCENE CONTEXT

In a dead, overgrown city at golden hour, a small wounded chihuahua soldier aims an old rifle at the girl he loves, who has turned into a zombie. He cannot shoot. He drops the rifle, opens his arms and closes his eyes as she charges and leaps at him. At the exact moment she reaches him, the image match-cuts into a golden wheat field in the past, where the same leap ends with her landing happily in his arms.




AUDIO PRIORITY

Absolutely no music. No score, no soundtrack, no melody, no humming, no musical stings. Only real diegetic sound. No dialogue, no words, no subtitles.

In every shot only the two chihuahuas exist. No other people, no other animals, no duplicates. Both always stand and move upright on two legs.

PHYSICS

Both chihuahuas move like small humans on two legs with real ground contact: Dudu's sprint has real stride and push-off, her leap real takeoff and airtime; Bubu absorbs the catch with real weight, staggering back. Clothing, ears, bow and backpack react with natural delay. In the wheat, stalks bend and spring back, seeds and pollen lift and drift in the light.

Flashback: fairytale memory, dreamy soft halation and bloom on every highlight, hazy lifted blacks, soft focus toward the frame edges, clearly visible film noise and grain, gentle warm light leaks, pastel-warm golden colors.

Real fur, real fabric, not CGI, not a game engine.




AUDIO

No music. Part 2 — The memory (10s)

My first version had them running and dancing through the wheat. It was cute and it felt like nothing. So I cut it down to ten seconds of stillness.




They lie in the wheat after the fall. He looks up at her, she looks down at him, shy, then back. He touches her cheek like he's trying to memorize it, and she rests her forehead on his. Then the last shot: the two of them sitting in the sunset, her head on his shoulder.




The most important line in this prompt isn't about the camera. It's the human performance lock. They have to move and act like a real couple, not like pets. And the audio note matters more than you'd think: no laughing, no little yips, no childlike sounds. Just wind and breathing. It makes them feel grown up, and it makes it hurt.




For the look I went full memory: vintage anamorphic glass, heavy diffusion, milky blacks, visible grain and light leaks, everything glowing.

Deeply in love, emotional, bittersweet.

@Image 3: an endless golden wheat field at sunset with a soft line of trees on the horizon. Used for geography, materials and light, not for camera angle.




HUMAN PERFORMANCE LOCK

Both chihuahuas move exactly like real humans in every shot: human lying and sitting postures, human shoulders, human hand gestures with their front paws used as hands — touching a face, wrapping an arm around each other, resting foreheads together, leaning a head on a shoulder. They never walk on four legs, never act like pets, never sniff, never wag excitedly. Their faces carry real, subtle human acting: soft eyes, small smiles, glances, shy looks away and back, eyes closing into a touch. The love feels normal, quiet and real, like two people.




LOCATION MAP

Everything happens inside @Image 3, in waist-high ripe golden wheat. The low sun sits just above the horizon behind them, so they are backlit with glowing golden rims. The field is full and unbroken, no path, no road.




FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING

The first visible frame is already both of them lying in flattened wheat: @Image 1 on his back, @Image 2 lying on top of him, her forearms resting on his chest, faces close, both breathless, smiling softly. No empty frame, no delayed reveal.

Only these two characters in every shot. No other people, no other animals, no duplicates.




FORMAT MODE

Controlled multi-shot sequence, five shots, slow tender HARD CUTS, the last shot held longer. Real-time motion with a slightly floating, dreamlike handheld feel; the final shot gently slows to half-speed. No fades, no dissolves, no transition effects.
