I wanted to see Miyu and Yuna, my two AI Influencers, jump on the Fetty Wap “679” trend. The idea was to take the performance from the reference clip and recreate it with both characters while keeping the movement and energy recognizable.

I used Higgsfield Genjutsu for the AI video work and CapCut for the finishing touches. Getting a result I liked took a few attempts. The biggest thing I cared about was facial identity: Miyu needed to stay Miyu, and Yuna needed to stay Yuna throughout the clip.

Here’s how I approached it, what went wrong, and a starter prompt you can adapt for your own characters.

1. Prepare the performance clip and character references

My reference was the Fetty Wap and Monty “679” performance from On The Radar Radio. I worked with a short segment of that performance.

https://youtu.be/6KHYB6RsIpM?si=Sf6XzCDUprPjHdO2

For your own version, choose the section you want to recreate and trim it to the intended beginning and ending. Watch both performers before generating: knowing who leads and where the second person appears makes the character assignments easier to describe.




I also had separate character references for Miyu and Yuna. Use clear, accepted references that show the faces and hairstyles you actually want in the result. These give you something consistent to compare each generation against.

5. Review the whole generation

A good opening frame is only part of the result. Watch the full video, then pause during head turns, fast movement, and moments when hands pass near faces.

I checked whether Miyu and Yuna still looked like their references, whether either original performer remained, and whether objects picked up unwanted details. Some attempts changed more than I wanted. I also ran into a cover candidate where Yuna’s face looked blurred.

That gave me a useful priority: protect the faces and the performance first. I accepted small nail details in the version I liked because they mattered less to me than keeping the overall clip strong.




6. Finish the edit in CapCut

Once I had a version I liked, the next steps were audio and presentation. My clip sounded quiet, so I increased the volume in CapCut. Choose the adjustment for your own file and listen back for distortion instead of copying a fixed volume increase.

Check the sound against the performance, review the start and ending, and choose a cover where both characters are clear. For this video, I wanted the cover to introduce Miyu and Yuna together, so a frame with one blurred face was a weaker choice.




This project reminded me how much the small decisions matter: clear character assignments, recognizable faces during movement, and knowing when a generation is good enough to keep. Miyu and Yuna ended up with their own take on the performance, and the retries are part of how I got there.
