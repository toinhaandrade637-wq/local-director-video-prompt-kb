Before You Start

This is the full prompt library behind a story-driven smart TV commercial: one family, one remote, six scenes. A grandma freezes a speeding van, an office worker pauses his boss, a grandpa rewinds to age four, a commuter rewinds a train, a schoolboy multiplies a lollipop, and the whole family ends up together in front of the TV. Six scenes, seventeen Seedance 2.5 generations, built from sixteen reference images.




Build the assets first. The family, the remote and the locations come back across every scene and again on the couch. If each generation invents its own version of them, the commercial falls apart. Lock them once as images, then reuse them.




The stills were made with Soul Cinema (character sheets, locations and the lollipop) and Seedream 5.0 Pro (the remote). Every scene is Seedance 2.5: one generation per scene, with its own cuts inside.




Everything is driven by a Claude Skill running on Claude Opus 5.5, and this is the part worth saying plainly: the Skill writes the video prompts for you. You describe a scene in a couple of plain sentences and attach the references, and the Skill turns it into a full shooting script: references tagged to the right shots, a lens and camera for every shot, timing, physics, lighting, sound and the locks at the bottom. The prompts below are shortened versions of what it wrote for this commercial.

Three things that decided how this one turned out, before you start prompting:

Build the trick into the prop. Every trick uses a real button on the approved remote: OK freezes time, the left arrow rewinds, plus multiplies. The first office version asked for PAUSE and PLAY buttons the remote doesn't have, and it had to be redone.
Describe what doesn't change. Tell a video model to freeze time and it adds grey filters, glow and ripples. The freeze lock lists everything that stays exactly the same, so the magic happens in a normal-looking world.
Anchor every transformation. The four-year-old appears on grandpa's exact spot at the fence, holding the same remote, so the viewer connects them without a morph. The change itself is hidden in a cut.
