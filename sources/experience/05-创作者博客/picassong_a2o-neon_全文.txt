Example of the A2O LITE Character Sheet





1. Character Reference Video
Prompt

Perform a strictly localized replacement of ONLY the two main foreground dancing characters in @Video 3.



TARGET ASSIGNMENT



Identify the two main characters in the opening shot:

- Target A: initially on the viewer’s LEFT → replace with @Image 10.

- Target B: initially on the viewer’s RIGHT → replace with @Image 11.



Track these same two identities throughout the video. These assignments must not change when the targets cross, turn, overlap, or change screen positions.



Use @Image 10 and @Image 11 exclusively for the appearance of their assigned targets. Never apply these references to any other character.



CRITICAL: PROTECT ALL REAR AND BACKGROUND CHARACTERS



In the later group scene, all characters standing or dancing BEHIND the two main foreground targets must remain exactly as they appear in the ORIGINAL video.



Preserve every rear character’s original:

- Identity and facial features

- Expression, blinking, and gaze

- Hairstyle, hair color, and hair movement

- Skin color and makeup

- Outfit, shoes, and accessories

- Body proportions and size

- Pose, choreography, timing, and position

- Lighting, shading, texture, and motion blur



Do not replace, redraw, restyle, recolor, enhance, or regenerate any rear character.



Do not give the rear characters the reference outfits, headwear, facial features, hairstyles, proportions, or materials.



Do not coordinate the whole group’s appearance with the replacement characters.



Treat all non-target characters as protected original footage. Preserve their visible source pixels frame by frame wherever they are not naturally occluded by the two replacement targets.



The presence of @Image 10 and @Image 11 must have ZERO effect on the appearance of the rear group.



LOCAL EDIT BOUNDARY



Limit all visual changes to the two tracked foreground targets and the minimum area required by their new silhouettes and contact shadows.



Do not use a broad edit region covering the entire group or the full frame.



When a target overlaps a rear character, preserve the original front-to-back ordering. Change only the foreground target; preserve the rear character’s visible portions exactly.



If a replacement silhouette reveals an area previously hidden by the original target, reconstruct only that newly exposed area consistently with the source. Do not alter any already-visible portion of a rear character.



If a target becomes fully hidden, do not replace the visible character in front of it.



Never select a rear character as a substitute target because of similar hair, clothing, face, or screen position.



If a target cannot be identified confidently in a frame, preserve that uncertain region rather than modifying another character.



REFERENCE-BASED APPEARANCE



For Target A and Target B only, replace the complete appearance using the assigned image:

- Face, eyes, eyelids, eyebrows, eyelashes, nose, and lips

- Skin tone, blush, and makeup

- Hair color, style, length, and texture

- Headwear, outfit, footwear, and accessories

- Head-to-body ratio, torso, limbs, hands, and feet

- Materials and surface finish



If an image is a character sheet, treat all views as ONE character. Use the close-up for facial details and the full-body, side, and rear views for consistent anatomy and design.



Do not reproduce sheet layouts, labels, backgrounds, or extra copies.



Do not retain the original targets’ clothing or anatomy where they differ from their assigned references. Do not mix the two reference identities.



PROPORTION LOCK



Keep each replacement character’s reference proportions stable in every frame.



Preserve the oversized head, compact torso, short limbs, and small hands and feet when shown in the reference.



Retarget the source choreography to the reference anatomy. Do not stretch limbs, lengthen the torso, shrink the head, or introduce visible joints absent from the reference.



Preserve the targets’ original floor positions, movement paths, depth, grounded steps, and jump timing.



PERFORMANCE LOCK



For the two replacement targets, preserve:

- Original choreography and rhythm

- Movement direction, sequence, and timing

- Head angles, body orientation, and gestures

- Hand actions and interactions

- Gaze direction and blinking timing

- Emotional expression and mouth-action timing



Express the original performance through the reference faces without reverting to the original facial geometry.



Keep the reference hairstyle, headwear, clothing, and accessories throughout. Animate their secondary motion naturally according to the replacement design.



Keep the number, shape, size, spacing, and placement of ears, tails, ribbons, and other distinctive details consistent with the assigned reference.



ORIGINAL FOOTAGE LOCK



Preserve the original background, floor, props, lighting setup, camera angles, camera movement, framing, focus, depth of field, color grade, cuts, transitions, duration, aspect ratio, frame timing, and audio.



Match the replacement targets to the source lighting and motion blur without changing the rest of the footage.



When neither target is visible, leave the entire segment unchanged.



Do not add text, captions, objects, extra characters, or scenes.



Maintain clean, coherent surfaces and stable facial details without flickering, texture crawling, patchy shading, or distortion.



AVOID



- ANY appearance change to the rear group

- Applying reference costumes or faces to background characters

- Whole-frame or whole-group restyling

- Identity swapping or target-tracking drift

- Replacing a non-target character during occlusion

- Original target features leaking into the replacements

- Changing reference body proportions

- Extra limbs, fingers, ears, tails, or accessories

- Foot sliding, clipping, or incorrect overlap

- Changes to choreography, camera work, editing, or audio



FINAL PRIORITY



Only the two designated foreground dancers receive the reference character designs.

Every rear and non-target character remains in its original video appearance, unchanged throughout the entire clip.
