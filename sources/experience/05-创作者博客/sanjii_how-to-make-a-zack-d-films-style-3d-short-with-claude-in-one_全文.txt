What's inside is the formula that makes a video feel like Zack's:




A curiosity-loop script. The first line opens a question your brain needs closed: the myth everyone believes, then the twist, how it actually worked, and a last line that flips the whole story. About 30 seconds, 75–85 words.
Character sheets for consistency. Every recurring character, prop and location is generated once as a reference sheet, then fed into every scene. The model never has to remember what the hero looks like. It sees it.
The signature 3D look. Glossy, stylized 3D with warm sun, deep blue skies and close-ups where every material reads: wood grain, rope, bronze, wet hide.
Baked-in annotations. Neon-green outlines and arrows for "look here", red dashed lines, cracks and impact bursts for danger and physics, rendered right into the scene.
Zack pacing. Six to nine hard cuts every 10 seconds, at least one extreme close-up of an eye, and one gag beat.
One narrator voice. The whole script in one continuous documentary-style take, in a voice you pick or your own clone.
The edit. Punch-in zooms on every reveal and screen shakes on every impact, cut into one vertical 9:16 MP4.

What Happens in the Chat

After you send the prompt, the skill runs the same five steps as a full studio:

Script. Claude finds the angle and writes the 30-second narration.
Visuals. It plans every shot and builds a character sheet for each person, prop and location.
Animation. It animates the video in 10-second blocks, with the green and red annotations baked in.
Voice. It records the full narration in one take.
Edit. It cuts everything together with zoom-ins and screen shakes and sends you one finished MP4.

A 30-second short takes about half an hour from prompt to final video, and you don't have to watch it work.
