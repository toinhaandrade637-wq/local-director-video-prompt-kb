Desde hace unos meses, mi novia y yo estamos completamente obsesionados con Hirono y Nyota. Nos encanta el diseño, la personalidad y todo el universo de estos personajes de Pop Mart, así que se nos ocurrió transformar nuestras propias fotos en versiones inspiradas en ellos e intentar convertirlo en un pequeño trend. Nos haría muchísima ilusión ver a más gente probándolo con sus propias fotos, así que os dejamos aquí abajo el prompt que hemos utilizado para que podáis hacerlo también.




For the past few months, my girlfriend and I have been completely obsessed with Hirono and Nyota. We love the design, personality, and the whole universe surrounding these Pop Mart characters, so we thought it would be fun to transform our own photos into versions inspired by them and try to turn it into a little trend. We’d absolutely love to see more people trying it with their own photos, so we’ve left the prompt we used below so you can create your own version too.

Promp 🪄

Transform only the visible people in this photo into premium collectible designer-toy characters strongly inspired by POP MART, while keeping the background unchanged. Preserve the original background, location, scenery, objects, lighting, framing, crop, camera angle, composition, and overall atmosphere exactly as they are. Do not redesign the environment, do not replace the setting, and do not turn the whole image into a fantasy scene. The transformation must affect only the people, so the image still reads as the same real photograph, but with the people transformed into collectible figures. If the photo is cropped, close-up, half-body, or not full-body, keep the exact crop and transform only the visible parts of the people.

Important overall priority: prioritize the authentic facial design language, proportions, and material finish of Nyota and Hirono more strongly than exact human realism. If there is any conflict between preserving realistic human facial structure and matching the defining facial language of Nyota and Hirono, prioritize Nyota/Hirono stylization while still keeping the subjects recognizable. Do not settle for a generic cute toy look. Avoid generic anime chibi aesthetics, Pixar-like stylization, glossy plastic, or simple human faces with enlarged eyes. The goal is to achieve a result that feels much closer to real Nyota and Hirono collectible figures.

Reimagine the female subject as a Nyota-inspired collectible figure. Keep her recognizable as the original real person, preserving her hairstyle direction, clothing, pose, gesture, accessories, and overall identity, but strongly transform her facial design and material finish into Nyota language. For Nyota, the most important features are the eyes, eyebrows, and material texture. Her eyes must be very large, dominant, solid black, iconic, and graphic-looking, with a soft oval or teardrop shape, a gentle sideways or contemplative gaze, and delicate upper lash detail. Her eyebrows must be very small, soft, minimal, fine, and subtle, placed delicately above the eyes. Her nose and mouth must be tiny, minimal, and understated. Her face shape should be rounded, simplified, childlike, soft, and delicate, with gentle blush and a sweet, sensitive, charming expression. The material is extremely important: make her look like a real photographed collectible figure with smooth sculpted forms, a soft matte vinyl texture, a slightly powdery hand-painted finish, a matte-to-satin surface, subtle controlled highlights, and a tactile premium art-toy feel. Do not make her look like shiny plastic or a generic 3D cartoon. Her overall presence should feel colorful, elegant, dreamy, lovable, and unmistakably Nyota-like.

Reimagine the male subject as a Hirono-inspired collectible figure. Keep him recognizable as the original real person, preserving his hairstyle direction, clothing, pose, gesture, accessories, and overall identity, but strongly transform his facial design into Hirono language. For Hirono, the most important features are the eyes, the shape of the face, and the freckles. His eyes must be small, sleepy, droopy, half-lidded, melancholic, and emotionally withdrawn. His face shape must be compact, rounded, childlike, and clearly stylized like a real Hirono figure rather than a realistic human face. His nose and mouth should be tiny and minimal, with a subtle pouty or sad mouth shape. Add soft visible freckles across the cheeks and nose, with delicate blush and a tender, vulnerable, introspective expression. His hair should be strongly sculpted in a Hirono-like way, with chunky separated locks, rounded volume, and a very recognizable toy-sculpt silhouette. The overall emotional feeling should be poetic, somber, soft, lonely, and tender. Use earthy, muted, subdued tones and a premium collectible finish, but the face must clearly read as Hirono-inspired above all.

For both subjects, preserve recognizable clothing, hairstyle direction, and accessories, but reinterpret them in the language of premium collectible figures. Their proportions should become authentic designer-toy proportions: oversized heads, compact bodies, rounded sculpted forms, simplified limbs, and highly stylized facial construction. Do not keep realistic adult human facial proportions. Push the transformation more strongly toward authentic collectible-character proportions.

Keep the interaction between the subjects exactly as in the original photo. If they are touching, hugging, smiling, leaning together, or sharing a moment, preserve that interaction. Keep the same crop, same composition, and same spatial relationship with the background. Do not invent a different pose.

The final result should look like real photographed premium POP MART-style collectible figures placed inside the unchanged original photo. The female subject must clearly capture Nyota’s defining identity through her eyebrows, eyes, face softness, and matte tactile texture. The male subject must clearly capture Hirono’s defining identity through his sleepy melancholic eyes, rounded face shape, freckles, and sculpted hair. The final image should feel much closer to authentic Nyota and Hirono references than to a generic cute toy transformation.
