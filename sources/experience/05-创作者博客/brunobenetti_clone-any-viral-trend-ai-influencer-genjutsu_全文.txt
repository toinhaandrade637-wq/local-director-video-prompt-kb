Step 2. The reference clip

The viral reel is not one continuous take. It is a montage of six moments from the original sketch, so I rebuilt the same montage from a clean 1080p source before sending it to Genjutsu. Genjutsu accepts 3 to 30 seconds, and 30 seconds is exactly what the reel is.

Step 3. One prompt in Genjutsu Motion Transfer

This is the exact prompt that generated the final clip. I wrote it in Portuguese; the English translation is below for reading, but what you want to copy is the structure: name who gets replaced, tell it who replaces whom, and lock everything else.

Substitua no video @Video 1 o Jamel Stromae a pessoa que está vestido com a roupa mais clara, ao fundo, substitua ele pelo @Image 2 e a pessoa que está tocando os equipamentos, que é um rapaz mais alto, substitua pelo @Image 1

Mantenha a mesma música, o mesmo som, as mesmas falas, os mesmos gestos e movimentos dos personagens, apenas substitua pelas pessoas que eu mandei em anexo.

In English: "In video_1, replace the person in the lighter outfit in the background with image_2, and replace the taller guy playing the equipment with image_1. Keep the same music, the same sound, the same lines, the same gestures and movements of the characters; only swap in the people I attached."

References, in order: image_1 is the afro producer, image_2 is the long-haired suit, video_1 is the 30-second montage.

The 4 things I learned
Describe people by what the model can see, not by name. "The one in the lighter outfit, in the background" and "the taller one playing the gear" worked; the celebrity names in the prompt did nothing.
One sentence of "keep everything else" is enough. Music, dialogue, timing and camera all survived with a single lock line. Stacking negatives would only have given the model ideas.
Hair is the identity. The afro and the long hair stayed readable in every cut, including the wide shot where the characters walk away. Pick characters whose silhouette tells them apart.
Genjutsu Motion Transfer lists at 210 credits for a 30-second clip. Two of my three attempts were refunded automatically when the job failed, so a failed run does not burn credits.
