In “Money Talks,” cash goes through the microwave, gets pressed on an ironing board and ends up covering the walls. A child climbs a ladder to a mattress resting on a tower of banknotes. An older woman settles into her chair to knit with money.

I built the film around familiar routines with one impossible detail. Everyone carries on as if this is normal. That calm behavior connects twelve short scenes across roughly 32 seconds.

Here's how the scenes work, with expanded prompts for recreating them. These prompts are reconstructed from the finished video; the preparation steps below describe a practical way to reproduce its look and action.

Before you start

The scene needs to feel familiar before its unusual detail lands. A spray bottle, plastic-covered sofa and television make the ironing scene feel like an ordinary afternoon. A child's pajamas and teddy bear make the towering cash bed feel like part of a bedtime routine.

For each shot, choose one action the viewer can recognize immediately. Then work out how the money changes that action. Keep the performance matter-of-fact, and let the camera show what makes the situation strange.

Stage 1 — Prepare the visual references

For a recreation, collect references around the objects and rooms that carry the film:

Cash in different forms: loose notes, neatly banded bundles, shredded strips, dense stacks and rows covering a wall. Their thickness and behavior should change with their use.
Everyday objects: a microwave, clothesline, money counter, bicycle, small skateboard, iron, broom, knitting needles, duffel bag, ladder and wicker basket.
Lived-in spaces: modest bedrooms, a church, a sparsely furnished living room, a residential street and a weathered front porch. Plastic furniture covers recur throughout the film.
A consistent look: 4:3 framing, visible grain, warm window light, muted green shadows and slightly soft detail.

For image-to-video, establish the unusual arrangement in the starting image. Put the safe on its skateboard, the mattress on the money tower and the banknotes on the wall before asking for movement. That leaves the video prompt to handle the action and camera.

The notes should behave like paper: individual bills flex, bundles hold their shape, and shredded strips bend around the knitting needles. Give each form the right physical properties.

Stage 2 — Write around one clear action

The prompts below describe the setting, the subject's action, the camera move and the details that need to remain consistent. They stay close to what is visible in the edit.

Add this shared style block before each scene prompt:

4:3 cinematic music-video frame. Photorealistic 35mm film look, visible grain, slightly soft detail and natural motion blur. Lived-in rooms, worn objects and realistic skin texture. Warm practical lamps or late-afternoon window light, with muted green and teal shadows where the location supports them. Calm, matter-of-fact performances; everyone treats the surreal situation as ordinary life. Preserve faces, clothing and object placement within the shot. Give loose paper, bound bundles, fabric and metal distinct physical behavior. No added music, captions or title graphics.

The timestamps are rounded positions in the finished montage, not required generation lengths. Generate enough footage for a clear action, then select the part that reads best in the edit. Add the continuous music track during assembly.
