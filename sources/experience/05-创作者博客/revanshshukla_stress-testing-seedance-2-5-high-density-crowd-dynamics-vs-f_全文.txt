Generative engines notoriously collapse when forcing complex fluid dynamics to interact with multi-entity crowd tracking. For the critical disaster sequence in Echoes of Tomorrow, I pushed Cinema Studio 4 to generate a massive urban tsunami plate alongside dozens of fleeing foreground subjects.
​The compute cost was absolute leverage—this sequence alone flatlined my remaining credit balance. While the temporal coherence on the primary foreground subjects surprisingly held together, the engine's edge-calculation on the water impact warped severely. As you can see in my Resolve timeline, I had to apply a hard 1.433x crop to eliminate the peripheral hallucination and salvage the framing. I then brute-forced the scale of the scene using a heavy multi-track Fairlight audio pipeline.
​The visual foundation of Seedance 2.5 is elite, but it desperately needs a localized render-region feature. We shouldn't be burning premium compute credits calculating off-screen water physics that just end up on the cutting room floor.
