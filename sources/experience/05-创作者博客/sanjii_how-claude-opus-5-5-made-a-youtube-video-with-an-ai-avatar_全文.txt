Before You Start

Sanji gave Claude Opus 5.5 a brief: create a complete YouTube video using his AI avatar and cloned voice. The finished episode runs for about four minutes and seventeen seconds, combining a presenter, three demos, on-screen text, music and sound effects.




The opening makes the setup clear. After Sanji’s hook, the avatar introduces itself as Claude and explains that the voice is a clone. The AI remains the narrator through the rest of the episode.




Claude Opus 5.5 handles the production instructions. Higgsfield MCP connects Claude to generation and editing tools. Sanji supplies the source material, editorial direction and feedback.




To follow the workflow, start with the materials the video will reuse. A consistent presenter reference and voice help the episode feel continuous as it moves between the host and the demos.

Stage 1 — Prepare the Assets
Presenter and Voice

Use your own presenter reference and a clean voice sample, or materials from a presenter who has agreed to their use. Keep the same identity and voice throughout the episode. Make the AI presentation clear to viewers near the beginning.

Demo Footage

This episode features three examples: a Brawl Stars-style game, a headset animation and a Titanic sequence. Collect the relevant footage and original creator links before production so the edit can show the work and credit its source.

Choose clips for what they demonstrate. The game needs a fight that makes the mechanics visible. The headset needs its opening layers, lens close-up and reassembly. The Titanic sequence needs enough uninterrupted playback for viewers to see the ship and camera movement.

Editorial References

Attach the opening hook, a reference video for structure and a writing sample for tone. Include any claims that need verification alongside their sources. These materials give the agent concrete direction for the script and edit.

OPENING AND NARRATOR

Start with this hook in my avatar and voice:

“You’re not going to believe how insane this is. I asked Opus 5.5 to create an entire YouTube video using my AI avatar and voice. And what you’re watching right now is the result.”




Then clearly reveal that the presenter is an AI avatar and the voice is cloned. Keep Claude as the narrator for the rest of the video. Any statements about how the episode was produced must match the work actually performed. Acknowledge my supplied materials and feedback.




EXAMPLES

Use the three supplied examples in this order:

1. The Brawl Stars-style playable game.

2. The headset animation.

3. The Titanic animation.




Credit each creator using the original source. Verify claims about tools, build time and duration before including them. Do not invent sources or create substitute footage and present it as the original work.




For the game, select a fight that shows the mechanics and leave a short stretch without narration. For the headset, show the opening layers, lens close-up and reassembly. For Titanic, leave several seconds for the sequence to play on its own.




PRODUCTION EXPLANATION

Show how the script maps to selected footage. Build a short comparison from the headset passage: presenter and voice; then footage and text; then the final music and effects. Make each addition easy to recognize.




Time the narration to what is visible. Keep text readable, preserve useful creator credits, and place music below the voice. Use sound effects where they support a visible movement or cut.




THE BRIEF AND COST

Include a concise on-screen summary of the production brief, labeled as a summary. Explain which materials I supplied.




Track available generation charges and elapsed production time. Distinguish estimates from recorded spending, and separate generation from any other known costs. Do not present the reference estimate of approximately $120 for five to six minutes of presenter footage with voice as an actual bill for this production. Include additional takes in the cost report when their charges are available.




ENDING AND DELIVERY

End by asking viewers what they would like to see built next and inviting an idea in the comments. Mention resources below the video only when those links are actually included in the delivery.




Review the finished export for clipped text, narration-to-shot alignment, audio balance, creator credits and playback problems. Correct any issues found. Report which checks were completed and any remaining limitations.

What Does a Video Like This Cost?
