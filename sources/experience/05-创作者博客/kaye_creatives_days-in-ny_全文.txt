Early dusk inside a dim, unlit Manhattan apartment on approximately the fortieth floor.




The woman stands beside a large window, facing outward toward the distant skyline.




The window is in front of her, beyond the phone. It is the primary light source illuminating her face.




The upper camera looks outward through this window. The lower camera looks back at her, with the dark apartment behind her.




TOP PANEL — FIRST FRAME:

Look south across Manhattan from the apartment window.




A thin dark aluminum window frame borders the LEFT and BOTTOM edges. The glass has faint dust and water marks.




Below are dense rooftops with water towers, mechanical equipment and setback terraces. A long avenue extends into the distance with tiny headlights and taillights.

A small, intensely bright point with a short glowing trail is already visible in the upper-left sky, descending toward the horizon on the right.




BOTTOM PANEL — FIRST FRAME:

A tight selfie close-up from the top of her forehead to just below her chin. Her face fills most of the lower panel and is slightly off-center.




The phone is held at arm’s length, slightly below eye level.

Behind her is a dark, softly out-of-focus room: a vague wall, doorway and shelf. The skyline is not behind her.




Her eyes are fixed beyond the phone, slightly above and to its right. Her expression is initially calm and curious.




She never looks into the lens or checks the screen.

The impact happens approximately forty kilometers away.




The rising cloud becomes enormous relative to the foreground skyline. Its apparent scale comes from its distant size, without changing the dimensions of the Manhattan buildings.

One uninterrupted take in each panel at normal speed.

The upper image gives a small physical jolt without losing the distant impact area.

TOP:

The meteor travels from the upper-left sky toward the horizon on the right, becoming rapidly brighter.

Her expression shifts from quiet curiosity toward concern. Keep facial movements restrained.

At approximately 2.3 seconds, the meteor strikes beyond the distant horizon haze.

BOTH:

The split-screen boundary remains fixed. The flash is an exposure event within the continuous recordings, not a cut or transition.

The fireball rises and develops into a vast orange-and-black column.




A thick stem forms beneath a spreading mushroom-shaped cap. Large, slow rolling turbulence conveys its immense scale.

The original meteor trail remains suspended in the sky.

She opens her eyes again and stares beyond the phone, increasingly frightened.




Small, consistent warm reflections appear in both eyes. Keep them subtle and physically scaled.




Her breathing becomes fast and shallow. She does not scream.

The enormous cloud continues rising far above the skyline, glowing orange from within.

A faint vibration affects the window frame and image.

The same subtle vibration reaches her framing. Window glass rattles outside the selfie view.




Her eyes briefly glance downward toward the streets, then return to the distant event.




She shifts her weight backward slightly without leaving the window position. The phone remains near its original framing.

End while the distant event is still developing. Do not add a freeze frame or final pose.




LIGHTING CONTINUITY:

All illumination on her face comes from the unseen window beyond the phone.




Use the same sequence and timing in both panels:

cool dusk light,

a growing moving warm-white source,

one abrupt white flash,

then sustained orange-amber illumination.




Apply the light consistently in world space rather than copying the same brightness pattern onto both images.

PHYSICAL CONTINUITY:

Keep the skyline, rooftops, window frame and horizon stable.




The cloud evolves continuously with large-scale turbulence. It does not duplicate or abruptly change shape.




The expanding front remains centered on the distant impact location, with dust and spray trailing behind it.




Her hair and hoodie remain still apart from movements caused by her own flinch and breathing. No outdoor wind enters the room.




The lower background remains the apartment interior throughout.




AUDIO:

Use ONE shared synchronized audio track.




Quiet room tone, faint muffled traffic far below, close breathing and subtle clothing movement.




One sharp gasp coincides with the flash.




From approximately six seconds, add a faint low building vibration, light window rattling and distant alarms below.




No explosion boom or arriving blast sound within the take. Do not duplicate audio between panels.




STYLE:

Photorealistic smartphone footage at dusk with a permanent split-screen.




Natural skin texture, mild sensor noise, slight digital sharpening and authentic exposure behavior.




No cuts, panel switching, slow motion, dramatic camera moves, beauty filter, cinematic color grading, added lens flares, music, captions, labels, visible phone, hands or other people.
