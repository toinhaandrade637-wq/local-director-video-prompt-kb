Two abilities, one idea. Feed an existing video plus your reference images into Higgsfield, and either transfer its motion onto your character or swap a single object inside it, while the camera stays locked.

Motion transfer (hf_mult_motion_control). Transfer motion onto your character. The motion, poses and camera movement come from the source video; the character and scene come from your reference images. Use it when you love a move in one clip and want your character to perform it exactly.
Object replacement (hf_mult_replace_object). Swap one element inside a finished clip. The entire source video is kept (motion, camera, background, timing) and only the element you target (a product, garment or character) is replaced from a reference image. Everything else stays untouched.

Resolution: 480p / 720p / 1080p.

Step 1: The tagging system

The tool identifies every media by its order in the tray. Tagging is by position, never by filename.

Inside the prompt you point to each media with a tag. The video is @Video1, and the images are @Image1, @Image2 and so on, in the order they sit in the tray. First image is Image1, second is Image2, whatever the file is called.

@Video1: the source video, first and only clip in the video tray. Drives the motion, or is the clip being edited.
@Image1: first image in the tray (e.g. the character / the background).
@Image2: second image (e.g. a face closeup for identity accuracy).
@Image3: third image (e.g. an outfit / look).
Golden rule:
 arrange your images in the tray in a fixed order (character, face, outfit...) before writing the prompt, then match the tags to it. Reorder them and you have to update the tags.
Step 2: How to use it

The same five moves work for both models. Pick what stays and what changes, then let the prompt do the rest.

Pick a mode. Motion transfer (motion_control) or object replacement (replace_object), by what you keep vs. change.
Upload the source video. One video = @Video1. It drives the motion, or it is the clip being edited.
Upload reference images. Character, garment, object. Mind the order: it becomes @Image1, @Image2...
Write the prompt from a template. Lock what stays (identity, motion, camera) and state exactly what changes, with the right tags.
Choose resolution and run. 480p to test fast, 720p default, 1080p for the final.
Step 3: Prompt templates

Copy, and replace the placeholder parts with your own details.

Motion transfer

Motion and camera come from @Video1; the character comes from the images.

TASK - MOTION TRANSFER:
Transfer the exact motion, body movement, pose changes and camera motion from @Video1 onto the subject from the reference images. Keep the timing, rhythm and camera path identical to @Video1.

SUBJECT / IDENTITY: use the woman from @Image1 - same face, hair, body and identity. Match her face precisely to @Image2 (closeup for identity). Dress her in outfit from @Image3.

SCENE: place her in describe environment / or keep from @Image1. Relight her to match the scene, keep skin tone natural.

Keep her identity consistent in every frame. Do NOT change the motion or the camera path - those come only from @Video1. Cinematic look, same aspect ratio.




Object replacement

Everything in @Video1 stays; only the target you name is replaced from the image.

TASK - OBJECT REPLACEMENT:
Keep everything in @Video1 exactly the same: the person, all movements and performance, the camera motion and framing, composition, background, lighting, pacing, timing and every cut, and any on-screen text or captions.

Change ONLY the target - e.g. the handbag / the dress / the bottle: replace it with the new item from @Image1, matching its shape, color, material and details across every frame. Keep it correctly attached and lit as part of the scene.

Do not alter anything else. Everything except the replaced target stays identical to @Video1.

Object replacement: only the outfit is swapped from the reference image. Her face, every move, cut and camera angle stay from the source clip.




Step 4: Rules of thumb

Small habits that save failed renders and keep the edit faithful to the source.

"Keep everything identical." The more you spell out what NOT to change (face, motion, camera, timing, on-screen text), the more faithful the result. That is the secret to a clean edit.
Face closeup for accuracy. In motion transfer, adding a separate face closeup as an @Image gives far more accurate identity than a full-body shot alone.
Resolution in stages. Test at 480p (fast and cheap). Once motion and identity are perfect, run the final 1080p with the exact same prompt.
Moderation (NSFW). If a render starts then fails, it is usually the filter on frame content (revealing wardrobe at some angles) or profanity in the audio. Cover the look, or clean the audio.
