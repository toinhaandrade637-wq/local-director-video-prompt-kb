Step 2: Make your sky bison

One reference image for the bison keeps it the same animal in every shot: the fur, the arrow on its head, the horns and the saddle. Nobody is riding it in the sheet, so it doesn't add a stranger to your shots.

A two-panel real photograph reference sheet of an enormous gentle flying creature and its saddle, shot like documentary wildlife photography in soft flat overcast daylight over a pale grey sky. No studio light. No text, no labels.




THE CREATURE (a giant flying bison): as big as a city bus. A huge, soft, round body covered in thick, shaggy, cream-white fur with a darker tan tuft down the spine; six short, thick legs with big padded feet; a very broad, flat, paddle-shaped tail like a beaver's, covered in fur; a wide flat gentle face with small dark kind eyes, a big wet dark nose and a wide mouth; a large brown arrow-shaped marking of darker fur starting on the forehead and pointing forward, running back over the top of the head; two short, thick, pale horns curving outward and slightly up from the sides of the head. Real fur with weight, clumping and wind movement, real skin at the nose. Not cartoonish, not a toy.




THE SADDLE: a large flat wooden-framed saddle platform strapped on its back with thick leather harness straps under the belly; low polished wooden rails around the edge; worn saffron and deep orange canvas cushions and padded bench seats for about six passengers; brass fittings; reins running forward to the head.







LEFT PANEL: three-quarter view from slightly above, the whole creature flying over open water, saddle visible, nobody riding it, fur rippling in the wind.

RIGHT PANEL: the view from inside the saddle, seated at passenger height, looking forward over the wooden front rail: the top of the creature's furry head with the brown arrow marking, and the two horns, open sky and far water beyond.

Photographic, 35mm film look, natural colour, soft grain. Not a 3D render, not an illustration.

Step 3: Generate each shot

Settings for every shot: Seedance 2.5, 4:3, 480p, 5 seconds (7 for the last one). I made each one as a draft, ran it two or three times and kept the best take. Every prompt uses the same three references in this order: @Image1 your character sheet, @Image2 the bison sheet, @Image3 your selfie. If you're not a guy, change he and his in the prompts to fit you.

I turned the sound off. Even when I wrote "no music" in the prompt, Seedance kept adding music to the clips, so I generated the picture only and added the wind and the bison sounds myself.

Step 4: Put it together

Cut each clip down to its best two to four seconds and open on the silhouette crossing the sun. Lay a slow, nostalgic song over it, and keep a little wind and the bison's breathing underneath so it never feels silent. End on the bison gliding away into the sun and hold the last frame.

What made it look real
Expose for the bright sky, not for you. The sun sits behind you and washes part of the frame, you sit a little darker than the sky, and everything shares the same haze. If you're crisp and evenly lit against a soft sky, it looks like a green screen.
Give every camera a real place to be: a long lens from far away, a camera fixed to the saddle, or a person flying alongside. A camera floating a few metres from you in open sky is what makes it feel fake.
Make the clouds three-dimensional: tall towers, layers, gaps and a little colour in the shadows. A flat white sea of cloud looks like a render.
Say how big the bison is. Unless you tell it "longer than a bus, head wider than a car", it comes out looking like a baby.
A sky bison flies head-first, glides level without rocking, and its flat tail beats only up and down. Writing those three things fixed most of the weird motion.
Never write "glowing" for anything except the sun. One "glowing" in my prompt made the fur light up like magic.
