A 1930s luxury train crossing the Alps, a giant version of me leaning into the window, and a camera that rushes back down the whole carriage. All of it was shot at home, on a tripod, with one table and one lamp. This is the full breakdown: every step, every asset, every prompt.




I wanted a scene on a moving train in the mountains. A real version of that shot means a location, permits, a period carriage, a crew and a budget I didn't have. What I did have was a corner of my living room, a teacup and Higgsfield Genjutsu.

The principle is simple: shoot the performance, build the world around it. I film the acting myself, and Genjutsu Motion Transfer keeps every head turn, glance and line exactly as I performed it while it replaces the room, the costume and the scale.

The workflow has six steps:

Shoot two simple takes at home.
Build the empty train carriage as a location plate.
Make a character sheet that locks my look.
Turn one frame of my footage into the train.
Bring it to life with Genjutsu Motion Transfer.
Finish with colour and sound.

Copy any prompt below and adapt it to your own scene.

Step 1: Shoot two takes at home

Genjutsu only changes the world. Your timing, your hands and your face come from what you film, so this step decides the quality of everything after it.

Take 1, seated. I sit at a small table, stir a cup in small circles at chest height, tilt my head, glance to the side and say, dry and flat: "All of this?"

Take 2, the giant. A close-up of my face against a plain light wall. I lean in from the side as if toward a window, look down to where the small me would sit and say: "Shot at home." Then I snap my eyes straight into the lens and hold still for three seconds.

What made the footage work:

Camera: locked tripod, frontal, level, lens at eye height, subject dead center. Focus, exposure and white balance locked. 4K at 25 or 30 fps; if you shoot Log, export Rec.709 before uploading.
Set: a plain, uncluttered wall, the table centered in frame, a cup, saucer and spoon, tape marks for chair, table and tripod. The same wardrobe in both takes.
Light: a cool soft source from behind and above, which becomes the window. Warm practicals on both sides, which become the wall sconces. Soft front fill.
Sound: a lav mic, a clap for sync, 30 seconds of room tone per setup.
Step 2: Build the empty carriage

The location plate is an empty frame of the world, with no people in it. It becomes the location reference in Genjutsu and the world my frame gets rebuilt into. I made it in Seedream 5.0 Pro at 16:9 and 2K, keeping the center safe for a 9:16 crop.

Three things sell it: strict symmetry, a centered window, and real, worn materials. Generate two to four variants and keep the one with the cleanest symmetry and the most believable mountains.

SCENE: A still frame from a feature film: interior of an empty 1930s luxury Alpine train dining compartment, no people. A real practical set photographed on location, not a render.

COMPOSITION: wide, perfectly symmetrical, frontal, level camera at seated eye height. A large window with rounded corners centered on the back wall; an empty burgundy velvet banquette beneath it; a small walnut table with brass edge in the foreground center with a white porcelain cup and saucer. Walnut panelling on both sides, brass sconces with cream shades mirrored left and right, burgundy velvet curtains tied back on both sides of the window.

MOUNTAINS: real Swiss Alps photographed through old glass: exposed dark rock faces and ridges between wind-packed snow, distant peaks paler and bluer from atmospheric perspective, thin haze in the valleys, a low band of cloud catching on one ridge, overcast soft winter light. The glass carries faint smudges, a thin film of condensation in the lower corners and a soft reflection of the sconces.

CARRIAGE: real aged materials: walnut veneer with uneven varnish, hairline cracks and rubbed edges, tarnished brass with dull patches, crushed velvet with flattened nap where people sat, slightly faded curtain folds, a chip on the saucer rim, worn table edge. Imperfect real-world geometry, tiny asymmetries.

LIGHT: overcast daylight from the window as the key, soft and cool; warm tungsten sconces as weak practicals; low-key, deep soft shadows, highlights rolling off smoothly, snow holds detail.

LOOK: fine natural film grain, true-to-life slightly desaturated colour, warm walnut and burgundy against cold grey-blue snow. Photoreal, not CGI, no text, no logos.




Step 3: Lock your look with a character sheet

The character sheet keeps my face, hair and tattoos identical in both the small and the giant version. I made it in Nano Banana Pro at 16:9, with four to eight clear photos of myself as references.

The grey background matters: it keeps the light neutral, so nothing in the sheet fights the train's own light later. Swap the character and outfit lines for your own.

Photorealistic character reference sheet of the young woman from the reference photos, on a plain seamless light-grey studio background, no scene, no props, no text, no labels. Clean, even, soft studio lighting with no harsh shadows, so every detail is clearly readable. Consistent identity, proportions and outfit across every view.

Layout: the top row shows four full-body views, evenly spaced and the same scale: front view, three-quarter view, side profile, and back view. Warm brown eyes, strong full dark eyebrows, a straight defined nose with a slight bridge, full natural lips, an oval face with defined cheekbones. Light-olive skin with a natural rosy flush across the cheeks and nose, a few faint freckles, natural matte skin texture with visible pores, minimal bare makeup, a calm, slightly knowing expression, no glasses. The train costume is a separate reference: a brown tweed 1930s jacket, an ivory silk blouse with a soft tie and a pearl necklace. A flat-lay or a photo of the real pieces both work.

Step 4: Turn one frame into the train

I grabbed one sharp frame from take 1, the exact moment I wanted the shot to open on, and rebuilt only the world and the costume around me in Seedream 5.0 Pro. My pose, framing and face stay as filmed. The references are my frame, the location plate, the character sheet and the costume.

Edit the first reference image, a frame from my clip. Keep me exactly as I am: same pose, same position and size in frame, same head tilt, same face and expression, same hands, the cup and spoon in the same place. The camera framing does not change.

Replace the room with the 1930s luxury Alpine train compartment from the location reference: walnut panelling, brass sconces mirrored left and right, a burgundy velvet banquette directly behind me, a large centered window with snowy Alps, burgundy curtains tied back. Put a small walnut table with a brass edge under my cup.

Dress me in the costume reference: brown tweed 1930s jacket, ivory silk blouse with a soft tie, pearl necklace. Keep my identity from the character sheet: espresso-black bob with a soft fringe, warm brown eyes, light-olive skin.

Light it like the plate: overcast cool daylight from the window behind me rimming my hair, weak warm sconces on both sides, soft natural shadows. Real skin with visible pores, matte, natural flush.

Photoreal live-action film still, real set, fine natural film grain, true-to-life slightly desaturated colour. Not CGI, no text.

Before moving on, check four things: the face is still yours, the hands have five fingers, the cup sits exactly where it was, and the window is centered behind you.

A few rules made the instruction work. Name what each reference controls. Timecode the action, one beat per line. Say "frame for frame" and which seconds of each clip to copy. Describe real things: real skin, a real lens, real worn materials. End with locks that restate what must not drift.

SCENE CONTEXT
A young woman sits alone in a 1930s luxury train compartment crossing the Alps, stirring tea. As the camera pulls back, a giant version of her leans into the window from outside and speaks. An impossible scale reveal, photographed for real, played straight.

ACTIVE REFERENCES (each 100% matches its reference)
@image1: location. Walnut panelling, mirrored brass sconces, burgundy velvet banquette, large centered window with snowy Alps.
@image2: identity. Slim young woman, jet-black chin-length bob, blunt full fringe, dark brown eyes, light-olive skin.
@image3: costume. Brown tweed 1930s jacket, ivory silk blouse with soft tie, pearl necklace.
@video1: the seated woman moves exactly as in @video1 1.0s-4.0s, real time, frame for frame: head tilt, half-lowered eyes, dry smirk, glance to frame-right, right hand stirring in small circles, left hand holding the cup at chest height, lips.
@video2: the giant moves exactly as in @video2, real time, frame for frame: lean-in from frame-left, head and shoulder angle, expression, lips.
Only room, clothes, scale and colour change.

FIRST FRAME
Close-up of the seated woman's face, dead-center, frontal, pose of @video1 at 1.0s. No giant yet.

FORMAT MODE
One continuous shot, the camera does not cut on its own.

OPTICS
18° close-up, widening smoothly to 47° by 3.0s, held until 6.0s, then fast to a 107° ultra-wide by 7.5s, held. Real spherical lens, shallow depth of field in the close-up, tack-sharp eyes, deep focus in the final wide.

CAMERA
Center axis, level, her eye height. Smooth zoom-out 0.0s-3.0s, held. At 6.0s a fast decisive pull-out straight back down the center aisle at 15 km/h, the full length of the carriage, settling at the far end at 7.5s, held. Rides with the carriage sway.

ACTION
0.0s-3.0s: she performs @video1, stirring, and says dry and flat: "All of this?"
3.0s-5.0s: the giant performs @video2, leaning into the window from frame-left outside, her face filling the whole window, looking down at the small woman, and says dry and flat: "Shot at home." The small woman's eyes rise to the window.
5.0s: on the end of "home", both snap their eyes into the lens together, deadpan, mouths closed.
5.0s-6.0s: both stare into the lens, perfectly still.
6.0s-7.5s: the long pull-out: empty velvet booths, lamp-lit tables, sconces and windows slide past on both sides. The compartment shrinks to the center at the far end, the small woman at her table, the giant face filling her window, both staring down the aisle into the lens.
7.5s-9.0s: held symmetrical ultra-wide from the far end, ceiling to floor. Both still, eyes on the lens, one slow blink at most.

PERFORMANCE / SKIN
Real human skin on both faces: visible pores, fine peach fuzz, uneven tone, tiny imperfections, matte. Living eyes with moist catch-lights, individual hair strands. On the giant face every pore and eyelash shows at its huge scale.

TRAIN MOTION
60 km/h. Steady rail sway, small bumps at the joints; cup and curtains tremble. Outside, landscape slides frame-right to frame-left: far peaks drift slowly, pines and telegraph poles rush past with real parallax.

LIGHTING
Overcast 6500K daylight through the windows as key; weak 3000K tungsten sconces. Shadows of passing trees and poles sweep across interior and faces. Giant face lit by the same daylight, faint sconce reflections on the glass. Honest low-key exposure, protected highlights.

MATERIALS
Real walnut grain, worn velvet pile, brass with fingerprints and patina, slightly smudged glass.

STYLE
Photoreal live-action footage from a real cinema camera, fine natural film grain, true-to-life slightly desaturated colour.

POSITIVE LOCKS
Live-action, real people, real set. Motion from @video1 and @video2 only. Same person from @image2 in costume from @image3. Train moving the whole shot. From 5.0s both look into the lens to the end.
