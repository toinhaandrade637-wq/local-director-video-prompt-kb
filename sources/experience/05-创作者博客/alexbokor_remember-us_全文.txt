Every zombie movie has the same scene. Someone you love turns. Someone has to make the call. It usually ends with a gunshot and a sad piano.




I wanted to flip it.




You've probably seen the "hardest goodbye" trend: a survivor in a ruined city aims at the dog they love, can't do it, and the memories hit. It's a great format. So I asked one question: what if the dog survived, and I was the one who turned?




Meet Bubu. A tiny chihuahua who walks on two legs, wears a jacket two sizes too big and carries a rifle almost as tall as he is. He's been alone for a long time. Then he finds me.




The rest of the story fits in one sentence: he can't pull the trigger, so he lets one old photo do the talking.




Everything was made in Higgsfield. Stills with Nano Banana Pro and Seedream 5 Pro, motion with Seedance 2.5. The music is "Light Waltz" by Mattia Vlad Morleo, licensed through Artlist. Below are the three exact video prompts behind it, plus the thinking behind each one. Steal them, remix them, make your own goodbye.




How to use them: every prompt lists its references as @image1, @image2 and so on. Attach your images in exactly that order, or your dog ends up with your face.




Part 1 — The present (25s)

This is the heartbreak half, and the hardest one to get right.




The first versions moved way too fast. A dog drops a gun, throws a photo, done in three seconds. It felt like nothing. So I slowed everything down and gave each emotional beat four or five seconds to breathe: the aim, the shaking barrel, the moment his paws give up, the kneel. One shot comes straight from the original trend: the wide frame where the survivor looks tiny between the buildings while laying the weapon down. Nothing sells loneliness like a small figure in a huge, dead space.




Two lighting lessons came out of this. Backlighting the zombie looked epic, but you couldn't see my face, and the face is the whole point. Moving the sun to the side fixed it: half warm, half shadow on both of them. The photo moment needed the most time. He takes it out, looks at it, strokes our faces with his bandaged paw, and a tear lands on the print. That's the shot people remember.




Last detail: the zombie doesn't try to catch the photo. He just lets it fall at his feet. That was the most important change in the whole prompt.

Part 2 — The flashback (15s)

The flashback is the opposite of everything above: fast, bright and loud with waves and laughter.




It starts exactly where Part 1 ends. The photo lies on the asphalt and the camera dives into it. No glow, no magic dissolve, just a hard match cut straight into the moment the photo was taken, already in motion. One frame it's a picture, the next it's a summer afternoon.




The photo itself is the running gag. We're both doing the bodybuilder pose in matching fruit shirts. I lose it and start laughing; Bubu stays dead serious. After that, the memories hit every second and a half: running in the foam, a disc catch, lemonade at the beach bar. Then the edit finally slows down for one long hug at the waterline. The cuts are quick on purpose. Happy memories never come back in order. They come back in flashes.

Part 3 — The ice cream (4s)

Every edit needs a little room to breathe, and this one needed four extra seconds to sit on the music. So I made the messiest shot I could think of: one giant melting ice cream, one tiny greedy chihuahua.




He goes in face first, comes out covered in vanilla and strawberry, and keeps the exact same dead-serious face he has in the photo. Same joke, new costume. The only thing I changed after the first try was my reaction. Not a big laugh, just a quiet, warm smile, the way you look at someone you already know you're going to miss.
