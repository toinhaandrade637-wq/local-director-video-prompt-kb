By Valentin (Fotachu) · Founder, Fotachu Studios

I make animated films from start to finish using generative tools. That covers the characters, the worlds, the key art, the storyboards, the shots and the final cut. I run Fotachu Studios from Málaga, Spain. I also work with the Higgsfield team every day as a Creator Partner, one of the five official Bug Hunters, and a Discord moderator.

2026 has been my biggest year so far. Here's what happened.


The wins
Higgsfield API Challenge: winner, $50K (September 2026)

My entry was FotaGen, a complete AI creation app built on the Higgsfield API. I didn't want to make a demo. I wanted to build a real product that creators could open and use. It's a full app around the platform's models, designed for how people actually create, on both phone and desktop. It won the $50,000 top prize.

Higgsfield Cinema Studio V4 Challenge: winner

This was a second 2026 win, and it came from Cinema Studio, the tool I use more than any other.

Higgsfield Global Film Festival: Top 50 of 67,223 films, plus a production grant (August 2026)

My film One Day With Mr Otter made the shortlist of 50 out of 67,223 entries. The jury was Ed Catmull, Phedon Papamichael, Paul W. S. Anderson and Alex Proyas. The Higgsfield team also gave the film a production grant.

Higgsfield X Article Challenge: winner, $5,000 (February 2026)

Higgsfield's reply to it is still one of my favorite comments: "The passion in this one is real. Love seeing anime and AI come together like this!"

That makes three Higgsfield challenge wins in one year, plus the festival shortlist.

Case study: One Day With Mr Otter

One Day With Mr Otter is a 22-minute spy comedy seen through a child's imagination. I wrote, storyboarded, animated and edited it alone in 15 days.

Runtime: 22:09
Generations: 722
Assets: 769
Time: 15 days, about 50–60 hours of work
Made on a phone: about three quarters of it, in Cinema Studio
Cost: about $2K, compared with an estimated $30M for a live-action version

To me the phone figure matters most. A 22-minute feature that a festival shortlists, mostly made on a phone, shows how far the tools have come.

How I work

Every project uses the same pipeline:

Visual development: characters, creatures, worlds, key art, and color and lighting direction.
Direction: story, script, storyboards, shot design, camera language and blocking.
AI production: pipelines across several models, locking style and identity so characters stay on-model from shot to shot, plus motion control and lipsync.
Edit and sound: pacing, music and the final cut.

I build too. I use Python, HTML and web apps, and I integrate the Higgsfield API. FotaGen came from there.
