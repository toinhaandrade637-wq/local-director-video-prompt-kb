OBJECT SWAP — RECAST THE HEROINE'S FACE AND HAIR ONLY




TARGET: the single recurring young woman who appears in every era of this

vertical video — the one taking the selfies (1980 boardwalk, 1983 launch

field, 1860 street, 1972 helicopter, 1955 backlot, 1985 disco) and also seen

in third person as the basketball player in the red #7 jersey (1990), from

behind in the school hallway (2000) and in the diner booth in hair rollers

(1970). She is the same person throughout; every appearance of her is the

target. No one else is.




REPLACE WITH: the face and hair of the woman in Image 1.




FACE — her exact facial identity from Image 1: face shape, eyes, brows, nose,

lips, skin texture, beauty marks. Fully recognizable in every angle the

footage shows — frontal wide-angle selfie, 3/4, profile, back of the head —

and identical across every era and every cut. The original performance stays

and the new face performs it: wide-eyed surprise, laughing, open-mouthed

excitement, covering the mouth with a hand, shocked pointing, sipping through

a straw. Each scene's period makeup is kept and applied to her features:

red lipstick and glam in the 1980 and 1985 scenes, a natural fresh face in

all the others.




HAIR — color, texture and length from Image 1: straight, fine, medium

ash-brown, shoulder-length, side-swept fringe, face-framing strands. It

replaces the original long dark waist-length hair in every scene — no long

dark hair remains anywhere. Styling follows each scene's props, done with

her hair: 1980 — gathered up in the colorful scrunchie, fringe out; 1990 —

pulled back under the red headband; 1970 — wound onto the pink and purple

rollers; everywhere else worn down, moving with the head, the wind and the

handheld camera. In the 2000 hallway shot from behind her hair now ends at

the shoulders, and the white shirt and green backpack straps continue

naturally where the long hair used to be.




HEAD ACCESSORIES STAY AS FILMED and sit correctly on the new face and hair:

the yellow-frame sunglasses (1980), the hoop earrings (1980), the aviation

headset and microphone (1972), the red headband (1990), the hair rollers

(1970).




KEEP EXACTLY AS FILMED — do not touch:

  in the white dress and her assistant, the students in the hallway, the

  disco crowd, the diner patrons

hour backlight (1980, 1860), soft overcast daylight (1983, 1955), cockpit

daylight (1972), arena spotlights (1990), fluorescent hallway (2000),

colored disco light with sequin bounce (1985), warm window sun in the diner

(1970). Same wide-angle distortion, focus and motion blur as the original

face in each shot; no sharp cut-out edges, no floating face.
