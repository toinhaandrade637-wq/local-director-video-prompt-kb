MOTION IS MANDATORY — READ THIS FIRST, IT OVERRIDES EVERYTHING




This is a LIVE FILMED SHOT, not a still photograph with a moving character on it. The entire environment is in continuous visible motion for all 20 seconds. If any of the four things below are static, the shot has failed.




1. CLOUDS MOVE. The whole cloud sea drifts steadily and visibly from screen-right to screen-left across the whole 20 seconds. This must be measurable: a given cloud tower that starts at x 90% has travelled to about x 60% by the end. Nearer cloud moves faster, far cloud slower, giving real parallax between layers. Cloud tops swell and boil, edges curl over and shear off, wisps tear away and dissipate, gaps open and close. The pink-lilac streaks in the upper sky also drift. NOT a static matte painting. NOT a frozen backdrop. NOT a still sky with only the character animated.




2. THE FERRIS WHEEL TURNS. It rotates continuously from frame one to frame last — a slow, heavy, unmistakable rotation. Measurable: a cabin that starts at the bottom of the wheel has climbed to roughly the three o'clock position by the end. Cabins are visibly travelling around the rim, each rocking slightly on its pivot as it comes over the top. It never stops, never stutters, never sits still for a single frame.




3. WIND BLOWS THROUGH EVERYTHING. A constant breeze from screen-right toward screen-left for all 20 seconds. It is visible at all times in: grass rippling in slow travelling waves rolling across the clifftop, blades bending and springing back; pink flower heads nodding and shivering; her hair lifting and streaming to screen-left; her skirt pleats fluttering and pressing to her legs; her jacket collar stirring; the empty swing chains drifting a few degrees and settling; loose petals and seed fluff lifting off the meadow and travelling left. The grass is NEVER a frozen carpet.




4. BUBBLES FLOAT AND TRAVEL. The soap bubbles are constantly moving — rising, drifting sideways on the breeze, wobbling as their surfaces flex, spinning slowly, occasionally popping and vanishing while new ones drift in. Measurable: a bubble in the foreground crosses a large part of the frame during the shot. Each moves on its own path at its own speed, not as a locked group. They are NEVER hovering in place like pinned dots.




Everything above runs continuously and simultaneously, independent of what Sati is doing, from 0.0s to 20.0s.




COMPOSITION — MATCHES THE UPLOADED LOCATION REFERENCE




Frame layout:

LEFT EDGE to x 44% — a rounded grass clifftop thick with pink wildflowers, sloping up from the lower left, ending at a sheer weathered limestone brink with exposed roots trailing down the rock face. The rock drops away into cloud below.

THE SWING SITS AT x 34%. A single dusty-pink shell-backed seat with a fluted scallop backrest and a slim curved armrest hoop, hanging on two thin bright metal chains running straight up and out of the top of frame. Seat at x 34%, y 42%, hovering in open air just past and above the cliff lip, seen from the side, facing screen-right.

x 44% to x 100% — cloud, sky and the fish. A white-gold cumulus sea below, layered banks receding into warm haze, sunset sky above with pink-lilac cloud streaks and a warm glow at the horizon on the right.

Ferris wheel at x 76%, y 58%, pale and skeletal with small pastel-colored cabins, standing among the cloud tops, half-veiled in haze.

Sun low behind the cloud to the right, warm gold light raking right to left.

Bubbles drift through foreground and midground at varied sizes, thickest along the lower left over the flowers. Drifting sparkles and pink petals in the air.




This layout is locked. The frame never recenters, never lets land into the right side, never puts the swing in the middle.

There is no tram, no cat and no lambs anywhere in this film.




@fish — ONE FISH SO VAST IT FILLS THE ENTIRE SKY

Exactly ONE goldfish, matching the fish reference 100%: bright orange-red back fading to pale silver-white belly, glossy overlapping scales, long flowing translucent white-and-orange fins, wide forked veil tail, round dark eye with a gold ring, small mouth.




ITS SIZE IS THE WHOLE POINT — IT IS SKY-FILLING.

This is not a big fish. It is a fish the size of a mountain range, a creature so enormous that once it is out from behind the cliff it FILLS THE ENTIRE SKY of the shot. Its body spans the full width of the frame — head off one edge, tail off the other — and its height fills the frame from top edge to the cloud sea below. There is no open sky left behind it. Everything from x 44% to x 100%, top of frame to bottom, becomes fish and the cloud swirling around it.

Reference points that must read on screen: the ferris wheel is a tiny toy against its flank, smaller than a single one of its scales. Individual scales are the size of buildings. Its eye alone is larger than the whole cliff. Its dorsal fin rises off the top of the frame entirely. Its pectoral fin is longer than the cliff is tall. The cliff, the swing and Sati are specks in the corner of the frame in front of it.

Because it is so vast, the camera can never contain all of it at once — it is cropped by the frame edges, and we only ever see a section of a much larger animal continuing beyond the picture. That cropping is what sells the scale.

It never shrinks, never reads as merely large, never fits comfortably inside the frame. If the whole fish fits in shot with room around it, it is far too small.




THE EMERGENCE AND THE SWIM.

At 0.0s its immense head is already coming out from behind the cliff mass in the background — already so big that the head alone is many times the size of the cliff. Over the first six seconds the body slides endlessly out from behind the rock, more and more of it filling the sky, until by 6.0s the frame's entire right side is a wall of orange flank, scales and translucent fin. The long veil tail is still emerging even later, sweeping into frame behind it.

It swims steadily screen-left to screen-right, a slow, immense, unstoppable procession across the sky. Because of its size the motion is majestic and unhurried, but it still visibly advances — landmarks on its body, like the great pectoral fin or the dark eye, travel measurably across the frame during the shot.

Path: head clears the cliff at x 42% at 0.0s; sky fully occupied by 6.0s; the eye passes roughly x 70% around 11.0s; by 20.0s the head has long left the right edge and the mid-body and the beginning of the tail fill the frame. Direction never reverses, it never turns toward camera, always seen in side profile.

It stays behind the cliff plane in depth. It never crosses in front of the cliff, the swing or Sati, never enters the left third of frame where the land is. Sati never notices it and never reacts — she swings on obliviously with a god-sized animal filling the sky behind her.




IT SWIMS THROUGH THE CLOUDS. Entire cloud banks break apart against its head and stream along its flanks like surf. Its belly ploughs through the cloud sea, throwing up huge slow curtains of vapor. Cloud pours over its back and off its dorsal fin. Long ragged wisps peel off its trailing fins in ribbons and churn in a vast wake behind it. The cloud layer is visibly disturbed everywhere the fish touches it.

Its body undulates in a slow colossal lateral S-wave running head to tail — one full sweep taking eight to ten seconds because of its mass. The veil tail sweeps side to side with tremendous force, displacing enormous volumes of cloud. The translucent fins ripple and trail with heavy delay, glowing amber where the low sun passes through them. Building-sized scales flash slow speculars as it rolls slightly.







CAMERA

The camera floats in open air over the cloud sea, off the cliff, roughly 30 meters from the swing, at about the height of the seat — the exact viewpoint of the location reference. It looks back toward the cliff, perpendicular to the swing's plane of travel, so everything reads in PURE PROFILE: the seat edge-on, Sati's side view, the cliff brink as a hard edge line, the arc running flat left-to-right across the screen.

WIDE SHOT. Sati is a small distant figure, roughly 6 to 8 percent of frame height. She never grows, never gets closer, never dominates the frame, never crosses into the right half.

The camera does not move. No pan, no follow, no push, no crane, no drone drift, no rotation off the profile. It never follows the fish and never pulls back to fit it in. Only the faintest hover-breath. One angle, one shot size, 20 seconds, no cuts.




REFERENCES

@location — the cliff-and-cloud-sea image. Source of truth for composition, terrain, grass and flowers, rock and root texture, cloud look, swing design, ferris wheel design, bubbles, sparkles and light direction.

@fish — the goldfish image. Matches 100% for coloring, scale pattern, fin and tail shape, translucency and profile silhouette — only at mountain scale.

@girl — woman early twenties, slim, short black hair with blunt fringe, tortoiseshell sunglasses pushed up on her head, green zip track jacket with blue side stripes and blue collar and cuffs, pink-and-blue checked pleated mini skirt, dark green knee socks, black loafers. 100% matches the reference. At this distance she reads by silhouette, posture and her green-and-pink colors, not by facial detail.




SCALE

Adult woman of about 1.65 meters with correct proportions relative to the seat: hips fit the 55 cm seat with room to spare, shoulders wider than the backrest, head above it, feet hanging naturally below. She is small because the camera is far away, not because she is miniature. The cliff below her is many times her height.

The fish dwarfs everything: the cliff, the ferris wheel, the cloud towers, the woman. It is the largest thing in the world of this shot by an enormous margin.




SWING GEOMETRY — DO NOT DISTORT

One rigid seat exactly as in the location reference: dusty pink, fluted shell backrest, flat seat pan, slim curved metal armrest hoop, about 55 cm wide, for one adult.

Two thin bright metal chains run from the seat straight up and out of the top of frame, catching small specular glints. Perfectly straight, thin and taut. They never bend, curve, warp, stretch, multiply, cross, sag unevenly or fray.

The seat stays rigid throughout — never melts, bends, changes shape or size or color, never becomes a plank or a tire.

The seat faces screen-right, out over the cloud sea. Sati faces screen-right. The seat never rotates to face camera.




ACTION — 20 SECONDS, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE




0.0s to 7.0s — she walks in from the left, the leviathan fills the sky behind her.

First frame already has her walking, the fish's vast head already emerging, the clouds already drifting, the wheel already turning, the grass already rippling and the bubbles already travelling. No empty opening, no still opening frame.

She enters at x 4%, y 52%, tiny, in profile, mid-stride, walking left to right up over the flowering clifftop toward the swing, leaning slightly into the wind. Real weight in the walk — steady stride, arms swinging, hair streaming to screen-left, skirt fluttering, flower heads brushing her legs and springing back.

Behind her, from 0.0s to 6.0s, the fish's endless body slides out from behind the rock and progressively fills the entire sky.

At 5.8s she reaches the swing and keeps going, passing behind it and stepping around to its right-hand side, ending at x 38%, y 50% on the brink to the right of the seat.

At 6.4s she stops there, takes the near chain in one hand, steadies it as it sways in the wind, and looks at the seat and then out screen-right.




7.0s to 10.0s — she sits from the right, and almost falls.

SHE MOUNTS THE SWING FROM ITS RIGHT-HAND SIDE. Standing to the right of the seat, she turns to face screen-right, reaches back for both chains and lowers herself onto the seat sideways from the right, sliding across it.

At 7.6s her weight lands and the swing kicks away from her — the seat swings out screen-right under the load while her feet are still on the rock.

At 8.0s she is caught between the two: hips on the moving seat, feet dragging off the brink, nothing under her. Her body pitches backward and to the right over the void, one arm flies wide, her free hand grabs for the far chain and misses, her legs kick out over open air, skirt flaring in the wind. In profile it reads instantly — her silhouette hanging past the edge line with only cloud and the vast flank beyond.

At 8.7s her hand finds the chain and locks on. Both chains snap taut under her full weight and the seat lurches hard, swinging as one rigid object. She hauls her torso upright and settles properly onto the seat at 9.3s. She does not fall. She does not go over.

By 9.8s she is seated, both hands on the chains, knees and ankles pressed together, feet hanging naturally below the seat, breathing hard, hair whipping across her face, the swing still rocking from the scare.




10.0s to 20.0s — the swing.

At exactly 10.0s she pushes off and begins to swing, and from that second to the last frame she is in continuous pendulum motion, gaining height every pass. Ten full seconds of swinging.

The forward arc carries her screen-right out over the cloud sea, reaching roughly x 50% at her widest; the back arc brings her screen-left over the clifftop. She drives with both legs together — legs swinging out straight with toes pointed at the forward peak, both knees folding back under the seat at the rear peak, torso leaning back then forward. Knees and ankles stay pressed together the whole time; legs never spread, never scissor.

Side-on, the full pendulum geometry reads: the chain line swinging through a widening angle from its vertical at x 34%, the seat tilting with the arc, only drifting cloud below her feet. Hair and skirt stream behind her direction of travel and settle at each reversal. She cuts through drifting bubbles and petals as she arcs.

Behind her the sky-filling fish sails on, its flank a moving wall of orange and silver across the entire right side of the picture, cloud breaking and streaming along it, the tiny ferris wheel turning in front of its immensity.

Even at her widest forward reach she stays in the left half of frame.

By 20.0s she is still climbing, a tiny speck of green and pink against a sky made entirely of fish. Ends mid-arc, still in motion. No stop, no settle, no freeze.




ATMOSPHERE

The air is visible and full and in motion. Warm low light rakes through it catching fine dust, pollen, seed fluff and pink petals blown left across the frame, lighting up as bright specks against the backlight, with lens-catching sparkles along the flowered edge and on the chains. All of this particulate drifts continuously, never suspended. Thin haze layers the depth so the far cloud banks and the ferris wheel sit behind a soft veil, separated from the near cliff. The fish's sheer size means its far end fades into that haze, its near flank sharper — atmospheric perspective across a single creature, which sells the scale.




OPTICS

84° diagonal field of view, wide rectilinear anamorphic, 35mm film. Anamorphic: horizontally oval bokeh on the bubbles and sparkles, gentle horizontal stretch and softening toward the frame edges with the center sharp, soft horizontal streak flare when the low sun clips the lens. Soft immediate foreground of grass, flowers and bubbles, with the cliff, swing and fish holding detail. Real 35mm grain, gentle halation around the sun and bright cloud tops, warm pastel color response.

Photorealistic. Real rock, real roots, real grass, real fabric, real wet-looking scales and translucent fins, real atmospheric depth. Not illustration, not stylized cartoon.

Rectilinear: straight lines straight, horizon flat and level, chain lines dead straight. No fisheye, no barrel curve, no bulging center, no circular image, no black lens border, no vignetted corners.







PHYSICS

Walking has real weight: heel contact, weight transfer, hip shift, toe push-off, grass compressing and springing back.

The near-fall has real consequence: the unweighted seat swings away the instant she puts weight on it from the right, her center of gravity crosses the edge line, arms windmill, the chains take her full weight with a hard jolt and lurch under load.

The swing is a true pendulum: momentary weightlessness and briefly slack chains at each peak, maximum speed and full tension at the bottom, body compressing into the seat. Her leg drive adds energy so each cycle climbs higher and the arc angle visibly widens. Hair, skirt pleats and jacket hem lag her body and settle after each direction change. Chain links shift, rattle and click at every load reversal. The seat carries her weight as a rigid object and tilts with the arc rather than deforming.

The fish moves with aquatic physics at mountain mass: an enormously slow travelling S-wave head to tail taking eight to ten seconds per sweep, the veil tail sweeping laterally on each long beat and driving the body forward, fins lagging far behind and rippling with heavy delay. Everything about it is slow, heavy and inevitable — never twitchy, never quick. Entire cloud banks are displaced around its head and churn closed in a vast turbulent wake.




LIGHTING

Single low sun behind the cloud layer to the right, warm gold, raking across the frame toward screen-left. Cloud tops rimmed hot and glowing, and those rim highlights shift and re-form continuously as the cloud drifts. The grass edge and flower heads catch a bright warm rim. Sati is rim-lit in profile: hot edge along her face, chest, arms and the front of her legs, back side falling into warm shadow, chains catching bright specular glints.

The fish is backlit and immense: its translucent fins and tail glow amber where the sun passes through them, its orange back rimmed hot along the spine, its pale belly picking up soft bounce from the cloud sea below, building-sized scales flashing slow speculars. Its bulk throws a huge soft shadow across the cloud layer and dims the light on the cliff whenever the thickest part of its body crosses the sun. Bubbles flash rainbow speculars as they turn. Highlights bloom and halate on film. Shadows stay warm and open, never crushed to black. Exposed for the sky and her rim. No hard front key, no studio fill, no cold light.




AUDIO

No music. No score, no soundtrack, no instrumental bed, no ambient pads, no drones, no strings, no piano, no swells, no risers, no percussion, no melody at any point. Silence is preferred over any musical element.

No dialogue. No voice, no words, no gasp, no scream, no narration, no offscreen voices. No subtitles, no on-screen text.

Diegetic sound only, distant and airy: a steady wind through open air rising and falling, grass and flowers rustling, far-off footsteps, the chains snapping taut and lurching, chain links creaking and rattling at each load reversal, fabric fluttering, a faint low mechanical hum from the distant ferris wheel, and a deep subsonic rush of displaced air on each slow sweep of the fish's tail.




POSITIVE LOCKS

THE FISH FILLS THE ENTIRE SKY. Its body spans the full frame width and the full frame height on the right side, cropped by the frame edges, continuing beyond the picture in both directions. The ferris wheel is a toy against its flank; a single scale is the size of a building; its eye is bigger than the cliff. If the whole fish fits in shot, it is far too small.

Exactly ONE fish. It emerges from behind the cliff over the first six seconds and swims left to right across the sky for the rest of the film, slow and inevitable, never reversing, never turning toward camera, always in side profile, always behind the cliff plane, never crossing in front of Sati.

THE CLOUDS ARE MOVING in every frame — visibly drifting right to left with parallax by depth, building, curling, shearing, and breaking against the fish. Never a frozen sky.

THE FERRIS WHEEL IS TURNING in every frame — slow, continuous, cabins visibly travelling around the rim. Never frozen.

THE WIND IS BLOWING in every frame — grass rippling in waves, flowers nodding, hair streaming, skirt fluttering, chains drifting, petals lifting. Never a still landscape.

THE BUBBLES ARE TRAVELLING in every frame — rising, drifting, wobbling, popping. Never hovering in place.

This is a live filmed shot, not a still image with one animated element.

Sati mounts the swing from its RIGHT-HAND SIDE. The near-fall happens as she sits: the seat swings out from under her, she hangs over the void for a beat, catches the chain and recovers. She does not fall.

She begins swinging at exactly 10.0s and swings continuously for the final ten seconds, gaining height every pass, still in motion in the last frame.

Composition matches the location reference: cliff and swing on the left. The swing stays at x 34%.

Camera floats over the cloud sea, side-on to the swing, pure profile, static. No pan, no push, no rotation, no pulling back to fit the fish in frame.

Sati is small — 6 to 8 percent of frame height. She never dominates the frame and never crosses into the right half.

One continuous take, 20 seconds, one camera position, one shot size. No cuts.

The swing is one rigid correctly proportioned seat on two straight chains. Never warps, bends, melts, stretches, duplicates or changes shape.

Knees and ankles pressed together while swinging. Legs never spread, never scissor, never straddle.

Photorealistic live-action film look.
