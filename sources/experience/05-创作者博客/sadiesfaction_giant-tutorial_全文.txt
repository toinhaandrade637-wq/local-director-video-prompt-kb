Sitting upright at 13.5 meters she towers far above the hedgerows and above the field trees; cows near her hip reach only her ankle.

She glances at the fish in her left fingers, turns her shoulders a little toward the water, and tosses it out with a loose, offhand flick of the wrist — the ordinary unstudied motion of someone tossing a pebble into a stream. No wind-up, no throwing stance, no drama, no follow-through pose. Her arm falls back to her side straight after. Because of her size, that small casual flick sends the fish sailing an enormous distance: it arcs high through the golden air as a small orange speck, clearly readable against the sky and hills as it travels right and down. The effortlessness of the gesture against the distance covered is itself the scale proof.

A brief orange flicker under the surface, then it is gone. Two cows on the near bank lift their heads at the sound, then go back to grazing. She stays where she is and watches; she never moves toward the water.
