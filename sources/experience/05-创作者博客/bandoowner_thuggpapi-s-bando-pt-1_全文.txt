Before you start

The humor depends on how the characters behave. Everyone goes about their business as if this is ordinary life. A man stands calmly with his arms crossed while his clothes burn. A father puts a durag on his newborn as tenderly as he would adjust a baby hat.

That means the prompt needs to describe both the unusual detail and the character's response. “Make it funny” leaves too much to chance.

Stage 1 — Prepare the references

To recreate this kind of video, start with a small collection of visual references. Four groups cover the main ingredients:

Characters and clothing: durags, white tank tops, heavy chains, oversized puffer jackets and wedding outfits. For the hospital scene, the father and baby's matching black durags are especially important.
Props: colorful gummies, electronic scales, a money counter, strapped bundles of cash, red children's tricycles and gold grillz.
Locations: a cramped workshop lined with plastic sheeting, a street outside a neighborhood shop, a church with stained-glass windows and a hospital room.
Overall look: a 4:3 frame, visible grain, soft detail, deep shadows and warm light. The aquarium introduces a saturated blue, while the hospital uses a cooler palette.

If you're animating a still image, make sure the visual joke is already present in that image. The aquarium should contain bundles of money. The adult riders should be sitting on tiny tricycles. The baby's waves should be clearly visible. Then the video prompt can focus on movement.

Stage 2 — Keep the prompt focused on action

For each scene, answer four questions: who's in the frame, what they're doing, where they are and how the camera moves. Describe the performance separately: casual, serious and free of exaggerated comic reactions.
