Before You Start

The core idea: text prompts are bad at camera. Every other part of a shot — faces, materials, light, physics, sound — a video model handles well, but "the camera dives from above, sweeps into the cabin, then drops behind the car" comes back different every single time. So take that job away from the prompt entirely. Block the move in 3D, render it flat and grey, and let the prompt spend all its authority on what the world looks like.




And you don't need to know Blender. Claude builds the blocking for you.




Step 1. Download Blender, it's free: https://www.blender.org/




Step 2. In Claude, open Settings, go to Connectors and hit Add.




Step 3. Search for Blender in the directory and connect it.

That's the whole setup. From here you just chat: describe the scene, the camera and the cuts you want, and Claude builds it in your open Blender file while you watch it happen in the viewport. Ask for changes in plain language until the move feels right.




This is the brief I used for the chase:




Connect to my Blender and build a 30 second previz for a car chase. Grey boxes only, no textures, no lights. A city street between tall buildings, a sports car drifting down it, three police cars chasing it. Make the camera cinematic: start high above the street, dive down behind the car, then cut to a few different angles. Show me what you built so I can tell you what to change.




Two things drive the generation from here. The previz controls camera, staging, cut timing and blocking. The image references control everything visible: who is in the car, what the car is, which city, which light. Nothing crosses over.

Why grey and not detailed? Because anything you texture in the previz can bleed into the output. Grey boxes carry motion and space, and nothing else.

🚗 Step 3: The Hero Car Needs Every Angle

One photo of a car gives the model one angle, and everything it cannot see it invents. Cover every angle on one sheet and it has nothing left to make up. 🎯

One thing worth saying: this sheet is not AI generated. Those are real Porsche press photos of the actual car, and I built the sheet myself in Canva.




That was a deliberate choice. A generated car looks like a car, but it invents its own vents, its own wing, its own proportions, and then those invented details drift again in every shot of the film. Real photos of a real car give the model something exact to hold onto, so what ends up on screen is the actual Porsche, not the model's idea of one.




Same trick works for any product you need to keep accurate: don't generate the reference, build it from real photography.
