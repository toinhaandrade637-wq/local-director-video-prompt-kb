Same workout. A completely different world.

I filmed a normal gym clip, then used Higgsfield to place the performance in an alpine clearing beneath a giant stone arch. Here is the workflow, reusable prompts, and the fixes that made the result more convincing.

The aim: an impossible location that still feels like a casual phone recording.

1. Start with the full original video

Use your own vertical footage. Keep the entire sequence: the opening moment, setting the camera down, walking to the bar, every repetition, and the recovery at the end. My clip was roughly 22 seconds and included four pull-ups.

Check the uploaded clip's duration before generating. An early test ended after about seven seconds. A prompt asking for a longer video cannot restore action missing from the uploaded source or override the selected mode's duration limit.

For a new shoot, keep the camera still once it is down, leave your hands and bar visible, and avoid harsh coloured lights when possible. A mirror can be removed, but remember to remove its reflection of you as well.

Before — original gym footage. The full source clip, including all four pull-ups and the recovery. This is the real performance used for the environment swap.

2. Design the location using a frame from your clip

Take a clear frame after the camera has been set down. Use it as an image-edit reference in Higgsfield. Keep your body, the camera angle, ground height, and bar position as the anchors. Change the environment around them.

My scene uses a giant natural stone arch, a snowy mountain visible through it, and a waterfall on the left. The exercise frame stays simple: two slender wooden posts supporting a visible round metal bar.

4. Check these details before keeping a take
Looks pasted in? Compare the light direction and colour on the person with the landscape. Fix coloured gym spill and missing contact shadows.
Perspective feels wrong? Match the source camera height, upward angle, bar position and ground plane. The scenery must fit the camera.
Bar looks bulky or strange? Specify two slim posts and a single visible round exercise bar. Remove extra beams and hanging hardware.
Birds look fake? Ask for one pass and a permanent exit. If they still loop, remove them from the next attempt.
Video ends too soon? Check the source upload and duration settings, then inspect the last frame of the output. Count every repetition.
Hands slip through the bar? Review the jump, grip and every repetition at normal speed and frame by frame. Regenerate if contact fails.
5. Audio: what actually happened

The visual Genjutsu result in this workflow was silent, even when the prompt requested ambient audio. Later native-audio attempts did not give me a usable result. This is a visual tutorial; I cannot promise that adding sound instructions to the prompt will create a finished soundtrack.

Check your output's audio separately. Do not assume an outdoor-looking edit has outdoor sound, or that the original gym music has been removed.

Make it your own

Swap the alpine scene for a jungle clearing, a quiet castle valley, or another place you wish you could train. Keep the person and camera grounded in the shot, then let one extraordinary background feature do the work.

Save your selected reference, final prompt and full-length result together so you can repeat the look. Expect a few iterations; these prompts are starting points, not a guarantee of identical results.
