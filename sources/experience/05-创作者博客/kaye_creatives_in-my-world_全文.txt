3. The dachshund does not move. He looks at the knight, then raises his eyes to the duck and holds the look. One ear twitches. He returns to the board.

4. Slowly he lifts one front paw, slides a dark bishop along the diagonal with the side of the paw, sets the paw down, and presses his clock button with it. The duck leans forward over the board again and freezes, then dips its bill into its espresso cup without taking its eye off the position.

The table in the center, the dachshund on the left, the duck on the right, both fully visible on their chairs, the garden behind them and the brick townhouse soft in the background.




ACTING TASK (both are fully invested; the work happens in the eyes):

SCENE DIRECTION (shared, unspoken): this is a serious game between equals, and it gets full concentration.

DACHSHUND. MOTIVE: he lost the last one. GOAL: find the duck's plan before it lands. TACTIC: his eyes run along the lines of the board from piece to piece, calculating. After the duck's move he checks the duck's face: a trap or a mistake? Only when he has his answer does the paw move.

DUCK. MOTIVE: it knows it is the stronger player and wants to prove it again. GOAL: keep the plan hidden. TACTIC: it studies the wrong side of the board on purpose, one eye and then the other, before going for the knight. After the dachshund's reply it goes still and recalculates, the eye fixed on the bishop.

Real short fur, real feathers ruffled slightly by the breeze, the beret staying on.

WORKER 2. He stays one brick ahead. Eyes on his mortar, a glance at the brick going down, back to the mortar.

WORKER 3. He counts what is left on the deck with a look up, then goes back to the pallet.
