SCENE CONTEXT

Midday in a sunny harbour town. The hero and the sharpshooter friend, two young pirates, have just robbed a Marine warship moored at the town quay: the hero sneaked aboard and stole the Marines' treasure map while the sharpshooter friend kept lookout, and the hero left the recording running low on the quay to film the heist. The video starts with the hero sliding down the last metre of the warship's mooring rope with the rolled map in his teeth. What made it look real
Every prompt treats the camera as a real phone in a friend's hand, or propped on the ground in the hook while you steal the map. Don't ask for shake: the model adds fake shake that looks like an effect. A small, natural drift of the hand is enough.
No studio light anywhere. Only the light that's really in the scene: the midday sun, the grill fire, the lanterns on the deck.
Every clip starts already mid-action, so no second is wasted.
Give every person one small fumble and real weight. You land harder than you expected, the sharpshooter nearly slips, nobody moves cleaner than a real person could. Perfect choreography is what makes it look fake.
Describe how things behave, not just how they look. The map is old parchment that's been rolled up for years: it's stiff, it curls back up and it crackles.
Never put spoken words in quotes in a prompt, or the video will talk in a made-up voice. Describe the look on the face instead.
The Devil Fruit fire comes out of the open palm, held palm up, with no glow on the skin. Real skin never lights up from inside, so a glowing hand always looks fake.
