I uploaded a few photos of my studio, and GPT-6 Astra turned them into a detailed 3D model of the space. It worked out the layout from the photos and recreated the studio in Blender through Higgsfield MCP.




See the studio reconstruction.




Seeing a familiar room recreated in 3D immediately gives you ideas. What if the desk moved to the other wall? Would different furniture make the space feel more open? What would warmer lighting do to the room at night?

That is where I would take this next: three different versions of the same space, each exploring a different direction.




You could start with your bedroom, your office, or that room you have been meaning to redesign. The photos give you a starting point. Then you can work through the possibilities.

Before You Start

Choose one room and gather photos that show it from several angles. Include the corners, doors, windows, and major pieces of furniture. Overlapping views help establish where things sit in relation to each other.




If you know the room dimensions or the size of a large object, include those measurements. Photos give visual context; measurements give the model a reliable scale. Ask Astra to flag anything it has to estimate, especially areas the photos do not show.




Use Astra with Higgsfield MCP and access to its 3D tools. To work directly in an open desktop Blender scene, follow Higgsfield’s Blender Bridge setup guide.

The prompts below are suggested starting points written for this guide. Adapt them to your photos and the changes you want to explore.

Step 1 — Recreate the Room You Have

Start with the current space. Getting the walls, openings, and main furniture in the right places gives every later version a useful baseline.

Step 2 — Try Three Different Versions

Give each version a clear purpose. One can explore a better arrangement of what you already own. Another can introduce different furniture. A third can focus on the atmosphere created by lighting and materials.




Keeping the room structure fixed makes the comparison more useful: you are exploring what could change inside your actual space.

Create Three Design Directions

What it does: Requests three separate alternatives built from the same base model, with consistent views for comparison.

Starting from Original Room, create three separate design versions. Preserve the original model. Keep the room dimensions, walls, doors, and windows unchanged in all three versions.




Version 1 — Rethink the Layout

Use the existing furniture. Explore a different arrangement that improves movement through the room and makes better use of the available space.




Version 2 — Try Different Furniture

Explore a coordinated set of alternative furniture, including different shapes, sizes, and storage options. Keep everything proportionate to the room. Treat new pieces as design concepts unless I supply specific products and dimensions.




Version 3 — Change the Atmosphere

Keep the original furniture arrangement. Explore warmer evening lighting, layered light sources, and a different combination of colors and materials.




Keep door openings and circulation paths clear in every version. Present each design from the same overhead view and the same two interior camera positions. Use consistent lighting for Versions 1 and 2 so the layout and furniture changes are easy to compare. Show the lighting changes intentionally in Version 3.




Label each version and explain its main changes and tradeoffs.

Start With Your Own Space

A few photos can be the beginning of a much more useful design conversation. Recreate the room, explore three directions, and keep refining the ideas that fit how you want to live or work in it.




Before buying furniture, check the relevant dimensions in the real room. The model helps you explore; those measurements help you decide what will fit.
