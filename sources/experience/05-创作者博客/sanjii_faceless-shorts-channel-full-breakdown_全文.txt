Before You Start

This is a faceless format: no filming, no studio, no team, and almost no expenses. Claude with the VidIQ extension handles the research and the ideas, RankReel builds the actual video, and your only real job is posting every day. The timing works in your favor too — fewer people are starting YouTube automation than at any point in the last four years, while Shorts RPMs have climbed — so the competition is thinner exactly when the payouts got better.




Two things to set up before the first video: the free VidIQ Chrome extension, and RankReel for the editing. Both take a couple of minutes.

Stage 1 — The Niche and the Inspiration

The niche is ranking videos: you compile viral clips from platforms like TikTok and Instagram, put them together, and rank them — adding your take on which ones are the funniest, the most insane, the best. Everybody knows this format, it's the easiest content to make, and channels running it get millions of views on videos that are literally just "ranking the funniest football moments." Don't take anyone's word on the earnings either — pull up any of these channels with VidIQ and look at the estimated revenue yourself.




Start by collecting inspiration channels. Find a few good ranking channels, then like and comment on a bunch of their videos — your feed becomes ranking content almost immediately, and from there you can just scroll Shorts and it keeps surfacing new channels in the niche for you.

Stage 2 — The VidIQ Setup

Look up "VidIQ Chrome extension" on Google — the VidIQ extension for YouTube and Instagram, completely free — and install it in Chrome.




Then go to claude.ai and create a free account. When you refresh the screen, VidIQ appears right inside Claude — the extension adds itself automatically. One thing left: click the VidIQ dropdown, go to "Connect YouTube Insights," and click Authorize on the page it opens. One click, and the dropdown now shows as connected.

Stage 3 — The Research

Go to one of your inspiration channels — ideally a newer one that's already pulling hundreds of thousands of views per video. Click "More," scroll to the bottom of the pop-up, click "Share channel," then "Copy channel ID."




Back in Claude: click the plus, open Connectors, choose "Add from VidIQ for Claude," and click "Competitor breakdown." Paste the channel ID, click "Add prompt," and hit send. It asks for a few permissions as it runs — click "Always allow" — and about five minutes later you have a full research breakdown of the channel.




The breakdown exists for one purpose: ideas. Ideas are what make ranking videos go viral — the biggest channels in the niche win because their topics are more creative and more unique than everyone else's. So in that same chat, ask:

Give me a list of viral ranking video topics based on what has worked best for this channel.

Claude comes back with the channel's proven, top-performing topics — the ones most likely to go viral if you recreate them, like "ranking the most insane near-miss driving moments." Pick the one you like, and take it to the next stage.

Stage 4 — The Video in RankReel

RankReel is built specifically for ranking videos: you enter your title, paste TikTok clips into ranked slots, name each clip, set a custom playback order, and it generates a finished vertical video — all in one place.

https://rankreel.higgsfield.app/




Enter your topic as the title first, and format it the way the big channels do. Their titles are always two lines — not three, not one — so hit return about halfway through the text. Then highlight the keywords, the words that make a viewer instantly understand the video — "football moments," not "best" — and give them a color. Red works, light blue works, teal looks the cleanest. Now anyone landing on the video reads the keywords in a split second, and that's what stops them from scrolling away.




Then the clips. TikTok is the best source because its video search is so good: search your topic and grab clips — but only viral ones, with millions of views. If a clip didn't go viral on its own, it won't go viral for you. Download a clip, paste it into a ranking slot, hit send, and give it a short name so viewers know what each entry is — the big channels name them things like "red card"; "instant karma" works too.

Repeat until you have the right count: five to seven clips, with six as the sweet spot. Too many and people don't finish the video; too few and you're leaving watch time on the table.




Last step, and it matters: custom playback order. The top channels never rank in clean descending order — they show number two first, jump to six, bounce around, and save number one for last, so the viewer never knows what's coming and keeps watching. Turn on custom playback order, hit shuffle, drag anything you want by hand, then hit generate video. That's a finished ranking Short.

Ready To Make Your Own?

From here the whole system is three moving parts: Claude with VidIQ for the research, RankReel for the video, and you posting one to two Shorts every single day. That last part is the one you can't skip — open the videos tab of any big channel in this niche and you'll see a new Short every day, sometimes two, without a single missed day. That consistency isn't a coincidence; it's the reason.




One video takes about ten minutes end to end. So find your inspiration channels, run the breakdown, make the first one, and post it — because a few weeks of daily uploads is what a monetized channel is actually made of.
