Don’t forget to use the Excluded Styles Claude gave us → so the generated songs don’t vary too much in style or include genres and instruments we don’t want:

In the More Options tab:

select Gender just to be safe 
set Custom Duration to 1:30
Leave everything else as is, and enter a Title:

and just tap ✨create✨




Suno always generates two versions with similar seeds. Listen to both and pick the “winning generation.”

Each song costs 5 credits, or 10 credits per pair. The standard Pro subscription ($10/month) includes 2,500 credits, which is around 500 songs per month.

Step 2. Core plot and idea vision

Sooo, let's start making video itself!

Here is my message to Claude about my vision and idea

PROMPT FOR CLAUDE

I have a big idea for a music video for the track REPLICA. Help me develop it into a detailed, scene-by-scene script.

Overall concept: Singer Karima arrives at a club at night to perform. Everything starts like a typical evening for a star: a limousine, a red carpet, fans, camera flashes, autographs. But gradually, she begins to notice strange things, and the evening turns into a nightmare. Copies of her are walking around—clones with her face. The track is about originals and fakes, about who’s real, and the entire story revolves around that.

Here’s how I picture it:

Opening: Seoul at night, wet asphalt, a club on a street corner with purple neon, a crowd of fans holding phones. The camera is almost at ground level, with a puddle in the foreground and the title REPLICA overlaid on the image. A black limousine pulls up.

Karima steps out of the car: first, silver boots on the red carpet, then the camera tilts up along her metallic jumpsuit. Security guards in black suits stand beside her. She smiles at the fans and signs autographs.

The first strange moment: someone in the crowd suddenly yanks her hair. She turns around but can’t tell who did it. Standing in the crowd is a girl with blue hair and a baseball bat resting on her shoulder. She has Karima’s face. She smiles and disappears.

In her dressing room, Karima tries to compose herself, rubbing her temple. She looks up at the mirror, but her reflection doesn’t follow her movements: it sits perfectly still and smiles. She screams and falls off her chair. The mirror cracks, and the clone climbs out of it and crawls toward her across the floor, like something out of a horror film.

She runs down a backstage corridor. Another Karima steps out of a side door: wearing a men’s suit, with her hands wrapped like a boxer’s. A chase, a fight in the corridor, an overhead strike in slow motion.

Karima escapes through the back exit into an alley, where two of them are already waiting for her: a cyborg Karima and the Karima in the suit. It’s pouring rain. She steels herself and takes them on. I want a brutal, realistic fight in the rain: wet hair, splashing water, a wall run, flips.

She wins and stands exhausted over her opponents. Then someone prods her shoulder with a bat. It’s the blue-haired girl. Karima’s POV: the bat swings straight into the camera. Cut to black.

Karima wakes up in her dressing room: it was all a dream. A knock at the door: “Karima, you’re on.”

Ending: a security guard holds the door open for her, and she walks down the corridor toward the stage. The guard watches her leave, then peels off his face—it’s a mask. Underneath is another Karima. She smirks and steps into the dressing room.

Between the story scenes, insert shots of Karima singing directly to the camera: onstage under a spotlight, in an elevator, in the corridor, and on the red carpet.

Style: Korean cyberpunk, Seoul at night, neon, rain, cinematic realism. Lots of handheld camerawork and camera shake, low angles, POV shots, close-ups of eyes. Colors: purple neon, warm orange light, teal shadows. I want this to look like a big-budget film rather than a music video.
