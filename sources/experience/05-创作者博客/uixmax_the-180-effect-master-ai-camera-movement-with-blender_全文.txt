The previz (Pre-visualization) the process of creating rough moving sequences that show how a scene will be shot and edited before any real filming or final generation happens. 







Create your own cinematic sofa transformation video with a 180° camera arc. This is a hands-on assignment: design your own room, character and outfits, build a simple Blender camera previz with ChatGPT, then animate the result in Seedance 2.5.




Your goal is to recreate the CAMERA TECHNIQUE, not the original video's room, sofa, face or wardrobe. Make the visual concept yours.

Step 4 — Build the camera previz in Blender

The previz is deliberately simple: gray furniture, a red human-shaped placeholder, a floor and an animated camera. Its purpose is movement and timing, not visual quality.

Copy this Blender prompt:

Create a minimal Blender camera previs for a 12-second vertical 9:16 video at 24 fps, with 288 frames total.




SCENE
Use only a grey floor, a sofa made from two simple grey rectangular blocks, and a reclining person represented by one red rectangular body block and a sphere for the head. Position the body on the sofa, with the head at one end. No walls, anatomical details, clothing, decorations, textures, or bevels. Keep all scene geometry stationary.




CAMERA PATH
Create four consecutive 3-second camera passes.

For each pass:
— Start at the foot end of the sofa, with the camera lens 5 cm above the floor.
— Follow a 180-degree vertical semicircle over the person, along the sofa’s longitudinal axis.
— End at the head end, again 5 cm above the floor.
— Use a 4.2-metre radius, with the semicircle’s centre 5 cm above the floor.
— Keep the camera aimed at a fixed point at the centre of the subject, 0.85 metres above the floor.
— Maintain continuous pitch through the overhead position, allowing the image to become inverted during the descent. Do not automatically level the horizon.
— Use a fixed 43 mm lens. No sideways drift or horizontal orbit.




SPEED AND TRANSITIONS
Repeat this rhythm in every pass:
fast entrance → smooth deceleration near the overhead view → acceleration into the exit.

Never stop the camera during the visible pass. The next pass must begin immediately at speed, without an additional slowdown or pause at the transition.





Each pass occupies 72 frames:
— 71 visible frames travelling along the complete 180-degree arc.
— One black frame hiding the camera reset to the starting position.

Use black frames at 72, 144, 216, and 288. The reset between passes is intentional and must remain hidden. Do not animate a visible return journey beneath the floor.

The four passes are placeholders for four future outfits. Do not model clothing or change the character’s appearance in Blender. Add timeline markers for the four passes.

DELIVERY
Check camera positions, clearance from geometry, identical timing across all four passes, and the exact 12-second duration.

Save an editable .blend file for camera-preview approval. Do not render until I approve. After approval, export a vertical H.264 MP4 using Workbench at 1080 × 1920 and 24 fps.




Review: Inspect the camera from the front, from above and through the camera view. If it stops short of 180°, ask ChatGPT to correct the end angle without changing anything else.
