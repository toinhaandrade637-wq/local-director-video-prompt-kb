I filmed one take in my living room. Higgsfield Genjutsu turned it into a neon gas station with a Lambo, a black cat, an empty field and a friend who doesn’t exist. Here’s every prompt and reference I used.




Step 1. Film one take at home

Film one continuous take and act like everything is already there. You can get creative here....Lean on a piece of furniture like it’s your car. Kneel down like you’re feeding a cat. Fist bump a light stand like it’s your friend. Say your lines live so the lip sync matches.




Give every fake thing a real stand-in. Genjutsu is much better at turning your console into a car than adding a car out of thin air. My stand-ins: a white console for the car, a light stand for my friend.




Step 2. Cut it into sections

I split my clip in two because a lot changes in mine: a car, a cat, a whole new location, night to day and a friend. Genjutsu does best with a few changes per generation. Splitting also gives me a clean cut right on the reveal, and if one part comes out wrong, I only redo that part.




Part 1 is the gas station, part 2 is the field.

Only changing one or two things? Then you can do it all in one go. Upload the whole clip and describe everything in one prompt.




Step 3. The gas station and the car




I uploaded part 1 of my original video to Genjutsu and added a photo of the car. That’s all you need. You don’t need a photo of the location: Genjutsu builds it from your words and keeps your camera move.




The car replaces the furniture I was leaning on. That gives Genjutsu the right spot and size for the car, and it’s why the lean looks real.




TIP: Test at 480p first to save credits. When a take looks right, render it again at 720p or 1080p.

Step 4. Turn the car into a cat




We’re now using the clip we just generated as the reference in Genjutsu. Trim your gas station clip so it starts right before you want to swap something (in my case, the car) and upload it again. Only the car changes.




❗️Why edit the generated clip instead of your original?❗️ 
Because the gas station, the neon and the camera stay exactly the same. So when you cut from the car to the cat, it looks like only the car changed.

Step 5. Repeat it for anything you want

That’s the whole trick. Every change after this is one of the same two moves: upload a part of your clip and describe what it becomes, or put a generated clip back in and swap one thing.




Here’s how I repeated it for the rest of my video:




A new location: I uploaded part 2 and turned my living room into an empty field.
A friend: my light stand became a guy I fist bump. Pick a stand-in at head height, so your friend lands right where your hand goes.
Night and day: I made the night version from my day clip, so it’s the exact same field and only the time changes.

Run a few takes and pick the best one.




Step 6. Edit




In your editor of choice just cut the Genjutsu clips together and that's it!



Tip: Cutting on the word is what sells it: the change happens the second you say it. And the original at the bottom is the proof that you never left your living room.





Genjutsu keeps your audio, so you can line everything up by the waveform.
