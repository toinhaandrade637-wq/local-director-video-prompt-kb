OBJECT SWAP — REPLACE THE WOMAN'S FACE ONLY. @image1 = the new face.




SOURCE: @Video 1 · 11s 10.62 s, 24 fps, 1280×1080, five shots. Nothing about its motion, camera, cuts, timing, lighting, VFX or grade may change. The only edit is the facial identity of the one woman who appears in shots 3, 4 and 5.




WHAT CHANGES

In every frame where the woman's face is visible (3.83–5.46 s close-up, 5.46–7.46 s wide shot in profile, 7.5–10.62 s three-quarter extreme close-up), replace her face with the woman from Image 1: face shape, eyes, eyebrows, nose, lips, skin tone. The new face follows the original head frame by frame — same head tilt, same turns, same blinks, same eye direction, same lip position, same smirk. Take NOTHING else from Image 1: not her hair, not her clothing, not her lighting, not her background, not her expression.




WHAT NEVER CHANGES

– hair as filmed: dark hair pulled back, loose strands over the temples and across the face, braided tail over the shoulder

– wardrobe: black oversized blazer, black wide trousers, ivory high-neck lace blouse with lace cuffs

– earrings exactly as they appear in each shot

– makeup: smoky blue-grey eyeshadow, dark red lips

– the black butterfly with pale concentric eye-markings, every frame of its flight, every wingbeat, its size on screen and its position

– the hotel corridor, the warm sconce light, the red patterned carpet, the film grain and colour grade

– all five camera positions (all static), all four cuts and their exact timing

– the audio

EYE-GLOW RULE — the most important rule of the swap

The glowing rings in the transition and in the reveal must sit exactly on the eyes of the NEW face, at the coordinates above, and fade into the new face's natural irises on the same frames. The butterfly's markings and her eyes are one pair of eyes carried across the cut — never two different objects.




OCCLUSION RULE

In shot 5 the butterfly stays in front of the new face, in the same place, covering the same part of the lips and cheek on every frame. The new skin and lips are reconstructed only where the original skin and lips were visible.
