Step 1. Describe, do not prompt

This is the brief I gave Claude, almost word for word. Notice there is no lens, no light, no camera jargon. Only what happens and where.

Seven locations, modern Italian crime story, one continuous take: an abandoned street, a basketball court, a drift lot, a boardroom, a restaurant, a casino and a rooftop at late afternoon (not night). The hero is the same guy in every cut: me. On the street he greets a friend. On the court he does not play, he talks on the phone while the others play. In the drift lot he stands still in the center while the car circles around him. Upstairs, in the meeting, he sits down with the boss. In the restaurant, a date. In the casino he pushes a bet. On the rooftop he stays on one spot, draws a compact submachine gun, seats the magazine, racks the bolt, aims and fires at the end.

That is director blocking. It is exactly what we do on a real set with tape on the floor, before the camera is even switched on.

Step 2. The previz

The plugin connects Claude to Blender through a single link. I did not model anything and I did not animate the camera by hand. Claude placed the blocks and the camera, 30 seconds exactly, because that is the maximum length of one Seedance 2.5 generation.

The blocks are stand-ins: tall boxes are people, the box with the round head is the hero, the low box is the car, the long slab is the boardroom table, the small purple slab is the restaurant table, the cylinder is the casino table.

Every transition between locations is a camera move, never a cut: the crane rises to top-down and darkens, the pan crosses a wall, a pillar wipes the frame.

Step 3. The grey video becomes the prompt

I handed the rendered blocking back to Claude, together with my character sheet and Mariana's sheet (she plays the date). Claude probed the clip, pulled one frame per second, and anchored every beat of the prompt to a second of the source: transitions at 3.2s, 6.6s, 9.9s, 12.6s, 15.7s and 20.1s; on the rooftop the weapon at 21.0s, magazine 23.0s, bolt 25.0s, aim 27.0s, the single shot at 29.2s.

When you give the model the second, it stops guessing. It is not inventing the camera, it is following it.

Three things I learned

The location can be text only. The @location_1 tag had no image attached and the abandoned street still came out consistent with the blocking, because the geometry was already in the video. Locations are the cheapest reference to skip when the blocking is strong.

Anchor every beat twice. Numeric timecode plus the physical event ("20.1s, a rooftop pillar wipes the frame"). The exteriors landed on the exact second. The interior pan drifted by about a second (the restaurant shows up closer to 14s than 12.6s), which is the segment I would tighten with a harder lock on a retake.
