A soft, low-contrast film character sheet with quiet, delicate texture: the entire image lives in a broad, gentle middle register — highlights roll off early into soft cream while keeping full detail, shadows sit lifted, open and airy, micro-contrast stays low and tender, skin and fabric resolve with smooth, quiet, organic detail, edges stay rounded and creamy under a gentle veil of optical diffusion, as through an old uncoated vintage cinema prime, with a soft halation bloom breathing from the brightest points — the whole sheet reading like a delicately printed film photograph with a modern neutral colour grade.




Within that look, the saved reference element @selfie is the identity AND hair reference for the person: that exact face, age, build, body proportions, hair colour, hair length and exact hairstyle — hairline, parting, bangs, cut and texture, everything about the head and hair — are canon and must match the element 100%. @图片 2 is used strictly as the WARDROBE reference only: the pink-and-white striped blazer with wide notched lapels, the light blue vest, the white collared shirt, the grey tie, the grey pleated skirt, the pink tights, the pink-and-white low-heeled shoes and the black handbag, with their exact colours, layers, closures, silhouettes and folds. Do NOT copy the hairstyle, hair colour or hair length from the attached photo — ignore the photo's hair completely; all hair comes only from the saved reference element. Generate the character sheet: three stills of this same person arranged side by side on one sheet: FULL-BODY FRONT view on the left, FULL-BODY BACK view in the middle, and a THREE-QUARTER PORTRAIT on the right. Identical outfit and proportions across all three panels, standing on a seamless PURE WHITE studio background that keeps its own even, luminous brightness, reading as clean paper, with clear space around the figure and a soft contact shadow grounding them. Carry over only the person and their costume: they stand alone in clean, empty white studio space.




FRONT panel: the frame is cropped at the base of the neck, exactly at the collarbones, and runs down to the shoes. The head is COMPLETELY ABSENT from this panel — no head, no skull, no hair, not a single strand of hair, no facial features and no chin whatsoever; only bare neck and shoulders enter the very top edge of the frame, cleanly and flatly cut, with empty white space above the neck. Facing camera square, arms relaxed at the sides, weight even on both feet, the costume's front reading clearly — blazer open, its lapels, buttons and pockets, the vest, the shirt collar and grey tie, the pleated skirt, the pink tights and pink-and-white low-heeled shoes, the handbag at the side. BACK panel: also cropped at the base of the neck, the head and all hair completely absent from this panel, no strand of hair anywhere in frame, only the bare nape and shoulders entering the very top edge, cleanly cut with empty white space above; turned fully away, the costume's back completed faithfully in the same construction, the sleeves at their sides, the hem line, the black handbag held at the side, the heels of the footwear.

THREE-QUARTER PORTRAIT panel: this panel alone carries the head — a CHEST-LEVEL close portrait, the frame running from just above the top of the head down to the upper chest, so the head and face fill most of the panel; the exact head of the saved reference element carried over one-to-one — its precise hair colour, length, hairstyle, hairline and parting, the natural asymmetry between the left and right halves of the face, the true set of the eyes, nose and mouth, every crease, mark and skin texture kept, real skin softly resolved, the same lived-in cinematic face simply relit, with the full hairstyle inside frame and the costume's collar and blazer shoulders visible below.




The light is neutral and even across all three panels — a soft ambient wash, as if the whole space sits under a broad overcast sky: directionless, calm, arriving frontal and even, wrapping the figure gently, open transparent shadows, every fabric reading true, the exposure holding the low-contrast filmic register declared above. The figure's outline meets the pure white background softly along every edge. The colour story comes from the costume itself: its dominant colour as the leading mass against the pure white field, its secondary tones as the supporting masses, and the smallest bright detail as the single accent.
