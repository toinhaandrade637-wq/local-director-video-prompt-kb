1.2M views. 12K followers. 14 days. 12 reels. One intern who dies in every single one.

That's @everyonemeetgary. Gary is a silent 3D intern. In every episode he tries something adults actually do (shots at the office party, energy drinks, seawater on a raft), just a lot more of it. A calm narrator explains what's happening inside his body, the way you'd explain it to a five-year-old. Then Gary dies. Next episode, there's a new Gary with a higher number on his badge.

Topic: what happens if [NAME] takes an absurd amount of [something adults actually do: shots, energy drinks, seawater, sleepless nights].




Structure, in this order:

1. Hook, first 3 seconds: "What if [NAME] [did the absurd amount]?" plus one paradox line that names the danger without spoiling the death. Example: "The shots won't kill him. The nap afterwards will."

2. Tagline: "This is [NAME]. [Role] number N. Don't ask about the other N-1."

3. A dose ladder of 4 rungs with a visible counter (1, 5, 10, 15…). Each rung: what he feels, then the one real fact behind it, then one literal cartoon picture of it, then a dry one-line joke.

4. A re-hook every 10 to 15 seconds.

5. A twist: the real danger is not the obvious one.

6. His death on screen: wordless, no gore.

7. One honest safety tip the viewer can actually use.

8. A last line that calls back to the opening, then "Follow for more."




Voice: one calm, deadpan narrator who talks to [NAME] like a polite, slightly bored teacher. [NAME] never speaks. Most lines 4 to 12 words. No "Here's the thing", no "Plot twist".




Facts: real, checked science only. Give a source for every number and flag anything uncertain.




Return the narration as a numbered list with an emotion tag in brackets on each line ([deadpan], [cheerful], [quietly]), and next to each line one sentence describing the shot.

Why Gary Works

Three ideas carry the whole account:

The contrast is the joke. The topics are adult and a little dangerous. The explanation is for a kid.
He never talks. One silent character and one calm voice. You feel for him because he can't explain himself.
The format writes the next episode. The badge number goes up every time: "This is Gary. Intern number 54. Don't ask about the other 53." Now the comments ask about the other 53.

Four things that saved me the most rerolls:

Generate each take at the real length of its line. Never stretch a take with slow motion; people notice instantly.
Give the action a 1–2 second run-up. Seedance tends to start the first action late, so a calm opening keeps the key moment on its word.
Keep the camera at chest-up or wider. Tight face close-ups are the ones that get flagged.
Put text in the edit, not the prompt. Generated text comes out as gibberish, so counters and labels go on top later.
6. Cut it on the voice

What it does: turns the takes into the episode. Lay the narration down first, put one take under each line, and change the picture on the word. On top of that:

a counter sticker that climbs with the dose ("SHOTS 15 · BAC 0.30");
a big number slam on every fact;
music well under the voice, about 12 dB quieter.

For the cover, use the most extreme frame of the episode. I build my edits in code; any editor does the same job.

What 1.2M Views Taught Me

Shares per 1,000 views

Put it in the body. My four most-shared episodes are all about something Gary puts in his body: alcohol, mushrooms, cigarettes, seawater. They get 1.2–1.6 shares per 1,000 views. The rest get 0.5–1.0, and that includes energy drinks, so it isn't just "anything he swallows".
Make the number arguable. The top comments argue about the dose. The shrooms reel's top comment, with 484 likes, is someone working out where exactly the limit is. You don't have to ask for comments; the number asks for you.
Hook with "What if" and a paradox. "What if Gary [absurd amount]?" plus one line that names the danger without spoiling it.
Use the most extreme frame as the cover. All my top reels do: the shark's jaws, the party, the skeleton lit up by lightning. My text-only covers were my weakest posts.
Let the lore build itself. "Don't ask about the other 53" was a throwaway joke. Now people ask for the other 53.
Give them a reason to send it. One real safety tip turns "lol" into "send this to your friend who always says he'll sleep it off".
