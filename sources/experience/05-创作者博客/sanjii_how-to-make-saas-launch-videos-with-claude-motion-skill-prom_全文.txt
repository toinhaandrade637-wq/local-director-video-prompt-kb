The Storyboard

What it does: Turns your brief into the source of truth for scene order, timing, on-screen content and camera. Claude never reorders, adds or drops a scene from it. The example inside is the one used in the video, for a B2B AI-agents product.

Write the storyboard for my launch video from my brief, in exactly this format: five scenes (hook, problem, product, payoff, logo + CTA), each with what's on screen, the motion, the camera and its frame range at 60 fps, adding up to 30 seconds. Follow this example:




[PRODUCT] launch video — storyboard

30.0 s · 1920 × 1080 · 60 fps · 1800 frames




Scene 1 · Pile-up (hook) — 0:00–0:04, frames 0–240

On screen: dark background. Task cards ("Update CRM", "Export leads", "Weekly report", "Sync invoices", "Chase approvals"…) drop in and stack into a pile. Counter top-right: "Open tasks 0 → 200". Kinetic headline: "200 tasks. Before lunch."

Motion: cards drop with overshoot, staggered. Counter counts up over ~1 s. Headline word by word.

Camera: slow push-in 100% → 106%.




Scene 2 · Copy-paste grind (problem) — 0:04–0:11, frames 240–660

On screen: three app windows: CRM, spreadsheet, inbox. Cursor copies a row in the spreadsheet, arcs over and pastes it into the CRM, hesitates, moves on to the inbox. Text: "Copy. Paste. Repeat."

Motion: cursor on arcs, click presses, highlighted rows.

Camera: easy-ease pan left → right, following the cursor.




Scene 3 · Agents kick in (fix) — 0:11–0:19, frames 660–1140

On screen: [PRODUCT] dashboard (Figma SVG). Cursor types "Hand off today's busywork" into the prompt bar and clicks Run. Four agent cards come in: CRM Agent, Data Agent, Inbox Agent, Report Agent. Tasks fly from the pile into the cards.

Motion: Run button press. Cards staggered, each card works with a progress bar.

Camera: zoom into the prompt bar on the click (≈130%), then ease back to 100% for the cards.




Scene 4 · Payoff — 0:19–0:25, frames 1140–1500

On screen: checkmarks appear on the agent cards. Counter drops 200 → 0. Text: "Zero busywork."

Motion: counter counts down over ~1 s. Hold the clear board ≥ 1.5 s.

Camera: pull back to reveal the whole board, clear.




Scene 5 · Logo + CTA — 0:25–0:30, frames 1500–1800

On screen: board scales away. [PRODUCT] logo builds. Tagline: "AI agents for the work nobody wants to do." CTA: "Live on Product Hunt · [product].com".

Motion: logo build, tagline with offset, slow drift to the end.

Camera: gentle push-in.

Stage 2 — The Prompting Framework

Download the skill and add it to Claude: upload the zip in Claude's Skills settings, or unzip the launch-motion folder into ~/.claude/skills/ if you work in Claude Code.

launch-motion.skill
SKIL




What's inside is everything a senior motion designer carries around in their head:

Story. Grab attention, show the problem, show how the product fixes it, finish with the payoff. If a scene doesn't serve one of those, it's out.
Timing. Anything important stays on screen for about a second and a half. Count-ups last about a second.
Motion rules. Nothing moves linearly. Things come in over about half a second with a slight overshoot and leave faster than they came in. Every click gets a tiny press, and the cursor moves in an arc, like a real hand.
The golden rule. Nothing should ever fully stop. Claude works the way you would in After Effects: related elements sit under one parent (a null object), keyframes are offset a couple of frames apart, and the next move starts before the last one has settled.
Tech specs. 1920 × 1080 at 60 fps, everything positioned from the center, with safe zones so the 9:16 and 1:1 cutdowns keep everything important in frame.

Two strict rules before you hit generate:




Storyboard first, then animation. Without a storyboard Claude starts guessing: scenes in random order, everything moving at once, no story.
If it's motion, you have to teach it. Feed Claude your UI components and brand, and let the skill define the easing and the baseline.

Bonus — The Pitch Message

This is the proposal that lands launch-video clients on Upwork, even without a portfolio. The first two lines are about their product, because that's all the client sees in the preview. The concept replaces the portfolio, and the small first milestone means the client takes almost no risk. The same template is inside the skill.




Hi [Name],

Just went through [Product]. The [specific feature, e.g. "AI search across all your docs"] is the moment that will sell this in a launch video. Here's how I'd do 30 seconds:

0–5s: Hook. The problem in one line ("[pain point]"), bold kinetic type.
5–20s: Product in action. [Feature] animated straight from your UI, with fast camera moves and no fluff.
20–30s: Payoff. [Key benefit], your logo, and a clean CTA for [Product Hunt / your site].

How it works:
1. Storyboard and styleframes in [3–4] days, so you see exactly how it will look before any animation starts.
2. Full animation in [X] days, with 2 revision rounds.
3. Final delivery in 16:9, plus 9:16 and 1:1 cutdowns for socials.

Milestone 1 covers only the storyboard and styleframes, so you can judge the look with minimal risk.

Quick question: do you have the UI in Figma, or should we work from screenshots?

[Your name]




Before you apply, check four things: the client has payment verified, $10K+ spent and a hire rate above 50%; the post is less than 30 minutes old; there are fewer than 15 proposals and nobody is interviewing yet; and the company's site shows it's real, ideally freshly funded and about to launch.
