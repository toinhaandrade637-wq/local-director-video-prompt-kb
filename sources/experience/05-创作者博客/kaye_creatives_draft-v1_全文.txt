Replace the main performer with the person represented by @selfie across the ENTIRE uploaded @Video 1.




GLOBAL REPLACEMENT RULE:

Every visible part of the main performer belongs to the replacement person in EVERY FRAME: face, ears, neck, shoulders, torso, arms, hands, fingers, legs, and rear-facing silhouette.




Apply this rule continuously across all shots, including POV hands, distant figures, overhead views, profiles, silhouettes, partial bodies, and appearances without a visible face. A visible face is NOT required to activate the replacement.




Create one consistent replacement person and maintain their identity, complexion, anatomy, and proportions throughout the complete sequence.




REFERENCE RESPONSIBILITIES:

@selfie defines facial identity, complexion, and visible physical characteristics. Use natural, coherent adult anatomy for body regions not shown in the portrait.




For a male reference character, use a consistent male bodily appearance throughout the face, neck, shoulders, torso, limbs, hands, and silhouette. For a female reference character, use a consistent female bodily appearance. Avoid exaggerated muscles, curves, or other invented extremes.




Ignore the selfie's clothing, neckline, collar, accessories, scalp hairstyle, background, lighting, and pose.




The SOURCE VIDEO defines hairstyles, wardrobe design, makeup, actions, staging, locations, camera, editing, and audio.




BODY REPLACEMENT TAKES PRIORITY INSIDE THE PERFORMER:

The original performer's anatomy and body outline are editable. Reconstruct the visible person using the replacement anatomy rather than retaining the source body beneath a new face.




Replace exposed hands and skin as actively as facial features. Keep the replacement complexion consistent under each shot's existing lighting. Adapt shoulder width, neck, torso, limbs, and silhouette coherently.




Preserve clothing design while adjusting its fit and drape to the replacement body. Do not preserve the original body shape solely to maintain the original garment outline.




APPLY THROUGHOUT THESE SOURCE SHOTS:




0.00–3.60s — Kitchen POV:

The visible hands, fingers, wrists, and any exposed forearms belong to the replacement person. Replace their anatomy and complexion while preserving the exact gestures, finger articulation, rings, knitted sleeves, glass interaction, falling water, and breaking action. Keep correct contact and object timing.




3.60–5.00s — Miniature room:

Replace the giant face, visible fingers, and the miniature seated version of the main performer. Both versions must represent the SAME replacement person, including their bodily appearance. Preserve their different source scales, positions, poses, clothing designs, and the room.




5.00–6.60s — Giant fish:

Replace the entire visible main performer: face, neck, raised arm, hand, exposed skin, body proportions, and silhouette. Preserve the source hairstyle, light shirt, gesture, giant fish, sky, and field.




6.60–7.96s — Overhead boat and water:

Replace the performer’s visible body, limbs, hands, skin, silhouette, and any visible facial features, even when tiny or turned away. Transfer the original movement to the replacement anatomy. Preserve clothing design, overhead perspective, boat, water, bubbles, and splash.




7.96–10.84s — Meadow:

Maintain the replacement face AND body through every running, turning, and airborne frame. Adapt the grey outfit and pink footwear to the replacement proportions while preserving their design. Keep the original hairstyle, action, timing, and camera movement.




10.84–12.36s — Beach:

Replace facial identity, ears, neck, shoulders, and visible bodily appearance. Preserve the source tied-back hair, loose strands, earrings, dark coat, pale collared shirt, expression, and dusk lighting.




12.36s–end — Seated rear view:

Replace the rear-facing performer’s anatomy and silhouette, including shoulder breadth, neck, torso, arms, and visible skin. The person seated in the chair must have the replacement bodily appearance despite the hidden face. Adapt garment fit while preserving source hair, wardrobe design, pose, chair contact, landscape, cloud, and glowing ring.




PROTECTED STYLING:

Keep each source hairstyle exactly where it belongs: length, cut, color, bangs, parting, tied sections, loose strands, and movement. Preserve source makeup and character effects.




Keep every garment's type, construction, colors, materials, patterns, collars, sleeves, layers, fastenings, and accessories. Only fit and drape change to accommodate the replacement anatomy. Never transfer clothing from the selfie or carry one scene's outfit into another.




PERFORMANCE AND COMPOSITION:

Transfer the original expressions, gaze, mouth movements, gestures, and actions to the replacement person with the original timing. Preserve screen placement, movement paths, balance, grips, and contact with objects.




Keep hidden anatomy hidden. Do not insert a face into a hands-only composition or turn a rear-facing person toward the camera.




Preserve all other people, animals, props, locations, surreal scale relationships, camera movement, cuts, lighting, grading, duration, aspect ratio, and original audio.




Across the whole video, every visible occurrence of the main performer—whether a face, a hand, a distant body, or a rear silhouette—must consistently belong to the same replacement person.
