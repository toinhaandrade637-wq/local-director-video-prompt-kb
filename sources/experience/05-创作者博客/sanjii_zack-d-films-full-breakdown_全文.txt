Before You Start

This build has two rules. First: assets before motion. Every character, location, and prop is generated once as a still, then attached as a reference to every scene it appears in — that's what keeps three separate generations looking like one film. Second: the style lock. Every prompt below ends with the same style sentence, word for word. It never changes between prompts, and that's exactly why the film never drifts.




Three tools cover the whole production: image generation for the reference stills, Seedance 2.5 for the 30-second scene blocks with the audio baked in natively, and SeedAudio for the narration. And you don't have to write any of this yourself — the Claude Skill below writes the complete production plan, every prompt included, from a single sentence.

Stage 1 — The Assets

The film is "The Parker Incident" — Spider-Man's origin story treated as a real incident. It needs five assets: one character, one location, three props. The second style, the large-scale investigation, needs four more. Everything below is an image — the only video generations in the whole build are the three blocks in Stage 3. Generate the assets before touching a single scene, and keep every extra angle you like — extra angles are free consistency ammo.

Stage 2 — The Prompting Framework

Download the skill, upload it to Claude under Customize → Skills, and open a fresh chat. Then give it one line:




Make me a Fern-style documentary about the origin of Spider-Man. Treat it as a real incident.




It comes back with the complete case file: the minute-by-minute timeline, a scene table, an image prompt for every asset, a 30-second motion prompt for every scene, the full narration with a voice card, and an attach list per scene. Swap the idea in that one line and the same flow produces any other documentary — including the second format.




One rule before generating anything: stills first. Every scene's consistency comes from attaching the Stage 1 references, and Stage 3 is where they all come together.

Stage 3 — The Scenes: Three Generations

The whole film is three scenes — Intake at 08:41, the routine; The Contact at 08:45, the incident; The Anomaly at 09:14, the aftermath — and each scene is exactly one Seedance 2.5 generation: a continuous 30-second block, one location, no cuts, audio baked in. Three generations, and the visual foundation of a 1:30 documentary is done.




The loop is the same for every block. Take the scene's motion prompt from the blueprint. Open Seedance 2.5, set the duration to 30 seconds, turn native audio on. Upload every still from that scene's Attach line as references — up to 50 fit, so the extra angles go in too. Paste the prompt, generate, move to the next block.




Here's what a scene pack looks like when the skill hands it over — Scene 1, the one from the video:




Continuous 30-second single-scene sequence, one location, no cuts.
SETTING: LOC-01, the east genetics lab, morning, cold daylight through blinds.
CHARACTERS: CHAR-01 — a young man around nineteen, slight build, short dark hair, wire-rim glasses, pale-blue lab coat over a grey shirt.
SEQUENCE: 0–8s he badges in and walks the center aisle, seen from the high-corner surveillance angle; 8–16s lateral dolly follows him past humming equipment to the far bench; 16–24s he stops at PROP-01, the glass terrarium, and leans in — slow push-in over his shoulder; 24–30s his hand unlatches the lid, hold on the opening seam.
Characters only gesture, they do not talk.
AUDIO: soft HVAC room tone, footsteps on sealed concrete, one metallic latch click, low sparse suspenseful score. Stylized low-poly 3D documentary reconstruction render; simplified geometric human figures with minimal facial detail; clean soft-textured materials; realistic cinematic lighting with soft volumetric shafts; muted desaturated palette with a single amber accent; gentle filmic haze and shallow depth of field; high-end production render; no text, no watermark; 16:9




Attach: CHAR-01, LOC-01, PROP-01 + all extra angles you generated.




Three details in that prompt do the heavy lifting. The SEQUENCE is written in timed beats, which is what lets one generation carry an entire scene — from the badge-in to the latch — instead of stitching five mismatched clips. The AUDIO line means the block arrives with its room tone and score already in it; the narration is the only sound you add later. And the last sentence is the style lock — the same sentence, word for word, that ends every asset prompt in Stage 1. Same references, same lock: three separate generations that read as one film.




Scenes 2 and 3 arrive in the blueprint in exactly the same shape — the contact at the open terrarium with the specimen attached as a reference, then the empty lab at 09:14 with the badge left on the bench — each one a single block with its own attach list.




And the second style isn't a new pipeline, it's the same loop. The skill outputs the map and overlay assets — MAP-01 and the OVL prompts above — plus one continuous search-sequence prompt, and a single block returns the flight path drawing itself, the radar sweeping, a ping expanding, and the search grid crawling across the water.

Stage 4 — The Voice

The narration is generated in the Audio tab with SeedAudio. Start from a deep, neutral, low male read with minimal warmth — or clone your own. Slow the delivery slightly below default, lower the pitch a notch, and keep the volume even; the goal is someone reading evidence into the record, not a movie trailer. Generate the calibration sentence a few different ways, changing one dial at a time, until it sounds right:




09:14. The motion sensor above bench four triggers twice, nine seconds apart.




Then generate the full script as one take and save the voice as an asset. It's now part of the channel's identity, same as the characters and locations — from this point on, every episode sounds the same.
