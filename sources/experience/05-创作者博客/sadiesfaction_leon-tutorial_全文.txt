INPUTS

SCENE CONTEXT

A teenage girl walks straight across a dim wallpapered room in profile with a tall stack of pale pink ribboned boxes on her head and another stack in her arms, the camera travelling alongside her the whole way. She almost loses the whole stack in the first second, saves it, then moves with slow deliberate care, cutting one knowing look to the lens — recognize me? — before carrying on. She then rises into frame against the bare wallpaper, boxes scattered around her, mimes being buried in them with theatrical pride, silently asks the camera whether it recognizes the bit, then tosses a box aside and swans out of frame.




OUTPUT SETTINGS

16:9 widescreen. 15 seconds total. Composition is built for the standard wide frame: enough vertical room for the full head stack above her.




ACTIVE REFERENCES




CHARACTER REFERENCE — @girl: girl about 15 to 16 years old, slim, dark hair pulled into a low braided updo with a few loose strands at the temples. Long camel wool coat, buttoned once at the waist, over a pale mint green A-line dress, sheer tights, dark brown mary-jane shoes with a low heel. Both hands gripping the underside of a box stack at chest height, forearms locked, elbows tucked. Playing it up: mischievous, self-amused, mock-glamorous, fully aware of the camera and enjoying it. Micro-expressions carry the charisma — a sly one-sided smirk that appears and vanishes, a raised eyebrow, a tucked chin, a single knowing sideways eye flick to the lens without turning her head. Never a wide toothy grin, never mugging. 100% matches the character reference for face, hair, proportions and full outfit. Use this reference for identity and wardrobe only. Do not import its pastel corridor, pink walls, panelled hallway, tiled floor or chandelier.




PROP REFERENCE — @boxes: shallow rectangular pale pink cardboard boxes with slightly textured matte paper, a fine dusty-rose printed ornamental scroll border framing the lid, and pale blue satin ribbon tied in a soft bow with long trailing tails on some of them. Printed detail stays decorative and non-legible; no logos, no brand marks, no readable text. Three boxes stacked on her head, three stacked in her arms, four more of the same boxes on the floor in the later segments.




LOCATION REFERENCE — @location: interior room, cream diamond-patterned wallpaper, grey painted skirting board, herringbone parquet floor, tall three-door dark rosewood wardrobe with small brass escutcheons and hinges filling the right wall, open doorway on the left leading into a small tiled bathroom with sink, toilet, mirror and a bright frosted window, low wooden chest of drawers at the far left, corner of a bed at the far right. Use this reference for geography, wall texture, wood tone, wardrobe scale, floor pattern and light direction only. Do not inherit its camera height or framing.




LOCATION MAP

Camera works on the room side, facing the wardrobe wall, lens axis perpendicular to that wall. Screen-left is the bright open bathroom doorway, screen-center to screen-right is the wardrobe face, far right is the bed corner. The wide bare stretch of cream diamond-patterned wallpaper with grey skirting sits between the doorway frame and the wardrobe edge, and continues past the wardrobe on the far right. The only light source is daylight spilling out of the bathroom doorway from screen-left; the rest of the room sits in warm low ambient.

Walking path, segment 1: @girl crosses the room screen-right to screen-left in full profile, body broadside to camera, on a dead straight line 1.3 meters in front of the wardrobe doors, exactly parallel to the wall. The camera travels screen-left on a parallel track 4 meters out from her line, moving at exactly her speed and holding a constant 4 meters of separation for the whole segment. She never drifts nearer or further from the lens, never walks on a diagonal, never curves.

Close-up position, segments 2 and 3: the camera has repositioned to face the bare wallpaper wall, and @girl stands 0.6 meters in front of it. Her background is flat cream diamond-patterned wallpaper only. No wardrobe in frame, no doorway in frame, no bathroom, no bed, no furniture edges. Same room, same light direction from screen-left.




FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING

The first visible frame already contains @girl in profile with both box stacks fully inside frame, already mid-stride and already off balance. No empty establishing frame, no delayed entrance, no empty-room opening.




Segment 1 blocking (16:9, medium tracking shot, profile):

MEDIUM SHOT: framed from her waist up through her shoulders, her braided updo and the full head stack above. Waistline sits at the bottom frame edge, y 100%. Hips, legs and feet stay out of frame for the whole segment.

Wardrobe fills x 34% to x 100% at frame start; by the end of the segment the background behind her is wallpaper with the doorway glow at the left edge.

Floor is below the frame; no parquet visible in this segment.




Segment 2 blocking (16:9, close-up on wallpaper):

Segment 3 blocking:

Same close-up framing, same camera position, same wallpaper background as segment 2, head at x 50%, y 40%, floor boxes unchanged in the same positions.




FORMAT MODE

Controlled three-segment sequence, 15 seconds total, with a HARD CUT at 5.5s and a HARD CUT at 11.0s. Real-time motion, no slow motion. No subtitles, no music, no spoken words.




OPTICS

LENS LOCK SEGMENT 1 = 47° diagonal field of view, standard normal lens character, camera 4 meters from the walking line at all times, camera height 1.5 meters, level lens, no upward or downward angle. Natural human-eye perspective, zero distortion, natural body proportions, wallpaper and wardrobe readable behind her, comfortable depth of field, the full head stack held inside the frame.

LENS LOCK SEGMENT 2 = 29° diagonal field of view, short telephoto portrait lens character, camera 4.5 meters from subject, camera height 1.4 meters. Close framing achieved through lens reach, not proximity. Face razor-sharp, the wallpaper behind her compressed and softened into a shallow creamy texture field, floor boxes slightly soft in the near foreground.

LENS LOCK SEGMENT 3 = 29°, identical to segment 2.

No lens drift inside any segment.







CAMERA

Segment 1 is a tracking shot: the camera physically travels screen-left with her on a smooth lateral dolly, parallel to her walking line, matched to her speed, so she stays pinned at the same spot and the same size in frame while the room slides past behind her. The move is fluid and stabilized with a faint human weight to it — a slight ease as it starts, a subtle settle when she stops, no mechanical stiffness. During the opening near-drop the dolly slows to a halt with her and holds; when she resumes her careful walk, it eases back into motion at her slower pace and stays glued to her. No pan away from her, no zoom, no tilt up or down, no push in or pull out, no roll, no handheld shake, no orbiting. The camera stays on her profile side for the whole segment and never swings around to her front or back. Focus rides her face and the stack continuously.

Segments 2 and 3: camera locked dead still at eye level facing the wallpaper wall, no movement at all, focus on her eyes; it does not follow her out of frame at the end.




ACTION TIMING

0.0s to 1.4s — NEAR-DROP, immediately. The first frame catches her already lurching: the whole head stack has swung hard toward her front, the top two boxes fanned apart and hanging over the edge, ribbon tails flying. Her arms are full so she cannot reach up. She stops dead, counter-leans her torso back, ducks her head under the falling side and makes two fast micro-corrections with her neck and shoulders. The boxes tip back, clap flat into line and rock to a stop. She freezes completely for a beat, eyes wide, shoulders up by her ears. Nothing touches the floor. The dolly halts with her and holds.

1.4s to 2.2s — She exhales, lowers her shoulders, resets her neck to dead vertical and lifts her chin. The dolly eases back into motion with her first careful step. From here the walk is slow, careful, deliberate.

2.2s to 3.4s — Careful walking, camera gliding alongside at her pace. Small measured steps, heel set down softly then weight rolled forward, torso rising and settling only slightly, head held rigidly level like a glass of water is up there. Elbows clamped to her ribs around the arm stack. Ribbon tails barely swing. The stack sways no more than one degree and she damps it with tiny neck adjustments. Eyes forward on the path, jaw set in concentration, a faint proud smirk at the corner of her mouth.

3.4s to 4.2s — THE LOOK. Still in full profile, still walking her careful pace, still held at the same spot in frame by the moving camera, she slides her eyes to the lens from the corner of her eye, one eyebrow lifting, chin tipping a few degrees toward camera: a silent "recognize me?" She holds the look for three quarters of a second, smirk deepening on one side.

4.2s to 5.5s — She flicks her gaze back forward, resets her head to level and keeps walking screen-left at the same careful pace into the doorway light shaft; her profile edge and the pale box lids brighten as she enters it. Stack stays dead steady. The dolly stays with her right through the cut.

5.5s HARD CUT

5.5s to 5.7s — Frame holds on the bare wallpaper wall, boxes already scattered across the lower band.

5.7s to 6.6s — @girl rises into frame from the bottom edge, head first then shoulders, coming up slowly and deliberately like a magician's reveal, one loose strand of hair falling forward. She stops, locks eyes into the lens and lets a slow smirk land.

6.6s to 8.0s — She lifts both hands beside her shoulders, palms up, and sweeps them wide across the boxes on both sides of the frame like a hostess presenting a prize. Holds the pose, one slow blink.

8.0s to 9.2s — She drops one hand, lifts the box in her right hand up beside her cheek and presents it to camera with a small proud tilt of the head, as if it explains everything. Eyebrows raised, lips closed.

9.2s to 10.2s — She lowers the box, spreads both arms once more over the scattered boxes and shrugs slowly with both shoulders, radiating mock pride at the wreckage, chin dipped, smirk holding.

10.2s to 11.0s — The presentation drains out of her. Hands settle, she straightens up and looks straight into the lens, waiting for a reaction, smirk fading.

11.0s HARD CUT

11.0s to 12.6s — Same framing on the wallpaper. Her eyebrows lift high, head tilts a few degrees screen-right, eyes wide and expectant, gaze locked into the lens: a silent question, do you really not recognize it. She holds, tips her head the other way, blinks once.

12.6s to 13.6s — The smirk drops completely. Eyes flatten into flat teenage disappointment. One short scoff exhale through the nose, no words; shoulders sag, one eyebrow stays up in judgment.

13.6s to 15.0s — She swings the box in her right hand up and tosses it underhand toward camera-left with a dismissive flick of the wrist, out of the lower left of frame, then turns to her own left, tosses her chin over her shoulder for one last unimpressed look at the lens and walks out of frame screen-left in two steps. The camera does not follow. Frame ends on the bare wallpaper with the remaining floor boxes and one still-rocking ribbon tail.




PHYSICS

The boxes are light hollow cardboard: low mass, high wobble, they tip and rock easily and never behave like heavy objects. In the opening near-drop the stack has real angular momentum — the top boxes slide against each other with friction, hang past the tipping point, then fall back and clap flat with a dry cardboard snap and two decaying rocks. For the rest of the walk the stack is nearly motionless because her head is, with only sub-degree oscillation damped by neck micro-corrections. Satin ribbons are smooth and slippery: they fly and lag during the save, then hang almost still during the careful walk, catching a narrow specular highlight along each fold. Her coat is medium-weight wool: the shoulders and lapels shift with the counter-lean, hold fold creases, and do not flutter. The careful walk shows real weight transfer even with the feet offscreen — visible in the slow vertical torso rhythm, the soft hip drop and minimal shoulder counter-rotation, everything damped as if she is walking on ice. Because the camera moves with her, background parallax reads correctly: the near wardrobe edge sweeps past faster than the far doorway. The thrown box travels a low arc, spins slowly around one corner, and its ribbon whips behind it. Her loose hair strand flies in the save and settles after. Dust motes drift only inside the doorway light shaft.




LIGHTING

Motivated single source: soft daylight from the open bathroom doorway on screen-left, consistent in every segment. In segment 1 she walks toward that light in profile, so her leading screen-left side catches a soft directional edge on the forehead, cheekbone and the front edges of the box lids, while her rear side and the depth of the coat stay in warm low-key shadow; the lit edge strengthens steadily as the tracking move carries her nearer the doorway.

In the close-ups her screen-left side catches soft directional light with a clean cheek highlight and an eye glint that reads the smirk; her screen-right side falls into warm soft shadow. The cream wallpaper behind her carries a soft horizontal falloff, brighter on the screen-left side, sinking into warm shadow toward screen-right, so she separates from the wall by tone rather than by fill light. Her own soft shadow falls onto the wallpaper toward screen-right. The pale pink box lids stay the brightest surfaces in frame with the printed border still visible; the blue satin picks up a thin sheen. Exposure is set for the lit edge and the doorway, not for the shadow side; shadows stay rich and soft, not lifted. No flat frontal fill, no extra practical lights, no lighting change across either cut.







AUDIO

No dialogue and no narration. Lips stay closed the whole shot except the relief exhale at 1.4s and the single nose scoff at 12.6s. Sound is room tone only: faint bathroom hum from the doorway, a dry cardboard scrape and sharp clap as the boxes fall back into line, two decaying cardboard rocks, one sharp relief exhale, then careful soft shoe steps on parquet with almost no heel click, thin satin rustle, one short breathy scoff, a soft hollow cardboard slap and slide when the thrown box lands, two exit footsteps. No music, no added sound effects, no laughter track.




POSITIVE LOCKS

Segment 1 is a lateral tracking shot: the camera travels with her and keeps her at the same spot and same size in frame the whole time. Only the background moves across frame; she does not slide out of frame, and the camera never loses her.

Camera separation stays a constant 4 meters and the lens axis stays perpendicular to her path; no push in, no pull back, no arc around her.

The near-drop happens in the first 1.4 seconds and is the only balance crisis; from 2.2s onward she walks slowly and carefully with a steady stack and no further wobble.

Nothing falls at any point: the near-drop resolves with every box still on her head. No box touches the floor, no box leaves the stack, no spill.

Segment 1 stays a medium shot the whole way: waist up, feet and legs never enter frame, framing size never changes.

Segment 1 holds her in full profile: body broadside to camera, camera travelling on the same side. She never turns to face camera, never turns her back; the look at 3.4s is an eye-and-chin flick only that keeps the profile.

The full head stack stays inside the top of the frame for the whole of segment 1; the top box is never cropped off.

Segments 2 and 3 play entirely against the bare cream diamond-patterned wallpaper: no wardrobe, no doorway, no bathroom, no bed, no chest of drawers, no window anywhere in those frames.

Wallpaper pattern, color and weave stay identical to the location reference in the close-ups; no new pattern, no color shift, no pastel walls, no wood panelling.

Frame stays a true 16:9 composition in all three segments; no aspect change across the cuts, no letterboxed bars inside the image.

@girl is the only person in the shot; no other characters, no reflections of other people anywhere.

Her identity, face, hairstyle and full outfit match the character reference exactly in all three segments; the performance lives in expression and body language only and never distorts her face away from the reference.

Expressions stay subtle and controlled: one-sided smirk, raised brow, eye flicks. No wide open-mouth grin, no cartoon mugging, no exaggerated facial stretching.

ACTION TIMING

0.0s to 0.8s - Frozen mid-performance energy, mouth open, chest pumping. The answer lands. Her eyebrows shoot up, eyes widen for a split second, and the corner of her mouth twitches toward a grin she immediately kills.

0.8s to 1.6s - The grin dies. She snaps her mouth shut, presses her lips into a flat line, and lets her eyelids drop to half-mast in a slow unimpressed blink. One eyebrow stays up. Chin tips a few degrees toward screen-right, deadpan, giving the partner a long flat look.

1.6s to 3.2s - She pulls a huge theatrical breath in through her nose, shoulders climbing toward her ears, cheeks puffing slightly, eyes rolling up and away for a beat. Then the whole thing collapses: a long audible sigh out through her mouth, shoulders dropping hard, spine slumping, head lolling a few degrees to one side, bangs stirring on the exhale. Her whole body deflates like a punctured balloon.

3.2s to 3.8s - Beat of stillness at the bottom of the sigh. Eyes reopen, land back on the partner, one eyebrow still stubbornly raised. Nose wrinkles briefly in mock disgust at being beaten.

3.8s to 5.0s - She speaks the single line, dry and slightly breathless, with a small resigned head tilt on the first word: "Your turn now." Lips move only for these three words. On the last word she flicks her left hand out in a loose lazy gesture toward the partner, palm up, and lifts her chin. The pink cast stays down at her side.

5.0s to 6.0s - Lips close and stay closed. She holds the look, tongue pushing briefly into her cheek, both eyebrows lifting in a silent well-go-on challenge. One slow blink, then a tiny sideways smirk breaks through at the very corner of her mouth. Frame holds on that.
