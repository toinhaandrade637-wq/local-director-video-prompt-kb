Ultra-realistic amateur iPhone 16 Pro video, filmed handheld by a person in a second small aircraft, 

roughly 50 meters away from the subject. Authentic iPhone look at long range: over-sharpened image, 

aggressive HDR, wide-angle edge distortion, deep focus, digital noise in the shadows, faint lens flare 

and window reflection, heavy rolling-shutter jello, visible heat shimmer and haze between the camera 

and the plane.




MOTION — CRITICAL: both aircraft are moving forward fast through the air. The subject plane is NEVER 

static in frame. It drifts inside the shot — pulling ahead toward the front of the frame, the operator 

losing it and panning to recover, the plane sliding back toward center. It bobs and dips a few meters 

in the turbulent air, rolling slightly left and right. The gap between the planes visibly breathes, 

40 to 60 meters.




PARALLAX — CRITICAL: wisps of thin haze and small cloud fragments whip through the space between the 

camera and the plane, streaking past the lens. The city grid directly below rips past in violent 

horizontal motion blur. Mid-distance rooftops slide past slower. The far horizon crawls almost 

imperceptibly. The propeller is a translucent disc. The airframe vibrates, the open door judders 

against the airstream.




ONE CONTINUOUS UNBROKEN TAKE, no cuts, no angle changes. The camera holds a single fixed side-profile 

position at roughly 50 meters and never orbits, never switches sides, never closes the distance — only 

the zoom changes. Constant handheld shake amplified by distance, the operator continually correcting 

and re-centering the fast-moving plane.




AUDIO: roaring wind and engine noise, phone microphone clipping. The male voice of the person filming: 

first stunned — "oh my god... oh my god!" — then, at the moment of the flip, erupting into disbelieving 

laughter and shouting, voice cracking, breath audible over the roar.




AIRCRAFT: @Image 3 white Piper PA-28 Warrior II, single-engine low-wing light plane, red and grey side stripe, 

black registration lettering, fixed tricycle gear, three-blade propeller blurred into a disc. Slightly 

weathered paint, sun glare on the windshield. Flying fast and level at 700 meters. Nobody else on 

board — the cabin is empty except for her.




LOCATION: @Image 2 racing above New York City at 700 meters. Below: the dense Manhattan street grid ripping past 

in motion blur, rooftops and water towers, yellow cabs as tiny streaks. One World Trade Center, the 

Empire State Building and the Midtown towers standing up through the haze, Central Park a green 

rectangle, the Hudson and East Rivers glinting in the sun, bridges strung across the water. Bright 

midday sun, clear sky, real urban haze on the horizon.




CHARACTER: [tag your character sheet here] young woman, mid-20s, slim athletic build, long dark hair in a tight high ponytail. Dusty 

pink hoodie with the hood down, worn TUCKED IN — the hem is tucked fully inside the high waistband of 

her wide-leg charcoal grey cargo trousers all the way around, front and back, and held there by a 

cinched belt. The hoodie's own ribbed hem is drawn tight underneath. Underneath the hoodie she wears a 

fitted long-sleeve base layer, also tucked in, so no skin can be exposed at any angle. Wide-leg charcoal 

grey cargo trousers with thigh flap pockets, high-waisted, sitting well above the hips. White training 

sneakers, clear transparent-lens glasses, green aviation pilot headset with a boom microphone worn over 

the lowered hood. Stomach and lower back are completely covered at every moment, from every angle, 

including fully inverted. At this distance her face reads only as a general impression — the pink 

hoodie, the ponytail and the headset identify her.




0.0–3.0s — WIDE SHOT. The plane sits small in frame, drifting forward and bobbing as Manhattan tears 

past beneath it. The right cabin door slams open against the airstream and holds, juddering. The 

operator reacts off-camera and re-centers the plane with a small pan.




3.0–6.0s — SLOW ZOOM IN, starting exactly as she begins climbing out of the cockpit. Long uneven 

pinch-zoom with reframing hesitation and heavy digital-zoom mush — soft, noisy, smeared. She fights the 

airstream, one hand locked on the door frame, plants both feet on the wing and rises to standing.




6.0–8.0s — She lets go of the door frame and walks carefully out along the wing toward the tip, arms 

slightly out for balance, leaning hard into the wind, each step deliberate. The ponytail streams flat 

backward from under the headset, the tucked pink hoodie rippling tight against her torso with the 

lowered hood flapping hard against her back but staying down, wide cargo trousers hammering around her 

legs. She stops near the wingtip, plants her feet, and sets herself.




8.0–9.5s — She launches into a backflip: explosive jump straight up, knees tucking tight to chest, arms 

wrapped around her shins, full backward rotation in the air above the wing, body compact and controlled. 

The tucked hoodie stays completely inside the waistband through the whole rotation — no gap at the 

waist, no skin at the stomach, no skin at the lower back, torso and back fully covered while inverted. 

The hood stays down. Only the ponytail whips freely. Real gymnastic physics, natural arc, wind visibly 

dragging at her mid-rotation. The operator erupts — screaming and laughing in disbelief.




9.5–11.5s — She lands on both feet on the wing. The impact makes the aircraft lurch and wobble, the wing 

dipping and the whole airframe shuddering. She overbalances backward, arms flailing wide, genuinely 

about to fall off — then catches it, drops sharply into a deep crouch with knees bent low and both hands 

down on the wing surface, weight recovered. A held beat of real danger.




11.5–14.0s — SLOW ZOOM OUT begins. Staying low, she scrambles back along the wing, hauls herself up 

against the wind and ducks into the cabin, pulling the door shut behind her. Through the side window she 

turns, looks straight at the camera, smiles and waves.




14.0–16.0s — WIDE SHOT again. The aircraft banks hard into a fast right turn, wings tilting, and 

accelerates away over the city, visibly pulling ahead and shrinking as it outruns the camera. The 

operator pans to follow and falls behind, still laughing.




Bright natural midday sunlight, no color grading, phone-camera contrast, real urban haze. Photorealistic, 

natural physics, single continuous take, 4K, 24fps, authentic amateur smartphone footage. The hoodie 

stays tucked inside the waistband through every single frame — stomach and lower back remain fully 

covered from all angles, including the entire inverted rotation and the landing.
