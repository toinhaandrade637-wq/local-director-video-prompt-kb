Believable AI video starts before you animate. Build a clear character reference, establish the location, then direct each shot. This workflow turns those three ideas into a repeatable process with Seedream for stills and Seedance for motion.




1. Build a clean character reference

Open Higgsfield Image, choose Seedream, and upload your character image. Start with three useful views: a close-up, full body from the front, and full body from behind. Keep the outfit, proportions, and lighting consistent.

Create a reference sheet of the character in my uploaded image with exactly three panels: a close-up portrait, full-body front view, and full-body back view. Preserve the face, hairstyle, body proportions, clothing, and accessories. Use a neutral grey background and soft, even light across all panels. Natural skin and fabric texture. No text, extra poses, or outfit changes.

Check every panel against the original. Save the approved views as separate images as well as a sheet. Three views are a practical starting point, not a guarantee: add a clear side view when your shot needs it. Avoid contradictory references.

2. Give the scene its own references

Choose one location image, then use Seedream to build a wide view and two matching angles. Define where the characters and props sit, where the light comes from, and which landmarks stay visible.

Using my location image, create three views of the same set: a wide establishing view and two opposing medium views. Keep the furniture, landmarks, weather, time of day, and light direction consistent. Preserve believable spatial relationships. No people or text.

Compare the angles before continuing. A table should not jump across the scene, and the sun should not switch sides between cuts. Save the approved views separately.

3. Direct the frame in Seedream

Upload the relevant character and location references together. Generate one full-frame still per shot. For a conversation, plan a wide two-shot, a medium shot of each speaker, and a close-up reaction. Keep their screen positions and eyelines consistent.

A live-action cinematic still using my character and location references. [Shot size], [camera angle], [lens focal length] perspective. The character is [position and action], looking toward [eyeline]. Light comes from [specific source and direction]. Preserve identity, wardrobe, set layout, and natural skin texture. [Film look], restrained color, subtle grain. No text.

For example: an eye-level medium close-up, 50mm perspective, soft sunset light from camera-left, and a Kodak 250D-inspired color palette. Lens and film-stock names are visual cues, not literal capture settings or a realism switch. Fix hands, faces, and geometry before animation.

CREATE YOUR CINEMATIC FRAME →




4. Animate one approved shot at a time

Open Higgsfield Video, choose Seedance, and upload the approved still as a reference. Match the aspect ratio and start with a short motion test. For a 4:3 film, use 4:3 if available; otherwise compose safely for a 4:3 crop in your editor.

Animate the attached still as one continuous shot. The character [one simple action] with subtle breathing and natural timing. The camera [one precise movement or stays locked]. Preserve the face, outfit, eyeline, scene layout, and light direction. Add [one subtle environmental movement]. No cuts, exaggerated expressions, or new objects. Audio: quiet location ambience, no music.

For dialogue, add the exact line, identify the speaker, and allow enough time for natural speech. Split longer exchanges into separate clips instead of forcing fast delivery. Check the words and lip sync in every result.

ANIMATE YOUR SHOT →




5. Check continuity, then finish

Watch every clip at normal speed. Check face consistency, hands, contact with objects, shadows, and background stability. If something breaks, simplify that shot or repair its source still.

Assemble the approved clips in your video editor. Match color, preserve screen direction, add a continuous ambience bed, and place subtitles away from faces. Export an MP4 and watch the full export once more.

Your first test: one character, one location, three matching shots. Make those work before expanding the scene.
