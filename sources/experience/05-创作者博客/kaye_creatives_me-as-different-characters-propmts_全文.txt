16:9 HORIZONTAL. 8 SECONDS. ONE SHOT, ONE CAMERA, NO CUTS. One continuous unbroken take. Photoreal, cinematic, handheld, 35mm lens.




=== INPUTS ===

[tag your character sheet here] SHE IS THIS WOMAN. Take her face, her likeness, HER COMPLETELY SHAVEN HEAD with its fine dark shadow of stubble, and her costume: THE BURNT-ORANGE SAFFRON MONASTIC ROBES — a long-sleeved under-robe, an ankle-length lower robe, and A LARGE OUTER SHAWL WRAPPED ACROSS ONE SHOULDER AND UNDER THE OTHER ARM, its loose end falling long. Real coarse hand-dyed cotton with real creases and real uneven colour.

TAKE FROM image_1 ONLY her face, her head and this costume — nothing of the white studio background, NONE OF ITS FLAT STUDIO LIGHTING, none of the panel layout, AND NOT THE SANDALS. SHE IS COMPLETELY BAREFOOT.

@Image 2 THE LOCATION. Reproduce it exactly: the narrow sloping suburban back street with its grey asphalt and worn white lines, the grassy embankment and low kerb, the concrete utility poles and the dense web of black overhead cables crossing the sky, the pale white and dark brown two-storey houses stepping down the slope, the low green hills and the small town spread out below, the hazy summer sky — and, rising far beyond the rooftops, THE ENORMOUS PALE WHITE SEATED STATUE, soft and blue-shifted with distance, with the black cables passing in front of it.




=== THE SHOT ===

THE CAMERA IS AHEAD OF HER AND TRAVELLING BACKWARD DOWN THE HILL AT HER SPEED, at about chest height, three or four metres in front — AND IT IS SET OFF TO ONE SIDE, AT A THREE-QUARTER FRONT ANGLE, so we see HER FACE CLEARLY the whole time and also see past her up the street.

SHE RIDES TOWARD THE LENS. HER FACE IS THE SUBJECT.

BEYOND HER SHOULDER, ABOVE THE ROOFTOPS AND SLIGHTLY OFF TO ONE SIDE WHERE THE STREET BENDS, THE COLOSSAL WHITE STATUE STANDS IN THE HAZE, with the black cables crossing in front of it.

SHE IS RIDING A PLAIN WOODEN CRUISER SKATEBOARD — natural varnished top, black grip tape, soft cream wheels, dull metal trucks. NO GRAPHICS, NO PRINT, NO LOGOS, NO STICKERS ANYWHERE.

SHE IS BAREFOOT. Both bare feet on the deck, toes spread and gripping the grip tape. The hem of her lower robe hangs to her ankles and flutters just above the rolling wheels.

SHE RIDES EASILY AND CALMLY — knees soft, weight low, one shoulder slightly forward, arms loose and low. Cruising, not performing. NO TRICKS, NO JUMPS, NO FLIPS.

HER FACE IS SERENE AND SLIGHTLY ABSENT, eyes half-closed against the wind, completely at ease. SHE DOES NOT LOOK AT THE CAMERA — she looks past it, down the street, at where she is going.

HER SAFFRON ROBE IS THE ONLY SATURATED COLOUR IN THE FRAME. THE LOOSE END OF THE OUTER SHAWL IS PULLED BACK AND STREAMS OUT BEHIND HER, snapping and rippling in the air she is making, while the front of the robe is pressed flat against her body by it.




=== THE EIGHT SECONDS ===

0.0 – 2.0s — she is already rolling, coming down the slope toward the lens. Her face fills the middle of the frame. The robe streams back off her shoulder. The wires slide past overhead. The statue sits beyond her shoulder in the haze.

2.0 – 4.0s — SHE PUSHES ONCE. One bare foot lifts off the deck, swings down and pushes off the road in a long relaxed stroke, then comes back up onto the tail. She gains speed and closes slightly on the camera. Her robe pulls harder. She blinks slowly.

4.0 – 5.5s — SHE CARVES. One long slow lean across the empty street, the deck tilting up on its edge, her body angled, THE SHAWL SWINGING WIDE ACROSS THE FRAME and settling back. She comes back to centre.

5.5 – 6.5s — THE CAMERA SLOWS AND LETS HER CATCH IT. She comes on and PASSES CLOSE BY THE LENS — her shoulder and the streaming cotton filling the frame for a moment — AND THE CAMERA SWINGS ROUND WITH HER AS SHE GOES BY, one continuous turn, the street smearing past.

6.5 – 8.0s — NOW WE ARE BEHIND HER. She rolls away down the hill, her back to us, the shawl streaming out toward the lens, GETTING SMALLER as the street opens out — the houses falling away to reveal the town and the green hills below — AND THE COLOSSAL STATUE SITTING IN THE HAZE DEAD AHEAD OF HER. She rides straight toward it and does not stop. Hold to the last frame.




=== HOW THE CAMERA MOVES ===

IT IS HELD IN SOMEBODY'S HANDS AND IT IS ALIVE THE WHOLE TIME — a constant fine unsteadiness, the frame breathing, sitting a degree off level and correcting, a small bounce with the operator's own movement, drifting a few centimetres and being pulled back. Never violent, never whipping.

IT TRACKS HER SPEED IMPERFECTLY — falling a little behind when she pushes, catching up on the carve, so the distance between camera and subject changes slightly all the way through.

THE SWING AT 5.5–6.5s IS ONE CONTINUOUS PHYSICAL TURN, NOT A CUT. The street must be seen smearing across the frame as it turns.

No zoom, no tilt away, no orbit apart from that one turn.




=== HOW IT LOOKS ===

Photoreal, cinematic, shot on film. BRIGHT HAZY SUMMER LIGHT, high sun slightly diffused, everything washed and hot, short soft shadows. Muted colour with a faint green cast in the shadows — pale asphalt, dull white and brown houses, green embankment, soft blue-grey hills, milky sky. THE SAFFRON ROBE IS THE ONLY WARM SATURATED THING IN THE PICTURE.

Real asphalt with real tar seams and real grit, real faded road paint, real weathered concrete poles, real sagging cable, real coarse hand-dyed cotton, real skin with real pores and real light raking across the stubble of her scalp, real bare feet on real grip tape.

THE STATUE STAYS SOFT, HAZY AND BLUE-SHIFTED, clearly further away and less contrasty than the houses, WITH THE BLACK CABLES PASSING IN FRONT OF IT. It is never sharp, never pasted on, never a cutout.

Natural film colour, gentle contrast, soft filmic highlight roll-off, fine grain, real motion blur. No HDR, no digital sharpening, no oversaturation, no teal-and-orange grade, no lens flare.




=== SOUND, NO MUSIC ===

THE DOMINANT SOUND IS THE WHEELS — a continuous grainy rolling rumble on asphalt, changing pitch with the surface, rising as she gains speed, chattering over the road seams, with one clean scuff as her bare foot pushes off. IT PASSES THE MICROPHONE AND FALLS AWAY BEHIND IT at 6 seconds, and the whole soundfield turns with the camera.

Around it: A LOUD DENSE WALL OF CICADAS from the trees and the embankment, the flat empty air of a hot afternoon, a faint distant road hum, one far-off crossing bell.

Her breathing, quiet and even. The dry flap of heavy cotton in the wind.

No music, no score, no song, no voices, no narration.

SHE OCCUPIES THE LOWER THIRD OF THE FRAME AND SITS SLIGHTLY LEFT OF CENTRE. We see the back of the chair, the back of her sleek dark head, the structured brown shoulders of her jacket, and HER RIGHT ARM AND HAND RESTING ALONG THE ROLLED ARM OF THE CHAIR ON THE SIDE NEAREST US. She sits upright, composed, ankles crossed, completely at ease.

ON THAT ARMREST, JUST BESIDE HER HAND, LIES A SMALL DETONATOR: A PLAIN MATTE DARK GREY METAL BOX about the size of a paperback, with ONE LARGE DOMED RED BUTTON on its top face and nothing else. NO TEXT, NO NUMBERS, NO LABELS, NO LIGHTS, NO LOGOS, NO WIRES, NO ANTENNA.

BEYOND HER, THE UPPER TWO THIRDS OF THE FRAME: the green hills rolling away in layers to a LOW HORIZON, and above them the enormous blue sky with its white cumulus. THE HORIZON SITS LOW SO THAT THERE IS A GREAT DEAL OF SKY.




=== THE EIGHT SECONDS ===

0.0 – 2.0s — STILLNESS AND A BEAUTIFUL VIEW. She sits and watches. Nothing happens.

The wind moves across the turf in long slow waves, one after another, running away from us over the hills. A few loose strands of her hair lift at the temple. The peplum of her jacket stirs. The cumulus stands still on the horizon. It is idyllic and completely peaceful.

She does not move except to breathe.




2.0 – 3.0s — HER HAND MOVES. Unhurried, without looking at it, SHE SLIDES HER HAND ALONG THE ARMREST AND ONTO THE BOX. Her index finger comes to rest on the red button. She does not tense. She keeps looking out at the view.




3.0s — SHE PRESSES IT. The button goes down with a small firm travel and a click, and she lifts her finger off it.




3.0 – 3.8s — AND NOTHING HAPPENS. Eight tenths of a second of exactly the same peaceful landscape, the same wind in the grass, the same still clouds. THIS PAUSE IS DELIBERATE AND IT MUST BE THERE.




3.8 – 4.3s — THE FLASH. FAR AWAY ON THE HORIZON, BEYOND THE FURTHEST HILLS, A SILENT WHITE FLASH — for three or four frames THE ENTIRE FRAME IS BLEACHED FLAT WHITE and she is a hard black silhouette in the middle of it.

The white falls away — AND FOR A SECOND THE WHOLE GREEN LANDSCAPE IS FLOODED WITH HOT ORANGE LIGHT from that one point, every hill lit from the horizon, HER SHADOW AND THE CHAIR'S SHADOW THROWN LONG AND HARD BACK TOWARD THE CAMERA ACROSS THE GRASS. The green goes momentarily gold.




4.3 – 6.5s — THE COLUMN RISES, AND IT RISES SLOWLY BECAUSE IT IS ENORMOUS AND VERY FAR AWAY.

A brilliant orange-white fireball swells on the horizon, dims to a deep glowing orange, and A THICK BOILING COLUMN OF GREY-WHITE CLOUD CLIMBS UP OUT OF IT INTO THE BLUE SKY, its surface rolling over and over on itself as it goes. It spreads at the top into A WIDE FLAT MUSHROOM CAP that keeps growing outward. A low collar of dust rolls out along the ground beneath it.

IT IS COMPLETELY SILENT. There is still no sound at all except the wind.

IT IS FAR AWAY: soft-edged, slightly hazy, slightly blue-shifted at its edges, and it grows at the slow measured rate of something colossal seen from a great distance. It is never sharp, never fast, never close.

SHE DOES NOT MOVE. She watches it. Her hand goes back to resting on the armrest.




6.5 – 7.3s — AND THEN IT ARRIVES. FIRST THE SOUND: a vast deep rolling boom that starts as a crack and turns into a long low rumble, arriving several seconds after the light.

AND WITH IT THE SHOCKWAVE, AND WE SEE IT COMING BEFORE IT HITS: A VISIBLE WAVE RACING TOWARD US ACROSS THE HILLS, FLATTENING THE GRASS IN A HARD MOVING LINE, hill after hill going down and springing back.

IT REACHES HER — HER HAIR AND THE PEPLUM OF HER JACKET AND THE HEM OF HER SKIRT ARE BLASTED BACK AWAY FROM THE BLAST, the leather chair rocks and creaks, the turf around it lies flat — AND THE CAMERA IS HIT AND JOLTS HARD for a few frames.

She braces very slightly and that is all. She does not flinch, does not duck, does not turn.




7.3 – 8.0s — IT PASSES. The grass springs back up. Her hair settles. The chair stops rocking. The column goes on climbing and spreading on the horizon, silent again except for the last of the rumble rolling away.

She resettles her hand on the armrest, perfectly composed. HOLD ON THAT TO THE LAST FRAME.




=== HOW THE CAMERA MOVES ===

IT IS ESSENTIALLY STATIC — locked behind her shoulder, with only a faint live drift of a few millimetres, the way a held camera breathes. No pan, no tilt, no zoom, no push in, no orbit.

THE ONE EXCEPTION IS THE JOLT AT 6.5s, when the shockwave hits it: a hard shake for a few frames and then it settles back to exactly the same framing.




=== HOW IT LOOKS ===

Photoreal and cinematic. High bright midday light before the flash — vivid green turf with visible blade texture, deep blue sky, brilliant white cumulus, clean air. She is lit by that daylight and nothing else, and the flat white studio light of the reference appears nowhere.

Real heavy wool with real weight and real weave, real cracked leather with real patina, real matte metal on the box, real grass, real dust.

AFTER THE FLASH, THE ONLY WARM COLOUR IN THE FRAME IS THE FIREBALL AND THE LIGHT IT THROWS. The landscape returns to its own green as the fireball dims.

=== SOUND, NO MUSIC ===

AND THEN NOTHING — no explosion sound at all while the column rises. THE SILENCE OVER THOSE SECONDS IS THE POINT.

6.5–7.3s — AND THEN IT ARRIVES ALL AT ONCE: a hard cracking report followed immediately by A VAST DEEP ROLLING BOOM, low and enormous, overloading the microphone, with the roar of the pressure wave passing over the grass and the whip of cloth.

No music, no score, no voices, no speech, no screaming.
