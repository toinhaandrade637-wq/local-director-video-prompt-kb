Preserve the natural, observational feeling of preview.mp4 while using the timing and 16:9 compositions specified here. No title graphics, subtitles or narration.

SCENE 1 — MEDIUM SHOT, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, 0.0–8.0s




REFERENCES: @Image 4 = man; @Image 5 = woman; @Image 6 = Brooklyn waterfront. Kaye = @character_sheet1.




CAMERA: handheld at eye level, medium framing held on the couple and the gap between them. No zoom, pan or tilt. The hand tremor is nervous at the start, settles when Kaye steps between them, and ends almost still, with only breathing drift. Keep the hand exchanges visible.




LOCATION AND LIGHT: Brooklyn waterfront promenade beside the Manhattan Bridge, bright early afternoon. Railing and river behind the characters, bridge overhead, Manhattan skyline across the water. Natural side/back sunlight gives hair and shoulders a bright rim; soft skylight keeps faces readable. All three cast shadows in the same direction.




STAGING: the man stands frame-left with one hand on the railing, half-turned toward the water; the woman stands frame-right facing him, approximately an arm's length away. Tears are already on her cheeks. For this short version, the unlit cigarette is already in his free hand. Their feet remain on their spots. Kaye enters the gap from frame-right: grey-blue BAD AURA REMOVER coverall, white gingham-trimmed apron, yellow gloves, tissues and one red rose in the pocket. The spray bottle stays in her right hand; her left hand performs the exchanges. The compact vacuum stays secured at her hip, unused.




ACTION:

0.0–1.2 — she presses him for an answer, hands cutting the air; he avoids her eyes and looks toward the water. Kaye steps between them and assesses her face, then his.

1.2–2.2 — Kaye gives the woman a tissue with her left hand. The woman lowers her head and dabs her tears.

2.2–3.2 — Kaye takes the man's unlit cigarette with her left hand and slips it into her apron pocket.

3.2–4.6 — with the same hand, she retrieves the rose and places it in his empty hand. She holds his eyes, looks toward the woman, then back to him: understood? He finally looks at the woman.

4.6–5.8 — two short sprays above their heads. The backlit mist drifts sideways in the breeze; a gull on the railing takes off at the hiss.

5.8–6.3 — Kaye checks their faces and allows one small satisfied smile.

6.3–7.2 — she steps out frame-left. Both briefly look after her.

7.2–8.0 — they turn back to each other, the rose between them. Hold.




ACTING TASKS:

WOMAN: she wants one honest answer because his silence feels like abandonment. Her eyes search his face after each question. The tissue interrupts that search; at the end she simply waits for him to meet her gaze.

MAN: he wants to stay without saying something he cannot take back. His eyes escape to the river and ground while his feet refuse to leave. The rose interrupts that avoidance; he checks the woman's eyes to see whether it is safe to speak.

KAYE: this is one routine call in a long shift. She treats the problem like a surface to clean: assess, perform the procedure, check the result, move on. Her first smile comes only when the procedure has worked.

Both members of the couple are fighting to keep the relationship, not to end it. Natural blinks and engaged gaze throughout.




LIVING BACKGROUND AND WIND: gulls cross at different distances, traffic and a train move on the bridge, river chop catches sunlight, a ferry wake spreads. Distant pedestrians continue walking. Wind moves curls, jackets, apron, rose petals and spray. Background movement never hides the important exchanges.




SOUND: river wind, gulls, water, traffic and train rumble; the woman's fast indistinct voice, his breathing, two spray hisses, wings and departing footsteps. No music.

STYLE: cinematic live-action realism, readable skin texture, wet tear tracks, real fabric, rubber gloves, water and birds; natural colors and daylight.




SCENE 2 — LEFT-TO-RIGHT PAN, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, 8.0–13.0s




REFERENCES: @Image 8 = office; @Image 7 = giraffe.

LOCATION AND LIGHT: spacious daytime office with very high ceilings, panoramic mountain windows and the exceptionally long desk. Sun rays cross the room, patterned neck and desktop, with fine dust visible in the light.




STAGING: the giraffe sits at the left short end, body in profile, both hands on the keyboard. His neck leaves the collar and extends horizontally just above the clear desktop for approximately six meters. At the far right, his head hangs a few centimeters before the monitor, glasses low on the muzzle. The neck is already this length and shape in the first frame and remains anatomically continuous throughout.




ACTION:

8.0–8.7 — steady typing; the neck runs out of frame-right and the head is not yet visible.

8.7–11.8 — pan along the patterned fur and crossing sunlight toward the far end. The typing continues offscreen; mountains slide through the window background.

11.8–13.0 — arrive at the head and hold. His eyes stop at one figure, compare it with another place on the screen, briefly check the newly typed characters, then return to the suspicious figure. He mutters one indistinct word.




ACTING TASK — GIRAFFE:

An ordinary working day. His name goes on the document, so a missed mistake would be his. He wants to find the one thing that does not add up before sending it. The small text and his distant hands make the task difficult. He searches line by line, compares figures and quietly repeats one to hold it in memory. Natural blinks; ears turn toward small office sounds. No awareness that his anatomy is unusual.




SOUND: air-conditioning, keyboard clatter becoming more distant through the pan, then a soft indistinct whisper. No music.

STYLE: maximum naturalness, real fur, real weight, natural daylight and colors, photoreal live action.




SCENE 3 — WIDE TO CLOSE-UP, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, 13.0–17.5s




REFERENCES: @Image 9 = elderly woman; @Image 10 = phone booth and street.




CAMERA: handheld on one fixed wide-angle lens, 18–24mm. Begin side-on in a wide shot with the lettering readable, then move gently forward and around the booth's open edge. End close to her face with the street still wide and deep behind. Natural breathing and human drift, no mechanical stabilization, no cuts or zoom.




LOCATION AND LIGHT: New York sidewalk on an autumn evening just after sunset. Cool skylight on her face, a warm shop-window glow on one cheek, warm headlights and streetlights in depth. Available light only.




STAGING: the old open booth's side panel reads CALL / YOUR / MOM in black marker. The elderly woman stands in profile with the receiver at her ear and the handbag in the crook of her receiver arm. Tears are already on her cheeks; the free hand finishes a storytelling gesture.




ACTION:

13.0–14.4 — she is already delivering the end of a cheerful story and laughs. The camera begins its approach.

14.4–16.0 — the camera comes around toward her face. She tries to start another sentence, stops and listens to the receiver.

16.0–17.5 — close-up. No answer comes. She slowly raises her eyes toward the sky, keeping the receiver at her ear. Hold.




ACTING TASK — GRANDMA:

Her mother died long ago; this receiver is the one place where she still talks to her. She wants to tell mom everything and make her laugh as she once did. She fills pauses with stories so silence cannot answer. The laugh briefly waits for a laugh in return; the next sentence fails. She stops searching for her mother's face in front of her and looks upward. The tears are already there; she plays the cheerful call, not the crying. Natural blinking and precise listening.




WIND AND PASSERS-BY: cold wind moves her silver hair, scarf and coat hem; leaves race along the pavement. People hurry past independently in both directions, collars raised, never looking at her or the sign.




SOUND: wind, leaves, traffic, footsteps and distant siren; her warm indistinct voice and laughter. By the end, wind and breath become more prominent. No music.

STYLE: natural-light, intimate wide-angle photography in the spirit of Lubezki and Malick. Real wrinkles, pores, age spots, wet tear tracks, wool and knit texture; no smoothing or stylization.




SCENE 4 — MEDIUM SHOT WITH HANDHELD DOLLY OUT, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, 17.5–20.5s




REFERENCES: @Image 11 = cellist; @Image 12 = animal listeners; @Image 13 = tree and bench.




CAMERA: frontal, handheld documentary observation at seated eye level. Begin medium on the man and cello, then walk backward a few short steps to reveal the bench, dog and branch. End medium-wide. Small imperfect reframing and breathing, no pan, zoom or cuts.




LOCATION AND LIGHT: beneath the old park tree on a sunny summer day. Moving dappled sunlight falls across the musician, cello, bench and grass. Light wind stirs leaves, white hair and beard. Natural light only.




STAGING: cello between his knees, endpin planted in the grass, bow in his right hand, left fingers on the neck. The ordinary tabby sits on the bench to his left, screen-right. The dog sits on the grass at his feet to his right, screen-left. The robin perches on a low branch above his shoulder. All three face him.




ACTION:

17.5–19.5 — he is already playing a slow, warm phrase as the camera backs away. Bow hair stays on the strings between fingerboard and bridge; left fingers press and rock. His eyes check the dog, then the cat, with a brief glance toward the branch.

19.5–20.5 — hold the wider frame as he sustains the note. All three listeners have stayed; only now does he allow his eyes to close.




ACTING TASK — GRANDPA:

This is the audience that always stays until the end. He wants to keep all three through the last note, knowing a harsh stroke could send them away. He plays to each listener with a small checking glance, softening the phrase as needed. Never look into the camera.




LISTENERS: the dog follows the bow arm and tilts its head once; the cat slowly narrows its eyes, one ear turned toward the cello; the robin turns its head and makes one tiny hop along the branch. Calm breathing, blinking and subtle fur or feather movement; stillness never becomes a frozen image.




SOUND: one live, warm cello phrase with park air around it, leaves, breeze, distant voices and faint operator footsteps. No other music.

STYLE: observational documentary realism, natural colors, real aged skin and hands, fabric, fur and feathers, physically correct bow contact.




SCENE 5 — SIDE TRACKING SHOT, ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, 20.5–24.0s




REFERENCES: @Image 14 = Paris bridge; @Image 15 = hummingbirds. Kaye = @character_sheet2.

LOCATION AND LIGHT: bridge over the Seine in Paris at blue hour. Stone balustrade, ornate lit lamps, river, bridges, Haussmann buildings and Eiffel Tower. Cool dusk light falls on her; passing lamps cast moving warm pools over her and the birds.




STAGING AND WIND: Kaye rides a classic city bicycle left to right, both hands on the handlebars, leaning slightly into the speed. Red smock, green mid-calf skirt, cream stockings, T-strap shoes, round glasses and teal beret match @character_sheet2. Her hair streams back and fabric flutters. The loose skirt remains clear of chain and spokes; tires stay grounded and pedal strokes drive wheel rotation.




ACTION:

20.5–21.5 — she is already riding briskly, a few hummingbirds beside her at shoulder height. A quick check toward one bird, then back to the clear road.

21.5–22.6 — one bird matches her forward speed just above her hand, briefly hovering relative to the moving bicycle, then darts ahead. Her eyes follow it and return to the road.

22.6–24.0 — more birds rise into a loose, glittering ring above her, traveling with her. She glances up briefly, smiles and looks back to the road, continuing to pedal.




ACTING TASK — KAYE:

This is her hour: the day is over and nobody needs anything from her. She wants to keep the flock with her to the end of the bridge without losing her riding line. HUMMINGBIRDS: tiny real bodies, thin beaks, fast blurred wingbeats and iridescent emerald, turquoise, violet and ruby feathers. Independent darts, hovering adjustments and lateral accelerations, never gliding or moving as a rigid formation. Their overall forward movement keeps pace with the bicycle.




SOUND: wind rush, tires, chain, distant city, close wing buzz and a few thin chirps. No music.

STYLE: cinematic photoreal live action, natural dusk colors, real skin, fabric, hair, bicycle mechanics and hummingbird flight.
