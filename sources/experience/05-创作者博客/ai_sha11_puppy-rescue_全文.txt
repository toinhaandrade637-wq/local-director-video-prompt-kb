Puppy Rescue is a short film told in five AI clips. On a rainy night, a girl stops for a puppy that everyone else walks past. A year later, he leads her back to the same spot.

Before You Start

Build every asset before you generate a single video: the characters, the locations and the props. They appear in every scene, and locking them first is what keeps five separate AI clips looking like one film.

Prompts: Claude wrote every prompt. The video prompts came from the Seedance Shotlist Director skill, which turns a scene into a structured Seedance prompt. Higgsfield shares the skill free in this breakdown post.
Images: generated in Higgsfield with Soul Cinema, Seedream 5.0 pro and Image 2.
Video: every clip generated in Higgsfield with Seedance, vertical 9:16.
Names: each asset is saved as a Higgsfield Element with an @name, like @dry or @street. The prompts call assets by those names, so name yours exactly the same.

Every asset below is either a reference sheet or an empty location plate. Attach them to your video prompts so the model copies them instead of guessing.

The style header

What it does: The top of every video prompt locks the look: the light, the colour rule, the lens, the acting and the physics. All five clips reuse it, so they feel like one film. It also keeps the sound to effects only; the music goes in during the edit.

Style: 8K IMAX. Photorealistic — no 3D render, no game engine.
Lighting: Motivated light only — every light has a visible source: street lanterns, a table lamp, the sun. Contre-jour backlight, camera on the shadow side, atmospheric haze or rain mist for depth. No unmotivated fill light.
Color: 60:30:10 — dominant / secondary / accent. Rust-orange and terracotta belong only to the girl and her world: they are always the warm accent.
Camera: Physical cine lens. 180° shutter motion blur.
Skin: Pore-level realism — natural freckles, vellus hair, capillary flush, pore-shadow matching on-set light.
Acting: Hollywood — micro-pauses before reactions, precise eye-line, living eyes with catch-lights, chest rise from breathing. Characters never standing, always reacting.
Physics: Gravity and inertia respected — mass has real weight, correct contact shadows. No floating props.

Assets first. If your character sheets and locations are messy, your film will be messy.
Test at low quality first. Problems show up just as clearly in a cheap low-resolution test, so save your credits for the final.
