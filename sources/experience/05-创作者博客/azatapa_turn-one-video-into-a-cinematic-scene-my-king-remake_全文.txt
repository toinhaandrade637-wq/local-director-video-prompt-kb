One reference. New angles. More emotion.

I recreated a legendary arm-wrestling moment as a cinematic scene, using one video reference. Here is the workflow and the exact prompt behind the remake.

The 12-second reference

I trimmed the original recording to the moments I wanted to keep. This 12-second clip was the only generation reference: no photos and no character sheets.

The cinematic result

The scene runs for approximately 20 seconds. Close-ups, reactions and new camera positions give the moment more room to breathe. This is an AI recreation of the reference, finished with my edit in After Effects.

Recreate the workflow
Trim your reference. Keep the essential action and emotional beats. My input was 12 seconds long.
Open Cinema Studio in Higgsfield. Choose Seedance 2.5 and upload the video as the reference.
Direct the sequence. Paste the prompt below and set a 20-second duration. The reference anchors the people, setting and action; the prompt asks for new angles and timed story beats.
Review and refine. Check faces, hands, continuity and the timing of each reaction. Regenerate or adjust the prompt when a shot drifts.
Finish in After Effects. I shaped the final colour, grain and sound in the edit.
Why 12 seconds became 20

The extra time comes from added shots and reactions, rather than simply slowing the source down. The prompt gives the father, the athlete and the daughter their own close-ups, so the tension builds before the release.

This prompt describes the story in my example. For a different clip, keep the timed-shot structure and rewrite the people, actions and emotional beats to match your reference.

Copy the video prompt

Use one video reference. No separate image references are needed. Copy the prompt below, then open Cinema Studio with Recreate.

Street arm wrestling. A regular man vs. a bodybuilder. His daughter continuously cheers. In this cinematic recreation, the athlete intentionally yields. 20 seconds, 9 shots.




The uploaded reference video defines the characters, setting, and motion dynamics. Do not repeat the original camera angles. Keep the characters and action consistent across shots.




Controlled nine-shot sequence with hard cuts at 2.0s, 3.0s, 4.0s, 7.0s, 9.0s, 11.0s, 14.0s, and 17.0s. Independently select varying cinematic camera angles and shot sizes for each segment. Extend the scene through reaction shots and story beats at natural playback speed.
