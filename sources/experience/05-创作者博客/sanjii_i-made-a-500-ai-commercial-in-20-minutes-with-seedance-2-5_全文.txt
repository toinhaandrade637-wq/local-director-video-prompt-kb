Before You Start

This is the full prompt library for a 30-second food commercial: a colossal industrial road roller drives over a cheeseburger, and what comes out the other side is a pizza. Sixteen shots, one Seedance 2.5 generation, built out of exactly seven reference images.




Build the assets first. Two food scientists, the hangar, the burger, the pizza, the road roller and the client's logo all come back over and over across those sixteen shots — if each shot invents its own version of them, the commercial falls apart. Lock them once as images, then reuse them.




The stills were made with GPT Image 2 (products), Higgsfield Soul character sheets (the scientists), Soul Cinema (the location) and Seedream 5.0 Pro (the machine). The commercial itself is Seedance 2.5 at 1080p.




Everything is driven by a Claude Skill, and this is the part worth saying plainly: the Skill writes the video prompts for you. You describe the commercial in a couple of plain sentences, the Skill turns it into the full shooting script — references tagged to the right shots, camera, lenses, timing, physics, lighting, sound and the consistency locks at the bottom.

SKIL




Three things that decided how this one turned out, before you start prompting:

Design the before and after together. The burger sits in a small kraft box; the pizza sits in the same box opened flat. Keep the packaging identical and the transformation reads on its own.
If something keeps breaking between shots, give it its own rule. Describing the box more carefully inside each shot never fixed it. A single hard lock — the carton never moves or turns — did.
Every prompt has a complexity budget. More emotion, more camera variety and more elaborate food shots each sounded like an upgrade, and together they made the generations worse. When a version works, ship it.

The Location — Industrial Food Hangar

What it does: Generates the massive industrial food-testing hangar the whole commercial takes place in. Three things have to be right in the plate, and they matter more than how pretty it is: a wide open lane down the centre with the lab equipment pushed to the sides, cool industrial light inside against bright daylight through the open door, and a smooth polished concrete floor with nothing in the roller's path. Every stray cable or rock is one more object the video model has to simulate under a four-metre steel drum.

Photorealistic high-end commercial production still, empty location shot, no people in frame.




SETTING: A massive double-height industrial testing hangar inside a corporate food-science research compound. Walls of corrugated steel paneling painted muted charcoal grey, exposed dark-grey structural steel columns and beams. Expansive peaked roof supported by a grid of exposed steel trusses and rafters, with skylights running along the ridge letting natural daylight filter down. Smooth light-grey polished concrete floor with minor scuffs, subtle reflections and practical working-facility imperfections — clean but utilitarian, not a pristine white lab.




LAYOUT: The wide central lane of the hangar is completely clear, wide enough for heavy machinery to pass through. Along the left and right walls, rows of stainless-steel laboratory workbenches are arranged as food-chemistry stations, receding deep into the background for strong depth layering (foreground / midground / background).




PROPS: Lab benches with polished stainless-steel frames and tabletops, loaded with scientific glassware — beakers, Erlenmeyer flasks, test-tube racks, graduated cylinders filled with colorful liquids (yellow, green, red). Grow-light stands with trays of fresh green herbs, baskets of ripe red tomatoes, whole onions, jars of pickles. LIGHTING: High-contrast cinematic lighting. COLOR PALETTE: Cool industrial tones — slate grey, metallic steel, concrete blue — punctuated by pops of vibrant color: amber hazard lights, green lab plants, red tomatoes, colorful chemical liquids.

The Road Roller

What it does: Generates the machine reference — a clean, oversized tandem road roller on a seamless background. The same prompt was run across four image models and Seedream 5.0 Pro held the steel, hydraulics and mechanical detail best. Note that the asset doesn't need to look four metres tall: the reference controls identity, the video prompt controls scale.

Photorealistic studio shot of a colossal, exaggerated industrial tandem road roller (asphalt compactor) on a seamless light gray background. The machine is deliberately oversized and menacing: the FRONT steel drum is enormous — roughly twice the diameter of the rear drum and taller than the operator cabin's floor line, a huge cylindrical wall of worn yellow-tan steel with scratches, ground-in dust and faint rust streaks. The rear drum is visibly smaller, making the front drum read as the dominant mass of the vehicle. Deep navy-blue painted steel body built thick and heavy: oversized bolted steel plates, chunky hinges, massive tow points, vents and access panels, subtle realistic wear and dust on the paint. Enclosed glass operator cabin on top looks small compared to the front drum, small orange beacon light above the cabin, side mirrors. Absolutely no logos, no branding, no text anywhere on the vehicle.




Camera: dramatic low-angle three-quarter view from near ground level, positioned closer to the front drum so it looms large in the foreground while the body recedes — wide-angle 24mm perspective with mild foreshortening that exaggerates the front drum's scale, whole machine still fully in frame, everything in sharp focus.




Lighting: soft diffused studio lighting with slightly stronger top light so the drum's curvature reads as heavy rolled steel; gentle gradient on the cylinder, soft but grounded contact shadow under both drums — the shadow pressed flat as if the floor carries real weight.




Style: photorealistic, clean product-asset look, 8k detail, physically plausible materials — brushed worn steel drum, matte painted body, glass with faint reflections.

Macros: one warm hard key raking the toppings, glints on melted cheese, background in dark soft bokeh.




AUDIO

SFX only: echoing footsteps in a huge hall; the carton set down with a papery tap; pen scratches on clipboards; a heavy ignition click, gauges ticking, a deep diesel awakening; slow hydraulic hiss and the heavy grind of steel drums over smooth concrete; a swallowing sub-bass roll as the drum covers the burger; near-silence with receding engine for the reveal; hurried footsteps converging in S10; wet sizzles and soft topping impacts for the macros; a low warm room tone under the endcard.




POSITIVE LOCKS

In S1 the scientist enters from frame-left, takes a couple of steps and places the burger at frame-right, carton open toward himself; legs and hands only, lowering on one bent knee — never squatting. The roller is COLOSSAL in every shot and always creeps slowly — never fast. The engine starts only at the S5 button press. The floor is always clean smooth polished concrete — no gravel, no rubble, no litter. The transformation happens hidden under the machine: burger before S6, pizza from S9 onward, never both in frame. The flattened box in S9–S14 is the same kraft board as the carton in S1–S6. @图片 1 and @图片 2 keep identical faces, glasses, coats and gloves in every appearance. Clipboards are matte grey with the @图片 7 logo on the back — no red clipboards. The @图片 7 logo appears nowhere else except the final endcard — no other logos or text in frame. S7 and S8 are the only handheld segments — real human shake on both. The background stays alive in every wide and medium lab shot: bench work plus walking scientists. The roller is the only vehicle.




Photoreal. Cinematic. 16:9. SFX only, no music, no subtitles, no human voice. Spherical lenses, natural round bokeh — subject razor sharp, background melting one gentle step soft — clean glass, no streaks, no flares. Natural grain, organic color, soft contrast, filmic look. Camera: confident and alive — every shot moving from its first frame: whips, crash zooms, dollies, arcs — mechanically smooth, never shaky, never static.
