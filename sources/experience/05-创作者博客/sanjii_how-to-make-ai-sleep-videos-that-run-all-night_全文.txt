Why sleep videos break the algorithm

Sleep videos win on the one metric YouTube rewards most: average view duration. On a normal video, holding someone for four minutes is a big win. With a sleep video, the viewer clicks play, turns off the lights and lets it run all night.

Most YouTube gurus tell you to hustle nonstop, burn through AI credits and fight for every second of watch time. This corner of YouTube skips that fight. This guide covers the whole process, from picking a niche to uploading a finished two-hour video. Every visual is made inside Higgsfield, and the music comes from code Claude writes for you. Step 1: Pick a niche with high demand and low competition

The biggest search term is almost always a trap. Beginners chase the highest-volume keywords; the money is where monthly searches are large and competition (scored 0 to 100) sits near the bottom.

vidIQ keyword research, October 2026 · 10 keywords

Two kinds of keywords look tempting and fail:

Overheated. "White noise", "rain sounds" and "relaxing jazz" have huge volume, but legacy giants like Relaxing White Noise (4.5 million subscribers) have locked them down.
Too small and shrinking. "Spaceship ambience" has low competition, but search interest just dropped 71%. "Medieval tavern ambience" scores only 18 on competition, yet interest is down 39%.

Low competition on its own is useless. A niche is worth entering only when three things hold at once:

High search volume: hundreds of thousands of searches a month.
Low competition: every winning keyword below scores 35 or less.
Positive growth: search interest rising over the last 30 days.

Generation 1: The Scene

The scene is your product: viewers buy it with their eyes. Open Higgsfield, set the aspect ratio to 16:9 and use Nano Banana 2.1 at 4K resolution, or the Soul Location model.

Three golden rules for the scene:

The window rule. Inside is warm and safe; outside is raw weather. "Rain on window" gets 338,000 searches a month on its own, because that contrast triggers deep comfort.
One story anchor. Add a single object that brings the room to life: a sleeping cat, an open book on a blanket or a steaming ceramic mug.
Zero people. An empty room lets viewers project themselves into the space.

Step 3: Assemble a 1.5–3 hour video

You do not need a 10-hour file. The 803K-view Rain + Fireplace video is only 1 hour 31 minutes long, and other recent top performers run about 3 hours.

Take your loop and your music into any editor (CapCut, Premiere) and repeat them along the timeline until you reach two to three hours. Or ask Claude to run two ffmpeg commands for you:

# 1. Re-encode the 30-second loop at 8 Mbps (takes seconds)
ffmpeg -i loop.mp4 -c:v libx264 -b:v 8M -c:a aac loop_8m.mp4

# 2. Repeat video and music to 3 hours, piano mixed under the rain
ffmpeg -stream_loop -1 -i loop_8m.mp4 -stream_loop -1 -i piano.wav -filter_complex "[0:a][1:a]amix=inputs=2:weights=1 0.5[a]" -map 0:v -map "[a]" -c:v copy -c:a aac -t 3:00:00 video_3h.mp4

Three hours at 1080p comes out around 11 GB. It exports fast and will not overload your machine.

Mid-roll ads off is what keeps the title honest. Claim "No Ads" and then play a pre-roll, and viewers feel misled. "No Mid-Roll Ads" is 100% true, and nobody gets blasted awake by an ad at 3 AM.

One video can be enough to get monetized. The YouTube Partner Program needs 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours. The 803K-view video, even at a modest 10-minute average view duration, generated over 130,000 watch hours: more than 30 times the threshold.

Keep each upload distinct: change the room, the weather and the angle rather than reposting the same scene.
