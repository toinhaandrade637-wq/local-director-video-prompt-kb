I built a Japanese sushi alleyway in Unity and walked a first-person camera through it for thirty seconds. Narrow corridor, paper lanterns, roller shutters, a few bar counters, no people. Then I ran it through Genjutsu three times to see how far the location could be pushed while keeping the walk.

In this tutorial we are testing Higgsfield's new model where we are using a game engine's basic 3D  first person walkthrough and transforming it into 3 different worlds

STEP 1: Upload recording of first person walkthrough built from any game engine (i used Unity)

Most Genjutsu prompt sets you see are built around a character: a hero still, an opponent still, a two-shot. That format assumes there is a body in frame to map the motion onto. Mine had no body. The camera was the character. Everything below came out of solving that.

STEP 2: reference images

Prompts alone give you a different trader at every counter. Genjutsu takes up to forty reference images and that is the lever. I generate stills first: four environment plates per world — corridor wide, stall interior, overhead canopy, material close-up — and character sheets.

STEP 3: PROMPTS
Attaching the references images, follow the format of these prompts for each version.
Version 1 — The Ember Souk

THE EMBER SOUK — carved cliff-city





1. shot on ARRI Alexa Mini LF, 32mm anamorphic, photorealistic

first-person POV, preserve source camera motion exactly, handheld

walking cadence, hard low sun shafts raking down a narrow canyon slot,

warm ember key from forge openings, high dynamic range, ochre-and-brass

grade with deep crushed shadows, volumetric ash and forge smoke,

subsurface skin scattering, visible pores and sweat, wet stone

specular, shallow depth of field, natural motion blur, subtle film

grain, IMAX-scale production design, 8K detail



2. Camera and eyeline — first person, no character in frame

Continuous forward walk down a corridor street barely two arm-spans

wide. No hands, no arms, no weapon, no body visible. Camera turns

into stall openings on both sides and holds, then tilts up toward a

slot of sky. Preserve the original walking rhythm and every head

turn exactly.



3. Near-wall layer

Sheer carved sandstone facades of a monolithic cliff-city, five

storeys of hand-cut window recesses receding upward. Ground level:

hammered brass roller shutters half-drawn, dyed-wool banners in deep

crimson and saffron hanging in the doorways, copper lantern-globes on

iron hooks throwing ember light, relief panels cut with a fictional

angular glyph script, soot streaks and tally marks, hand-ground

ochre pigment.



4. Overhead layer

Stone footbridges and rope walkways span the canyon slot high above,

strung with drying indigo cloth and copper chimes. Forge ash and dust

drift down through hard white sun shafts. A thin ribbon of

blown-out sky at the very top. Scrub and roots growing from the

stone joints, backlit gold.



5. Inhabitants — seated at counters and distant only

ANCHOR: a tally-keeper seated at a stone counter. Her left shoulder

and upper arm are sheathed in overlapping brass scales, and every

scale is stamped with a different debt glyph — she wears her ledger

on her body. Right arm bare and soot-black to the elbow. Ochre linen

wrap, heat-cracked leather apron, a belt of iron counting-rings.

Woven conical ash-hood with a wide brim that sheds falling ash,

smoked-quartz lenses on a brass brow-band pushed up onto it.

Weathered face lit from below by a bed of coals. Still, unhurried,

may glance briefly toward the lens as it passes — no held stare.

SECONDARY: forge workers at the other counters wearing brass

half-masks with a coiled ceramic filter cartridge across the mouth,

throats wrapped in layered ash-cloth, forearms bound in scorched

wrapping, hands scarred.

DISTANT: robed figures under conical hoods moving at the far end of

the corridor, backlit in the sun shafts, small in frame,

never in close-up.

Version 2 — The Drowned Court

THE DROWNED COURT — sunken coral civilization




1. shot on ARRI Alexa Mini LF, 32mm anamorphic, photorealistic

first-person POV, preserve source camera motion exactly, cold cyan key

from above with warm bioluminescent practicals, caustic light crawling

across every surface, high dynamic range, teal-and-pearl grade with

deep retained shadow detail, suspended particulate in the beams,

subsurface skin scattering, wet nacre specular, shallow depth of

field, natural motion blur, subtle film grain, 8K detail




2. Camera and eyeline — first person, no character in frame

Continuous wade forward through ankle-deep water down a narrow flooded

arcade. No hands, no arms, no body visible. Water surface below the

lens reflecting every light source. Camera turns into side alcoves and

holds, then tilts up to the vaulting. Preserve the original walking

rhythm and head turns exactly.




3. Near-wall layer

Ancient masonry overgrown with living coral — brain coral and fan

coral in pale jade and bone-white, mother-of-pearl inlay catching

light. Barnacle-crusted bronze shutters, kelp-strand curtains drifting

in a slow current across the doorways, jellyfish held in blown-glass

lantern spheres pulsing soft cyan, anemone colonies glowing in the

shape of shop marks, a fictional flowing glyph script scratched into

shell plaster.




4. Overhead layer

Vaulted arches built from colossal whale ribs, coral-fused, draped in

trailing sea grass. Far above, the underside of a distant water

surface fracturing sunlight into shifting caustic webs. Translucent

siphonophores hanging down into frame, faintly luminous. Slow bubbles

rising.




5. Inhabitants — seated at counters and distant only

ANCHOR: a lung-keeper seated at a coral counter. Head sealed in a

blown-glass and bronze helmet dome, and inside the dome, behind the

skull, a living jellyfish that provides all their light — the figure

is lit from behind their own head, a soft cyan pulse through the hair.

Face visible through the front glass, pale, a milky nictitating

membrane sliding across the eyes. Cloak of grafted living kelp that

drifts and shifts even in still air. Waxed cord binding the forearms.

Slow, deliberate, may glance briefly toward the lens — no held stare.

SECONDARY: tide-readers at the other counters, elders with pale coral

grown into the collarbone and shoulder over decades, hardened into a

plated mantle. Oiled sealskin wraps, blistered hands, salt crust in

the seams.

DISTANT: rounded silhouettes with faintly glowing glass domes wading

at the far end of the arcade, small in frame, never in close-up.

Version 3 — The Spore Bazaar

THE SPORE BAZAAR — mycelial undercity




1. shot on ARRI Alexa Mini LF, 32mm anamorphic, photorealistic

first-person POV, preserve source camera motion exactly, no daylight,

bioluminescent key from glowing gills and spore vessels, magenta and

violet dominant with jade accents, warm amber practicals at counter

level, high dynamic range, deep crushed blacks with retained shadow

detail, heavy spore particulate catching every light source,

subsurface skin scattering, damp organic specular, shallow depth of

field, natural motion blur, subtle film grain, 8K detail




2. Camera and eyeline — first person, no character in frame

Continuous forward walk down a corridor between colossal living fungal

stalks. No hands, no arms, no body visible. Damp packed-earth path

underfoot with reflective wet patches. Camera turns into alcoves and

holds, then tilts up into the canopy. Preserve the original walking

rhythm and head turns exactly.




3. Near-wall layer

Trunks of vast mushrooms three metres thick, pale grey-violet flesh

with deep vertical fissures, doorways and windows carved directly

into the living tissue and edged with hardened resin. Mycelial threads

webbing the gaps between stalks, faintly pulsing. Hanging shelf-fungi

used as awnings, undersides glowing magenta. Blown-glass vessels of

luminous spores swinging on cords in place of lanterns. Shop marks

burned into the flesh in a fictional looping glyph script.




4. Overhead layer

A closed canopy of enormous interlocking mushroom caps far above,

undersides a dense radial architecture of luminous gills in magenta

and pale jade, casting patterned light down the corridor. Hanging root

chandeliers and dripping resin strands. Constant drifting spore motes,

thick in the light shafts, some catching and flaring. No sky visible

at any point.




5. Inhabitants — seated at counters and distant only

ANCHOR: a spore-cultivator seated at a counter of dried fungal matter

polished to a horn sheen. Their left forearm is sealed inside a fitted

glass sleeve containing a deliberately cultivated luminous fungal

colony — living inventory and lamp in one, throwing magenta light up

onto the underside of their face. Across the shoulders a carved

wooden yoke hung with stoppered vessels of glowing cultures, so the

figure sits inside a personal halo. Layered charcoal and plum robes,

apron of pressed specimen sheets stitched together. Tall, pale, still,

may glance briefly toward the lens — no held stare.

SECONDARY: veil-breathers at the other counters, whole face covered

by a fine mesh veil weighted at the hem with brass beads to keep

spores from the lungs, eyes visible through the mesh, gloves with

the fingertips cut away, fingertips stained luminous jade.

DISTANT: hooded silhouettes with faint halos of hanging vessels

moving at the far end of the corridor, small in frame,

never in close-up.
