CHARACTER

Full-body studio lookbook fashion photo, straight-on eye-level shot of a young woman in her early twenties standing upright against a seamless white cyclorama, looking directly at the camera with a neutral expression. Choppy reddish-brown bob with blunt bangs, softly lit face. Single vertical 4:5 frame.




PROPORTIONS: average height and natural, realistic everyday body proportions — roughly 7 heads tall, NOT an elongated fashion-model or runway physique. Normal leg-to-torso ratio, relaxed natural stance. The camera is at true chest height and level (not low, not tilted up), shot on a normal ~50mm-equivalent lens so her legs are not stretched or lengthened.




OUTFIT: a crisp, fitted short-sleeve T-shirt with horizontal stripes in blue, yellow, and white, the knit stretching subtly across the arms and torso. A brown A-line miniskirt in brushed matte cotton/twill with a gentle flare. Glossy blue knee-high boots in smooth leather with a fine grain and soft wrinkling at the ankles. Both hands empty, arms slightly bent in a relaxed, casual pose.




TEXTURE NOTES: the tee reads as smooth cotton jersey with clean printed stripes; the skirt as soft brushed matte twill with no sheen; the boots as glossy supple leather with subtle creasing.




SETTING: seamless white cyclorama studio backdrop — the white floor sweeps up into the white wall with no visible corner or horizon line, pure clean white all around.




LIGHTING: flat, even, bright studio lighting — no directional or editorial light, evenly illuminated with minimal shadow, only a soft contact shadow at her feet.




AESTHETIC: crisp and clean, sharp focus throughout, moderate depth of field, no film grain and no digital artifacts, neutral observational mood.




HEX — garments: blue stripe/boots #51a1bf, white #f2f2eb, skirt brown #ad6955 #b39283 #d8bb99, muted red-brown #774e52 (hair/accents). Yellow stripe: a warm mid yellow.

SCENE

A girl sits alone in an old train carriage, seen from behind, watching the world race past her window. Outside, a forest tears by. Without any visible moment of change, that forest becomes the deep ocean. Gravity quietly stops working inside the car and she drifts up out of her seat, rising slowly through the carriage until she is floating high near the ceiling. Fifteen seconds, one unbroken shot.




REFERENCES

@REF_SHOT: the source for the light and the location. Take from it the entire lighting behaviour, the carriage interior, and the world outside the windows in both halves. Match its wedges of sunlight running down the seat crowns, their speed, their rhythm, their direction, their contrast, their colour. Match its carriage: the pale ribbed ceiling with luggage racks, the blue-grey bench seats with pale crowns, the chrome grab rails, the large split windows in pale frames, the dark aisle, the far door, the worn painted metal walls. Match its forest and its deep blue water, and match how one becomes the other without a visible boundary.

@GIRL: the child in the seat. Her face, hair, build and clothing come from this reference and from nothing else. The plain backdrop of the reference is discarded; she exists only inside the carriage.

@GIRL is the only person in the shot.




THE CAMERA

A wide master, locked off on the centre line of the aisle, at a height just above the seat backs, looking straight down the car in symmetric one-point perspective toward the far door. The whole length of the carriage is in frame with generous headroom overhead.

It never moves. No dolly, no push-in, no zoom, no pan, no tilt. It does not follow her as she rises; she moves inside a static frame.

The camera is bolted to the carriage, so it carries the train's shaking exactly as the interior does. Camera and car tremble as one object.




THE GIRL

She sits on the LEFT bank of seats, in a window seat mid-depth in the car, roughly seven meters from camera, with clear space above her.

SHE IS SEEN FROM BEHIND for the whole take. The camera never sees her face: not directly, not in profile, not in three-quarter, not reflected in the glass. What it sees is the back of her head, the shape of her hair, her shoulders above the pale seat crown, silhouetted against the bright window beside her. Her head is turned slightly toward the glass, the way a passenger sits when watching the world go by.

She is a real child on a real journey, not a performer. Everything she does is small, ordinary and unconscious. She just sits and looks out of the window. Her head sways with the car and settles. She shifts in her seat. Once she pushes hair back behind her ear and lets her hand drop. She follows something outside with her eyes and lets it go.

No posing. No dramatic beats. No looking at camera. No performing.




THE LIGHT

The sun sits low off the RIGHT side of the track, constant and unchanging, and the train races past objects that keep cutting it. Every gap between those objects throws one wedge of sunlight into the car, and because the objects come many times a second there are always several wedges inside the carriage at once, at different points along its length.

The light is not a global brightness change. It is discrete moving shapes travelling through a three-dimensional space. A wedge enters through the windows, crosses the car, and lands as a hard-edged diagonal band on the pale seat crowns, descending toward screen-left at about thirty degrees. It does not light the whole row at once: it hits one seat back, then slides onto the next, then the next, running down the row toward camera and out of frame in a fraction of a second.

Because several wedges are in the car simultaneously, in any given frame different seats are in completely different states: one crown blown white, the next half-lit with the edge crossing it, the next in deep shade, the next just catching the leading edge of another wedge. Every frame is a mosaic of separate brightnesses down the row, never a uniform exposure.

Each wedge rises to full brightness in a single frame, holds for one or two, and is gone in one more, with no afterglow. Some are single-frame stabs.

Wedges cross other surfaces independently and at their own positions: bands running along the ceiling ribs, slanted patches crossing the dark aisle floor, flares crossing the window frames and wall pillars. They do not move together.

Exposure is set for the shadows. Lit crowns clip to pure white with no detail; shadow sides crush to near black with no detail. Extreme contrast in every frame. Cold grade, shadows pushed blue, highlights cool white with no warmth, real film grain.

This is natural sunlight interrupted by passing objects. It is not a strobe light, not a flashing lamp, not an effect. Nothing inside the carriage emits light; the ceiling fixtures are off.

The light never pauses. There is no frame in the fifteen seconds without something moving across the surfaces. Between the brighter wedges there are always weaker ones still crossing: dim bands, half-strength edges, rolling shadow. No dark opening, no calm establishing beat, no quiet ending.




LIGHT AND SHADOW MODEL THE FORMS

The light does not just brighten things; it sculpts them.

The pale rounded crowns of the seat backs catch a wedge first and hold a brilliant curved highlight along their top while the dark body below stays in shade, so each seat reads as a solid rounded object rising out of blackness.

The chrome grab rails catch hard specular glints and throw thin curved shadows onto the seats behind them.

Each seat back casts its own shadow into the row behind it: lit crown, dark gap, lit crown, dark gap, receding into the depth of the car.

The ribbed ceiling picks up light in its grooves, bright on the ridges and dark in the channels. The luggage racks throw long shadows down the walls. Window frames and pillars cast hard shadow bars into the interior.

When a wedge crosses @GIRL it wraps her from behind: a brilliant rim along the top of her shoulder and the outer edge of her hair, the rest of her black. Her shadow moves across the seats in front of her with every new beam.

Nothing is ever lit flat. Every lit surface has a bright side, a turning edge and a shadow side.

The air is thick with fine dust, so every wedge is a solid visible shaft in the volume of the car, not just a patch on a surface.




THE TRAIN

It runs at full line speed for the entire take, in the forest and underwater alike. It never slows, never brakes, never coasts, never stops.

Outside the glass everything is a violent horizontal smear at all times: dark verticals ripping past many times a second, nothing close to the track holding any readable shape.

The carriage shakes like a real train in every frame. A constant fine high-frequency tremor through every surface. A slow lateral hunting sway that rolls the car a degree or two and comes back, irregular and unrhythmic. And hard vertical jolts at rail joints every couple of seconds that kick the frame, shudder the seat backs, ring the grab rails and shake dust off the racks.

While she is seated she is shaken by the car exactly like everything else. Once she is floating the shaking passes through the car around her but no longer reaches her: she hangs smooth while the carriage judders, which makes the weightlessness read instantly.




THE FOREST

For the first stretch, tall dense woodland crowds both sides of the track. Dark trunks in unbroken succession, sunlit green canopy behind and above them, undergrowth streaking past low near the ballast.

At this speed none of it is legible. Trunks are dark bands, foliage is smeared green, the canopy is a bright broken ceiling of leaves flickering beyond the glass.







THE OCEAN — THE TRAIN IS DEEP UNDER IT

For the second stretch the train runs far beneath the surface with the whole weight of the ocean above it. It is not on the water, not floating on the surface, not just below the surface, not on a bridge over the sea.

Through the windows there is water in every direction: dense blue on both sides, blue below, blue above. No sky. No horizon. No waterline. No visible surface. No boundary between air and water anywhere in frame.

Depth reads in the colour: deep saturated blue, darker toward the bottom of the panes and toward the far distance, lifting only slightly toward the top because the light comes from far above. Even at the very top of frame it is still clearly water.

Sunlight comes down from far above in long angled shafts, weakened by depth. Fine suspended particles and streams of small bubbles fill the water, all torn flat and rearward by the speed.

Fish pass by, but the train is far too fast for anything to linger. Silver schools flash in the mid-distance, flaring for a fraction of a second when a shaft crosses them, then gone behind a window pillar. Occasionally a single larger dark shape passes further out in the blue. The closest fish are pure horizontal streaks. They never keep pace, never swim alongside, never hold position, never get inside the car.

No coral, no reef, no rocks, no wrecks, no kelp, no seabed detail, no whales, no fantasy creatures. Just deep open water, shafts from far above, particles, bubbles and passing fish.




THE LOCATION CHANGES WITHOUT BEING SEEN

The forest becomes the deep ocean continuously and invisibly, in tiny increments every single frame, never in a step and never at a moment. There is no frame where a viewer could say the change happened.

No shoreline, no coast, no beach, no causeway, no waterline on the glass, no spray, no wave, no plunge, no dive, no submersion moment, no surface passing over the roof.

Every element hands over in parallel. Trunks become dark striations in the water. The bright leaf canopy above the windows does not become a water surface; it becomes deep blue open water, slightly paler than the lower panes because light comes from far above, but unmistakably water with no surface visible in it. Ground clutter becomes darker depth falling away. Dust outside becomes silt and plankton. Green walks evenly to deep ocean blue. Sky glare softens into diffuse water glow without ever dimming and without ever resolving into a sky. Bubbles arrive last and quietly.

All of this happens outside the windows. The interior does not participate.

The light does not acknowledge the change at all: same speed, same rhythm, same direction, same violence, in the water exactly as in the forest. Only the hue drifts from cold cyan-white toward blue-green and the wedge edges from leaf-torn to ripple-torn. If the underwater section reads as calmer, slower or softer than the forest section, it is wrong.




BOTH WINDOW WALLS STAY LIT

The windows are the brightest thing in this dark interior and they never go black.

In the forest they carry bright sunlit green beyond the trunks with glare burning through the gaps, always well above interior exposure. Underwater they carry lit blue with visible depth.

When the interior falls into deep shade the windows keep glowing and carry the frame, and @GIRL reads as a dark silhouette against them.




THE INTERIOR IS DRY AND SEALED

The water is outside the glass only. The inside of the car is exactly as dry and air-filled in the last frame as in the first.

The ceiling is ordinary painted metal throughout: ribbed panelling, perforated inserts, luggage racks, unlit fixtures. It never becomes water, never becomes a rippling surface, never becomes the sea seen from below, never gets wet, never drips.

No water anywhere inside: not on the ceiling, walls, aisle, seats, inner glass or on her. She is never wet.

No fish, no bubbles, no sea life ever get inside the car.




THE LOSS OF GRAVITY

Gradual, quiet and eerie. It arrives with the ocean and it happens in air, not in water.

First, gravity begins to fail while she is still sitting. Her hair lifts strand by strand instead of hanging down her back. Her clothing stops lying against her and starts to drift. The dust in the beams stops settling and begins to hang and turn. She feels lighter in the seat without knowing why, and shifts. Her shoulders move; her head drops as she glances down at herself. Her hand goes to the seat back beside her, casually at first, then harder. She presses down into the seat and it does not hold her. Small confusion, not panic.

Then she leaves the seat. Not by standing. Her body simply floats up out of it, still half in a sitting shape, knees bent, feet trailing, still turned away from the lens. Her hand holds the seat back a second longer and then slips off it. Her other hand goes out for something to hold and finds nothing.

Then she rises. Steadily and slowly through the volume of the car, past the seat crowns, past the tops of the window frames, up toward the luggage racks. Her body turns slowly and involuntarily, tipping off vertical, drifting toward horizontal, but never far enough to bring her face round to the lens. Her hair spreads out around her head. Her clothing floats loose. Her arms move with soft delay and reach for surfaces no longer within reach. She is awkward and human, not graceful: a real child who does not know how to be weightless, because nobody does.

By the end she is high in the car, floating near the ceiling, slowly turning, silhouetted against the glowing blue windows.

The whole rise is smooth and continuous. No jerk, no launch, no jump, no flying pose, no ballet. She is released, not propelled. She never swims, never strokes, never kicks, never blows bubbles.

Everything loose in the car does the same thing on the same schedule: dust hangs and turns, small particles rise, but nothing large moves and the seats and fittings stay bolted down.




TIMING

0.0s to 2.0s. She sits in the window seat, seen from behind, watching the forest. Her head sways with the car and settles. Wedges of light rip down the seat crowns and across the back of her head.

2.0s to 4.0s. She shifts in the seat. She pushes hair back behind her ear and lets her hand drop. Her head turns a few degrees to follow something already gone. A hard rail joint jolts her and she rides it without thinking.

4.0s to 6.0s. Outside, the forest is quietly becoming deep water. Inside, her hair starts lifting. She notices without understanding, glances down at herself, puts a hand on the seat back.

6.0s to 8.0s. She is lighter than she should be. Her grip tightens; she presses into the seat and it does not hold her.

8.0s to 9.5s. She floats up out of the seat, still in a sitting shape. Her hand slips off the seat back. A strong wedge of light crosses her as she lifts.

9.5s to 15.0s. She drifts upward through the car, turning slowly, past the seat crowns and the window tops toward the racks, ending high and near-horizontal beneath the ceiling. The carriage judders violently around her and she hangs perfectly smooth inside it. Final frame: @GIRL high in the car, floating and slowly turning, seen from behind, a dark shape against the glowing blue windows, deep ocean tearing past outside the glass.




AUDIO

Diegetic only. A violent continuous roar of wheels and bogies at line speed, dense low-end rumble under a rushing air sound along the shell, fast rail pulse, panels buzzing, hard knocks through the floor at every rail joint, the small creak of the seat as she shifts.

As the water arrives the air rush very gradually thickens into a water rush over a couple of seconds, so slowly that no single moment marks it. No bang, no crash, no impact. The wheel roar runs on unchanged underneath.

In the underwater half, dense underwater ambience over the same bogie roar at the same tempo, water tearing along the hull, low hum, bubble streams, rail knocks continuing.

No music, no voices, no narration, no subtitles. Nobody speaks.







LOCKS

Single continuous 15-second take. No cuts, no dissolves, no transitions, no montage, no slow motion, no speed ramp.

@GIRL is seen from behind for the whole take. Her face never appears, not even reflected in glass.

She sits until gravity releases her. She never stands up, never walks, never runs, never sits back down, never turns to camera.

She floats in air, never in water. Never swims, never gets wet. Her hair and clothing float as dry material in zero gravity.

Once floating, the car's shaking no longer reaches her.

The ceiling is painted train metal in every frame. Never water, never a rippling surface, never wet.

Every other seat is empty in every frame. No other passengers, no crew, no silhouette at the far door, no figure reflected in any window. No bags, no coats, no luggage.

No water inside the car at any point.

The train never slows, and never stops.

The forest becomes deep ocean invisibly, with no shoreline, no waterline, no surface, no sky, no submersion moment.

The light never pauses and never changes character between the two halves.

The camera never moves and never follows her.
