I wanted to see what it would feel like to spend a quiet evening in Minecraft. Here’s the simpler version of my workflow: make your character, use the references below, then generate a few short scenes.

Watch the finished reel

1. Make your character

Upload a clear selfie and a clothed full-body photo of yourself. Use the prompt below with GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst, 4:3. Keep the sheet you like and reuse that same image in every video scene.

The example below is mine — create your own instead of using my face. These prompts are adapted for your own character, so results will vary.

Use image 1 as the primary face and identity reference and image 2 as the body-proportion reference. Create one photorealistic 4:3 character sheet of this exact person on a flat neutral grey studio background. Preserve their own facial structure, skin tone, hair, facial hair and distinctive marks. Keep their natural build and realistic head-to-body proportions; do not shorten the legs or exaggerate the head. Three clean panels: full-body front three-quarter view, full-body rear three-quarter view, and a large head-and-shoulders three-quarter portrait. In the portrait, chin slightly lowered, eyes looking directly at the camera, relaxed attentive expression and natural asymmetry; no sad expression or forced smile. Outfit in every panel: matte black hip-length puffer jacket with moderate padding, no fur, black crew-neck shirt, a visible silver curb chain, black sweatpants with loose open hems, and clean white low-top Air Force 1 style sneakers. Empty bare hands, no phone. Same person and clothing across all panels. Soft neutral studio lighting, natural skin texture, level camera and undistorted proportions. No scene, snow, text or labels.

3. Generate the scenes

Open Cinema Studio → Video → Seedance 2.5. Use reference images / Omni mode, 4:3, 1080p, 6 seconds for each clip. Upload the images in the order listed above each prompt. Use your character sheet, not my example. These are asset references, not generated starting frames.

Copy a prompt or use Recreate, then attach its references. Generate the shots separately. Keep the same outfit, mountain and props. No generated music or dialogue; add your chosen track in the edit.

4. Put it together

Bring the clips into your editor in order: slip → view → close-up → fire → steak → quiet ending. Keep only the strongest moment from each clip and aim for about 15–20 seconds overall. Let the fire catching and the final view breathe a little longer.

Cut from the slip after you have regained your balance, so the next shot can begin calmly. Keep the sunset on the same side of the world when changing camera angles. Check that your face, outfit, feet and props stay consistent before choosing a take.

Add your music separately, keep the wind and fire low underneath it, and finish with a light touch of grain and halation. That’s it — your own quiet evening in Minecraft.
