CHARACTER AND LOCATION SWAP — Genjutsu. Replicate @Video 1 · 30s exactly — its timing, cuts, camera, staging and energy — changing only WHO performs and WHERE it happens.




INPUTS:

@Video 1 · 30s — the driving video (4:3, 30 s): two performers at a table. The LEAD (left, dark cardigan) builds a beat on a laptop + synth keyboard. The SECOND performer (right, beige cardigan) reacts and sings along, getting more and more animated. At ~21 s, after a white flash cut, the second performer is already in the right foreground close to the lens. At ~24.7 s there is a cut: both walk TOWARD the camera, dancing, while the camera pulls back, so they get smaller in frame.

@image4 — the CHARACTER (the lead): keep their face, hair, body type, outfit and accessories exactly as in the photo.

@Image 2 — the PET (the second performer): keep its species, breed, real size, colours, markings and any clothes or accessories exactly as in the photo. A REAL ANIMAL.

@Image 2 @Image 1 — the LOCATION: keep its architecture, furniture, colours and light exactly.




WHO REPLACES WHOM:

- LEAD → the character from  selfie@ mapped one-to-one onto his full performance.

- SECOND performer → the pet from @Image 2: his place in frame, his timing and his vocal parts only. Ignore his body movement and travel completely — take from him only the rhythm for the pet's head movement and singing.




LOCATION AND PROPS: everything happens in the location from @Image 2, @Image 1 shot like a bright, warm multi-camera sitcom in maximum photorealism, keeping the location's own light and colours. The table becomes a desk (or a suitable table already in the location) with a computer and a small synth keyboard in a style that fits the location — this is the instrument. The desk area and the final dance area are both inside this same location, clearly the same place.




TIMELINE:

0–21 s — At the desk. The character plays the keys and builds the beat, nodding and reacting, and raises their hands on the big moment like the lead. The pet stays in one fixed spot right beside the keyboard — sitting on the desk if it is a small animal, or sitting on the floor right next to the desk with its head at desk height if it is too big to sit on a desk realistically. Its body never moves: paws or feet planted, body and tail still, no shifting, no sliding, no swaying, no paw taps. Only its head moves — bobbing to the beat, turning toward the character and the screen, tilting, ears twitching, blinking. Whenever the second performer sings, talks or vocalizes in @Video 1 · 30s, the pet opens its mouth (or beak) and vocalizes the notes in sync with that voice, in its own species' way — mouth opening and closing on every sung note, an animal mouth shape, not human lips. On the big arms-up moment (~8–11 s) its body still does not move: it just lifts its head high and vocalizes wide open-mouthed.

~21–24.6 s — After the white flash cut, the pet is ALREADY in its close position in the right foreground near the lens — at the front corner of the desk, or on the floor beside it for a big animal — sitting still. It does not walk, lunge, lean or move toward or across the lens; its body stays still. It keeps its face to the lens, vocalizes the notes into it, tilts its head and blinks. The character keeps playing behind it.

24.7–30 s — After the cut, in a clear open area of the location (open floor indoors, open ground outdoors). The character dances slowly TOWARD the camera — small, slow steps, swaying, shoulders bouncing, arms loose, smiling — while the view slowly pulls back as in the original, so they get smaller in frame and everything behind them stays far behind. They never walk backward or deeper into the location. The pet moves slowly at their feet in its natural way (walking, trotting or hopping), staying beside them. Neither of them gets anywhere near furniture, walls or other obstacles.




PET SIZE (identical in every shot): the pet keeps its true real-world size for its species and breed, always measured against the character and the props. At the desk it is exactly as big as that animal really is next to the keyboard and the character's body; its head is in true proportion to the character's head (for example, a cat's head is about three-quarters the width of a human head). On the floor at the end, its height against the character's legs matches real life (for example, a cat's back reaches about mid-shin, a small dog's about the knee). It is the same pet at the same real size in the first shot and the last shot — never oversized at the desk, never shrunken at the end.




PET RULES (critical):

- Always a real animal of its species with natural proportions; it never rises into an upright human-like stance or walks on its hind legs (unless that is how its species naturally stands, like a bird), never takes a human pose, never copies the man's arm, hand, body or head-across-the-lens movements.

- Legs, paws, wings stay their natural length and shape — never stretched like a human arm, no fingers.

- Its head never swells, stretches or changes shape; it keeps its species' head shape and features, never human-like. Its face stays toward the lens — it never shows the back of its head to the lens.

- Any clothes or accessories from stay on and stay the same throughout.




PHYSICS: feet always on the ground; nobody steps onto, over or through furniture or objects; no clipping; the pet never slides or glides — it only moves by stepping, and only at the end; real weight, real contact shadows.




LIGHT AND COLOUR: maximum photorealism with a warm, bright sitcom feel, matched to the location's own light; both characters sit naturally in the space with matching light, shadows and film grain, not pasted on.




AUDIO: keep the original audio unchanged; the pet's mouth syncs to the second performer's vocal parts.




NEGATIVE: no pet moving, shifting, sliding or drifting at the desk — there only its head and mouth move; no paw taps, body sway or bounces at the desk; no change in the pet's size between shots — not huge at the desk, not tiny at the end; no pet with a closed mouth while the vocals play; no pet standing or walking like a human; no stretched, arm-like limbs; no human-shaped head or face on the pet; no pet approaching, lunging or moving across the lens at 21 s; no oversized pet head; no walking backward or deep into the location at the end; no stepping on, over or through furniture or objects; the location does not change between the desk shots and the final shot; no extra people or animals; the character keeps their exact face, hair and outfit, natural hands with five fingers; no warping, flicker or cut-out edges; no logos, text or watermarks; photoreal, not cartoon.
