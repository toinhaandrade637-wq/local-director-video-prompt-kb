I recreated one of TV’s most iconic battle scenes as a POV shot

I wanted to recreate the feeling of being inside a brutal snowy battlefield from a first-person point of view.
Not a clean remake. Not a polished crane shot.
I wanted it to feel like a real handheld POV moment — rough, cold, immersive, and close to the action.
The final shot is a mix of AI generation, 3D worldbuilding, camera tracking, compositing, and cleanup.

Final result

1) Build the battlefield base with GPT-6 Astra inside 3D Jutsu

i started by building the battlefield base with GPT-6 Astra inside 3D Jutsu.

i gave GPT-6 Astra inside 3D Jutsu a reference image of the kind of winter battlefield i wanted: cold ground, dead grass, arrows in the earth, distant forest, snowy mountains, and a heavy cloudy sky.

the goal here was not to generate the final shot in one go.
it was to get a strong starting point for the world.

Prompt for GPT-6 Astra

(use the reference image above in the Higgsfield Blender plugin, then open Scene Builder, set the model to GPT-6 Astra, and paste the prompt below.)

Use the provided reference image as the main visual guide and recreate a highly realistic 3D battlefield environment in Blender from a standing first-person POV.
9:16 format, Iphone camera wide 0.5 lens , 10.16 lenght in blender camera

Create a bleak open medieval battlefield with a cold northern atmosphere. The scene should be empty of characters and horses. Focus only on the environment.

The foreground should have detailed muddy ground with wet soil, trampled dead grass, uneven terrain, small depressions, rough battlefield texture, and a few scattered medieval arrows stuck in the ground or lying on the surface near the camera. Add natural irregularity and realistic surface breakup so the ground feels real and worn, not clean or procedural.

The midground should be a broad open battlefield plain, harsh and exposed, with subtle battlefield messiness and a raw, realistic feeling. If needed, include a few distant burnt wooden X-shaped battlefield structures placed far away and sparsely, so they do not dominate the shot.

The background should have a dark distant tree line of cold northern forest, with layered snow-dusted hills or mountains behind it. These distant elements should feel large, natural, and slightly softened by atmosphere.

The sky should be heavily overcast with thick gray-blue clouds, cold diffuse daylight, no visible sun, no warm sunset tones, and no dramatic fantasy lighting. The lighting should feel flat, cold, realistic, and harsh, like a real winter battlefield under dense cloud cover.

Add subtle atmospheric haze, a little depth in the air, and a faint sense of cold moisture. The whole environment should feel grounded, photorealistic, dirty, and natural, not stylized or cinematic-beautiful.

Important:
- no characters
- no horses
- no crowds
- no castles or buildings
- no fantasy effects
- no heroic composition
- no clean polished look

The final result should feel like a real raw medieval battlefield environment, viewed from the POV of a standing person, with realistic scale, believable terrain, and strong photorealism.







2) Refining the environment in blender

once the base was there, i took it into blender and pushed it further.

this was where the scene started to feel real.

i improved the ground, adjusted the materials, made the terrain feel more natural, and matched the mood closer to the shot i had in mind.




What i refined

3) filming the pov plate

for the live-action base, i filmed myself in my room with my phone.

i wore a rough outfit that could later be transformed into a darker medieval warrior look. i also used a simple prop in place of the sword and acted out the movement from a first-person point of view.

the idea was to capture the body motion and camera motion first, then upgrade the visual look later.

i acted out the moment like this:

standing in pov
dropping down as if hit or falling
rising back up
grabbing the sword
holding the frame in a way that still feels like a person is inside the scene




4) Converting the outfit and prop with genjutsu

after filming the plate, i created visual references for the outfit and the sword.

then i used genjutsu to transform the clothing and prop while keeping the original camera angle and movement.

this helped a lot because i could keep the natural handheld feel from the real footage, while replacing the rough homemade look with something much closer to the final world.

5) Camera tracking and compositing

once i had the plate, i tracked the phone camera in after effects.

then i transferred that motion into blender and aligned it with the battlefield environment.

this was the key step that connected the live-action movement to the 3d world.

after that, i rendered the environment and brought it back into after effects, where i masked and composited the transformed body into the battlefield.

the goal was to make the character feel fully inside the environment, not pasted on top.




compositing steps
track the original handheld phone footage
match the 3d camera to the tracked motion
render the battlefield pass
rotoscope and mask the transformed body
composite the body into the 3d environment
balance the color and atmosphere
6) Adding the cavalry

once the environment and character were working, the next big step was the cavalry.

i took a still from my battlefield view and used ai to add distant riders approaching from the far background.

then i used that as a base for motion generation and turned it into a moving cavalry charge with Seedance 2.5.

after that, i brought the result back into after effects, masked it where needed, and matched it to the existing camera movement and scene atmosphere.
