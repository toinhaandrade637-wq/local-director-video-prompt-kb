As a software engineer, my first question about any tool is: can I script it? When I found out that Higgsfield ships an MCP server, I wanted to know if I could run my image and video workflow from a chat with Claude instead of jumping between tabs.

Short answer: yes, and it changed how I iterate. In this post I show the exact workflow I used, the prompts, and the real credit costs, so you can copy it.

1. Setup in five minutes

Add Higgsfield as a custom connector in Claude: open Settings, then Connectors, choose Add custom connector, name it Higgsfield and paste the server URL (https://mcp.higgsfield.ai). Click Connect, sign in to your Higgsfield account and approve access. This is a one-time step. Then ask Claude to check your credit balance. If it answers, you are connected. Always check the balance first: it takes one message and saves you from a surprise mid-project.

2. Your first generation

Start small. Ask for one image, look at it, then decide what to change. I asked Claude for a hero image for this very post (Nano Banana 2, 16:9, 1k, 2 variants). This was my first prompt:

A software engineer's desk at night in Istanbul, glowing monitor showing a chat interface, Bosphorus lights in the window, cinematic lighting, shallow depth of field

What I learned from this first run:

A reference photo fixes who is in the picture better than any description.
If you care about what is on a screen, say it in the prompt. Generic wording gets you a generic screen.
Resolution is a setting, not a prompt. Check it before you judge the result.
I changed three things at once because all three were clearly wrong. For subtler problems, change one thing per attempt.
3. From a still to a video

The price check mattered here. Before generating anything, I asked Claude for the cost of the same 5-second, 720p clip on two models. Seedance 2.5 would have cost 35 credits, which is almost my entire balance. Grok Imagine Video 1.5 Lite cost 5 credits. For a blog illustration, the cheaper model was the right call.

Why this order works: iterating on a still costs about 2 credits, iterating on video costs 5 to 35. Lock the look first, then animate.

4. Three prompt blocks you can reuse

Block A: same person, new scene. Upload one reference photo, reuse it in every request, and only change the scene. Cost: 2 credits at 2k.

5. Keeping credits under control

My routine, which you can copy:

Check the balance before a session.
Draft with the cheapest settings that show the idea.
Only render the final video for the one winner.
Check the balance again and note what the session cost.

Here are my real numbers for making everything in this post: I started with 43.87 credits. Round 1 (two 1k variants) cost 3. Round 2 (two 2k variants with a reference photo) cost 4. Round 3 (one 5-second video, one stage image, three lighting variations and one detail fix) cost 13.5. I ended with 23.37 credits, so the whole post cost 20.5 credits.

6. Mistakes to avoid
Changing five things between attempts, so you cannot tell what helped.
Rendering video before the still is final.
Not checking the price first: the same clip cost 7 times more on another model.
Forgetting that the prompt is part of the result. Save the ones that work.
Wrap-up

Running Higgsfield from Claude did not replace the studio for me. It made the boring parts (comparing models, batching variations, checking prices and tracking spend) fast, so I could spend my time on the creative decisions.
