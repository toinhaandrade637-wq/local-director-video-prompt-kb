Video prompt 1
Input your selfie 
Make sure you tagged your selfie with @ in the prompt where it is marked red
and just tap ✨recreate✨

Perform a localized FACE-IDENTITY REPLACEMENT on the main female performer throughout the uploaded video, using  @selfie as the facial identity reference.




The source @video video is the sole authority for every costume, hairstyle, makeup look, scene, performance, and transition. The selfie supplies ONLY the underlying facial identity.




REFERENCE SCOPE:

Use @selfie for recognizable facial structure and features: eyes, nose, lips, cheeks, and facial proportions.




Completely ignore the selfie's hair, hairline, hairstyle, hair color, clothing, neckline, collar, shoulders, jewelry, glasses, makeup, background, lighting, and pose. Even a tiny visible piece of clothing or hair in the selfie must have ZERO influence on the video.




Do not replace the entire head or body. Keep the source head size, position, orientation, body proportions, and silhouette.




TARGET:

Replace only the recurring main female performer's face across her different cinematic roles. Follow her through changes of costume, hairstyle, makeup, framing, and location. Everyone else—including supporting actors, background performers, crew, and the child beside the train—remains unchanged.




HAIR AND WARDROBE ARE PROTECTED:

Preserve the exact hairstyle visible in EACH SOURCE FRAME: cut, length, color, bangs, parting, curls, braids, tied sections, loose strands, volume, hairline, and movement. Keep wigs and hairpieces exactly as filmed. Hair overlapping the face remains in front of the replacement face.




Preserve all original garments and accessories, including collars, necklines, ties, lapels, layers, patterns, seams, buttons, gloves, armor, capes, hats, glasses, and jewelry. Keep their colors, materials, fit, folds, and movement.




Never use selfie clothing to complete a partially hidden neckline or collar. Those areas must come from the source frame.




MAKEUP AND CHARACTER EFFECTS ARE PROTECTED:

Apply each source shot's existing makeup and effects to the replacement identity. Preserve lipstick, eyeliner, eyeshadow, blush, eyebrow styling, tears, wetness, redness, smudges, dirt, scratches, bruising, and any character makeup or prosthetics exactly where and when they appear.




Keep the amount, color, coverage, texture, and progression of these effects. Let them follow the replacement face naturally while retaining their source placement relative to facial landmarks. Do not clean up, beautify, remove, or replace them with the selfie's makeup.




PRESERVE THE EXISTING LOOKS:

• Office: short chestnut bob with bangs, dark rectangular glasses, dark pinstripe suit, mustard shirt, patterned tie, pocket square, and lapel decoration.

• Black-and-white comedy scenes: original monochrome rendering, comic styling, dark suit, shirt, neckwear, glasses, and the exact hat-on or hat-off state.

• Dim room with the circular window: tied-back dark hair with loose strands, glasses, dark green top, pale shoulder straps, and tearful facial appearance.

• Kitchen: shoulder-length brown hair, glasses, tan outer layer, striped top, and the original emotional makeup state.

• Warrior scenes: braided hair, armor, dark cloak, and the precise dirt and battle makeup on the face.

• Tunnel: original hair, headgear, goggles, green clothing, gloves, straps, and equipment.

• Train and superhero studio scenes: original loose hair, blue suit, gold details, and red cape.

• Warm close-up: original tied-back hairstyle, earrings, dark knit clothing, and warm scene lighting.

• Final makeup shot: chestnut bob with bangs, dark rectangular glasses, dark turtleneck, and the makeup brush touching the cheek.




Keep each look only in its corresponding source shots. Do not carry an outfit, hairstyle, or makeup state into another scene. Do not freeze the first look across the whole video.




EDITING AND PERFORMANCE:

Preserve every original cut, match cut, camera wipe, foreground crossing, and costume or location change at its exact source timing. During transitions, each visible part of the image must retain the appearance from its corresponding source frame.




Preserve expressions, blinks, gaze, mouth movements, speaking timing, head motion, and lip synchronization. Keep correct occlusion behind glasses, hair, shields, hands, the makeup brush, and passing people or equipment. Do not reveal a face that is hidden or turned away.




Preserve all backgrounds, props, choreography, camera movement, framing, focus, lighting, color grading, grain, motion blur, subtitles, graphics, duration, aspect ratio, and original audio. Keep black-and-white footage black-and-white.




Only the main performer's facial identity changes. All styling and all changes of styling remain exactly as established by the source video, frame by frame.
