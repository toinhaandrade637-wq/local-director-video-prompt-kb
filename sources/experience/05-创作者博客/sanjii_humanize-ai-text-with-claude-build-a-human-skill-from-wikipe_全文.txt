Before You Start

This is the exact setup from the video. You get Claude to learn every habit that gives AI writing away and ban all of them. Then you pack the rules into a skill you call with /human. I ran the output through AI checkers and every single one came back 0% AI.




The rules don't come from guesswork. Wikipedia editors keep a page called "Signs of AI writing", a field guide to the patterns that expose AI text, built from real cases they caught on the site. It changes as the models change. Detectors look for the same habits, so once they're gone there's very little left to catch.




What you need: Claude (the free plan works), with "Create and edit files" switched on in Settings > Capabilities and the Skill Creator switched on in Customize > Skills. Turn on web search too, for Prompt 2.

You'll see the difference in the next answer. The em dashes go first, and Claude needed that more than most: a July 2026 study cited on the same Wikipedia page found it's the only current model that uses em dashes more often than professional writers do. The stock transitions go next ("Additionally", "Moreover"), and so do words like "delve" and "testament" and every "it's not just X, it's Y" line. The last thing to break is that stiff rhythm where every sentence runs the same length.

Here's what's inside:

Every red flag on Wikipedia's page as of October 2026, including the newest ones that older humanizer prompts miss: the 2026 batch of model words like "quietly" and "leverage", and hedges like "should be seen as X rather than Y".
The em dash ban, plus a fix for what comes after it. Models that are blocked from em dashes fall back on commas and full stops alone, and Wikipedia now lists that flat punctuation as a sign of its own. The skill keeps colons, parentheses, questions and the odd semicolon in the mix.
Rhythm rules. The newest models tend to hold every sentence to 8 to 20 words, so the skill mixes three-word sentences with long ones, the way people actually write.
Plain words over stiff ones: "use" instead of "utilize", "tried" instead of "attempted", "has" instead of "boasts", "is" instead of "serves as".
A voice mode. Paste a few things you wrote and it copies your sentence length, your words, your punctuation and how formal you are, instead of some generic "human" voice.
A check mode: /human check lists every AI tell in a text without rewriting it.
A self-audit. Before you see anything, Claude runs the draft through a checklist and a small script that flags em dashes, banned words, same-length sentences and flat punctuation.
Hard limits. It never touches your facts, names, numbers or links, and it never pulls tricks like fake typos or hidden characters.

Two strict rules before you hit enter:

Give it your voice once. Without samples, /human writes like a careful stranger. With two or three things you actually wrote, it writes like you.
Refresh the rules every few months. The Wikipedia page keeps moving as new models come out (it now dates em dash overuse as a 2022 to September 2026 sign). The last prompt below pulls in anything new.
