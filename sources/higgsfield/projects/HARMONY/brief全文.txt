WHERE HARMONY COMES FROM




This story is loosely inspired by my own life. In November 2025, I proposed to my girlfriend. She said yes. We're getting married on November 29th, and her ring holds a jade stone, a small green promise: Jade is the name we've already chosen for our future daughter. That green stone became the heart of this film, and the reason behind the color of the final reveal.

I'm also a musician. Music changed the course of my life, and through the internet I've taught thousands of people to play the guitar. So when I imagined a man who had lost his colors, I knew exactly what would bring them back. In my life, music was the awakening. In this film, it is too.

Henri's story is not my story. My proposal got a yes on the very first try. HARMONY is my way of saying that music can show you colors you've never seen, and that through it, you can find your way back to joy.

THE FILM

Henri loves Jade, and asks her to marry him. She says no. And the reason is the colors.

Henri used to be a funny, good-humored musician. Over time he became a serious, melancholic, methodical man, and that cooled things between him and Jade. The thing is, he doesn't realize who he's become. That's why every refused proposal frustrates him. He can't understand what's missing, because what's missing is himself.

The film tells this through image. Everything is blue: the food, the clothes, the light. Nobody inside the story notices. The blue is today's Henri, cold and faded, seen from within. The only warm objects are the musical instruments, hanging forgotten on the wall. Color lives exactly where he stopped looking. The flashback of the past is in full color, because when he used to sing, their life had color in it.

Jade loves Henri. Her refusal is not a lack of love. It's a refusal to say yes to a man who forgot who he was. Her yes is waiting for the real Henri to come back.

He tries three times, over the course of months. The plant in the kitchen grows, marking time.

Until he finally sees what was always hanging on the wall: the guitar. He plays and sings to her, and something happens. The ending comes down to one great harmony, and it will be a surprise.


ABOUT THE SOUND




Every one of the nine music cues in this film carries the same melody, in the same key: the song Henri finally sings at the end. Before he sings it, you have already heard it as tension, as grief, as a hero's theme, in different harmonies and different moods. Because the key never changes, when the song finally arrives you recognize it without knowing why. The film teaches you the song before it is sung.




The video moves in time with the music. The wall clock, Jade brushing the flour off her clothes, Henri's footsteps as he rehearses: the action is set to the rhythm of the score, so that watching becomes a way of hearing, and hearing a way of watching.




All voices, guitar and score are AI-generated. When the two voices finally join, the song reaches its peak on the moment she says yes.

Intentional details of the film

The opening: her care

The beginning of the film shows the care Jade has for the house and for Henri. She cooks breakfast for him every single day, and that is how the film opens: with her hands cracking a blue egg into a bowl, the blue yolk sliding out. Her daily routine of love is introduced before any face is shown.

And one important detail: her nails are chipped, with flaking blue polish. This shows that she takes better care of the house and of him than of herself. Jade gives herself away every day in small gestures, and lets go of herself in the process. Her nails tell this story in silence, in every shot of her hands, from the first frame of the film to the very last.

HARMONIA: what gives the film its name

The film is called HARMONIA because everything in it moves toward a single moment: at the end, a song happens.

There isn't much that can be said without giving it away. But this much can be said: Henri spends the whole film trying to solve alone something that can only be solved by two. And harmony, in music, is exactly that: one voice alone can be beautiful, but it is when the second voice joins that the song can sustain itself.

When that song happens, the audience will understand the entire film at once: the blue, the instruments, the book, the refusals. Everything was waiting for it.

A CLOSING NOTE




This film asks the audience to do one thing: notice what nobody inside

it can.

Every choice in HARMONY was made so the viewer sits inside Henri's head

and still knows something Henri doesn't. That gap is where the film

lives. The moment it closes is the ending.

I did not want to make a film about a man who lost his colour. I wanted

the viewer to sit in that colour long enough to want it gone.
