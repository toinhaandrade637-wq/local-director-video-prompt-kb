A story as old as the mountains. A word strong enough to wake the dead.

✨ Context

BESA is an original cinematic interpretation of "Kostandini dhe Doruntina," a thousand-year-old Albanian folk legend. In the highlands, Besa is not simply a word. It is a binding moral force: an oath that outlives the man who gave it. The film asks one question: what kind of promise can command the dead?

🎭 Narrative

On the night of Doruntina's wedding, before she is carried far from her mountain home, her brother Kostandin gives their mother his Besa, his sworn word: whenever she longs for her daughter, he will bring her home.

Then death takes all nine of her sons.

At the ninth grave, the grieving mother demands the word her son gave her. And the earth answers.

The story unfolds across two converging timelines: the candle-gold world of the oath, the wedding and the loss, and the steel-blue present of a night journey that should be impossible. Dialogue is minimal; the film is carried through image, silence, sound and physical performance. No prior knowledge of the legend is required.

🎨 Visuals




Photorealistic historical realism in the Albanian highlands: a powerful family's stone 'kulla', a winter cemetery of exactly nine graves, remote mountain roads under one believable moon beneath the clouds.

Two visual worlds share the frame and gradually converge: the muted golden warmth of memory in candlelight and firelight, and the steel-blue winter darkness of the present in moonlight and mist. A single red wool thread crosses both timelines: the promise itself, the only warm color in the cold world.

Everything is tactile and of its time: stone, wool, linen, wood, earth, snow and horsehair. Spiritual and mysterious, never zombie horror, never fantasy spectacle.

🎵 Sound




The world is heard as it was: wind, stone, fire, breath and hoofbeats. No conventional score, only the instruments the highlands knew, led by the LAHUTA, the one-string epic fiddle whose voice has carried this legend for a thousand years.
