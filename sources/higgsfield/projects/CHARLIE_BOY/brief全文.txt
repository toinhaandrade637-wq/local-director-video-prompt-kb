A young soldier's initiation mission asks him to become a weapon. Weapons aren't supposed to have a choice.

✨ Context

I think that a huge aspect of working with AI is not falling behind when it comes to new tools and models. I take festivals and contests as an opportunity to express myself and allow my creativity to not be restrained, while at the same time having the chance to master the latest technology available.

🎭 Narrative

This story is quite personal, because I do relate to Charlie in a lot of aspects. Being young and foolish is part of growing up, and there comes a point where you must choose what kind of person you want to be. Are you going to let others decide for you? Are you going to obey authority without questioning it? Are you going to find your own righteous path? Are you able to find the good in you and do your part to make this world a little bit better, even if that won't change things? Those were the questions I was asking myself while doing this film.

🎨 Visuals

I just get inspired from everything that I love. For this short film there were some obvious references that you just can't avoid (I'm looking at you, Star Wars), so instead of trying to subvert them, I just surrendered myself to them. There were also more subtle references. My favourite director, Danny Boyle, inspired the Charlie look (he's basically a young Renton from Trainspotting) and I tried to portray the character with the same dignity, fearlessness and tenderness that he gave to the character of Spike in 28 Years Later. There's also a not-so-subtle reference to William Blake which I like to think is not just a gratuitous one but a thematic one:  I feel like Blake's poetry is all about innocence and the corruption of it, and I tried to give that same thematic purpose to Charlie Boy. And even though there are no actual references, the soundtrack in the making of this short film was mostly Interpol.

🎬 Production

Just me at the desk, drinking hundreds of cans of Diet Coke and having the most chaotic computer desktop you have ever seen. Definitely need to work on those aspects. Thankfully Camila managed to make some sense out of that chaotic energy, obsessively curating  thousands of generations. Bless her.

🚀 Status

There's a famous quote by David Fincher that says: "You don't finish films. You abandon them." I can say I finally abandoned Charlie Boy. It was really hard, though. But you gotta let go, man. It's a part of life.

🔗 Resources

Just Higgsfield and Suno. No storyboard; the script was done along with the generations. That's, to me, the beauty of AI: you don't have schedules, you don't have a crew of hundreds, why would you put limits on yourself? I like to think I have the same freedom as a painter or a writer, where the only limits are the ones of your imagination.

📝 Notes

I'm really proud of this film. And I really love Charlie. Hopefully we'll get to see him again soon.
