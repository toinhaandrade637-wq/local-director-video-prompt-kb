When souls lose themselves to trauma, depression, grief, or anger, they awaken hollowed out inside a mental hell that feeds on their memories, identities, and hope. One Hollow Heart must cross its living regions and heal the wound at the source. If he fails, the realm will consume what remains of him, and its prisoners will remain trapped forever.

A psychological dark fantasy film 


VOICES IN THE VOID : HOLLOW HEART [TEASER TRAILER]




✨ Context

I have spent more than a decade creating music videos and visual content, developing experience across directing, cinematography, editing, visual effects, graphic design, sound, and music. My work has always been driven by the same instinct: to create something that stands apart, push an idea as far as it can go, and take creative risks that others might hesitate to attempt. Now, that experience is expanding into narrative storytelling and film.

Voices in the Void: Hollow Heart grew from that creative instinct, but its foundation is deeply personal.

My content has often explored pain, identity, loneliness, relationships, and the struggles people carry internally. After experiencing periods of depression, loneliness, and unresolved trauma myself, I became deeply interested in understanding why certain experiences stay with us, how unresolved pain can affect the people around us, and how the mind can keep someone trapped long after the original experience has passed.

That search led to years of studying mental health, trauma, human behavior, grief, cognitive distortions, and the patterns that shape how we see ourselves and others. Understanding those ideas helped me make sense of experiences I had lived through and eventually helped me move beyond some of them.

Over time, supporters who connected with my work began trusting me with their own stories. They shared experiences of depression, grief, loneliness, difficult relationships, fear, anger, and moments when they felt lost. What I learned while trying to understand myself sometimes gave me the perspective to help someone else recognize that the place they were in did not have to be permanent.

That became the emotional foundation of Hollow Heart.

I wanted to take struggles that normally exist inside us and make them physically visible. Depression can drain a person of energy and motivation until getting out of bed or simply moving through the day feels overwhelming. In this world, that feeling becomes literal. Some souls have stopped moving for so long that they have begun sinking into the floors and walls around them. Trauma can physically take hold of someone and prevent them from moving forward. Throughout the film, internal experiences become environments, creatures, and consequences that can actually be seen and felt.

But beneath the darkness is hope.

The hero is fighting to save himself, but if he finds a way through his pain, his survival becomes proof to the other trapped souls that they are not beyond saving either.

Sometimes surviving your own darkness gives you the understanding to help someone else survive theirs.

Hollow Heart is one story within Voices in the Void, a larger world I want to explore through different characters, different mental health struggles, and the ways unresolved pain can reshape identity, perception, and hope.

At its core, the world asks one question:

What happens when the unresolved pain we carry inside us becomes a world of its own?

🎨 Visuals

Voices in the Void: Hollow Heart translates psychological suffering into environments, creatures, and physical laws.

The visual philosophy is simple. Nothing exists only because it looks interesting. Every major creature, location, transformation, and piece of anatomy begins with something human, then pushes that idea into physical form.

Trauma becomes something that can hold you. Depression becomes stillness and endless repetition. Grief becomes beauty that is painful to leave behind. Anger turns injury into a weapon. Hope becomes something fragile enough to be destroyed, but stubborn enough to grow again.

The film follows only one path through a much larger universe. Each realm extends far beyond the locations shown here, containing other settlements, ecosystems, creatures, and manifestations of the mental states that shape them. The Hollow Heart takes the fastest surviving route toward the source because the longer he remains in the Void, the more memory, identity, and hope he risks losing.

THE HOLLOW HEART




The Hollow Heart is the central visual symbol of the film.

The cavity through his chest is not the result of one wound. It is what years of unresolved trauma, depression, grief, and avoidance have become in the Void. He survived what happened to him, but carried it without truly healing until the emptiness he felt inside became physical.

His heart is gone, and the world is visible through the space where it should be.







As the journey wears him down, his clothing, scars, hair, and exhaustion change, but the cavity remains. His journey is not about erasing what happened to him.

It is about reclaiming the part of himself that pain convinced him was gone.

EMPATHY




Empathy represents something the Void has never been able to completely erase.

She is formed from the accumulated traces of compassion left behind in this world. Every moment someone listened. Every act of kindness. Every hand extended toward another person when they could no longer stand alone.







Her crescent shaped head represents incomplete light.

She cannot illuminate the entire darkness.

She only reveals enough of the path for someone to take another step.

Wherever she walks, small plants emerge beneath her feet. They bloom briefly and then die because the land has been saturated with suffering for too long to sustain them.







But another plant grows with her next step.

The world can destroy every individual bloom.

It cannot stop her from creating another.

Empathy can guide the Hollow Heart toward healing, but she cannot heal him herself. Compassion can accompany someone through suffering, but it cannot make the final decision for them.

THE TRAUMA REALM




The Trauma Realm represents what remains after something painful has ended, but part of the person is still living inside its aftermath.

Here, that damage has reshaped the world itself. Monumental figures have hardened into stone, pieces of architecture hang suspended in the air, and entire sections of the landscape appear fractured and unable to settle back into place.

The event may be over, but the world it created inside you can remain.




BROKEN HOMES

These fractured homes are not ruins from a battle. They represent lives that continued after trauma without ever fully healing from it.

Walls split apart, rooms hang suspended in the air, and pieces of entire homes remain disconnected from one another, mirroring what unresolved pain can do to families, relationships, and a person’s sense of safety.

People still live among the fragments because life did not stop when they were hurt.

They simply learned how to build around what was broken.







Above everything hangs an unnatural moon, reinforcing the feeling that this realm exists somewhere between memory and reality. Even the sky feels trapped in the same unresolved state as the people beneath it.

Do not explain the moon more than that. Let the image carry most of the mystery.







Some creatures within the Trauma Realm collect and hoard Hollow Hearts, physically clutching the damaged souls that arrive there rather than allowing them to move forward.

That behavior reflects the governing idea of the realm.

Trauma does not always remain because someone chooses to remember it.

Sometimes it is the thing refusing to release them.







THE HOLLOWED MINDS




The Hollowed Minds are what remains when a Hollow Heart loses too much of itself.

Some are created after dying and being reconstructed too many times within the Void, each return costing another piece of memory, identity, and hope. Others are souls who finally surrender, allowing everything negative inside them to consume what little of the person remains.

Their crescent shaped heads are not masks. They are the physical result of a mind being hollowed out until almost nothing human is left.







They hunt anyone who still carries hope. When they find it, they siphon it away, harvest the weakened soul, and return it to the realm before that person can escape.

They do not simply destroy hope. They make sure no one else sees proof that escape was ever possible.

They are basically the Void's immune system against recovery.

The Grasp tries to bring you back.

The Hollowed Minds make sure hope itself cannot spread.







THE TRAUMA TREE




The Trauma Tree is one of the oldest visible wounds in the region.

Its roots spread through bodies, homes, roads, and structures, binding the inhabitants to the place that damaged them.

The roots can even reconstruct someone after death, but survival comes at a cost. Each return takes something else from the person, another piece of memory, identity, or self. Eventually after to many deaths they return with no mind at all.

The tree offers survival without recovery.

People can continue living around the wound indefinitely without ever becoming free of it.







THE GRASP




The Grasp embodies control, attachment, and the part of trauma that refuses to let someone move forward.

When it senses a soul beginning to regain hope or attempting to leave, it becomes relentless. It hunts aggressively, seizes them with its enormous hands, and drags them back toward the place they were trying to escape. Control is not simply how it attacks. Control is its purpose.







Human faces emerge across its hands and limbs, suggesting the memories, fears, voices, and relationships that can become tangled with unresolved trauma. Some forms accumulate more and more hands until they become enormous crawling organisms, built almost entirely from the need to hold on.

The Grasp does not want you dead.

It wants you back where you were, convinced you were never meant to leave.




THE DEPRESSION REALM




The Library of Broken Dreams is only one location inside the much larger Depression Realm.

Beyond it are places shaped by isolation, exhaustion, hopelessness, self neglect, and the gradual disappearance of possibility.

The library represents one particular trap.

Rumination.

It appears endless because regret rarely provides its own ending.

Every corridor offers another shelf.

Every shelf offers another life.

Every life offers another version of who someone believes they could have been.

THE LIBRARY OF BROKEN DREAMS




Every book in the Library belongs to one person.

Its pages contain the life they lived alongside the life they believe they should have lived. Regrets, abandoned dreams, missed chances, relationships, failures, fears, and moments when they chose not to pursue something that once mattered to them are all preserved inside.







The books span every age of that person’s life, allowing them to return again and again to the moments they wish they could change.

Nothing inside can be rewritten.

The danger is not remembering the past. It is becoming so consumed by the life you did not live that you stop creating the life you still can.







THE THOUGHT TRAP




The Thought Trap lives above the Library of Broken Dreams, suspended in webs of paper, memory, and regret.

It feeds on repetition. The more a person replays the same failure, missed chance, or abandoned dream, the more entangled they become. Its webs stretch across the library, catching loose pages, broken pathways, and the souls trying to move through them.

In this realm, overthinking becomes physical.

The past is no longer just something remembered. It becomes something that traps you.




THE REGRETS




The Regrets are souls forced to read their own books again and again.

Each book contains the life they lived alongside the dreams they never fulfilled, the chances they did not take, the choices they cannot stop replaying, and the version of themselves they believe they should have become. They are trapped in an endless cycle of rereading everything they lost, abandoned, or overthought.




Depression can leave a person so heavy and drained that even moving feels impossible. In this realm, that feeling becomes literal. The longer they remain trapped in their own regrets, the more they begin sinking into the books, shelves, floors, and walls around them.

They are not simply remembering the past.

They are being condemned to relive it until someone, or something, helps them find a way out.







THE INNER CRITICS




The Inner Critic is self judgment given a body.

These creatures form from books, accusations, and remembered voices that have accumulated enough emotional weight to become predatory.

They keep The Regrets reading.

They prevent them from closing the book, leaving the chair, or believing their story can still change.

Some carry weapons formed from human faces.







Each face represents a voice that remained inside someone long after the person who spoke those words was gone.

You failed.

You waited too long.

You were never enough.

Eventually the cruelest voice no longer needs to come from another person.

The mind learns to repeat it by itself.







THE GRIEF REALM




Grief is intentionally one of the most beautiful regions in the film.

Enormous flowers and dreamlike organisms bloom across the landscape before slowly melting back into it.

They represent memories that can remain precious without remaining unchanged forever.

The danger of this realm is not forgetting.

It is becoming so devoted to preserving what was lost that life around the memory stops moving.

The water becomes the boundary between carrying the past and disappearing inside it.

The canoe is the fragile line separating those two states.







THE LOVESICK




The creatures beneath these waters are called The Lovesick.

Their bodies borrow from flowers, butterflies, coral, and other delicate forms because grief does not always trap someone through fear.

Sometimes it offers familiarity.

Beauty.

Comfort.

The feeling of remaining close to someone or something that is gone.

The Lovesick do not attack simply because the Hollow Heart approaches them.

They become violent when he chooses to leave.

That is the moment grief becomes possession.

They would rather pull him beneath the water than allow him to carry the memory forward without remaining trapped inside it.







THE ANGER REALM




The Anger Realm is where everything accumulated in the earlier realms begins moving outward.

It is not dangerous because anger is inherently wrong.

It is dangerous because unresolved pain has finally been given the ability to act.

The land resembles an endless battlefield built from reactions that cannot be taken back.







Trees become weapons.

Ruins become cages.

Conflicts continue long after anyone remembers what originally caused them.

Nothing here has learned how to release pain without transferring it somewhere else.







THE SELF SABOTEUR




The Self Saboteur carries its injury and its weapon in the same anatomy.

A massive blade passes directly through the place where thought should originate.

Its mind has become a weapon against itself.

Every violent action pushes that damage further.

It can hurt others, but it cannot attack anything without also deepening its own wound.

THE FALSE SELF




The False Self has worn so many identities for protection that the original face can no longer be found.

Confidence.

Cruelty.

Indifference.

Anger.

Whatever expression is required to avoid exposing what remains underneath.

The masks were originally forms of protection.

Eventually there was nothing left behind them.

THE PROJECTION




The Projection makes language physically dangerous.

Its tongue has become a blade.

What begins inside the creature is thrown outward toward everyone around it.

Every word can wound another person, yet the weapon must first cut through the creature's own mouth before reaching anyone else.

The design turns a simple human experience into anatomy.

Words can cut, and pain passed outward does not leave the person carrying it untouched.

THE GRUDGE

The Grudge is resentment that has been carried for so long it has become a living instinct.

Its massive hand shaped body exists for one purpose: to hold on. Every additional hand and crawling limb represents another betrayal, humiliation, argument, or wound it refuses to release. The more resentment it carries, the larger and more distorted it becomes.

It does not need the original conflict to still exist.

It keeps it alive by replaying it, feeding it, and reaching back toward it again and again.

For The Grudge, the wound is never over because letting go would mean losing the identity it built around being hurt.




WHAT THE WORLD LEAVES BEHIND

Hollow Heart is built from emotions that are difficult to explain but easy to recognize once they are given a shape.

The creatures, landscapes, and transformations are meant to feel unfamiliar at first, then strangely personal the longer you look at them.

The goal was never just to make the darkness look beautiful. It was to make it feel familiar.




🎬 Production

Voices in the Void: Hollow Heart was developed through a primarily solo digital production workflow. I handled the creative direction, visual development, creature and environment design, shot planning, AI generation, editing, compositing, visual effects, sound design, color work, and final assembly.

The environments and creatures were not generated first and explained afterward. Each major visual began with a story or psychological idea, then went through repeated design and animation passes until the imagery, movement, camera work, and symbolism felt consistent with the world.

AI allowed me to approach the project at a scale that would normally require a much larger crew, but the process still depended heavily on iteration and direction. Shots were tested, rejected, rebuilt, reframed, and edited repeatedly to maintain continuity across characters, environments, lighting, anatomy, and performance.

There were no traditional filming locations or large physical production teams. The film was built through a digital pipeline that combined AI image and video generation with traditional editing, compositing, sound, and post production techniques.

For me, the production challenge was not simply generating images. It was directing thousands of individual creative decisions until they felt like they belonged to the same film.

🚀 Status

Voices in the Void: Hollow Heart is in active development, with its core world, visual language, major realms, creature designs, and central story foundation now established.

The current work represents the first substantial realization of a much larger concept. The character design, environmental storytelling, psychological mythology, and cinematic language developed for Hollow Heart have created a foundation that can continue far beyond this first journey.

The next stage is focused on expanding the narrative, refining performance and continuity, exploring unseen regions of the Void, and pushing the overall experience to a higher level of cinematic polish.

Hollow Heart is intended to be one story within a larger Voices in the Void universe, with room for future episodes, new characters, separate stories, spin offs, and eventually even interactive experiences such as a video game.

What began as an experiment in turning internal struggles into a visual world has grown into something I believe can support many different stories while remaining connected by the same emotional and psychological foundation.

🔗 Resources

Audio Production
Empathy’s voice-over and the Inner Critic vocal performances were generated with Seed Audio 1.0. Original music was created with Suno. Additional dialogue, creature vocals, and environmental sound were generated natively within selected video generations where appropriate. Final audio editing, cleanup, balancing, layering, and mixing were completed in VEGAS Pro. Final audio stems used in the film are included in the festival project.

📝 Notes

Hollow Heart expands the larger world of Voices in the Void and represents how far I want to push its storytelling, visual language, and psychological mythology.

Building this world is highly iterative. A single creature, environment, or cinematic sequence can go through many rounds of design, testing, rejection, refinement, and continuity work before it becomes something I am willing to use. The ideas are not limited by what I want to create as much as by the time and generation capacity required to fully explore them.

Additional grant support and generation credits would give me the freedom to push that process further, build more ambitious sequences, explore unseen regions of the Void, develop new creatures and stories, and refine each idea until it reaches the level I originally imagined.

I already know what I want to build. More resources would simply allow me to go further without having to compromise the scale of the vision.

There were hurdles, setbacks, long sleepless nights, and moments when the whole process felt overwhelming enough to make me want to quit. But each time, I found a way to keep going. Quite an adventure.

Thank you for taking the time to read and watch. I am truly honored.
