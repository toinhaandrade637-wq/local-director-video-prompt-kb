On March 11, 2011, a veteran engineer at Fukushima Daiichi keeps the plant running through Japan’s most powerful earthquake, certain the station has survived the worst. When the first tsunami breaks against the seawall, he bends protocol and lets a young employee drive home to her family, who have stopped answering. Minutes later, a second, massive wave overwhelms the plant — and her car. As the cooling systems die and the reactor temperature rises, the engineer watches his faith in the station collapse with it.

✨ Context

Fukushima is a three-minute fiction film based on the real events of March 11, 2011 at the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear power plant. The characters are fictional, and the film is an artistic interpretation of the events rather than a literal reconstruction.




The film stays close to one engineer as a place he has trusted for decades begins to fail around him. The story explores the moment when professional certainty turns into helplessness, and a small human decision becomes part of a much larger catastrophe..

🎭 Narrative

A veteran engineer guides the plant through the earthquake and believes the worst has passed. After the first tsunami fails to breach the seawall, he lets a young employee leave to find her family.




Minutes later, a second wave overwhelms the station and catches her car. As the cooling systems fail and the reactor temperature rises, the situation moves beyond his control.

🎨 Visuals

The film uses a grounded, documentary-style visual language: natural camera movement, restrained performances and an environment that feels observed rather than staged.




The entire film unfolds as one continuous shot, keeping the audience with the engineer in real time as routine turns into chaos.

🎬 Production

The one-shot structure makes continuity central to the production. Characters, geography, camera movement and destruction have to remain consistent throughout the film.
