A burlesque western in the spaghetti-western tradition. A story of a con, of love, and of hopeless outlaws deeply cinematic, built for an international audience. Produced by Outreal Production.

✨ Context

Las Bodas del Río is our short film, in competition at the Higgsfield Global Film Festival, directed by Jacob & Ismaël. It embodies a single conviction: that you can tell a genre story funny, fast, character-driven with the ambition of real cinema. The project works in two directions: as a self-contained short film, and as a proof of concept for a longer-form story.

🎭 Narrative

Villamar, Luis Sebastián de Villamar  is a silver-tongued outlaw, a charmer who pulls off his heists with words rather than a trigger. For his finest job, he invites himself to the wedding of Isabella del Río, daughter of one of the richest and most feared senators in the county, married  to an American banker. Legend says the del Río family guards a treasure beyond all price.

Villamar gathers his crew: Ficelle, the barman you'd trust with your life until one drink too many; the Mountain, a giant of boundless kindness disguised as a priest, whom you must never wake; and two musicians, Blond Juan, a virtuoso and a hopeless lover of women, and Blond Quichotte, as gifted with a tune as he is catastrophic with a pistol.

The plan is perfect on paper. It unravels, methodically every role handed to exactly the wrong temperament until everything descends into chaos.

Theme. The film is about perfect plans undone by human nature and how, sometimes, it's that very spark of madness that changes everything. Villamar is no infallible mastermind; he's a storyteller rewriting his own legend. Beneath the burlesque runs a quiet irony: the ones who ruin everything are sometimes the ones who pull off the impossible.

🎨 Visuals

The signature. Spaghetti-western through and through: 35mm grain, reverb-drenched twangy guitar, a lonely whistle, mariachi trumpet, a teal-and-amber grade, wide skies and open desert. Grand and solemn with a constant comedic wink.

The rule of seriousness. The image stays cinematic and grave no matter how absurd the action. The comedy lives in the contrast between the solemnity of the frame and the foolishness within it. Never underline the gag with a cheap visual.

References. Sergio Leone and Ennio Morricone for the grammar; the weathered "wanted poster" imagery; the Spanish mission and the desert as a recurring horizon.

Palette. Clean whites and warm light for the wedding; teal and amber for the desert exteriors; golden end-of-day dust.
